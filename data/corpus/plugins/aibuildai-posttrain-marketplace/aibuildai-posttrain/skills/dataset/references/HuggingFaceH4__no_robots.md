# HuggingFaceH4/no_robots

10,000 human-written instruction/response pairs, mostly single-turn, hand-authored by paid annotators across ten task categories - no model was used to generate the data.

**HuggingFaceH4/no_robots** was built by Hugging Face researchers and modelled after the closed instruction dataset described in OpenAI's "Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback" (InstructGPT) paper [1], though no paper introduces this dataset itself - its own card is the only description [2]. The card's subtitle states it is "an instruction dataset that wasn't generated by GPTs" [2], and its summary describes the 10,000 rows as "instructions and demonstrations created by skilled human annotators", intended "for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to make language models follow instructions better" [2]. The Hugging Face alignment-handbook repository lists the dataset's release alongside the training code used to reproduce Zephyr-7B-β, but does not itself claim Zephyr was trained on it [3]. **The data is released under a noncommercial licence (CC BY-NC 4.0); commercial fine-tuning use is not covered by the grant.** It lives at https://huggingface.co/datasets/HuggingFaceH4/no_robots .

**Use it for**: SFT chat training on plain instruction/response pairs and short multi-turn dialogues, restricted to noncommercial use by its licence. The `messages` column is already a list of `{role, content}` dicts, the conversational SFT shape most SFT trainers expect directly - no prompt extraction or reformatting is needed. Maps to the SFT method card.

**Licence**: cc-by-nc-4.0 (README front matter and Hub tag both read `license: cc-by-nc-4.0`) [2][4]. Ungated, not private [4]. The one catch: the README's Licensing Information section names it as "Creative Commons NonCommercial (CC BY-NC 4.0)" [2] - noncommercial only.

**Shape**: 10,000 rows in one config (`default`), split `train` 9,500 / `test` 500, four columns (`prompt`, `prompt_id`, `messages`, `category`) [5][6].

**Hold out**: `test` split, 500 rows. No source states or implies overlap between this dataset and an external evaluation benchmark; the card names no other contamination risk.

**Origin**: built by Hugging Face researchers (the card's citation entry names Nazneen Rajani, Lewis Tunstall, Edward Beeching, Nathan Lambert, Alexander M. Rush and Thomas Wolf) [2]; every prompt and response is human-written by paid annotators, not model-generated [2]. Hub API at the check date: `downloads` 23,105, `downloadsAllTime` 164,038, `likes` 564 [4].

**Trained-on-by**: several public fine-tunes declare this dataset via the Hub's dataset tag, including `Weyaxi/Einstein-v6.1-Llama3-8B`, which lists `HuggingFaceH4/no_robots` among its training datasets and includes a `no_robots_sharegpt.json` file in its training config [7], and a cluster of smaller community fine-tunes and quantizations such as `TheBloke/mistral_7b_norobots-GGUF`, `monsterapi/zephyr_7b_norobots` and `mrm8488/mistral-7b-ft-h4-no_robots_instructions` [8]. No source ties Zephyr-7B-β itself to this dataset - its own paper trains SFT on UltraChat and preference-tunes on UltraFeedback, with no mention of `no_robots` [9].

**Introduced by**: no paper - the dataset card [2] (modelled after the InstructGPT paper [1]; released alongside the alignment-handbook training code [3]).

Decoded in-memory size differs slightly between the two: the README's `dataset_info` states 17,384,327 bytes, while the live `/size` endpoint states 17,198,409 bytes (`train` 16,310,949 + `test` 887,460) [2][5]. No source states sequence-length or token statistics.

The repository tree also carries two legacy Parquet files, `train_sft-00000-of-00001.parquet` and `test_sft-00000-of-00001.parquet`, alongside the `train-00000-of-00001.parquet` and `test-00000-of-00001.parquet` files the `default` config's `data_files` actually point to; the legacy files are not part of any loadable config [4][2].

## Quality

- The full category breakdown, stated in the card as a table over all 10,000 rows: Generation 4,560, Open QA 1,240, Brainstorm 1,120, Chat 850, Rewrite 660, Summarize 420, Coding 350, Classify 350, Closed QA 260, Extract 190 [2].
- Of the first 100 `train` rows read at offset 0, 94 are single-turn (one `user` message and one `assistant` message) and 6 open with a `system`-role persona message followed by several `user`/`assistant` turns, consistent with the card's description of the data as "comprised mostly of single-turn instructions" [2][10]. Of the first 100 `test` rows read at offset 0, 87 are single-turn and 13 open with a `system`-role message [11].
- The card's Annotation process, Curation rationale, Who are the annotators, and Personal and Sensitive Information sections are all marked `[More Information Needed]` - the card documents that the annotators were "skilled" but states no annotation protocol, agreement rate, or PII-screening process [2].
- No source states a measured contamination rate or duplicate rate for this dataset.

## Load it

Train on `train`, hold out `test`, and pin the revision this card's numbers were read at:

```python
import datasets

REV = "e6f9a4ac5c37faeb744ba9ecf0473184d7f8105b"  # main at the check date
train = datasets.load_dataset("HuggingFaceH4/no_robots", revision=REV, split="train")  # 9,500 rows
test = datasets.load_dataset("HuggingFaceH4/no_robots", revision=REV, split="test")    # 500 rows - hold out
```

**Trap**: the card's own Data Instances and Data Splits sections describe the splits as `train_sft` and `test_sft` [2], but the repository's `configs`/`dataset_info` YAML - and the only splits `load_dataset` actually resolves - are named `train` and `test` [2][4]; calling `split="train_sft"` fails. The repository does hold separate `train_sft-*`/`test_sft-*` Parquet files matching that legacy naming, but they sit outside the `default` config's `data_files` and are not what a plain `load_dataset` call reaches [4].

## Neighbors

All row counts below were read live at the check date [12]. This corpus prefers the original `messages`-format release for SFT; reach for a neighbor only when its format, language, or preference-pair shape is what the task needs.

- `AdamCodd/no_robots-alpaca` - reformats the data into Alpaca instruction/input/output fields and decomposes the `Chat` category's multi-turn dialogues into single-turn examples "to align with Alpaca's limitations regarding multi-turn interactions", which is why it carries 11,820 rows rather than 10,000 [12][13].
- `Doctor-Shotgun/no-robots-sharegpt` - a ShareGPT-format reformat at the same 10,000 rows [12].
- `jan-hq/h4_no_robots_binarized` - despite the name, its `dataset_info` shows the same `messages`-only shape and the same 9,500/500 split as this release, with the `prompt`, `prompt_id`, and `category` columns dropped; its own card is unwritten (`[More Information needed]`) [12][14].
- `BramVanroy/no_robots_dutch` - a Dutch rendering at 8,614 rows, short of the original 10,000 [12].
- `Rijgersberg/no_robots_nl` - its own Hub listing states it is a machine translation of the complete set of 10,000 `no_robots` examples, produced with GPT-3.5 [15]; it carries the full 10,000 rows [16].

## Where it came from

Built and released by Hugging Face; the card's citation entry names Nazneen Rajani, Lewis Tunstall, Edward Beeching, Nathan Lambert, Alexander M. Rush and Thomas Wolf as authors [2]. Every prompt and demonstration was written by paid human annotators, not sampled from a model - the card's subtitle and summary both state this directly, in contrast to GPT-generated instruction sets such as those the card contrasts itself against [2]. The card states no further detail on the annotation platform, task instructions given to annotators, or annotator identity: its Curation Rationale, Initial Data Collection and Normalization, Who are the source language producers, and Who are the annotators sections are all marked `[More Information Needed]` [2].

## Appendix: screening record

### Screening verdict

Usable as SFT chat training data, noncommercial use only: train on `train`, hold out `test`. The dataset's own card states the data is human-written, not model-generated, and intended for SFT (quoted in the opening paragraph) [2], and the screening row's note confirms this reading.

### The screening row

The row's own note: "10,000 instructions and demonstrations written by skilled human annotators; card says outright it was not GPT-generated." The row carries no flag.
