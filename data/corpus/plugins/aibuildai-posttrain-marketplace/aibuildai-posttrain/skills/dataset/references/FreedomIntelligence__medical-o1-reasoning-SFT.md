# FreedomIntelligence/medical-o1-reasoning-SFT

90,120 English/Chinese medical question-answer rows, each carrying a long free-text reasoning trace alongside the final answer, split across four configs of 19.7K-25.4K rows apiece.

**FreedomIntelligence/medical-o1-reasoning-SFT** is the supervised fine-tuning data behind HuatuoGPT-o1, introduced in "HuatuoGPT-o1, Towards Medical Complex Reasoning with LLMs" [1]. The paper builds a pool of verifiable medical problems reformatted from closed-set exam questions, then uses GPT-4o guided by a medical verifier to search for a correct multi-step reasoning trajectory per problem; successful trajectories are reformatted into a natural-language chain of thought and used to fine-tune the model in stage 1 of a two-stage (SFT-then-RL) training recipe [1]. Each row is a `(Question, Complex_CoT, Response)` triple: an open-ended medical question, GPT-4o's free-text reasoning trace, and the final answer [2]. **The `en_mix` and `zh_mix` configs interleave this medical data with unrelated general-instruction rows (math, geography, security studies, etc.), confirmed by fetching rows near the end of both mix splits [see A row / Quality]; the plain `en` and `zh` configs are medical-only** [2]. It lives at https://huggingface.co/datasets/FreedomIntelligence/medical-o1-reasoning-SFT .

**Use it for**: reasoning-trace SFT - each row's `Question` is the prompt and `Complex_CoT` plus `Response` form the target completion, teaching a model to produce an extended reasoning trace before its final answer. Use `en`/`zh` for medical-only fine-tuning and `en_mix`/`zh_mix` when general-instruction rows should be interleaved in; the served text carries no special `<think>`-style delimiter token between `Complex_CoT` and `Response` in the rows fetched here. See the SFT method card.

**Licence**: Apache-2.0 (`cardData.license: apache-2.0`, tag `license:apache-2.0`), ungated. No further catch found in the card or licence tags [2][3].

**Shape**: 90,120 rows across four configs, each a single `train` split: `en` 19,704, `zh` 20,171, `en_mix` 24,887, `zh_mix` 25,358; three string columns per config (`Question`, `Complex_CoT`, `Response`) [4][5].

**Hold out**: none of the four configs carries a held-out split. The paper's own contamination note is the relevant risk-management fact: its 40K-question source pool (drawn from the MedQA-USMLE and MedMCQA training sets) was "strictly screened to avoid contamination with the evaluation data" using the Med-PaLM2 method of filtering 64-consecutive-character overlaps, before any of it was used for SFT or RL [1]. No source states this dataset overlaps MedQA, MedMCQA, PubMedQA, MMLU-Pro, or GPQA test sets; treat those five benchmarks as evaluation-adjacent given the shared exam origin, and do not re-add their training portions on top of this data.

**Origin**: built and released by FreedomIntelligence (The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen) [1]; reasoning traces and answers generated by GPT-4o, guided by a medical verifier LLM, over questions drawn from human medical exam question banks [1][2]. Hub API at the check date: `downloads` 15,474, `downloadsAllTime` 206,077, `likes` 1,164 [3].

**Trained-on-by**: HuatuoGPT-o1-8B and HuatuoGPT-o1-70B, whose model cards list `FreedomIntelligence/medical-o1-reasoning-SFT` under `datasets` [6][7]; the paper is these models' own origin paper, training them on this data for 3 epochs at stage 1 before its stage-2 RL [1].

**Introduced by**: [1] (Chen et al.).

## Quality

- The paper's own ablation on an 8B model shows the `Complex_CoT` shape is what makes SFT on this data work, not just fine-tuning on the source exam questions: on MedQA / MedMCQA / PubMedQA / MMLU-Pro(Med) / GPQA(Med), fine-tuning directly on the original multiple-choice exam questions scores 60.0 / 55.5 / 74.1 / 54.3 / 46.9 - barely above the un-fine-tuned LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct baseline of 58.7 / 56.0 / 75.2 / 58.2 / 44.1 - while fine-tuning on this dataset's `(Question, Complex_CoT, Response)` triples scores 69.0 / 57.9 / 77.7 / 59.4 / 51.0, a clear gain on four of the five benchmarks [1].
- The reasoning traces and final answers are GPT-4o generations, validated by an LLM-based medical verifier (also GPT-4o) that checks each candidate answer against a ground-truth answer during trajectory search. The paper measured this verifier's reliability against 200 manually scored instances sampled from its stage 1 and stage 2 search: the GPT-4o verifier scored 96.5% accuracy in stage 1 and 94.5% in stage 2, versus 70.5% and 74.5% for an exact-match baseline over the same instances; a separately fine-tuned 8B verifier trained on 20,000 scoring samples reached over 90% accuracy [1].
- The source questions were "strictly screened to avoid contamination with the evaluation data" via 64-consecutive-character overlap filtering against MedQA and MedMCQA evaluation sets before release [1].
- Row-level check for the mix claim: fetching rows 24,800-24,804 of `en_mix/train` and rows 25,300-25,304 of `zh_mix/train` (datasets-server rows endpoint, near each split's end) shows genuinely non-medical prompts interleaved with medical ones - e.g. "Which of the jovian planets in our solar system are known to have rings?" and an arithmetic expression at `en_mix` rows 24,801-24,802, and an English matrix-rank question at `zh_mix` row 25,300 sitting between Chinese medical cases - confirming the mix configs blend general-instruction rows into the medical set rather than being medical-only under a different name [8]. No source states the exact share of general-instruction rows in either mix config, and none is invented here.
- No source states a measured duplicate rate or human-annotator agreement figure for this dataset; none is invented here.

Loading `en_mix` under the assumption it is "the English config, mixed only with more medical variety" will silently pull in unrelated general-instruction training data. The repository's `README.md` also names this behavior directly: "The file `medical_o1_sft_mix.json` contains a mix of medical and general instruction data" [2].

## Neighbors

- `FreedomIntelligence/medical-o1-verifiable-problem` - the upstream 40,644-row pool of open-ended medical questions with only a ground-truth answer and no reasoning trace, that this dataset's questions and search process were built from; two columns, not three, so it cannot be swapped in for SFT without first generating a `Complex_CoT` [1][9]. It is the input to this dataset's construction, not an alternative training set.
- `FreedomIntelligence/Medical-R1-Distill-Data` (`en` config, 22,000 rows) - reasoning traces distilled from DeepSeek-R1 instead of GPT-4o, over the same verifiable-problem pool [10][11]. Fetching its first row shows different column names than this dataset - `question`, `reasoning (reasoning_content)`, `response (content)` in place of `Question`, `Complex_CoT`, `Response` - so a loader written for this dataset needs remapping before it will run on the distilled release [11]. Its own card frames this dataset as the earlier, GPT-4o-based alternative: "You can also use our previously built medical verified long reasoning chains based on GPT-4o on [medical-o1-reasoning-SFT]" [10].
- `FreedomIntelligence/Medical-R1-Distill-Data-Chinese` - the Chinese-language counterpart to the R1-distilled set above, 17,000 rows, three columns [10][12]. Named by the R1-Distill-Data card as its Chinese sibling, not fetched for schema here.
- This corpus prefers the present GPT-4o-based release for reasoning-trace SFT, since it is the exact data the paper's HuatuoGPT-o1 models were fine-tuned on [1][6][7]; reach for the R1-distilled neighbors only when a DeepSeek-R1-style trace is specifically wanted.

## Where it came from

FreedomIntelligence (The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen) built the dataset for HuatuoGPT-o1 [1]. The paper reformats closed-set multiple-choice questions from the training sets of MedQA-USMLE and MedMCQA into open-ended "verifiable medical problems" with a unique ground-truth answer, released separately as `medical-o1-verifiable-problem` [1]. For each verifiable problem, GPT-4o generates an initial chain-of-thought and answer, which an LLM-based medical verifier checks against the ground truth; on rejection, GPT-4o iteratively extends the reasoning by applying one of four search strategies (Backtracking, Exploring New Paths, Verification, Correction) until the verifier accepts the answer or a maximum of three iterations is reached [1]. Accepted trajectories are reformatted by an LLM into a smoother natural-language `Complex_CoT`, and the pair `(Complex_CoT, Response)` becomes this dataset's SFT target for the corresponding `Question` [1]. Of the paper's 40K-question pool, roughly 20K went into this SFT step and 20K into the paper's separate stage-2 RL step [1]. The `zh` config is built the same way but over Chinese exam questions drawn from the CMB-Exam training set, and was used to train the paper's Chinese-domain model, HuatuoGPT-o1-7B-zh, on a Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct base [1].
