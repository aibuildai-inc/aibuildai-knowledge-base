# nvidia/HelpSteer3

132,937 human-annotated rows across five configs - preference pairs, helpfulness feedback, response edits, edit-quality contrasts, and principle-based judgments - covering General, STEM, Code and Multilingual domains in 14 languages.

**HelpSteer3** is NVIDIA's third-generation human-feedback dataset, built by contractors from Scale AI (General/STEM/Code) and Translated (Multilingual) annotating responses to prompts drawn from ShareGPT and WildChat-1M [7]. It lives at https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/HelpSteer3 . It is released as two linked papers: "HelpSteer3-Preference: Open Human-Annotated Preference Data across Diverse Tasks and Languages" introduces the `preference` config [1], and "HelpSteer3: Human-Annotated Feedback and Edit Data to Empower Inference-Time Scaling in Open-Ended General-Domain Tasks" introduces the `feedback`, `edit` and `edit_quality` configs [2]. The `principle` config's principle-and-fulfilment labels are generated by prompting DeepSeek V3 and then filtered following the method in "RLBFF: Binary Flexible Feedback to bridge between Human Feedback & Verifiable Rewards" [3]. **Each of the five configs trains a different downstream shape - reward-model preference pairs, critique generation, response editing, edit-quality discrimination, or principle-based reward signal - so pick the config that matches the target method before loading.**

**Use it for**: `preference` for reward-model / preference-optimization training (maps to the DPO or reward-model method card, after converting the `-3..3 overall_preference` score into a chosen/rejected or margin label); `feedback` and `edit` for SFT of a critique-then-revise pipeline (context+two responses to critique text, then response+feedback to edited response); `edit_quality` for training a model to follow feedback faithfully, using the `good_edited_response` versus `bad_edited_response` contrast; `principle` for principle-conditioned reward-model training as in RLBFF (response+principle to a Yes/No fulfilment label) [1][2][3]. None of the five configs ships prompts alone - every row carries the full multi-turn `context` plus response text.

**Licence**: CC-BY-4.0 (`cardData.license` is `cc-by-4.0`, tag `license:cc-by-4.0`), ungated (`"gated": false`, `"private": false`) [5]. The one catch: responses come from about 20 commercially-permissively-licensed LLMs, and the card states explicitly that none come from proprietary providers such as OpenAI [7] - this is why the CC-BY-4.0 grant is not complicated by a proprietary API's terms of service.

**Shape**: 132,937 rows across 5 configs, each with a `train`/`validation` split: `preference` 38,459/2,017, `feedback` 38,782/2,039, `edit` 13,740/721, `edit_quality` 3,111/163, `principle` 32,881/1,024 [4].

**Hold out**: hold out each config's own `validation` split before training on its `train` split - `preference` 2,017, `feedback` 2,039, `edit` 721, `edit_quality` 163, `principle` 1,024 rows. No source states an overlap between HelpSteer3 and a downstream evaluation benchmark; the leaderboard sections of the card report RM-Bench, JudgeBench and Arena Hard scores for models trained on this data but do not describe any decontamination step against those benchmarks, so this is not itself a contamination all-clear, only the absence of a stated risk [7].

**Origin**: built by NVIDIA; responses generated by roughly 20 permissively-licensed LLMs, annotation done by human contractors from Scale AI and Translated (about 7,000 annotators across more than 80 regions), principle/fulfilment labels generated by DeepSeek V3 [7]. Hub API at the check date: `downloads` 6,088, `downloadsAllTime` 68,747, `likes` 115 [5].

**Trained-on-by**: NVIDIA's own reward and generative-RM models, named in the dataset card itself - `Llama-3.3-Nemotron-70B-Reward`, `Llama-3.3-Nemotron-70B-Reward-Principle`, `Llama-3.3-Nemotron-70B-Reward-Multilingual`, `Llama-3_3-Nemotron-Super-49B-GenRM` and `Qwen3-Nemotron-32B-GenRM-Principle`; the card states these reward models score as high as 85.5% on RM-Bench (a benchmark introduced in [8]) and 78.6% on JudgeBench [7], and that "reward models trained with HelpSteer3 are the top performing models on JudgeBench" as of 24 Sep 2025 [7]. The card's own JudgeBench Scalar-RM table gives the decisive gap: `Llama-3.3-Nemotron-70B-Reward-Principle` scores 76.3 Overall against the table's strongest baseline not named as HelpSteer3-trained, the prior-generation `Llama-3.1-Nemotron-70B-Reward`, at 66.9 - a 9.4-point margin [7]. The `feedback`/`edit` configs train the card's Feedback-Edit inference-time-scaling system, named as `Llama-3.3-Nemotron-70B-Feedback`, `Llama-3.3-Nemotron-70B-Edit` and `Llama-3.3-Nemotron-70B-Select` [7].

**Introduced by**: [1] for `preference`; [2] for `feedback`, `edit` and `edit_quality`; [3] for the principle-generation and filtering method behind `principle`.

## Quality

- Every row in `preference` and `feedback` was independently annotated by 3-5 annotators, and only the three annotations that agree with one another most are released, "to remove outliers, which tend to misunderstand the task or overlook an important aspect of the task" [7].
- The card states that after annotation, vendors and NVIDIA's research team "performed extensive quality assurance" and "filtered away a substantial proportion of the initial data to produce the current dataset" [7].
- The `edit_quality` config exists because the card's authors "initially encountered a number of edited responses which did not follow the provided feedback" due to an unclear requirement communicated to the vendor, which was later corrected for some samples [7].
- No source states a measured contamination rate, duplicate rate, or annotator-agreement percentage for any config; none is invented here.
- Both Scale AI and Translated are stated to have implemented measures "to prevent annotators from using LLMs as part of the annotation workflow" [7].

**Trap**: `load_dataset("nvidia/HelpSteer3")` with no config name loads `preference` only, because the card marks `preference` as `default: true` in its `configs:` metadata [7]; the other four configs are silently skipped unless named explicitly. None of the five configs stores a single "chosen"/"rejected" field directly - `preference` carries `overall_preference` as a signed integer from -3 to +3 that must be converted to a label before DPO-style training, and `edit_quality` carries `good_edited_response`/`bad_edited_response` rather than a paired preference score at all [7].

## Neighbors

HelpSteer3 is a direct follow-up to two earlier NVIDIA releases, named as such in its own card [7]; use HelpSteer3 unless a project specifically needs the older attribute-rating format.

- `nvidia/HelpSteer2` - 21,362 rows, prompt+response with five 0-4 attribute ratings, plus a later-added `preference` config; introduced by its own paper referenced on its card [9][10].
- `nvidia/HelpSteer` - 37,120 rows, the original attribute-rated release, same five-attribute format as HelpSteer2 [9].
- Both predecessors carry the same `cc-by-4.0` licence as HelpSteer3 [10].

## Where it came from

Prompts come from two upstream pools, split by domain: ShareGPT for Code and Multilingual, WildChat-1M for General and STEM [7]. Two responses per prompt are generated by roughly 20 different commercially-permissively-licensed LLMs, each response from a different model, with no responses from proprietary providers such as OpenAI [7]. Human annotation follows from there: for `preference` and `feedback`, contractors from Scale AI (General/STEM/Code) and Translated (Multilingual) independently label each sample 3-5 times and the three most-agreeing annotations are kept; `edit` responses are written by a separate annotator pool based on the feedback for that sample; `edit_quality` splits edits into faithful ("good") and unfaithful ("bad") versions of the same feedback-following task [7]. The `principle` config's principle text and Yes/No fulfilment label are machine-generated by prompting DeepSeek V3 to convert the human feedback text into assessable principles, then filtered by the method described in the RLBFF paper [3][7].
