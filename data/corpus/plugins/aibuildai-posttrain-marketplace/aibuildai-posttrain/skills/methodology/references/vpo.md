# VPO

Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19320

DPO's loss with one extra term added: an expectation of the implicit log-ratio over a fixed calibration distribution, whose sign flips between pushing the policy toward it (offline, for pessimism) or away from it (online, for optimism).

**VPO** (value-incentivized preference optimization) is an RLHF method that folds the principles of optimism-under-uncertainty (online) and pessimism-under-uncertainty (offline) into a single closed-form policy loss, introduced by Cen, Mei, Goshvadi, Dai, Yang, Yang, Schuurmans, Chi, and Dai as "a unified approach to online and offline RLHF... which regularizes the maximum-likelihood estimate of the reward function with the corresponding value function, modulated by a sign to indicate whether the optimism or pessimism is chosen" [1]. Its parent is direct preference optimization (DPO), which reparameterizes the reward in closed form so that RLHF reduces to a classification loss over preference pairs, eliminating the separate reward-model-then-RL pipeline [2]. VPO keeps DPO's reparameterization but regularizes the underlying reward maximum-likelihood estimate (MLE) by the value $J^\star(r)=\max_\pi J(r,\pi)$ that a reward function would induce if optimized against, added with a $+$ sign online and a $-$ sign offline [1]. The paper gives two reasons for existing beyond unifying online/offline RLHF under one rule: standard techniques for constructing confidence intervals to drive optimism/pessimism become intractable once the policy is an arbitrarily-parameterized LLM, so VPO substitutes an implicit, tractable proxy instead of an explicit uncertainty estimate; and the reward function in the Bradley-Terry model is only identifiable up to a prompt-dependent shift, a "shift ambiguity" that VPO exploits by centering the reward against a fixed calibration policy $\pi_{\text{cal}}$, which doubles as the regularization mechanism [1].

No landmark production system's own documentation was found describing itself as using VPO (checked: trl's and verl's exported trainer/algorithm lists, below, carry no VPO entry). In the paper's own results, offline pessimistic VPO on ARC-Challenge stayed accuracy-stable past 1000 training steps while DPO's accuracy degraded with continued training on the same task and models [1]. In the paper's iterative online experiment on UltraFeedback with a Zephyr-7B-SFT base, the first of three VPO rounds reached a 22.53 length-controlled AlpacaEval 2.0 win rate, a 14.52-point gain over the Zephyr-7B-SFT base's 8.01, and the third round reached an MT-Bench average of 7.61 against Zephyr-7B-SFT's 5.30 and DPO's own third-round MT-Bench average of 7.46 [1, Table 1]. Lineage in one line: DPO (2023 [2]) -> VPO (2024 [1]) -> no dedicated bias-fixing successor found by title (Semantic Scholar's citing-paper list for this arXiv ID was checked up to 100 entries, title/year/citationCount fields only, sorted by citation count; the three most-cited titles - "RLHF Workflow: From Reward Modeling to Online RLHF" (272 citations), "Regularizing Hidden States Enables Learning Generalizable Reward Model for LLMs" (150 citations), and "Exploratory Preference Optimization" (109 citations) - none names itself a VPO successor or bias fix) [3].

**When to pick it**: pick VPO over plain DPO [2] when reward over-optimization or exploration coverage is the concern and a single scalar knob ($\alpha$, with its sign set by setting) is an acceptable substitute for an explicit uncertainty estimate; the paper's own offline result is that pessimistic VPO resists the accuracy collapse DPO showed past 1000 steps on ARC-Challenge [1]. Nearest offline alternative is IPO, which argues it is problematic to reduce pairwise preferences to Bradley-Terry pointwise rewards and proposes a different objective to avoid that reduction, without VPO's uncertainty-regularization term [4]. Nearest online neighbor is OAIF (Online AI Feedback), which turns DPO online by sampling fresh completions from the current policy and labeling them with an AI annotator each round, but has no exploration bonus of its own [5]; VPO's own online "Buffer" experiment is built directly on top of the OAIF setup, adding the optimistic calibration term [1].

**Variant of**: DPO [2].

**Data it needs**: the same preference triplets as DPO - a prompt $x$ and a preferred/unpreferred pair $(y_+, y_-)$ - plus a choice of calibration distribution $\pi_{\text{cal}}$, which the method does not fix and the paper sets per-experiment: the empirical distribution of ground-truth answers for offline ARC-Challenge, and the empirical distribution of previously-rejected ("negative") answers collected by the policy for the online TL;DR buffer setting [1]. Training scale in the paper: offline ARC-Challenge draws from a pool of 7787 multiple-choice questions, with 1172 of them held out as the test set (the paper does not separately state the training-split count), and each correct answer in the training split is paired against every incorrect answer for that question [1]; the online "Iterative" setting uses about 61K UltraFeedback preference pairs split into 4 chunks for successive training rounds [1]. Offline VPO is offline (one fixed dataset, one pass through reward-then-policy learning); online VPO is on-policy, sampling two fresh completions per prompt from the current policy every round and querying a preference oracle before the next update [1].

**Extra models**: only a frozen reference policy $\pi_{\text{ref}}$, exactly as DPO needs - forward passes only, no gradients [1][2]. No separate value network or reward model is trained: VPO's derivation substitutes the reward-model-implied value function directly, so nothing beyond $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ and the policy itself is optimized [1]. In the online setting the paper's own experiments use an external annotator (PaLM2-XS, or PairRM in the Iterative setup) to label freshly-sampled completions each round [1]; that annotator is a data-collection component of the online loop, not a model trained by the VPO loss. See Cost.

**Shipped by**: no framework implements it. trl's top-level package exports no VPO-named trainer class (`trl/__init__.py` at commit checked lists `DPOTrainer`, `GRPOTrainer`, and others, no VPO) [6]; verl's PPO trainer config exposes only `adv_estimator: gae` as the algorithm default with no VPO option in its config file [7]. Building it would mean adding a loss term to an existing DPO trainer - the practical objective (Eq. 14 below) needs only the same per-token log-probabilities a DPO trainer already computes, plus one extra batch of samples drawn from $\pi_{\text{cal}}$ - not a new sampling loop for the offline variant; the online "Buffer" and "Iterative" variants do need an OAIF-style rollout-and-relabel loop layered on top [1][5].

## How it works

One pass, offline: fit a reward MLE regularized by (minus, for pessimism) the reward's induced optimal value, then solve for the optimal policy under that reward, both steps collapsing into one DPO-style loss. Online: repeat that loss update every round on a preference dataset that grows by one freshly-sampled, freshly-labeled pair, with the regularizer's sign flipped to encourage (plus, for optimism) rather than penalize high induced value [1].

**The unregularized reward MLE**, the DPO/RLHF starting point, under the Bradley-Terry model of pairwise preference probability [1, Eq. 1-2]:

$$ \mathbb{P}(y_1 \succ y_2 \mid x) = \sigma(r^\star(x,y_1) - r^\star(x,y_2)), \qquad r_{\mathsf{MLE}} = \arg\min_r\, \ell(r,\mathcal{D}) $$

where $\sigma$ is the logistic function and $\ell(r,\mathcal{D})$ is the negative log-likelihood of the Bradley-Terry model over the preference dataset $\mathcal{D} = \{(x^i, y_+^i, y_-^i)\}$ [1].

**VPO's regularized reward MLE** [1, Eq. 10-12]:

$$ J^\star(r) = \max_\pi J(r,\pi), \qquad r_{\mathsf{VPO}} = \arg\min_{r \in \mathcal{R}} \; \{\, \ell(r,\mathcal{D}) - \mathsf{sign} \cdot \alpha \cdot J^\star(r) \,\} $$

$J(r,\pi)$ is the KL-regularized value of policy $\pi$ under reward $r$ (reward minus a $\beta$-weighted KL penalty to $\pi_{\text{ref}}$, the same quantity DPO's closed-form solution optimizes); $J^\star(r)$ is that value maximized over policies. $\alpha > 0$ sets the regularization strength, and $\mathsf{sign}$ is $+1$ online and $-1$ offline, so the estimator is pushed to favor high-value reward functions online (optimism) and to avoid them offline (pessimism) [1]. $\mathcal{R}$ is the set of rewards centered against a fixed calibration policy $\pi_{\text{cal}}$: $\mathcal{R} = \{ r : \mathbb{E}_{x\sim\rho,\,y\sim\pi_{\text{cal}}(\cdot|x)}[r(x,y)] = 0 \}$, which resolves the Bradley-Terry model's shift ambiguity [1, Eq. 11].

**Reduction to a DPO-style policy loss.** Using DPO's own closed-form policy-from-reward relation, $J^\star(r)$ simplifies to $-\beta\,\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\rho,\,y\sim\pi_{\text{cal}}(\cdot|x)}[\log\pi_r(y|x) - \log\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)]$ [1, Eq. 13], which turns the reward-space optimization into a policy-space loss with no inner max [1, Eq. 14]:

$$ \pi_{\mathsf{VPO}} = \arg\min_\pi \Bigg\{ -\!\!\sum_{(x^i,y_+^i,y_-^i)\in\mathcal{D}}\!\! \log\sigma\!\left(\beta\log\frac{\pi(y_+^i|x^i)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_+^i|x^i)} - \beta\log\frac{\pi(y_-^i|x^i)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_-^i|x^i)}\right) + \mathsf{sign}\cdot\alpha\beta\,\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\rho,\,y\sim\pi_{\text{cal}}(\cdot|x)}\!\left[\log\frac{\pi(y|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x)}\right] \Bigg\} $$

The first term is exactly DPO's pairwise loss with clip range absent (no clipping in this method); the second term is VPO's addition, an expectation of the same implicit-reward log-ratio evaluated on samples from $\pi_{\text{cal}}$ rather than the preference pairs [1]. There is no advantage function or per-token credit assignment in VPO's stated formula: like DPO, the gradient acts on whole-sequence log-probability ratios [1].

**Worked example.** Take $\beta=0.1$, one preference pair with log-ratios $\log\frac{\pi(y_+|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_+|x)}=0.8$ and $\log\frac{\pi(y_-|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_-|x)}=-0.3$: the DPO term is $-\log\sigma(0.1\times(0.8-(-0.3))) = -\log\sigma(0.11) \approx 0.639$. Suppose the calibration sample $y_{\text{cal}}$ has log-ratio $0.2$ and $\alpha=0.01$: online ($\mathsf{sign}=+1$) the extra term adds $1\times 0.01\times 0.1\times 0.2 = 0.0002$ to the loss, whose gradient pushes $\log\pi(y_{\text{cal}}|x)$ down (minimizing a positive term), i.e. away from $\pi_{\text{cal}}$-weighted samples; offline ($\mathsf{sign}=-1$) the same term subtracts $0.0002$, so minimizing the loss pushes $\log\pi(y_{\text{cal}}|x)$ up, toward $\pi_{\text{cal}}$-weighted samples. In the paper's own online experiment $\pi_{\text{cal}}$ is the distribution of previously-rejected answers, so pushing probability mass away from it is the intended optimistic, exploration-encouraging effect [1]; in the offline experiment $\pi_{\text{cal}}$ is the ground-truth-answer distribution, so pulling probability mass toward it is the intended pessimistic anchor against over-optimization [1]. At $\alpha=0.01$-$0.1$, the calibration term's magnitude is small relative to the DPO term in this example, consistent with the paper's own ablation range for $\alpha$ [1].

**Variant defined in the paper but not covered further here:** Section 4 derives a token-level VPO for a token-level MDP formulation of preference modeling [1]; this card covers only the sequence-level form of Eq. 14 above, which is what the paper's own LLM experiments run [1].

## Cost

**Theory, from the method's own math:**
- Time: identical model-forward-pass shape to DPO - one policy forward pass and one frozen-reference forward pass per preference pair [1][2] - plus one additional forward pass of policy and reference on samples drawn from $\pi_{\text{cal}}$ to evaluate the calibration term. No inner RL loop is run at training time: although $J^\star(r)$ is defined as a $\max_\pi$, Eq. 13's closed form eliminates that maximization, so a naive reading of Eq. 10-12 as requiring an inner policy-optimization subroutine per gradient step is wrong for the practical objective actually used [1].
- Memory: same trained-model count as DPO - one trained policy, one frozen reference held for inference only, no value network and no separately-trained reward model, since the reward's closed-form policy relation removes the need to keep an explicit reward model after substitution [1][2].
- Online-only cost: the "Buffer" and "Iterative" experiments both require sampling two fresh completions from the current policy and querying a preference oracle (an LLM annotator or PairRM) every round before the next gradient update, the same rollout-and-relabel cost structure as OAIF [1][5]. This is an empirical/practice-layer fact about the paper's own experiment setups, not a requirement of the loss formula itself, which is stated for a generic, already-formed preference dataset $\mathcal{D}$ [1].

**In practice, per framework:**
- None. No framework's own docs or source describe running VPO, so there is no framework-specific complexity, batching, or memory claim to report here; see Shipped by for what building it on top of an existing DPO trainer would require [6][7].

## How to use it

- Data preparation: identical to DPO - a dataset of $(x, y_+, y_-)$ triplets - plus a separate, explicit choice of $\pi_{\text{cal}}$, which the paper treats as a per-task design decision rather than something derived automatically from $\mathcal{D}$: the offline ARC-Challenge run sets $\pi_{\text{cal}}$ to the empirical distribution of ground-truth answers (available because ARC-Challenge is a graded multiple-choice task), and the online TL;DR buffer run sets $\pi_{\text{cal}}$ to the empirical distribution of previously-rejected answers collected by the policy [1].
- Reward/label convention: same Bradley-Terry pairwise convention as DPO - no separate reward model is trained; the implicit reward is $\beta(\log\pi(y|x) - \log\pi_{\text{ref}}(y|x))$ up to the additive calibration term, exactly as in DPO's derivation [1][2].
- Key knobs, paper values only (no framework ships this method, so there is no framework column to compare against):

| knob | offline ARC-Challenge [1] | online Buffer (TL;DR) [1] | online Iterative (UltraFeedback) [1] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\beta$ | 0.1$^\dagger$ | 0.1$^\dagger$ | not stated |
| $\alpha$ | swept 0.01-10, reported at 1 | swept, reported at 0.1 and 0.01 (with $\alpha/\sqrt{1+\text{steps}}$ decay) | not stated |
| $\mathsf{sign}$ | $-1$ (pessimistic) | $+1$ (optimistic) | $+1$ (optimistic) |
| $\pi_{\text{cal}}$ | ground-truth-answer empirical distribution | empirical distribution of negative (rejected) answers | empirical distribution of negative (rejected) answers |
| training length | 3000 steps | 5000 steps | 3 iterations over 4 UltraFeedback chunks |

  $^\dagger$ The paper's own sentence attributes this value to the DPO term specifically ("we set $\beta$ as 0.1 in DPO" for offline; "we set $\beta$ as 0.1 for the DPO term" for Buffer) rather than restating it as a VPO-specific setting [1]; Eq. 14 uses the same $\beta$ for both the DPO term and the calibration term, so this is the value VPO's own loss runs with, but the paper's own wording frames it through the DPO baseline.

  Two readings of that table: $\alpha$ was tuned by a 3-order-of-magnitude sweep (0.01 to 10) in the offline setting before settling on 1, so this is not a knob with a stable, transferable default across tasks; and the online $\alpha$ decay schedule ($\alpha/\sqrt{1+\text{training steps}}$) means the calibration term's influence is explicitly designed to shrink over training, not to hold constant.
- Trade-off the paper reports directly: in the online Buffer setting, larger $\alpha$ (0.1 vs 0.01) produced larger oscillation in win-rate against the SFT baseline during training, which the paper attributes to the optimistic term encouraging exploration into more diverse, less-tested data before the policy converges [1].

## While it runs

- Signals and their healthy shapes: the paper's own online-Buffer diagnostic is win rate against the SFT baseline, tracked at every 1000 training steps using a PaLM2-XS judge; VPO's win-rate curve shows larger oscillation than Online DPO's at higher $\alpha$, which the paper interprets as the exploration term's effect and which the paper reports shrinking as $\alpha$ decays [1]. The paper's own offline diagnostic is ARC-Challenge accuracy measured every 500 of 3000 training steps; DPO's accuracy is reported to drop after 1000 steps and continues to worsen with further training, while VPO's stays consistent over the same steps [1]. No framework logs these signals under a specific metric name, since no framework implements VPO.
- Published reference runs: Table 1 reports per-iteration AlpacaEval 2.0 and MT-Bench numbers on Zephyr-7B-SFT. VPO Iteration 1 reaches 22.53 LC win rate / 19.09 raw win rate / MT-Bench average 7.50; VPO Iteration 3 reaches 22.15 LC win rate / 19.58 raw win rate / MT-Bench average 7.61. The matching DPO baseline at Iteration 3 reaches 22.19 LC win rate / 19.88 raw win rate / MT-Bench average 7.46 [1, Table 1].
- Degeneracies and defaults: the sign of $\mathsf{sign}\cdot\alpha$ must match the online/offline setting or optimism and pessimism reverse - this is a manual switch stated as part of the method's definition, not inferred from data [1]. $\pi_{\text{cal}}$ has no general-purpose default; the paper uses two different, task-specific choices across its two settings and lists a more careful study of how to choose $\pi_{\text{cal}}$ as future work [1]. $\alpha$ likewise has no principled default; the paper lists developing an adaptive rule for choosing $\alpha$ without prior information as future work [1].
- Named successors: none found by title. Search performed: Semantic Scholar's citations endpoint for arXiv:2405.19320, fields limited to title/year/citationCount, up to 100 results sorted by citation count. The three highest-cited titles ("RLHF Workflow: From Reward Modeling to Online RLHF", 272 citations; "Regularizing Hidden States Enables Learning Generalizable Reward Model for LLMs", 150 citations; "Exploratory Preference Optimization", 109 citations) suggest related exploration/reward-modeling topics, but only titles were read - none names itself a VPO successor or a fix to a documented VPO bias, and no title in the retrieved list claims to supersede VPO. Abstracts/introductions were not fetched, so this bullet cannot rule out an unnamed successor [3].
- Known failure modes: from the paper's own text, VPO's online exploration term causes larger training-time oscillation in win rate at higher $\alpha$ before eventual convergence, a behavior the authors attribute to the exploration term encouraging sampling of more diverse, less-tested data [1]. Because no framework implements VPO, no maintainer issue-tracker search was performed - there is no library-issue source to check.
- What the gain is - and is not: the paper's own conclusion frames VPO's contribution as folding optimism/pessimism into RLHF without explicit uncertainty estimation, with theoretical regret guarantees matching standard RL counterparts and empirical robustness to reward over-optimization in the offline case [1]; the paper does not claim VPO adds reasoning or task capability beyond what DPO's same base-model policy can already reach, and its offline result is specifically about resisting accuracy degradation from continued training, not raising peak accuracy beyond what tuned DPO reaches on the same task before DPO's degradation sets in [1].
