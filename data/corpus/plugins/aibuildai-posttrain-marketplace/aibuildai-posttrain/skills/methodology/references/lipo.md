# LiPO

DPO generalized from pairs to a full ranked list of K responses per prompt, trained with a listwise ranking loss (LiPO-λ) that also weights each pair by how much reordering it would change a rank-quality metric.

**LiPO** (Listwise Preference Optimization) is a framework for aligning a language-model policy with human or reward-model preference data that arrives as a ranked list rather than a single pair, introduced in "LiPO: Listwise Preference Optimization through Learning-to-Rank" [1]. The paper is at https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01878 [1]. The paper frames every existing pairwise method - including its parent DPO [2] - as a special case of a general Learning-to-Rank (LTR) objective evaluated on a list of K responses, and proposes LiPO-λ, an instance built on the LambdaLoss ranking objective, as the best-performing member of that family [1]. Mechanically, each response gets an implicit score $s_i = \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_i|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y_i|x)}$, exactly DPO's implicit reward, and the loss is computed over all pairs within the list rather than one sampled pair [1]. The paper gives two reasons for going listwise: human raters commonly rank several responses to one prompt to amortize reading cost (it cites InstructGPT's 1-7 Likert-then-rank protocol and OpenAssistant's ranked reply trees as examples), and virtually all prior pairwise methods discard the graded label values of a list, treating a near-tie the same as a landslide [1].

The paper confirms two of its own listwise special cases have existing names: with list size K=2 and a pairwise-logistic loss, LiPO is algebraically identical to DPO's Bradley-Terry loss (its Proposition 2.1), and with a pairwise-hinge loss it is identical to RSO's normalized SLiC loss (Proposition 2.2) [1]. On Reddit TL;DR summarization with a T5-large policy and list size 8, LiPO-λ scored 90.60% proxy-reward win rate and 68.06% PaLM 2-L-IT AutoSxS win rate against the SFT target, ahead of DPO-BT (88.52% / 67.09%), the listwise-but-unweighted DPO-PL (88.27% / 67.23%), SLiC-norm (87.23% / 67.16%), and well ahead of the pointwise point-mse baseline (49.43% / 39.94%) (Table 1) [1]. Scaling to a T5-XXL policy preserved the ordering: LiPO-λ reached 97.32% / 83.79% versus DPO-BT's 96.22% / 82.38% on the same task (Table 2) [1]. Lineage in one line: Bradley-Terry/Plackett-Luce ranking models -> DPO and SLiC (pairwise special cases) -> LiPO framework and LiPO-λ (2024 [1]) -> no confirmed named successor found (searched this paper's own citing discussion in Sections 2-7; none named). The paper was matched by top-cited-collision-pick against a name-colliding but unrelated 2025 "Length-Informed Relative Policy Optimization" abbreviated the same way [1].

**When to pick it**: pick LiPO-λ over DPO [2] when your preference data is already collected as a ranked list of more than two responses per prompt (e.g., from a reward model scoring several sampled completions) and you want to use the whole list instead of subsampling pairs [1]. Prefer plain DPO when you only have pairwise chosen/rejected data - LiPO-λ with K=2 reduces to DPO-BT exactly, so there is no loss in staying with DPO for pairs [1]. Prefer an online method such as GRPO [3] or PPO [4] when you can score fresh samples from the current policy each step; LiPO, like DPO, trains offline on a fixed, pre-collected list per prompt [1].

**Variant of**: DPO [2], generalized from pairwise (K=2) to listwise (K≥2) preference data via a Learning-to-Rank objective [1].

**Data it needs**: for each prompt x, a list of K responses $\{y_1,...,y_K\}$ with graded relevance labels $\psi = \{\psi_1,...,\psi_K\} \in [0,1]$ (win-probability scores against the rest of the list, not just a rank order) [1]. The paper's own experiments used K=8 responses per prompt, sampled from a T5-large SFT policy at temperature 0.7 / top_k 40 and labeled via all-pairs comparison from a separately trained T5-XXL pairwise reward-ranking model, on Reddit TL;DR (93k human preference pairs) and AnthropicHH-helpful (161k/9k train/test) [1]; the paper states these dataset sizes but does not say which pipeline stage the 93k TL;DR pairs fed into. The method is offline: the response list for a given prompt is fixed before training and not re-sampled from the policy being trained; the paper's own future-work section names online re-elicitation of the list as unexplored [1].

**Extra models**: a frozen reference policy $\pi_{ref}$ (the SFT checkpoint), required by the score definition itself for every loss variant in the framework, no exception [1]. No value network. A pairwise reward-ranking model is needed only to construct the ranked list and its $\psi$ labels beforehand (the paper used a T5-XXL model for this) [1] - it is not queried during LiPO training itself. trl's fully supported, top-level `DPOTrainer` ships the same K=2 hinge special case with its reference model intact: its `loss_type="hinge"` option computes the loss from chosen/rejected log-probability ratios taken against the reference model's log-probabilities [5], matching $s_i$'s own dependence on $\pi_{ref}$. trl's separate, experimental `CPOTrainer` (`trl.experimental.cpo`, unsupported) ships a reference-free hinge variant whose loss uses only the policy's own log-probabilities [5] - a different, lighter special case, not what LiPO's score definition specifies.

**Shipped by**: no library implements the general LiPO-λ listwise objective (Eq. 10, K responses, the $\Delta_{i,j}$ Lambda weight). Hugging Face trl's fully supported, top-level `DPOTrainer` ships `loss_type="hinge"`; its docs credit this variant to RSO, which built a hinge-margin loss on top of SLiC's likelihood-normalization approach [5] - this is exactly the K=2 pairwise-hinge special case that [1]'s Proposition 2.2 shows is algebraically identical to part of the LiPO family, and it keeps the reference-model log-ratio that LiPO's score $s_i$ requires [5]. trl also ships a second, reference-free hinge variant under the experimental, unsupported `CPOTrainer` (`trl.experimental.cpo`) [5]. An unauthenticated GitHub repository search for "LiPO listwise preference optimization" returned zero repositories (checked 2026-08-09) [5]. verl's algorithm docs directory lists baseline, dapo, dppo, dro, entropy, gpg, grpo, opd, opo, otb, ppo, rollout_corr, rollout_corr_math, spin, and sppo, with no LiPO or CPO entry [6]. Building the general K>2 listwise loss would mean extending an existing pairwise trainer such as trl's `DPOTrainer`: replace the single chosen/rejected pair with all $\binom{K}{2}$ within-list pairs, add the label-aware $\Delta_{i,j}$ weight, and extend the data pipeline to carry a list of K responses with per-response scores per prompt instead of one chosen/rejected pair - a loss-and-data-loader change, not a new sampling loop, since training stays offline.

## How it works

Per training example: take the K-response list and its $\psi$ labels for a prompt, compute each response's implicit score $s_i$, form the loss over every pair in the list where one response has a strictly higher label than the other, weight each pair, and take the logistic loss on the score difference [1].

**Score.** Every response in the list gets a DPO-style implicit reward [1]:

$$ s_i = \beta \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_i|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y_i|x)} $$

**General LiPO objective** (Eq. 3) [1]:

$$ \mathcal{L}(\pi_\theta;\pi_{ref},\beta) = \mathbb{E}_{(x,\mathbf{y},\psi)\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[l(\psi,\mathbf{s})\right] $$

where $l$ is any ranking loss taking the labels $\psi$ and scores $\mathbf{s}=\{s_1,...,s_K\}$ as input; different choices of $l$ recover different named methods (pairwise logistic recovers DPO-BT at K=2, pairwise hinge recovers SLiC-norm at K=2, list-MLE recovers DPO-PL) [1].

**LiPO-λ objective** (Eq. 10), built on the LambdaLoss ranking objective [1]:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{lambda-loss}}(\pi_\theta;\pi_{ref},\beta) = -\mathbb{E}_{x,\mathbf{y},\psi\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\sum_{\psi_i > \psi_j} \Delta_{i,j}\log\left(1+e^{-(s_i-s_j)}\right)\right] $$

$$ \Delta_{i,j} = |G_i - G_j| \cdot \left|\frac{1}{D(\tau(i))} - \frac{1}{D(\tau(j))}\right|, \qquad G_i = 2^{\psi_i}-1, \qquad D(\tau(i)) = \log(1+\tau(i)) $$

The sum runs over every pair in the list where label $\psi_i$ exceeds label $\psi_j$ - not just adjacent-rank pairs or a single sampled pair [1]. $\tau(i)$ is the rank position of $y_i$ under the current model scores $\mathbf{s}$ (not the static label order), which the paper's ablation credits for a smoother optimization landscape than using the static label ranks [1]. $\Delta_{i,j}$ is the Lambda weight: it grows with the gap between the two responses' gains $G_i, G_j$ (a convex function of the label) and with the difference in their rank-discount terms, so pairs that are further apart in quality and/or higher-ranked matter more to the loss; the paper states this construction optimizes the DCG ranking metric [1]. The framework allows other choices of $G$ and $D$, but the paper adopts the standard DCG gain/discount pair without exploring alternatives [1].

**Worked example.** List of K=3 with labels $\psi = \{0.9, 0.5, 0.1\}$, current-model-induced ranks $\tau = \{1,2,3\}$ matching label order, so all three label-ordered pairs count: (1,2), (1,3), (2,3). Gains: $G_1=2^{0.9}-1\approx0.87$, $G_2=2^{0.5}-1\approx0.41$, $G_3=2^{0.1}-1\approx0.07$. Discounts: $D(1)=\log 2\approx0.69$, $D(2)=\log 3\approx1.10$, $D(3)=\log 4\approx1.39$, so $1/D=\{1.44, 0.91, 0.72\}$. Then $\Delta_{1,2}=|0.87-0.41|\cdot|1.44-0.91|\approx0.46\times0.53\approx0.24$, $\Delta_{1,3}\approx|0.87-0.07|\cdot|1.44-0.72|\approx0.80\times0.72\approx0.58$, $\Delta_{2,3}\approx|0.41-0.07|\cdot|0.91-0.72|\approx0.34\times0.19\approx0.06$: the pair spanning the largest label and rank gap (1 vs. 3) gets by far the largest weight, and the adjacent low-quality pair (2 vs. 3) gets the least, exactly the DCG-consistent weighting the paper describes [1].

## Cost

**Theory, from the method's own math**: the loss sums over every pair with $\psi_i > \psi_j$ inside a list of size K, so up to $\binom{K}{2}$ pairs are scored per prompt each step, versus 1 pair for plain DPO - the paper's own experiments compute scores for all 64 pairs at K=8 rather than sampling one [1]. Each of the K responses needs a forward pass through both $\pi_\theta$ and the frozen $\pi_{ref}$ to obtain its score $s_i$, so per-prompt compute and the number of log-probabilities held in memory scale with K, not with the number of pairs - the pairwise sum itself is cheap scalar arithmetic once the K scores exist. Memory holds two models (policy, frozen reference); no value network, matching DPO's memory profile [1].

**In practice, per framework**: no framework ships the general K>2 listwise loss, so no framework-level time/memory numbers exist for the method described in Eq. 10 (see Shipped by) [5][6]. For the K=2 pairwise-hinge special case, trl's fully supported top-level `DPOTrainer` with `loss_type="hinge"` keeps the reference-model log-ratio in the loss [5], so it needs one forward pass through $\pi_\theta$ and one through $\pi_{ref}$ per pair - the same memory profile as plain DPO. trl's separate, experimental `CPOTrainer` hinge variant drops the reference-model term from the loss computation entirely, using only the policy model's log-probabilities [5] - a lighter memory profile, but a different special case from the one LiPO's own score $s_i$ specifies. The paper's own reference implementation used a T5-large (770M) policy and T5-XXL (11B) reward-ranking model on TPU, batch size 32, learning rate 2e-5 with the Adafactor optimizer, and computed the ranking losses in Jax using the RAX learning-to-rank library [1]; it does not report wall-clock or GPU-memory numbers.

## How to use it

- Build the list: the paper samples K=8 completions per prompt from the SFT policy (temperature 0.7, top_k 40), then runs a T5-XXL pairwise reward-ranking model over all pairs to get a win-probability matrix $\Psi\in[0,1]^{K\times K}$, and sets each response's label $\psi_k = \frac{1}{K}\sum_i \Psi_{ki}$, i.e. its average pairwise win rate against the rest of the list [1].
- $\beta$ is set to 0.05 in the paper's experiments when computing the normalized scores $\mathbf{s}$ [1]. DPO's own paper uses $\beta=0.1$ as its default across tasks, and specifically $\beta=0.5$ for the Reddit TL;DR summarization task [2] - the same task LiPO's Table 1 experiments target - so LiPO's 0.05 sits an order of magnitude below the DPO paper's own TL;DR-specific value, not just its general default. trl's `DPOTrainer` defaults `beta` to 0.1 [5], the same reference-dependent role as LiPO's $\beta$, so this default is directly comparable and, like the paper's own $\beta=0.1$ default, sits well above LiPO's 0.05 TL;DR setting. trl's separate, experimental `CPOTrainer` also defaults `beta` to 0.1 [5], but that $\beta$ plays a different role there since CPO's hinge loss (unlike LiPO's score $s_i$ or trl's `DPOTrainer` hinge loss) does not divide by a reference-model likelihood at all [5], so that particular default is not directly comparable.
- Checkpoint selection: the paper picks, for each run, the checkpoint with the highest reward-ranking-model win rate against the SFT target, i.e. model selection uses the same proxy reward model as evaluation [1].

| knob | paper's value [1] |
| --- | --- |
| list size K | 8 (ablated in Fig. 3, not read in exact numbers off the figure here) |
| $\beta$ | 0.05 |
| batch size | 32 |
| learning rate | 2e-5 (Adafactor) |
| gain function $G_i$ | $2^{\psi_i}-1$ |
| discount function $D(\tau(i))$ | $\log(1+\tau(i))$ |

- Group-size trade-off: larger K gives more pairs and finer label information per prompt at the cost of K forward passes per prompt instead of 2; the paper's own Lambda-weight ablation (Fig. 4) finds the DCG gain/discount choice used above beats three alternatives it tests (constant weight, which reduces to plain DPO-BT; constant-gain-difference; constant-discount-difference) on both Reddit TL;DR and AnthropicHH [1] - this card did not read the figure's exact win-rate values, only the paper's stated ranking of the four options.

## While it runs

- Signals: the paper reports proxy-reward win rate (against a held-out T5-XXL reward-ranking model) and PaLM 2-L-IT AutoSxS win rate as its two automatic tracked metrics, both evaluated against the SFT target text [1]. It gives no guidance on what a healthy trajectory of either metric looks like mid-training, only end-of-training checkpoint-selection values.
- Published reference run: Table 1 numbers above (LiPO-λ 90.60% / 68.06% on Reddit TL;DR at T5-large, K=8) are the paper's own comparison point; no raw training-curve logs are published alongside the paper as far as this card's reading of the arXiv HTML version went [1].
- Degeneracies: the paper's own limitations analysis of prior methods applies to any degenerate list here too - a list of near-uniform labels (e.g. $\psi \approx \{0.51, 0.50, 0.49\}$) carries far less signal than a list with the same rank order but spread-out labels (e.g. $\{0.99, 0.50, 0.01\}$), and the paper states pairwise/list-MLE methods conflate these two cases while LiPO-λ's label-aware $\Delta_{i,j}$ weight is designed specifically to avoid it [1]. A list of size K=1 or K=2 with equal labels gives $\Delta_{i,j}=0$ for the only pair, contributing nothing to the loss, by the same reasoning as the worked example above.
- Named successors: none found. This card searched the LiPO paper's own text for follow-on names (Sections 2-7, references list) and found no method that cites LiPO as a bias-fixing predecessor; absence here reflects only what this paper self-reports, not a broader literature search.
- Known failure modes: the paper's own future-work section states its experiments use a fixed, pre-sampled list rather than one re-elicited from the policy being trained during training, and names closing that gap - "how to do the online learning where the list of responses are elicited from the policy being trained to reduce the distribution shift" - as open work [1], i.e. the paper itself flags a policy/data distribution-shift risk in the offline-list setup it evaluates. No GitHub issue tracker findings apply since no library implements the method (see Shipped by).
- What the gain is - and is not: the paper's own comparison isolates the ranking-objective choice holding data, policy size, and evaluation protocol fixed, and finds LiPO-λ's label-aware listwise weighting outperforms label-blind listwise (DPO-PL) and pairwise (DPO-BT, SLiC-norm) losses on the same lists at the same list size [1]; it does not test whether the gain persists at larger list sizes than 8, on tasks beyond summarization and dialogue, or on policies larger than T5-XXL (11B) [1].
