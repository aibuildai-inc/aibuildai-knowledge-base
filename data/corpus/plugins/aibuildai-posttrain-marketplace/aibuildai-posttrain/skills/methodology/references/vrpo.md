# VRPO

**Item home**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19223 (Zhu et al., "LLaDA 1.5: Variance-Reduced Preference Optimization for Large Language Diffusion Models", 2025).

DPO for masked diffusion language models: replace DPO's intractable exact log-likelihoods with Monte Carlo ELBO estimates, then kill the resulting variance with an optimal timestep/sample budget split and antithetic sampling shared between the policy's and the reference model's ELBO estimates.

**VRPO** (Variance-Reduced Preference Optimization) is an offline preference-optimization method for aligning masked diffusion language models (MDMs), introduced in the paper "LLaDA 1.5: Variance-Reduced Preference Optimization for Large Language Diffusion Models" as a fix for the variance that Monte Carlo likelihood estimation injects when DPO is applied to MDMs [1]. Its parent is DPO, whose loss replaces an explicit reward model with a closed-form policy-based reward implied by a Bradley-Terry preference model, trained on offline pairs of a preferred and dispreferred completion to the same prompt [2]. Unlike autoregressive models, an MDM cannot evaluate its exact log-likelihood; the paper substitutes DPO's log-likelihood ratios with evidence lower bound (ELBO) estimates, then proves that the bias and variance this substitution introduces into the DPO loss and its gradient are bounded by the variance of a per-pair "preference score" built from four ELBO estimates [1]. VRPO is the set of three unbiased techniques it derives to shrink that variance: increase the Monte Carlo sample budget, spend that budget on more diffusion timesteps rather than more masked samples per timestep, and reuse (antithetic) randomness between the policy's and the reference model's ELBO estimates on the same completion [1]. The paper gives two reasons beyond "DPO needs likelihoods": the ELBO estimator's own variance decomposition (Lemma 6) shows a real, computable cost that a fixed compute budget cannot make disappear by brute force alone [1], and the DPO loss's log-sigmoid nonlinearity turns that estimator variance into an actual bias in the loss and gradient, not just noise [1]. This card's own shortlisting search for "VRPO" turned up two candidates: this one, and a zero-citation 2026 paper, "GAE Falls Short in Imperfect-Information Self-Play Reinforcement Learning," that names an unrelated "Variance-Reduced Policy Optimization" under the same acronym; the LLaDA 1.5 paper is confirmed by being the only one of the two with a nonzero citation count.

VRPO's only reported adopter to date is its own introducing model, LLaDA 1.5: fine-tuning LLaDA 8B Instruct with VRPO for one epoch on 350K preference pairs raised GSM8K from 78.6 to 83.3 (+4.7), HumanEval from 49.4 to 52.4 (+3.0), MBPP from 41.0 to 42.8 (+1.8), IFEval from 62.2 to 66.2 (+4.0), and Arena-Hard from 10.0 to 14.3 (+4.3), with smaller gains on Math (+0.4), GPQA (+3.6), AlignBench (+0.5) and MTbench (+0.1) (Table 1) [1]. No landmark system beyond LLaDA 1.5 itself was found adopting VRPO by name (see While it runs for two later papers on the same likelihood/variance problem that were found instead). Lineage in one line: DPO (2023 [2]) -> VRPO (LLaDA 1.5, 2025 [1]), applied to the LLaDA base architecture (2025 [3]) -> the same intractable-likelihood problem is addressed by later, differently-named work (2025-2026 [5][6]).

**When to pick it**: pick VRPO specifically when preference-tuning a masked diffusion language model such as LLaDA, where exact log-likelihoods cannot be computed at all [1] — it is not a competing choice among methods for autoregressive models, it is what makes DPO-style training on an MDM viable in the first place. The nearest offline alternative is naive DPO-with-ELBO-substitution, without VRPO's allocation and antithetic-sampling fixes: the paper's own ablation shows that removing antithetic sampling causes a sharp rise in the preference-score estimator's variance, roughly a thousandfold in the reported configuration (Table 2, see While it runs) [1]. The parent is DPO, which is exact and variance-free for autoregressive models because their log-likelihoods are exact [2]. The paper's own Section 3.4 notes its variance-reduction techniques extend to any MDM alignment objective that subtracts two correlated ELBOs, including online, PPO- or GRPO-style objectives, but no such online-RL-for-MDM system is named as built or shipping [1].

**Variant of**: DPO [2].

**Data it needs**: offline preference pairs — a prompt $x$, a chosen completion $y_w$, and a rejected completion $y_l$ per row, exactly DPO's data shape [1][2]; no fresh sampling from the current policy is required or used. LLaDA 1.5 trained on 350K such pairs for one epoch, curated in four steps: filtering out low-quality samples, deduplication by similarity matching, reward-model-based ranking to select high-quality data, and partial replacement of responses with outputs from stronger LLMs [1]. A reward model appears only in that offline data-curation pipeline, not in the VRPO training loss itself.

**Extra models**: one frozen reference model, exactly as in DPO — the paper initializes $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ from LLaDA 8B Instruct, the same checkpoint used to initialize the trained policy [1]. No value network and no reward model inside the training loop itself. See Cost for what the ELBO estimation itself costs beyond that.

**Shipped by**: no library implements VRPO training. A GitHub repository search for "VRPO preference optimization" returned zero results (verified twice, most recently 2026-08-09). None of the four is confirmed to contain a VRPO training implementation. A GitHub code search for "VRPO" could not be run (the endpoint returned a 401 authentication error, so that half of the search is unresolved). The paper's own code, ML-GSAI/LLaDA-1.5, ships only inference code and the final model weights (on Hugging Face as GSAI-ML/LLaDA-1.5), not the VRPO training loop [4]. Building it would mean writing a new training loop, not adding a loss on top of an existing DPO trainer: it requires an MDM's forward masking process, per-timestep ELBO Monte Carlo estimation (drawing masked samples and evaluating the per-step denoising loss), the timestep/sample budget split, and antithetic-sample bookkeeping between the policy's and reference model's ELBO calls — none of which exists in autoregressive-only trainers such as trl or verl.

## How it works

The loop, once per preference batch: for each pair $(y_w, y_l)$, estimate the ELBO of the policy and of the frozen reference on both completions by Monte Carlo sampling of masked versions of each completion at several diffusion timesteps; combine the four ELBO estimates into a preference score; push that score through DPO's log-sigmoid loss [1].

**ELBO and its Monte Carlo estimator.** For an MDM, the exact log-likelihood is intractable and is approximated by its evidence lower bound (Eq. 4 in the paper):

$$ \mathcal{B}_\pi(y \mid x) \triangleq \mathbb{E}_{t \sim \mathcal{U}[0,1]} \, \mathbb{E}_{y_t \sim q(y_t \mid t, y, x)} \, \ell_\pi(y_t, t, y \mid x) \;\le\; \log \pi(y \mid x) $$

where $q(y_t \mid t, y, x)$ is the forward masking process at diffusion time $t$ and $\ell_\pi$ is the per-step mask-prediction loss [1]. This double expectation is estimated with $n_t$ i.i.d. timesteps and $n_{y_t}$ i.i.d. masked samples per timestep, for a total budget $n = n_t \times n_{y_t}$ (Eqs. 5–6 in the paper):

$$ \widehat{\mathcal{B}}_\pi(y) \triangleq \frac{1}{n_t} \sum_{j=1}^{n_t} \frac{1}{n_{y_t}} \sum_{k=1}^{n_{y_t}} \ell_\pi\!\left(y^{(k)}_{t^{(j)}}, t^{(j)}, y\right) $$

This estimator is unbiased for the ELBO, but its variance decomposes (Lemma 6 in the paper) as $\mathbb{V}\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_\pi(y) = \frac{1}{n_t}\mathbb{V}_t\mathbb{E}_{y_t\mid t,y}\,\ell_\pi + \frac{1}{n_t n_{y_t}}\mathbb{E}_t\mathbb{V}_{y_t\mid t,y}\,\ell_\pi$, whose first term shrinks only with more timesteps $n_t$, not with more masked samples per timestep [1].

**The DPO-with-ELBO loss** (Eqs. 7–8 in the paper) substitutes each log-likelihood ratio in DPO's loss (Eq. 3 in the paper, matching DPO's own Eq. 7 [2]) with a difference of ELBO estimates:

$$ \widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{DPO\text{-}E}}(\theta) = -\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m} \log\sigma\!\Big(\underbrace{\beta\big(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi_\theta}(y_w^{(i)}) - \widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(y_w^{(i)})\big) - \beta\big(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi_\theta}(y_l^{(i)}) - \widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(y_l^{(i)})\big)}_{\triangleq\, \hat{s}_\theta(y_w^{(i)}, y_l^{(i)})}\Big) $$

$\beta$ plays the same role as in DPO: it scales how strongly the preference gradient pulls the policy away from the reference. $\hat{s}_\theta$, the "preference score estimator," is the quantity whose variance the paper bounds and VRPO reduces [1].

**VRPO's two unbiased variance-reduction techniques**, applied under a fixed total budget $n$: (1) allocate the entire budget to timesteps, drawing one masked sample per timestep ($n_{y_t}=1$), which the paper proves minimizes the ELBO estimator's variance for fixed $n$ (Theorem 2) [1]; (2) antithetic sampling — reuse the same drawn timesteps and masked samples when estimating $\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi_\theta}(y)$ and $\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}(y)$ for the same $y$, which lowers the variance of their difference whenever the two ELBO estimates are positively correlated — a condition the paper argues holds because the policy and a frozen reference derived from it tend to agree on the same input (Theorem 3) [1]. Both are unbiased: allocation does not change the estimator's form in Eq. 6, and antithetic sampling is unbiased by linearity of expectation [1]. Neither technique adds sampling cost; a third technique, simply raising the total budget $n$, does add cost and is the one lever that is not free [1].

**Worked micro-example** (illustrative numbers, not the paper's measured ones, to make the allocation formula in Lemma 6 concrete): suppose the per-timestep variance term is $\mathbb{V}_t = 1$ and the per-mask-sample variance term is $\mathbb{V}_{y_t} = 4$, with a fixed budget $n=4$. Spending it all on timesteps ($n_t=4, n_{y_t}=1$) gives variance $\frac{1}{4}(1) + \frac{1}{4}(4) = 1.25$; splitting it evenly ($n_t=2, n_{y_t}=2$) gives $\frac{1}{2}(1) + \frac{1}{4}(4) = 1.5$; spending it all on mask samples at one timestep ($n_t=1, n_{y_t}=4$) gives $1(1) + \frac{1}{4}(4) = 2.0$ — confirming that all-timesteps is the lowest-variance allocation, the direction (though not the exact numbers) the paper's own ablation shows in Table 2 [1].

The paper also derives an outcome-only variant restricted to DPO (the one described above) and a general extension (Eq. 9 in the paper) noting the same two techniques apply to any MDM alignment objective built from a difference of two correlated ELBOs, such as PPO- or GRPO-style RL objectives, though it does not build or ship such an online variant [1].

## Cost

**Theory, from the method's own math**: estimating one ELBO to budget $n = n_t \times n_{y_t}$ costs $n$ forward passes of the mask-prediction model; a full DPO-E step needs this for both $y_w$ and $y_l$ under both $\pi_\theta$ and $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$, so total forward-pass count scales linearly in $n$ [1]. Memory is otherwise unchanged from DPO: one trained policy plus one frozen reference model, no value network, no reward model in the loop [1][2]. A naive reading might expect memory to scale with $n$ as well (holding $n$ masked copies' activations simultaneously); the paper's own accounting treats the $n$ Monte Carlo draws as sequential forward passes contributing to one scalar ELBO estimate, not as a batch that must coexist in memory at once [1].

**In practice, per framework**: none exists to report. The paper's own efficiency note is the only practical data point: at its default budget $n=8$, VRPO costs roughly 8x the forward-pass compute of a non-Monte-Carlo baseline ($n=1$), but the paper states this remains under 0.5% of pretraining compute overall for LLaDA 1.5 [1]. No trl, verl, or other framework documentation exists to compare against, since none ships VRPO (see Shipped by).

## How to use it

- Data: prompt plus a chosen and a rejected completion per row, identical in shape to DPO's preference data [1][2]. LLaDA 1.5's training set was collected internally, then filtered for low-quality samples, deduplicated by similarity matching, ranked by a reward model for quality, and partly replaced with stronger LLM outputs — a curation pipeline, not part of the VRPO loss itself [1].
- Reward/label convention: pairwise preference only; there is no scalar per-completion reward inside the training loss (a reward model is used only offline, to help build the dataset) [1].
- Knobs, with only the paper as a source since no framework ships this method — "not stated" means the paper's text was checked and does not give that value:

| knob | paper [1] |
| --- | --- |
| Monte Carlo budget $n = n_t \times n_{y_t}$ | 8 (default); ablated at 1, 4, and 8 |
| timestep/mask-sample split $n_t / n_{y_t}$ | $n_t = n$, $n_{y_t} = 1$ (all-timesteps, the proven-optimal allocation) |
| antithetic sampling | on by default |
| KL coefficient $\beta$ | 0.2 |
| preference pairs / epochs | 350K pairs, 1 epoch |
| batch size | 64 |
| optimizer | AdamW, weight decay 0.01, $\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.95$ |
| learning rate schedule | 15 warmup steps to a peak of $5\times10^{-7}$, then cosine decay |

- Trade-off a run designer faces: raising the budget $n$ linearly increases per-pair compute (about 8x at the paper's $n=8$ versus $n=1$) in exchange for a lower-variance, less-biased loss and gradient; the paper reports this stays under 0.5% of pretraining cost at its chosen scale, but that ratio will shift with model size and dataset size [1]. Given a fixed $n$, allocation and antithetic sampling are free variance reductions with no additional compute, so they should be turned on before spending any extra budget on raising $n$ [1].

## While it runs

- Signals and their healthy shapes: the paper's own diagnostic is the variance of the preference score estimator $\mathbb{V}\hat{s}_\theta$ and the preference-induced variances of the loss and gradient, $\mathsfit{V}_{\mathrm{pref}}$, reported from held-out ablation runs rather than as a metric name logged by any shipping trainer (Table 2) [1]. Lower $\mathbb{V}\hat{s}_\theta$ is healthier; the paper's ablation is the reference point since no framework exposes this metric under a fixed name.
- Published reference run and the deciding number: in the paper's ablation (Table 2), the base configuration ($n_t=4, n_{y_t}=1$, antithetic on) has estimator variance $\mathbb{V}\hat{s}_\theta = 2.2$ and GSM8K 82.8; turning antithetic sampling off at the same $n_t, n_{y_t}$ raises the variance to 2183.7 — roughly a thousandfold increase — while GSM8K drops only to 82.0 [1]. The paper reads this gap itself: antithetic sampling is confirmed by theory (Theorem 3) to sharply cut variance, but downstream benchmark performance is comparatively insensitive to that variance because it depends on many other factors as well [1]. The deployed LLaDA 1.5 configuration is $n_t=8, n_{y_t}=1$ (budget $n=8$), with the lowest reported variance (1.0) and the paper's headline GSM8K score of 83.3 [1].
- Degeneracies and defaults: the paper's Theorem 2 shows spending budget on additional masked samples per timestep instead of additional timesteps is a strictly worse allocation for a fixed total budget — Table 2's $n_t=1, n_{y_t}=4$ configuration has variance 7.3 versus the base configuration's 2.2 at the same total budget [1]. Antithetic sampling's variance-reduction guarantee (Theorem 3) assumes positive correlation between the policy's and reference's ELBO estimates on the same sample; the paper argues this holds because the two models share initialization, but does not test what happens once the policy has drifted far from the reference [1].
- Named successors: none that adopt the name "VRPO" itself, but the same problem it addresses is being revisited. A Semantic Scholar query of arXiv:2505.19223's citing-papers graph (238 entries returned, fetched 2026-08-09) surfaced two papers squarely on VRPO's problem: "LFPO: Likelihood-Free Policy Optimization for Masked Diffusion Models" states that applying RL to MDMs is fundamentally hindered by the intractability of exact likelihood computation, which forces existing methods (VRPO's approach among them) to rely on high-variance approximations, and proposes bypassing likelihood estimation entirely rather than reducing its variance [5]; "Bringing Stability to Diffusion: Decomposing and Reducing Variance of Training Masked Diffusion Models" derives a three-way decomposition of MDM training variance and six variance-reduction methods, a broader and independently-derived take on the same variance problem VRPO targets for the preference-tuning loss specifically [6]. Neither paper is named "VRPO" or claims to be its successor; a full read of either paper was not performed for this card, only their abstracts.
- Known failure modes: the paper's own Limitations section states two: VRPO needs additional compute to achieve lower variance, and although it reduces variance effectively, the benefit for some downstream benchmarks — it names MTBench and Arena-Hard — is limited [1]. No maintainer issue reports exist to check, since no library ships this method.
- What the gain is — and is not: the paper is explicit that VRPO's variance reduction targets optimization, not generalization — it states that the technique is theoretically designed to reduce bias and variance in the DPO loss and gradient estimates, which directly improves optimization rather than generalization, while downstream task performance is influenced by many other factors that make its sensitivity to estimator variance harder to isolate [1]. In line with that, the ablation shows large variance swings with comparatively small benchmark swings (see the deciding number above) [1].
