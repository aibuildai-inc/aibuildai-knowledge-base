# SDFT

On-policy distillation from your own in-context self: the student samples an answer from the plain prompt, a copy of the same model that additionally sees the expert demonstration re-scores those sampled tokens, and the student is trained by reverse KL toward that demonstration-conditioned distribution — no reward model, no separate teacher network.

**SDFT** (Self-Distillation Fine-Tuning), https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19897, is defined by Shenfeld et al. as "a simple method that enables on-policy learning directly from demonstrations" by "using a demonstration-conditioned model as its own teacher, generating on-policy training signals that preserve prior capabilities while acquiring new skills" [1]. Its parent is on-policy distillation, introduced by Agarwal et al. as a way to train a student on its own sampled outputs against a teacher's per-token distribution rather than on fixed teacher-generated text [2]; SDFT specializes this by manufacturing the teacher itself, in context, from the same weights as the student plus the demonstration [1]. Mechanically: given prompt $x$ and demonstration $c$, the student samples completion $y$ from $\pi_\theta(\cdot|x)$; a teacher $\pi(\cdot|x,c)$ — by default the same network conditioned on $x$ and $c$ together — re-scores the sampled tokens, and the student minimizes the reverse KL between its own distribution and the teacher's [1]. Three reasons the paper gives for existing: plain SFT trains off the demonstration text off-policy, which the paper ties to catastrophic forgetting of prior capabilities; a reward function for the target behavior is often unavailable, ruling out on-policy RL; and demonstrations are the input continual learning most often actually has [1].

The paper's own experiments were built on top of the Hugging Face TRL library [1], and its SDFT trainer has since shipped inside TRL itself under an experimental path [3]. In the paper's own results, SDFT reaches 89% strict-accuracy knowledge acquisition against SFT's 80% and continual-pretraining's 9%, with 98% out-of-distribution accuracy against SFT's 80% (Table 1) [1]; fine-tuning a reasoning model (Olmo-3-7B-Think) on answer-only medical data, SFT collapses accuracy from 31.2% to 23.5% while SDFT raises it to 43.7% (Table 2) [1]. Lineage in one line: on-policy distillation (Agarwal et al., 2024 [2]) → SDFT (Shenfeld et al., 2026 [1]); the paper reports no downstream adopters or named successors as of this reading. The right paper for this shortlist row's "SDFT" name was confirmed by fetching the arXiv abstract at 2601.19897 directly and checking its own definition and abbreviation against the row's `full_name` field [1]; the same three letters also name an unrelated, earlier method (Yang et al., 2024, "Self-Distillation Bridges Distribution Gap in Language Model Fine-Tuning" [4]), which distills a self-generated paraphrase of the training data to match the model's own original output distribution — a data-augmentation technique, not an on-policy continual-learning method — and is a different method despite the identical name and abbreviation.

**When to pick it**: expert demonstrations exist but no scalar reward does, and you want to add a skill without eroding prior capabilities — SDFT is not a substitute for on-policy RL but a complementary regime for exactly this supervision gap, per the paper's own Discussion [1]. Its parent, on-policy distillation, needs an already-capable external teacher model rather than manufacturing one in context [2]. Its nearest offline alternative is DFT (Dynamic Fine-Tuning), which stays off-policy — it re-weights the ordinary SFT loss by the token's own probability rather than sampling on-policy — and in the SDFT paper's own Table 5 it underperforms SDFT on both new-task accuracy and prior-task retention on every one of the three Skill Learning tasks tested [5][1]. Its nearest online-RL neighbor is GRPO: GRPO needs an explicit reward and samples a group of completions per prompt to form a relative baseline, where SDFT needs only a single on-policy rollout per example and gets its training signal from token- or logit-level teacher probabilities instead of a group-relative advantage [6][1].

**Variant of**: on-policy distillation [2].

**Data it needs**: per example, a prompt $x$ and a demonstration $c$ (TRL's dataset schema names these columns `prompt` and `privileged_context` [3]); no reward, no chosen/rejected pair, no explicit label beyond the demonstration text itself. Training is on-policy: the completion the loss is computed on is sampled fresh from the current student each step, not read from a static dataset of responses [1]. The paper trains Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with datasets in the low tens of thousands of examples per task and reports full fine-tuning of all parameters on a single H200 GPU per experiment [1].

**Extra models**: the definition always needs a second scored distribution, $\pi(\cdot|x,c)$, but the paper is explicit that this need not be separate weights: "unless mentioned otherwise, we use an exponential moving average (EMA) of the student parameters for the teacher" [1], and the paper's own Algorithm 1 updates the teacher parameters $\phi \leftarrow \alpha\theta + (1-\alpha)\phi$ every step [1] — one extra weight-sized copy held in memory, no gradients. No reward model and no value network are part of the method [1]. TRL's `teacher_model_kind` config exposes three teacher choices and states its default, `"base"` (a frozen copy of the initial student weights), as "matching the paper" [3]; that claim conflicts with the paper's own stated default of EMA quoted above — see While it runs. TRL's `"live"` option needs no extra weight copy at all, reusing the current student as teacher [3].

**Shipped by**: trl, under `trl.experimental.sdft` (`SDFTConfig`, `SDFTTrainer`) [3]. This is not exported from the top-level `trl` package — the top-level `trl/__init__.py` on the `main` branch (read 2026-08-09) lists `DPOTrainer`, `GRPOTrainer`, `KTOTrainer`, `RLOOTrainer`, `RewardTrainer`, `SFTTrainer` among its trainer exports, and no `SDFTTrainer` [7] — and TRL's own experimental-features page states the stability contract for everything under that namespace: "Anything under `trl.experimental` may change or be removed in any release (including patch versions) without prior deprecation. Do not rely on these APIs for production workloads" [8]. The same SDFT trainer doc page is also present at the pinned `v1.9.2` release build (fetched 2026-08-09, current PyPI release [9]), so it is available in the installable stable release, not only on an unreleased branch — but it is experimental within that release too [10][8]. verl does not implement SDFT: a full recursive listing of the verl-project/verl repository tree at `main` (GitHub Git Trees API, commit `4a2cba7`, 2026-08-08, 1,425 paths, not truncated) contains no path matching "sdft" or "self_distil" anywhere in the codebase, not only in its `docs/algo` directory of 15 algorithm pages (baseline, dapo, dppo, dro, entropy, gpg, grpo, opd, opo, otb, ppo, rollout_corr, rollout_corr_math, spin, sppo), which also has no SDFT entry [11]. ## How it works

Loop in one line: sample a completion from the student, score those same tokens under the demonstration-conditioned teacher, take a gradient step that pulls the student toward the teacher's distribution [1].

**The objective** is the reverse KL divergence between student and teacher, estimated by sampling from the student itself [1]:

$$ \mathcal{L}(\theta) = D_{KL}\big(\pi_\theta(\cdot|x) \,\|\, \pi(\cdot|x,c)\big) = \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi_\theta(y|x)}\left[\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y|x)}{\pi(y|x,c)}\right] \quad (1) $$

Decomposed autoregressively and differentiated with the teacher treated as fixed, this gives a token-level gradient estimator [1]:

$$ \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi_\theta}\left[\sum_t \sum_{y_t \in \mathcal{V}} \log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_t|y_{<t},x)}{\pi(y_t|y_{<t},x,c)}\, \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_t|y_{<t},x)\right] \quad (2) $$

The paper names and ablates three gradient estimators for this quantity [1]. The **token-level (partial) estimator** uses only the sampled token at each step, $\hat g_{token} = \sum_t \log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_t|y_{<t},x)}{\pi(y_t|y_{<t},x,c)} \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_t|y_{<t},x)$, and is biased because it ignores how an early token shapes later token distributions [1]. The **full analytic per-token estimator** marginalizes over the whole vocabulary at each timestep, $\hat g_{analytic} = \sum_t \sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}} \log\frac{\pi_\theta(v|y_{<t},x)}{\pi(v|y_{<t},x,c)} \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(v|y_{<t},x)$; it has strictly lower variance than the sampled-token version, remains biased at the sequence level, and is what the paper's own main experiments use, reusing quantities already produced by the forward pass [1]. The **Rao-Blackwellized estimator**, following Amini et al. (cited by the paper for the technique, not independently checked here) [1], additionally integrates analytically over next-token distributions while keeping Monte-Carlo sampling over prefixes, giving an unbiased KL and gradient at higher compute cost [1]. All three of TRL's `distillation_mode` choices — `"sampled_token"`, `"full_logits"`, `"topk_logits"` — implement the same reverse-KL family of objectives, but TRL's default, `"topk_logits"`, distills only the top-100 tokens by probability mass rather than the full vocabulary the paper's default `"full_logits"`-equivalent estimator uses [3]; that is a different, cheaper approximation from the paper's own default.

**Worked example**, one token, toy vocabulary $\{A,B,C\}$, student $\pi_\theta = (0.5, 0.3, 0.2)$, teacher $\pi(\cdot|x,c) = (0.7, 0.2, 0.1)$: if the student samples $y=A$, the sample-loss term from Eq. (1) is $\log(0.5/0.7) \approx -0.336$, and minimizing it pulls $\pi_\theta(A)$ up toward the teacher's higher weight on $A$.

**As inverse RL**: the paper reframes the same objective as maximizing an implicit reward. Starting from trust-region-regularized RL, $\pi_{k+1} = \arg\max_\pi \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi}[r(y,x)] - \beta D_{KL}(\pi(\cdot|x)\|\pi_k(\cdot|x))$, the optimal next policy has the closed form $\pi_{k+1}^*(y|x) \propto \pi_k(y|x)\exp(\frac{1}{\beta}r(y,x))$, so the reward can be recovered as $r(y,x) = \beta[\log\pi_{k+1}^*(y|x) - \log\pi_k(y|x)] + C$ [1]. The paper's In-Context Assumption substitutes the demonstration-conditioned policy for the unknown optimal next policy, $\pi_{k+1}^*(y|x) \approx \pi(y|x,c)$ (Eq. 4) [1], giving an intrinsic reward $r(y,x,c) = \log\pi(y|x,c) - \log\pi_k(y|x)$ (Eq. 5), independent of $\beta$ since a linear reward transform does not change the optimal policy [1]. In the toy example above this reward is $\log(0.7/0.5) \approx 0.336$: the demonstration-conditioned teacher prefers $A$ more than the current policy does, so the implicit reward pushes the policy toward it.

## Cost

**Theory, from the method's own math**: one extra forward pass per step to score the teacher's log-probabilities on the sampled tokens (Eq. 2 needs both $\pi_\theta$ and $\pi$ evaluated on the same sequence) [1]. If the teacher is a separate weight copy (EMA or frozen-base), that copy occupies extra memory but needs no gradients or optimizer state — cheaper than a second trained model, comparable to holding a frozen reference model. If the teacher is `"live"` (the current student), no extra weights are held, but the extra forward pass over the demonstration-conditioned prompt remains. Generation is on-policy every step, unlike SFT which never generates.

**In practice, per framework**: the paper's own accounting (its TRL-based implementation [1][3]) reports approximately 2.5× the FLOPs and roughly 4× the wall-clock training time of standard SFT [1], attributed to the added on-policy generation step. TRL's teacher options change this differently: `teacher_model_kind="live"` reuses the student for generation and, if paired with `use_teacher_server=True` and a vLLM server, can score teacher log-probabilities on that same server, "removing the teacher from the training step entirely" [3]; `"ema"` under pure-LoRA PEFT training holds the moving average in a dedicated teacher adapter rather than a second full model copy, while `"ema"` is unsupported for non-pure-LoRA PEFT configurations (e.g. with `modules_to_save` or `bias`) because a separate EMA copy cannot be parameter-matched to the student in that setting [3]. `use_liger_kernel=True` swaps in a fused JSD loss that avoids materializing full-vocabulary logits, but only under `distillation_mode="full_logits"`, and is incompatible with `distillation_is_clip` [3]. verl does not implement SDFT, so it carries no cost entry here [11].

## How to use it

- Data: one prompt column and one demonstration column per example — TRL's schema is `prompt` and `privileged_context`, combined by the template `"{prompt}\n\n{privileged_context}"` by default (`teacher_prompt_template`, overridable) [3]. The demonstration is shown only to the teacher; by default the student still generates from the plain prompt, though setting `generate_from_teacher=True` samples from the demonstration-conditioned prompt instead, trading on-policy fidelity for higher-quality rollouts [3].
- No explicit reward or label convention: the "reward" is the implicit, per-token log-probability gap between teacher and student derived above, never surfaced as a scalar the user supplies [1].
- Key knobs, with each source's own value ("not stated" = the source was checked and does not give it; "not checked" = this card did not verify that source's value for this key):

| knob | trl default [3] | paper [1] |
| --- | --- | --- |
| teacher parameterization † | `teacher_model_kind` = `"base"` (frozen initial weights); doc states this default as "matching the paper" | EMA of student params, "unless mentioned otherwise" (Algorithm 1: $\phi \leftarrow \alpha\theta + (1-\alpha)\phi$); base and live are ablated, not the stated default |
| KL/distillation estimator ‡ | `distillation_mode` = `"topk_logits"` (top 100 tokens) | full analytic per-token estimator (main experiments, Appendix B.1) |
| rollouts per prompt | `num_generations` = 8 | 1 (Appendix B.1: "a single on-policy rollout per training example") |
| EMA rate $\alpha$ | `teacher_update_rate` = 0.05 | swept over $\{0.01, 0.02, 0.05\}$, best chosen by validation |
| learning rate | 5e-5 | swept over $\{5e{-}6, 1e{-}5, 5e{-}5\}$, best chosen by validation |
| max completion length | 256 | 1024 (Knowledge Acquisition) / 2048 (Skill Learning) |
| epochs | `num_train_epochs` = 3.0 | swept over $\{1,2\}$ (Skill Learning) / typically 4 (Knowledge Acquisition) |

  † TRL's default is a frozen copy of the initial weights; the paper's stated default is an EMA of the student's own weights — not the same teacher-update rule, despite TRL's docs describing its default as matching the paper. ‡ TRL's default truncates to the top-100-token probability mass per step; the paper's default estimator marginalizes the full vocabulary — a cheaper, higher-variance approximation of the same underlying KL quantity, not an identical setting.

  Three readings of this table: the teacher-default line is a direct conflict between what TRL's docs claim ("matching the paper") and what the paper's own Section 3 and Algorithm 1 state (EMA by default) [3][1] — pick a teacher deliberately rather than trusting either source's framing of "the default." The rollout-count line matters for the method's own efficiency argument: the paper's Discussion explicitly contrasts SDFT's single rollout against GRPO's group sampling as a practical advantage [1], and TRL's default of 8 generations per prompt departs from that argument — check whether your `num_generations` setting still gives you the efficiency the paper reports. The estimator line is a like-for-like mismatch: TRL's default truncates to the top 100 tokens' probability mass per step, while the paper's default estimator marginalizes the full vocabulary [3][1] — a cheaper, higher-variance approximation of the same quantity, not an identical setting under a different name.
- Trade-off: the paper found SDFT benefits from more epochs than SFT (typically 2 for Skill Learning, 4 for Knowledge Acquisition), while "SFT tends to overfit rapidly and showed no performance gains beyond a single epoch in most cases" [1] — carrying over an SFT epoch count under-trains SDFT.

## While it runs

- Signals: this card did not find a dedicated SDFT logging/metrics guide from TRL comparable to its GRPO logging page; TRL's SDFT trainer doc (read 2026-08-09) covers configuration and usage but not a named list of health-check metrics [3]. No first-hand monitoring guidance beyond the config surface is claimed here.
- Published reference runs: the paper's own Table 1 (knowledge acquisition: SDFT 89% strict / 100% lenient / 98% OOD accuracy vs. SFT's 80% / 95% / 80%, continual pretraining's 9% / 37% / 7%, and an Oracle-RAG upper bound of 91% / 100% / 100%) [1] and Table 2 (Olmo-3-7B-Think, answer-only medical fine-tuning: base 31.2% accuracy / 4612 avg. output tokens, +SFT 23.5% / 3273 tokens, +SDFT 43.7% / 4180 tokens) [1] are the paper's own published numbers to compare against; no independent reference run outside the paper was found.
- Deciding number: Table 5's Science Q&A row reports, as (new-task accuracy, average of six prior-capability benchmarks): Base 32.1 / 65.5, SFT 66.2 / 53.4, SFT+re-invoke 66.0 / 60.2, DFT 54.8 / 60.2, SDFT 70.2 / 64.5 [1][5] — SDFT is the only method in that row that both beats every other method's new-task accuracy and stays closest to the base model's original 65.5 prior-capability average, i.e. it dominates on both axes rather than trading one for the other.
- Degeneracies and defaults: (1) the teacher-parameterization default conflict between TRL's docs and the paper, detailed above [3][1]. (2) "The effectiveness of SDFT depends critically on the base model's in-context learning capabilities... smaller models with weak ICL abilities fail to provide meaningful teacher signals" [1] — a base model too weak at in-context learning makes the teacher signal meaningless, per the paper's own scaling experiments. (3) TRL's `"ema"` teacher mode is explicitly unsupported for non-pure-LoRA PEFT configurations, since a separate EMA copy cannot be parameter-matched to the student in that setting [3].
- Named successors: none found. This card checked the paper's own text for follow-on citations and found none as of this reading [1]; no separate search of citing literature was performed.
- Known failure modes, from the paper's own Discussion and Limitations section [1]: "Learned Artifacts" — because the teacher is conditioned on the demonstration, it sometimes prefaces answers with phrases like "Based on the text..." or "Following the example...", and the student can reproduce these markers even though it never sees the demonstration itself; the paper reports that masking the loss over the first few completion tokens suppresses this without hurting accuracy, calling it "fundamentally a heuristic fix" with "a more principled solution remain[ing] an open problem" [1] — this maps directly to TRL's `num_loss_tokens_to_skip` knob [3]. Separately, the paper reports that SDFT "struggles when the desired behavior requires a fundamental shift in the model's generation patterns," giving as an example that turning a non-reasoning model into one that produces explicit chain-of-thought traces "proved difficult" [1].
- What the gain is - and is not: the paper's own framing places SDFT as "not an alternative to on-policy RL, but addresses a complementary learning regime" for demonstration-only settings without a reward function, and notes SDFT can serve "as an effective initialization for subsequent RL fine-tuning" since it raises pass@k diversity, not as a replacement for RL fine-tuning itself [1]. The method is scoped to learning from demonstrations that add a skill or knowledge; it is not shown to induce a different generation style (e.g., inducing chain-of-thought where none existed) [1].
