# LAB

Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01081

A taxonomy-driven synthetic-data pipeline plus a replay-buffered, multi-phase supervised fine-tuning schedule: an open teacher LLM generates the entire instruction-tuning dataset one taxonomy leaf at a time, and the student is trained knowledge-first, then skills, replaying earlier-phase data so it does not forget what it just learned.

**LAB** (Large-scale Alignment for chatBots) is defined by its introducing paper as "a novel methodology designed to overcome the scalability challenges in the instruction-tuning phase of large language model (LLM) training" [1]. It has two parts: a taxonomy that organizes instruction-tuning data into knowledge, foundational-skills, and compositional-skills branches, and a multi-phase instruction-tuning procedure with replay buffers; the paper states the taxonomy "serves the purpose of ensuring high diversity and quality" of the generated data while the phased training "ensures training stability and prevents catastrophic forgetting" [1]. LAB is itself an extension of two separate prior lines the paper places itself against: Self-Instruct-style synthetic data generation, in which a teacher LLM bootstraps new instructions from a handful of human seed examples [2], and the progressive, phased training framework introduced by Orca 2, which trains on datasets of increasing difficulty in incremental stages [3]. The paper gives three reasons for the design: human-annotated or GPT-4-generated instruction data is costly, and the terms of use of proprietary teacher models "typically forbid using the model to improve other models" [1]; random seed sampling in prior SDG methods (Self-Instruct, Alpaca) smooths the prompt distribution and pushes a teacher model toward over-sampling its own dominant response modes, under-covering the long tail of tasks [1]; and training on all data jointly, or in phases without replay, risks catastrophic forgetting between phases [1].

LAB is the method behind the InstructLab project; that project's own README links the same arXiv paper and states "InstructLab uses Large-Scale Alignment for ChatBots [1] (LAB), a new alignment tuning method for LLMs that leverages synthetic data" [4]. In the paper's own results, the LAB-trained Labradorite-13B (Llama-2-13B base) scores an MT-Bench of 7.23, above the best other Llama-2-13B-based model in the comparison, WizardLM-13B-v1.2, at 7.20, and the LAB-trained Merlinite-7B (Mistral-7B base) scores 7.66, above the best other Mistral-7B-based model, Zephyr-7B-beta, at 7.34 (Table 3) [1]. Lineage: Self-Instruct (2023 [2]) and Orca/Orca 2's progressive training (2023 [3]) -> LAB (2024 [1]) -> InstructLab project models Labradorite-13B and Merlinite-7B [1][4] -> project tooling split in 2025 into the still-active instructlab-training (SFT backend) and the successor synthetic-data framework sdg_hub, with the original instructlab-sdg package archived [5][6][7]. The paper was confirmed as a single-candidate exact title match on arXiv for "LAB: Large-Scale Alignment for ChatBots" (arXiv:2403.01081).

**When to pick it**: pick LAB when you need to instruction-tune a base model at scale from a defined taxonomy of tasks, without human annotators or a proprietary teacher, and you can supply 1-3 example pairs per task plus (for specialized domains) source documents. Contrast with its own SDG ancestor, Self-Instruct, whose random seed sampling the LAB paper argues under-covers the task long tail [1][2]; contrast with the concurrent GLAN method, which also uses a human-curated taxonomy but needs a proprietary teacher (GPT-4) and, unlike LAB's knowledge-SDG, cannot generate data for domains outside the teacher's own training coverage [1]; contrast with RLHF-based alignment (Llama-2-chat), which the paper describes as requiring tens of thousands of human-written instructions and about 1 million human-annotated preference comparisons [1]. LAB itself is supervised fine-tuning only - it has no preference-optimization or RL stage of its own.

**Variant of**: a taxonomy-guided variant of Self-Instruct-style synthetic data generation [2], combined with a two-step progressive training schedule the paper describes as similar to Orca 2's [1][3].

**Data it needs**: a taxonomy whose leaf nodes each carry 1-3 manually written instruction-response example pairs, organized under three branches - knowledge (split first by document type, e.g. textbooks or manuals, then by domain, e.g. finance, each domain paired with source documents of permissible license for knowledge-SDG), foundational skills (seeded from four public sources cited by the paper: the FLAN collection [Longpre et al., 2023], MAmmoTH [Xiang Yue et al., 2023], a Stack Overflow question-answer mining dataset [Yin et al., 2018], and MuSiQue [Trivedi et al., 2022]), and compositional skills (tasks combining knowledge and foundational skills) [1]. The paper's own run generated 1.2 million synthetic samples from its taxonomy - 617K knowledge, 588K skill - using Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct-v0.1 as the teacher [1]. At train time the format is standard instruction/response pairs; instructlab-training's own entry point consumes messages-format (`role`/`content`) JSONL [5]. LAB's SFT stage is offline: the full synthetic dataset is generated once, before training starts, and training is standard supervised fine-tuning over that fixed dataset, not on-policy sampling from the student being trained [1].

**Extra models**: no value network, no reward model, and no frozen reference model at training time - LAB is SFT, not RL, so none of PPO/GRPO/DPO's auxiliary models apply [1]. A teacher LLM is required, but only during offline data generation, where it plays four roles in sequence: instruction generator, instruction evaluator, response generator, and instruction-response pair evaluator (Skills-SDG stages 1-4) [1]; the paper's own teacher was the open-weight Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct-v0.1 [1]. The instructlab-training package that ships the SFT step performs only training and loads no teacher or evaluator model - the synthetic dataset must be produced separately by an SDG stage first [5][7]. See Cost.

**Shipped by**: instructlab-training (`from instructlab.training import run_training, TrainingArgs, TorchrunArgs, DeepSpeedOptions`), PyPI package `instructlab-training`; its GitHub repository was not archived and had commits pushed as recently as 2026-07-31 as of this 2026-08-09 check [5]. training-hub (`from training_hub import sft`), PyPI package `training-hub`, wraps the same instructlab-training backend behind a simplified call and is actively maintained by the Red Hat AI Innovation Team, last pushed 2026-08-06 [8]; its own support matrix lists SFT as "Implemented" via the InstructLab-Training backend [8]. For the synthetic-data half: the original `instructlab-sdg` package (`from instructlab.sdg import generate_data, Pipeline, LLMBlock, ...`) is archived, and its README now redirects further SDG work to sdg_hub [6][7][9]. sdg_hub (`from sdg_hub import Flow, FlowRegistry`), PyPI package `sdg-hub`, is the actively maintained successor (last pushed 2026-08-05) [9], but its documented built-in flows list, read in full, does not name a LAB-taxonomy flow [10] - reconstructing the paper's four-stage skills pipeline in sdg_hub, or installing the archived instructlab-sdg, is currently required rather than a one-line import.

## How it works

Build or extend a taxonomy of leaf-node tasks with a few hand-written examples each -> for every leaf, run the teacher through a four-stage generate/evaluate loop to synthesize instruction-response pairs -> split the resulting data into knowledge, foundational-skill, and compositional-skill buckets -> supervised-fine-tune the model through ordered phases, replaying earlier-phase data at each later phase [1].

**Taxonomy** (Section 3.1): the knowledge branch is first split by document type (textbooks, manuals) and then by domain (finance, statistics), with each domain carrying its own source documents plus a handful of example question-answer pairs, which the paper says gives "better control over the licensing of text documents" [1]. The foundational-skills branch covers mathematics, coding, linguistic ability and reasoning, seeded from the four public datasets listed above [1]. The compositional-skills branch covers tasks that combine knowledge and foundational skills, such as the paper's own example of writing a financial-results email, which needs both domain knowledge and basic arithmetic [1]. Adding a new task means adding a leaf node with 1-3 example pairs [1].

**Skills-SDG** (Section 3.2.1), four teacher-model prompt stages per leaf node, each with its own prompt template [1]:
1. Instruction generation - the teacher, given the leaf's example question(s) (Figure 3's template asks for "diverse questions on {task}" following listed principles), produces new questions for that leaf [1].
2. Evaluating synthetic instructions - the teacher, now acting as evaluator, filters out generated questions that fail relevance, safety, or answerability principles [1].
3. Generating responses - the teacher answers the surviving questions, using separate prompts/personas for creative domains (writing, roleplay) versus precise domains (STEM, extraction) [1].
4. Evaluating instruction-response pairs - the teacher scores each pair on a 3-point scale (Figure 4): 1 for an incorrect, irrelevant, unsafe, or incomplete answer, 2 for a correct but terse answer, 3 for "a perfect answer from an AI Assistant" that is comprehensive, detailed, and safe; only high-scoring pairs are kept [1].

**Knowledge-SDG** (Section 3.2.2): the same leaf-node examples are used, but the teacher is additionally given source documents, manuals, or books for that domain and must ground its generated answers in them, with the teacher again used as an evaluator to check the answers stay faithful to the source material [1]. This is why LAB does not need a teacher whose own training data covers every target domain, unlike GPT-4-teacher pipelines the paper contrasts itself against [1].

**Taxonomy-driven sampling rationale**: the paper argues (Figure 2) that random multi-example seed sampling, as in Self-Instruct, approximates the average of the whole seed pool per prompt, producing a smoothed input distribution that pushes the teacher toward its own dominant output modes; sampling seed examples from a single leaf node at a time keeps each task's prompts concentrated on that task, which the paper argues guarantees the teacher generates data for every leaf rather than mostly its favorite ones [1].

**Multi-phase training** (Section 3.3, Table 1): two phases. Knowledge tuning runs in two steps - step 1 trains on short-response knowledge samples with no replay, step 2 trains on long-response knowledge samples plus foundational-skills samples, replaying step 1's data [1]. Skill tuning starts from the best knowledge-tuning checkpoint and trains on compositional-skills samples, replaying both knowledge-tuning steps' data to address forgetting across the two-phase split [1]. Checkpoint selection uses MMLU during knowledge tuning and MT-Bench during skill tuning [1]; the paper states it deliberately favors earlier, lower-intermediate-score checkpoints because they generalize better than checkpoints selected for peak intermediate benchmark scores [1].

**Worked example** (paper's own Table 2 run) [1]: for Labradorite-13B, KT step 1 trains 630K short knowledge samples for 5 epochs at learning rate 2e-5, context 2048, effective batch size 3840, 385 warm-up steps, no replay; KT step 2 adds 230K long-knowledge-plus-skills samples for 7 epochs, replaying the 630K from step 1; ST trains 550K compositional-skill samples for 7 epochs at context 4096, replaying the combined data from both KT steps. Merlinite-7B uses the same sample counts and batch size but learning rate 1e-6, 800 warm-up steps, and 4/4/7 epochs across KT1/KT2/ST.

**Loss**: the paper gives no explicit loss formula for the training step; instructlab-training's README describes its training as fine-tuning on messages-format conversational data [5] - standard causal-language-model next-token cross-entropy, the field's usual SFT convention, is what this implies but is not a formula the paper itself states.

## Cost

**Theory, from the method's own math**:

- Time: SDG cost is paid once, up front, before any GPU training starts, and scales with 4 teacher-model prompt calls per leaf-node stage times the number of leaves and samples per stage [1]. Training cost is three sequential SFT passes over growing, replay-inflated datasets - KT/1 trains 630K samples with no replay, KT/2 trains 230K new samples while replaying the 630K from KT/1, and ST trains 550K new samples while replaying the combined KT/1+KT/2 data - so later phases process more total samples per epoch than their "new data" count alone suggests (Table 1, Table 2) [1]. No generation happens inside the training loop; this is not an RL/rollout method [1].
- Memory: one model is trained at a time; the offline teacher used for SDG is inference-only and never needs gradients or optimizer state [1]. A naive reading of the multi-phase design might assume every phase's checkpoint must stay resident together; the paper's own procedure only requires the previous phase's single checkpoint to start the next phase [1].

**In practice, per framework**:

- instructlab-training [5][11]: `run_training` launches via `torchrun`/`TorchrunArgs` for single- or multi-node runs. Its `TrainingArgs` Pydantic config, read from the package's own source, requires `max_seq_len`, `max_batch_len`, and `effective_batch_size` with no library-supplied defaults for any of the three - the operator sets them explicitly, trading memory for throughput - while `num_epochs` defaults to 1 if left unset [11]. The README frames `max_batch_len` as the token budget per GPU per batch and the value to lower first on out-of-memory errors [5].
- training-hub [8][12]: `sft()` exposes the same parameters (`num_epochs`, `effective_batch_size`, `learning_rate`, `max_seq_len`, `max_tokens_per_gpu`, `warmup_steps`, and others) as `Optional[...] = None` pass-throughs to the instructlab-training backend, read from the package's own `sft.py` source [12]. training-hub's `sft.py` adds one independent fallback that instructlab-training itself does not have: if `max_batch_len` is left unset, it defaults it to `max_seq_len` or, failing that, to a hardcoded 60000, whereas instructlab-training's own `TrainingArgs` requires `max_batch_len` with no default at all [11][12] - so a training-hub call can silently start with a memory-relevant value instructlab-training alone would have rejected as missing.
- These cost figures are per implementation as documented/sourced above; they do not carry to sdg_hub or instructlab-sdg, whose cost is the offline SDG stage covered in the theory bullet, not the SFT stage.

## How to use it

- Data prep: build or extend taxonomy leaves for the target tasks, each with 1-3 hand-written example pairs; for knowledge leaves, attach source documents that carry permissible licenses [1]. Run the 4-stage Skills-SDG pipeline (or Knowledge-SDG for grounded domains) with a teacher model, discarding pairs the teacher itself scores 1 on the 3-point rubric [1]. Convert the surviving pairs to messages-format JSONL for instructlab-training [5].
- Label/reward convention: there is no reward model or preference label - the training target is the teacher-generated, teacher-filtered response text itself, used directly as the SFT completion [1].
- Key knobs, with each source's own value ("not stated" = the source was checked and does not give the value):

| knob | paper, Labradorite-13B (Llama-2-13B) [1] | paper, Merlinite-7B (Mistral-7B) [1] | instructlab-training [11] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| learning rate | 2e-5 | 1e-6 | required, no library default |
| effective batch size | 3840 | 3840 | required, no library default |
| epochs, KT/1 / KT/2 / ST | 5 / 7 / 7 | 4 / 4 / 7 | `num_epochs` defaults to 1 |
| context length, KT / ST | 2048 / 4096 | 2048 / 4096 | required (`max_seq_len`), no library default |
| warm-up steps | 385 | 800 | not stated as having a package default |

  Two readings of this table: instructlab-training requires the operator to set learning rate, batch size, and sequence length explicitly rather than inheriting the paper's values as defaults, so a run that leaves these unset will fail to start rather than silently reproduce the paper; and the paper's own learning rates (2e-5 for the Llama-2-based model, 1e-6 for the Mistral-based one) differ by 20x between the two base models it trained, so copying one model's learning rate onto a different base model is a documented mismatch, not a safe default.
- Phase-order trade-off: the paper reports that starting with knowledge and foundational-skills training before compositional-skills training "leads to significantly better benchmark performance" than the alternative order, though it does not publish the alternative-order numbers in this paper [1]. Replay-buffer size grows every phase (0 at KT/1, KT/1's 630K at KT/2, KT/1+KT/2's combined data at ST), trading extra per-phase compute for resistance to forgetting [1].
- Install, launch, and distributed-training flags belong to the instructlab-training and training-hub framework pages; this card stops at the knobs that define the method.

## While it runs

- Signals: the paper's only stated monitoring signals are the checkpoint-selection benchmarks themselves - MMLU during the knowledge-tuning phase, MT-Bench during the skill-tuning phase - used to pick the best checkpoint at the end of each phase rather than to intervene mid-phase [1]. instructlab-training's README documents no LAB-specific live metric beyond noting the codebase can send logs to standard experiment trackers; it does not name specific values or thresholds to watch [5].
- Published reference runs: the paper's own Table 3 is the reference comparison. Labradorite-13B: MT-Bench 7.23 (average of 3 runs), MMLU 58.89, ARC 61.69, HellaSwag 83.15, Winogrande 79.56, GSM8K 40.11 - against the best other Llama-2-13B-based model in the table, WizardLM-13B-v1.2, at MT-Bench 7.20, MMLU 54.83, ARC 60.24, HellaSwag 82.62, Winogrande 76.40, GSM8K 43.75, and against Llama-2-13b-chat (SFT+RLHF) at MT-Bench 6.65, MMLU 54.58, ARC 59.81, HellaSwag 82.52, Winogrande 75.93, GSM8K 34.80 [1]. Merlinite-7B: MT-Bench 7.66 (average of 3 runs), MMLU 64.88, ARC 63.99, HellaSwag 84.37, Winogrande 78.24, GSM8K 44.58 - against the best other Mistral-7B-based model, Zephyr-7B-beta, at MT-Bench 7.34, MMLU 61.07, ARC 63.74, HellaSwag 84.19, Winogrande 78.06, GSM8K 34.04, and against Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 at MT-Bench 6.84, MMLU 60.37, ARC 63.65, HellaSwag 84.76, Winogrande 76.80, GSM8K 41.85 [1]. All MT-Bench, MMLU, ARC, HellaSwag, and Winogrande scores in the table are reported on the same evaluation settings, matched to the LMSYS Chatbot Arena Leaderboard's protocol, per the paper [1].
- Degeneracies and defaults: the paper reports that using cosine learning-rate decay across the phase boundaries destabilizes later training phases, hypothesized to be because the learning rate decays near zero and narrows the loss landscape, complicating the next phase's gradient updates; LAB instead uses a small learning rate with an extended linear warm-up at the start of each phase [1]. The paper also states it deliberately avoids overtraining, preferring earlier checkpoints over higher-scoring later ones for better generalization [1].
- Named successors: none found. Queried the Semantic Scholar Graph API for all 53 papers citing arXiv:2403.01081 and read every returned title [13]; none is presented, by its own title, as a method that fixes a specific documented bias in LAB's taxonomy or multi-phase training. The InstructLab GitHub organization's active repositories (instructlab-training, sdg_hub, training-hub) likewise do not name a bias-fixing successor method in their READMEs.
- Known failure modes: the paper has no dedicated Limitations section (checked the full extracted text: Introduction, Related Work, Methodology, Results, References only) [1], so no first-hand paper-stated failure mode is available. A search of `instructlab/training`'s closed GitHub issues for "catastrophic forgetting" and "replay buffer" returned zero results as of 2026-08-09; no maintainer reply naming a LAB-specific numerical trap was found in that search.
- What the gain is - and is not: the paper's own results discussion states that its training method "ensures that the model is not only good at multi-turn conversation but also maintains its knowledge or reasoning capability," pointing to the non-MT-Bench metrics (MMLU, ARC, HellaSwag, Winogrande, GSM8K) as evidence the phased, replay-buffered schedule avoids the capability loss that motivated it [1]. The paper is explicit that this level of performance is reached "using the open-weights MIXTRAL-8X7B-INSTRUCT-V0.1, which is relatively weaker teacher model at orders of magnitude less cost" than the GPT-4 teacher used by the compared systems [1] - the claimed gain is competitive quality at much lower data-generation cost, not an outright win on every metric: Labradorite-13B's GSM8K score of 40.11 is below both WizardLM-13B-v1.2's 43.75 and Orca-2's 48.22 in the same table [1].
