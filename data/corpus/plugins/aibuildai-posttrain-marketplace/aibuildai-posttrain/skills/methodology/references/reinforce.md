# REINFORCE

Sample a completion from the current policy, score it, and push up the log-probability of every token in it in proportion to (reward minus a baseline) — no critic network required.

**REINFORCE** is a Monte Carlo policy-gradient algorithm for training a stochastic policy against a scalar reward signal, introduced by Ronald Williams for connectionist reinforcement-learning networks with stochastic units [1]. The paper's journal page is at https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00992696, and the same paper is openly readable at https://people.cs.umass.edu/~barto/courses/cs687/williams92simple.pdf [1]. The defining paper shows that weight adjustments made by these algorithms lie, on average, along the gradient of expected reinforcement, without explicitly computing a gradient estimate or storing information from which one could be computed [1]. REINFORCE has no parent method in this corpus: it is the classical stochastic policy-gradient ancestor that later online RL methods for LLMs build on and modify. The mechanism is the score-function estimator: for a sampled action (in the LLM setting, a full completion), scale the gradient of its log-probability by the reward it received minus a baseline, and average over samples [1]. The paper gives three reasons for studying this class of gradient-following algorithms rather than a variance argument for the baseline: like backpropagation, the gradient offers a general heuristic basis for algorithms that are often simple to implement and effective in practice; gradient computation can serve as the core of more sophisticated algorithms; and studying algorithms that resemble a gradient analysis can enhance understanding of existing algorithms [1]. Theorem 1 establishes only that the expected update is nonnegatively correlated with the true gradient for any baseline choice, not that any particular baseline lowers variance — the paper's own Section 8.3 states that its analysis offers no basis for choosing among baselines and that a baseline's effect on convergence speed is established only empirically, not by Theorem 1 [1].

REINFORCE was revived for LLM alignment by Ahmadian et al., who model an entire LLM generation as a single action (a bandit formulation) rather than one action per token, and apply the REINFORCE estimator with a simple moving-average baseline to RLHF fine-tuning [2]. Their reward-optimization comparison on TL;DR Summarize and Anthropic-HH found REINFORCE with a moving-average baseline reached final win-rates of 70.7 / 37.9 / 55.3 (TL;DR / HH-Pythia / HH-Llama) against 67.6 / 29.2 / 32.0 for PPO and 70.4 / 36.4 / 52.3 for a learned-baseline "Vanilla PG" variant on the same reward models, with their proposed REINFORCE Leave-One-Out (RLOO, k=4) variant reaching 77.9 / 43.7 / 64.1 [2, Table 1]. That paper is the basis for TRL's `RLOOTrainer` [3]. Lineage in one line: REINFORCE (Williams, 1992 [1]) → revisited for LLM RLHF as REINFORCE-with-baseline and RLOO (Ahmadian et al., 2024 [2]) → further variants REINFORCE++ (2025 [5]), Tapered Off-Policy REINFORCE / TOPR (2025 [6]), and Reinforce-Rej (2025 [7]); GRPO and PPO are separately-descended, PPO-family online methods that this card contrasts against rather than descends from [8][9]. The paper's identity was confirmed against a shortlist automated match to arXiv:2312.08935 ("Math-Shepherd"), whose own abstract uses "reinforce" only as a verb describing step-by-step PPO training and never defines or names the REINFORCE algorithm [10] — the name-search hit was wrong, and the true defining paper, pre-dating arXiv, was located by following the citation chain in [2], which cites Williams (1992) directly for the estimator it uses.

**When to pick it**: pick REINFORCE when every sampled completion can be scored with a scalar reward and the simplicity of having no critic and no clipped surrogate objective is the priority over the lower gradient variance that a group- or batch-relative baseline gives; Ahmadian et al.'s own results put plain REINFORCE-with-baseline behind their leave-one-out variant RLOO but ahead of a PPO baseline on the same reward models [2, Table 1]. Prefer RLOO [3] or GRPO [8] when multiple completions per prompt are affordable, since both replace the moving-average baseline with a same-batch or same-group baseline that Ahmadian et al. show reduces variance further [2]. Prefer PPO [9] when a learned per-token critic and a clipped trust region are wanted despite the extra model. Prefer DPO [4] when there are preference pairs and no way to score fresh on-policy samples: DPO is offline, trained directly on pairs of completions to the same prompt without an explicit reward model or online sampling [4].

**Variant of**: none — REINFORCE is the foundational stochastic policy-gradient algorithm in this corpus [1]. RLOO [2][3], REINFORCE++ [5], and TOPR [6] are later variants built on it; GRPO [8] and PPO [9] are separately-descended, non-REINFORCE online methods this card only contrasts against.

**Data it needs**: prompts from the target task distribution and a scalar reward per sampled completion (a reward model or a programmable reward function); no preference pairs are required for the REINFORCE update itself. On-policy: Ahmadian et al. sample completions fresh from the current policy each step; their TL;DR Summarize training split holds 116K human-written instructions and 93K human-annotated preference pairs, and their Anthropic-HH split holds 112K training preference pairs, with the RL stage reusing the same prompts as the SFT stage [2]. Rewards are produced by a reward model trained on each dataset's preference pairs [2]. A pre-collected, fixed completion set cannot be substituted; the baseline in Williams's own formulation is likewise updated online, not fit offline [1].

**Extra models**: none required by the algorithm itself — Williams's original REINFORCE has no value network and no reference model [1]. Ahmadian et al.'s LLM adaptation shapes the reward with a KL penalty against a frozen reference policy before it reaches the REINFORCE update, the same convention PPO uses, so a frozen reference model is needed only if that KL-shaping is applied, not for the baseline itself [2]. Their moving-average baseline is a running scalar, not a trained model, in contrast to their "Vanilla PG" ablation and to PPO, both of which train a separate learned baseline / value network [2]. Details in Cost.

**Shipped by**: no framework surveyed here ships a trainer literally implementing Williams's REINFORCE with a moving-average baseline. TRL exports `RLOOTrainer`, described in its own docs as implementing the REINFORCE-style optimization from Ahmadian et al. with a leave-one-out baseline rather than a moving average [3]. verl's PPO-family actor supports `algorithm.adv_estimator` values `reinforce_plus_plus` and `reinforce_plus_plus_baseline` (REINFORCE++ [5]) and `rloo`, but has no plain "reinforce" option [11]. Building the paper's exact algorithm — a single-baseline, no-clip REINFORCE update — would mean adding a new advantage/loss on top of an existing online sampling loop (e.g., swap RLOOTrainer's leave-one-out baseline for a running scalar), not writing a new rollout loop, since the generate-then-score infrastructure already exists in both frameworks [3][11].

## How it works

The loop, one prompt at a time in the LLM formulation: sample one (or more) completions from the current policy; score each with a reward; subtract a baseline from the reward; scale each token's log-probability gradient by that baseline-subtracted reward and average [1][2].

**Williams's general update rule** [1, Sec. 4], for a network unit with output distribution $g_i(\xi, \mathbf{w}^i, \mathbf{x}^i)$ and weight $w_{ij}$:

$$ \Delta w_{ij} = \alpha_{ij}\,(r - b_{ij})\,e_{ij}, \qquad e_{ij} = \frac{\partial \ln g_i}{\partial w_{ij}} $$

$\alpha_{ij}$ is a nonnegative learning-rate factor that may depend on $\mathbf{w}^i$ and time but not on the current output or reward; $b_{ij}$ is a reinforcement baseline, conditionally independent of the unit's output given the weights and input; $e_{ij}$ is called the characteristic eligibility of $w_{ij}$ [1]. The paper names any algorithm of this form a REINFORCE algorithm, an acronym for "REward Increment = Nonnegative Factor times Offset Reinforcement times Characteristic Eligibility" [1]. Its central result (Theorem 1) is that the inner product of the expected weight update $E\{\Delta\mathbf{W}\mid\mathbf{W}\}$ with the true gradient $\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}E\{r\mid\mathbf{W}\}$ is always nonnegative, and if the learning-rate factor $\alpha_{ij}=\alpha$ is the same constant for every weight, then $E\{\Delta\mathbf{W}\mid\mathbf{W}\} = \alpha\,\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}E\{r\mid\mathbf{W}\}$ exactly — the expected update is the true gradient, up to a positive scalar, without ever forming a gradient estimate from a model of the environment [1].

**LLM full-sequence form** [2, Eq. 6–8], treating an entire generation $y$ as one action given prompt $x$:

$$ \mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D},\; y\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot|x)}\big[R(y,x)\,\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y|x)\big] $$

with variance reduced, while remaining unbiased, by subtracting any baseline $b$ that does not depend on the sampled $y$:

$$ \mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D},\; y\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot|x)}\big[(R(y,x)-b)\,\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y|x)\big] $$

Ahmadian et al.'s parameter-free choice is a moving average of the rewards seen so far in training, $b_{\mathrm{MA}} = \frac{1}{S}\sum_s R(x^s, y^s)$ over the $S$ steps taken [2]. Worked example: suppose the last four scored completions received rewards $0.6, 0.4, 0.9, 0.5$, giving $b_{\mathrm{MA}} = 0.6$; a new completion scoring $r=0.9$ gets signal $r-b_{\mathrm{MA}}=+0.3$ (its log-probability is pushed up), and one scoring $r=0.4$ gets $-0.2$ (pushed down) — the same arithmetic Williams's baseline subtraction performs, just with $b$ tracked as a running average of episode rewards instead of a per-unit reinforcement baseline [1][2]. Neither Williams's original formulation nor Ahmadian et al.'s moving-average variant clips the probability ratio or bounds the update the way PPO's surrogate objective does [1][2][9]; their RLOO variant instead replaces the moving average with a leave-one-out mean of the other $G-1$ samples in the same batch, $b_i = \frac{1}{G-1}\sum_{j\neq i} r_j$ [2][3]. TRL's shipped `RLOOTrainer` goes further than the paper's RLOO and wraps that leave-one-out advantage in a PPO-style clipped surrogate objective, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{RLOO}}(\theta) = -\frac{1}{G}\sum_i \min\!\big(r_i(\theta)\hat A_i,\ \operatorname{clip}(r_i(\theta),1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)\hat A_i\big)$ with $r_i(\theta)=\pi_\theta(o_i|q)/\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(o_i|q)$; in TRL's default fully-online, single-step setting the ratio equals 1 at the point of sampling, so the clip rarely binds unless the batch is reused across `num_iterations` > 1 [3].

## Cost

**Theory, from the method's own math:**

- Time: one policy forward pass to sample the completion(s), one score for each (a reward-model or programmatic-reward call, not necessarily differentiated through), and one policy forward/backward pass to compute $\nabla_\theta \log\pi_\theta(y|x)$ for the update [1][2]. No repeated minibatch epochs over the same rollout batch are part of the algorithm's own definition, unlike PPO's multiple-epoch design [9].
- Memory: the baseline in Williams's formulation and in Ahmadian et al.'s moving-average variant is a running scalar, not a trained model, so no extra model's weights, gradients, or optimizer state are held beyond the policy itself [1][2]. This is the theoretical basis for Ahmadian et al.'s statement that REINFORCE-with-baseline and RLOO need one fewer loaded model copy than PPO and their learned-baseline "Vanilla PG" ablation, which each train a separate baseline/critic network [2]. If a KL-shaped reward is used, add one frozen reference-model forward pass, no gradients [2].

**In practice, per framework:**

- TRL `RLOOTrainer` [3]: ships the leave-one-out, clipped-surrogate variant described above, not the moving-average one. `beta` (the KL coefficient against a frozen reference policy) defaults to 0.05, and the KL penalty is folded into the per-completion reward before the leave-one-out baseline is subtracted, so with `beta>0` a frozen reference model is loaded [3]. `epsilon` (the clip range) defaults to 0.2 [3]. `num_generations` defaults to 2, i.e. two completions per prompt is the minimum batch RLOO needs for its leave-one-out baseline to be defined [3]. Generation is bounded by TRL's usual batch/accumulation settings or handed to vLLM (`use_vllm`) [3].
- verl [11]: no plain-REINFORCE `adv_estimator`; `reinforce_plus_plus`, `reinforce_plus_plus_baseline`, and `rloo` are configured the same way as `gae`/`grpo` through `algorithm.adv_estimator`, with the shared `algorithm.use_kl_in_reward` (default `False`) controlling whether a frozen reference model's KL is folded into the reward at all [11]. The in-reward KL coefficient itself is inconsistently documented on the same page: the sample config block lists `kl_coef: 0.005` under `algorithm.kl_ctrl`, while the parameter's own prose description states the default is 0.001 [11] — this card reports both readings rather than picking one. Forward/backward memory is bounded by verl's `ppo_micro_batch_size_per_gpu` and related batch-size keys, not by anything specific to the reinforce-family estimators [11].
- Do not carry TRL's RLOO defaults to verl's reinforce-family estimators or vice versa; both frameworks implement REINFORCE-descended baselines, not the paper's own moving average, and their KL defaults differ (`beta=0.05` in TRL [3] vs `use_kl_in_reward=False` in verl [11]).

## How to use it

- Prompts: from the target task distribution; Ahmadian et al. used the dedicated SFT split of TL;DR Summarize (116K human-written instructions) for the TL;DR RL stage, and for Anthropic-HH (112K training preference pairs) reused the same prompts as its SFT stage, since that dataset has no separate SFT split [2].
- Reward: one scalar per completion, from a reward model or programmatic function; Ahmadian et al. initialize both the reward model and the policy from the same SFT checkpoint [2]. If a KL penalty is folded into the reward (their setup, matching PPO's convention), it needs a frozen reference model as noted in Extra models [2].
- Key knobs, with each source's own value ("not stated" = the source was checked and does not give it; "not checked" = this card did not verify that source's key). Rows marked † are TRL's RLOO / verl's reinforce-family descendants, not Williams's or Ahmadian et al.'s plain-REINFORCE baseline, and do not compare directly to the paper column:

| knob | Williams 1992 [1] | Ahmadian et al. 2024 [2] | TRL `RLOOTrainer`† [3] | verl reinforce-family† [11] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| baseline type | per-unit reinforcement baseline $b_{ij}$ | moving average of past-episode rewards | leave-one-out mean of batch | leave-one-out / global (per `adv_estimator` choice) |
| samples per prompt | 1 (single action per trial) | 1 | `num_generations` default 2 | not checked |
| KL coefficient | not applicable (no KL term) | present via reward shaping, value not stated in fetched abstract/sections | `beta` default 0.05 | `use_kl_in_reward` default False; `kl_coef` documented as 0.001 in prose but 0.005 in the sample config on the same page |
| clipping | none | none | `epsilon` (clip range) default 0.2 | not checked |
| learning-rate factor | $\alpha_{ij}$, nonnegative, may vary per weight | not stated | not checked | not checked |

  Two readings of that table: Williams's algorithm and its LLM revival by Ahmadian et al. are both unclipped, single-baseline methods, and neither framework ships that exact form — both ship the leave-one-out descendant instead, so a run configured against this table's TRL/verl columns is running RLOO or REINFORCE++, not plain REINFORCE. The KL coefficient is off by default in verl's shared reinforce-family config and on by default (`0.05`) in TRL's RLOO, so the same nominal method drifts in reference-model strength purely from which framework's defaults are inherited.
- Baseline choice is the practical trade-off a run designer faces: a moving average (Williams/Ahmadian et al.'s plain REINFORCE) needs only one sample per prompt but adapts slowly and lags a fast-shifting reward distribution, while a leave-one-out or group baseline (RLOO/GRPO) reacts immediately to the current batch but costs $G$ completions per prompt instead of one [1][2][3].

## While it runs

- Signals and their healthy shapes: Ahmadian et al.'s own comparison plots test reward against training steps for each method, reporting that RLOO outperforms all other methods consistently while Vanilla PG consistently outperforms PPO on the same reward models — a rising, then plateauing, test-reward curve is the healthy shape their figure shows for the REINFORCE family [2]. No first-hand REINFORCE-specific guidance on entropy or gradient-variance monitoring was found in the sources fetched for this card; TRL's RLOO doc logs `kl` (only if `beta` is nonzero) and per-completion length/clip statistics as its own signals [3], but those are RLOO's, not plain REINFORCE's.
- Published reference runs: Ahmadian et al.'s Table 1 win-rates (REINFORCE w/ baseline: 70.7 / 37.9 / 55.3 on TL;DR / HH-Pythia / HH-Llama, against PPO's 67.6 / 29.2 / 32.0 and RLOO k=4's 77.9 / 43.7 / 64.1, all against the same reward models) are the known-good comparison point when no other threshold exists [2, Table 1].
- Degeneracies and defaults: a moving-average baseline computed from very few prior steps is a noisy, high-variance estimate of $E[r]$, which the baseline-subtraction argument in Williams's own unbiasedness proof does not protect against — the proof shows any $y$-independent baseline keeps the estimator unbiased, not that a specific baseline is low-variance in a short window [1]. TRL's RLOO requires `num_generations` ≥ 2 for its leave-one-out baseline to be defined at all [3], the same "group of one has no baseline" degeneracy documented for GRPO and RLOO elsewhere in this corpus.
- Named successors: REINFORCE++ replaces prompt-level (local) advantage normalization, as used by GRPO and RLOO, with normalization across the entire global batch, which its authors present as a more stable, effectively unbiased estimator whose bias vanishes as batch size grows [5]. Tapered Off-Policy REINFORCE (TOPR) uses an asymmetric, tapered importance-sampling correction to allow fully offline training and unified handling of positive and negative examples without a KL penalty [6]. Reinforce-Rej filters out prompts whose group of samples is either entirely correct or entirely incorrect, which its authors find improves KL efficiency and stability over unfiltered REINFORCE-style updates [7].
- Known failure modes: only from the sources fetched for this card — no maintainer-issue search was performed for a plain-REINFORCE trainer (none is shipped by TRL or verl to file issues against, per Shipped by), so this bullet reports only what the papers state. Reinforce-Rej's own analysis attributes GRPO's known effectiveness largely to discarding prompts with entirely incorrect responses rather than to reward normalization, implying that an unfiltered REINFORCE-style run on prompts the policy cannot yet solve at all wastes gradient signal on uniformly-zero-reward groups [7].
- What the gain is — and is not: Ahmadian et al.'s central claim is that simpler REINFORCE-style optimization, without PPO's clipped trust region or per-token value network, matches or outperforms PPO and offline "RL-free" methods such as DPO and RAFT on their reward-optimization and win-rate comparisons [2] — the paper's contribution is showing PPO's extra machinery is not necessary in the RLHF setting they test, not a claim that REINFORCE adds reasoning or capability beyond what PPO-style RL already provides.
