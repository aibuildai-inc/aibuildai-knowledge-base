# GKD

Distill a teacher into a smaller student by having the student generate its own training sequences and scoring them token-by-token against the teacher's distribution, instead of training only on a fixed set of teacher- or human-written sequences.

**GKD** (Generalized Knowledge Distillation) is a knowledge-distillation method for auto-regressive sequence models introduced in "On-Policy Distillation of Language Models: Learning from Self-Generated Mistakes" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13649), which frames it as unifying supervised and on-policy distillation: the student can be trained on a mixture of a fixed dataset and its own sampled outputs, under a choice of divergence between student and teacher token distributions [1]. Its parent is Supervised KD, where the student is trained to imitate the teacher's token-level probability distribution over a fixed set of ground-truth or teacher-generated sequences, going back to Hinton et al.'s original knowledge-distillation paper [2]. GKD's stated motivation is that fixed-sequence KD suffers from a train-inference distribution mismatch: the student is scored against sequences it did not generate, so once errors compound during its own decoding it faces states never seen during training; on-policy generation removes that mismatch, and letting the student pick its own divergence (rather than always forward KL) helps when the student lacks the capacity to match the teacher's full distribution [1].

GKD's own on-policy variant is now shipped in TRL under a differently-named, non-experimental trainer: TRL's `DistillationTrainer` documents itself as implementing GKD's on-policy distillation directly, citing this paper [3]. In the original paper's task-specific experiments (T5-XL teacher distilled into smaller T5 students on summarization, translation, and arithmetic reasoning), on-policy GKD's own performance gain over the initial (un-distilled) student, averaged across student sizes, was 2.1x the size of the gain achieved by baseline KD methods on summarization, 1.7x on machine translation, and 1.9x on arithmetic reasoning - i.e. a ratio of GKD's improvement to the baselines' improvement, not GKD's improvement measured directly against the student (Figure 1) [1]. On the paper's task-agnostic instruction-tuning experiment, the teacher is FLAN T5-XL and the paper states in its setup text that it is distilled "into the FLAN T5-Base" [1], but the results text that reports the un-distilled student's scores (52.4% MMLU / 41% BBH for the teacher, 35.6% MMLU / 31.25% BBH for the student) instead calls the student "the student T5-large model" [1] - the paper is internally inconsistent about which student size this experiment used, and this card does not resolve it. The paper's introduction reports this task-agnostic result as a 2% and 1% absolute accuracy improvement on the held-out BBH and MMLU benchmark suites respectively (Figure 10) [1], and states that it does not run SeqKD in this setting because of that baseline's computational inefficiency for generating data from the teacher during training [1]. Lineage: Supervised KD (Hinton et al. 2015 [2]) / SeqKD (Kim & Rush 2016 [4]) -> GKD (2023 [1]) -> shipped on-policy special case as trl `DistillationTrainer` [3]. The paper match was confirmed by an exact-title search on arXiv: the shortlist's title "On-Policy Distillation of Language Models: Learning from Self-Generated Mistakes" matches arXiv:2306.13649 exactly, and the paper's own abstract states it introduces "Generalized Knowledge Distillation (GKD)" [1].

**When to pick it**: pick GKD when you have a larger teacher model and want to compress it into a smaller student while correcting the exposure-bias gap that plain sequence-level or token-level imitation leaves behind, and you can afford to run the student's own decoder during training [1]. Its parent, Supervised KD [2], is the offline alternative: cheaper per step (no generation from the student), appropriate when a fixed teacher-generated or ground-truth corpus is good enough. SeqKD, training on teacher-generated sequences via ordinary supervised fine-tuning, is the paper's other offline baseline; the paper's own text calls SeqKD computationally inefficient for generating data from the teacher during training, and for that reason skips it in the task-agnostic experiment, so SeqKD is not cheaper than on-policy GKD's generation cost - it was also outperformed by on-policy GKD where the paper does run it [1]. The nearest online neighbor is RLHF/RLAIF: GKD's own paper shows the two are not exclusive — Equation 5 combines a scalar-reward RL objective with the on-policy distillation loss in one weighted sum, to fine-tune with RL while limiting the "alignment tax" via distillation from a teacher [1].

**Variant of**: Supervised KD [2] (which is itself built on classic KD [2]); GKD's on-policy limit ($\lambda=1$) is also what TRL ships as `DistillationTrainer` [3].

**Data it needs**: input prompts, optionally paired with fixed target sequences (ground-truth or teacher-generated) when $\lambda<1$; no reward signal, no preference pairs. TRL's `GKDTrainer` expects each row as a list of chat `messages` with `role` (`system`, `assistant`, or `user`) and `content`, i.e. conversational SFT-style data [5]. It is on-policy when $\lambda>0$: a Bernoulli draw per batch decides whether the student generates fresh completions that step or the batch is taken from the fixed dataset, so at $\lambda=1$ every batch is freshly sampled from the current student and no completions are ever reused across the whole run [1]. The paper's task-specific experiments used out-of-the-box T5 fine-tuning datasets (XSum, WMT en-de, GSM8K); the task-agnostic instruction-tuning run used FLAN2021 with 5.36M examples over 62 tasks [1].

**Extra models**: one frozen teacher model, forward passes only, needed at every training step to score the (student- or dataset-) sequences (a required part of the method's own definition, not a framework default) [1]. No value network, no separate reward model, and no reference model for a KL penalty distinct from the teacher (the teacher plays that role directly, via the divergence in the loss) [1]. Cost details in Cost.

**Shipped by**: trl, with one caveat about which trl. `trl.experimental.gkd.GKDTrainer` is present in the released 0.27.2 that a pinned task environment typically installs; `trl.DistillationTrainer` exists only on trl `main` and is absent from that release, so a reader pinned to a release reaches GKD through the experimental trainer alone. Two different top-level entry points cover different parts of GKD's design space: `trl.experimental.gkd.GKDTrainer` (import from the `experimental` subpackage) implements the full paper — arbitrary student-data fraction $\lambda \in [0,1]$ mixing on-policy and fixed-dataset batches [5]; `trl.DistillationTrainer`, exported from TRL's top-level package (not experimental), implements only the fully on-policy special case ($\lambda=1$ fixed) with a memory-efficient chunked JSD loss and optional vLLM-accelerated generation, and documents itself as citing and implementing this paper [3]. A closed TRL pull request states that `GKDTrainer` now warns at init time when its config ($\lambda=1$, temperature$=1$) is exactly reproducible by `DistillationTrainer`, pointing users toward the latter for that case [6]. No other framework in this corpus's shortlist implements GKD.

## How it works

Each step: draw $u\sim\mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)$; if $u\le\lambda$ sample a batch of prompts and let the student generate completions on-policy, otherwise take a batch of (input, output) pairs from the fixed dataset; then take a gradient step on the chosen divergence between teacher and student token distributions, computed on that batch [1].

**The objective**, given as an unnumbered boxed equation in the paper [1]:

$$ L_{\mathrm{GKD}}(\theta) := (1-\lambda)\,\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim (X,Y)}\big[\mathcal{D}(p_T \| p_S^\theta)(y|x)\big] \;+\; \lambda\,\mathbb{E}_{x\sim X}\Big[\mathbb{E}_{y\sim p_S(\cdot|x)}\big[\mathcal{D}(p_T \| p_S^\theta)(y|x)\big]\Big] $$

$p_T$ is the teacher's token-level distribution, $p_S^\theta$ the student's; $x$ is the input, $y$ a full output sequence; $\lambda\in[0,1]$ is the student-data fraction; $\mathcal{D}$ is any divergence between per-token distributions, applied at the sequence level as the length-normalized sum of the per-token divergence [1, Eq. 2]:

$$ \mathcal{D}(p_T \| p_S^\theta)(y|x) := \frac{1}{L_y}\sum_{n=1}^{L_y} \mathcal{D}\big(p_T(\cdot|y_{<n},x)\,\|\,p_S^\theta(\cdot|y_{<n},x)\big) $$

The paper's chosen divergence family is the generalized Jensen-Shannon divergence, JSD($\beta$), which interpolates between forward and reverse KL [1, Eq. 1]:

$$ \mathcal{D}_{JSD(\beta)}(P\|Q) = \beta\, D_{KL}\!\big(P \,\|\, \beta P + (1-\beta) Q\big) + (1-\beta)\, D_{KL}\!\big(Q \,\|\, \beta P + (1-\beta) Q\big),\qquad 0<\beta<1 $$

JSD($\beta$) behaves like forward KL as $\beta\to 0$ and like reverse KL as $\beta\to 1$ [1]. Setting $\lambda=0$ recovers plain Supervised KD [1, Eq. 3]; setting $\lambda=1$ recovers pure "on-policy distillation" [1, Eq. 4]. Two special cases the paper names explicitly: ImitKD (Lin et al. 2020) and f-distill (Wen et al. 2023) are "mixed" variants at $\lambda=0.5$ using forward KL and total-variation distance respectively, and both are used as baselines the paper compares against [1].

Worked example with $\beta=0.5$ (standard JSD, one token, two-outcome toy vocabulary): teacher $P=(0.9,0.1)$, student $Q=(0.5,0.5)$. Mixture $M=0.5P+0.5Q=(0.7,0.3)$. $D_{KL}(P\|M)=0.9\log(0.9/0.7)+0.1\log(0.1/0.3)\approx0.9(0.2513)+0.1(-1.0986)\approx0.1163$ nats; $D_{KL}(Q\|M)=0.5\log(0.5/0.7)+0.5\log(0.5/0.3)\approx0.5(-0.3365)+0.5(0.5108)\approx0.0872$ nats. $\mathcal{D}_{JSD(0.5)}=0.5(0.1163)+0.5(0.0872)\approx0.1017$ nats. This is a single-token illustration of the divergence formula itself, not a claim about either framework's numeric implementation.

## Cost

**Theory, from the method's own math:**

- Time: whenever $\lambda>0$, each on-policy batch requires the student to auto-regressively generate a full completion before any gradient step can be computed — a full decode, not a single forward pass — and the teacher must then score that completion, adding one more full forward pass per batch over the generated tokens [1]. At $\lambda=1$ every single training batch pays this generation cost, unlike PPO/GRPO-style RL where generation happens once per rollout that may be reused for several gradient updates.
- Memory: two models must be resident at once — student (trained: weights + gradients + optimizer state) and teacher (frozen: weights only, no gradients or optimizer state, but a forward pass over the full vocabulary distribution at every output position) [1]. Unlike PPO, GKD needs no value network; unlike GRPO/PPO's KL penalty, it needs no separate frozen reference copy of the student, since the teacher plays that role directly in the divergence term. A naive reading of the divergence formula (Eq. 2) implies the full per-token teacher distribution over the vocabulary must be materialized for every output token to compute $\mathcal{D}$; the framework bullet below shows one implementation avoids holding this in full.

**In practice, per framework:**

- trl `DistillationTrainer` [3]: uses a chunked JSD loss to reduce loss-computation memory [3]. Generation is on-policy every step (this trainer only implements $\lambda=1$) [3], optionally accelerated with vLLM in either colocated or server mode [3]; `max_completion_length` (default 512) bounds the generated length [3].
- trl `GKDTrainer` [5]: is described in the docs as a wrapper around `SFTTrainer` that additionally takes a `teacher_model` argument [5]; a closed GitHub issue reports a training-speed regression under this trainer between TRL v0.21.0 and v0.25.1. A non-maintainer commenter on that issue proposed that the teacher's forward pass was being repeated during backward-pass recomputation, but the maintainer's final reply explicitly rejects that theory ("the teacher itself is not recomputed during backward, as your own forward-count diagnostic confirmed") and instead attributes the regression to `torch.nn.DataParallel` auto-wrapping the student model (triggered because `n_gpu>1` was visible to the process) combined with the overhead of `gradient_checkpointing=True`, which became TRL's default in v0.22.0 per the maintainer's own link into the source at that commit [7].
- Check the installed TRL version and its dependencies before comparing trainers. In TRL 0.27.2, `GKDTrainer` supports the Liger fused chunked JSD loss when `liger-kernel` is installed and `use_liger_kernel=True`; the option is off by default [11]. This is separate from generation: that version's GKD trainer source has no vLLM generation path [11]. `DistillationTrainer` documents both chunked loss and optional vLLM generation, but only covers $\lambda=1$ [3].

## How to use it

- Data: conversational rows with a `messages` field (system/user/assistant turns) for `GKDTrainer` [5]; `DistillationTrainer`'s quick-start example uses a prompt-only dataset and generates the rest on-policy [3]. There is no separate reward/label column — the teacher model itself is the supervision signal at every position.
- A closed TRL issue documents a concrete data trap: if a dataset row has only a `prompt` column and no assistant-turn content, the data collator masks every label position as prompt, so the loss is identically zero — GKD distills the completion tokens, and needs conversational rows that include the assistant's turn, not prompt-only rows [8].
- Knobs, with each source's own value ("not stated" = the source was checked and does not give it; the paper column reflects its choices for the reported experiments):

| knob | trl `GKDTrainer` default [5] | trl `DistillationTrainer` default [3] | paper [1] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| student data fraction $\lambda$ | `lmbda` 0.5 | fixed at 1.0 (only mode supported) | varied: 0 (Supervised), 0.5 (Mixed), 1 (On-policy) |
| divergence interpolation $\beta$ | `beta` 0.5 (standard JSD) | `beta` 1.0 (reverse KL) | task-dependent: JSD(0.1) on WMT, forward KL on other tasks |
| sampling temperature† | `temperature` 0.9 | `temperature` 1.0 | not stated in main text; teacher softmax temperature 0.1 (GSM8K/reasoning, Table A.2), 1.0 (WMT/translation, Table A.3) per appendix tables |
| max generated length | `max_new_tokens` 128 | `max_completion_length` 512 | task-specific: Max Output Length 64 for XSum/summarization (Table A.1), Output Length (Tokenized) 80 for WMT/translation (Table A.3) |
| learning rate | not checked | not checked | 0.0003 (task-specific), 0.0001 (FLAN instruction tuning) |
| training steps | not checked | not checked | 40,000-100,000 (task-specific), 50,000 (FLAN) |

  † The three "sampling temperature" cells are not the same quantity: a closed TRL pull request states that at `temperature=1.0`, `GKDTrainer` applies temperature to generation only, while `DistillationTrainer` applies it to both generation and the divergence computation, so the two trainers' losses differ at any other temperature value; the paper's "teacher softmax temperature" is a third, separate setting applied only to the teacher's distribution during data generation, not to the student [1][6].

  The trainers' default $\beta$ values sit on opposite ends of the interpolation: `GKDTrainer`'s 0.5 is standard JSD, `DistillationTrainer`'s 1.0 is pure reverse KL — a closed TRL pull request notes these defaults differ and must be set explicitly when migrating between the two trainers [6]. The paper found the optimal divergence to be task-dependent rather than fixed, so neither default should be assumed correct without checking against the target task [1].
- $\lambda$ trade-off: higher $\lambda$ means more on-policy batches, closer alignment between training and inference distributions, and more generation cost per batch (see Cost); the paper found on-policy ($\lambda=1$) and mixed variants consistently outperformed the purely supervised ($\lambda=0$) variant across student sizes in its ablations [1].
- `GKDTrainer` requires an Instruct-tuned model with a chat template; a closed TRL issue traced a crash to using a base model (`meta-llama/Llama-3.2-1B`) with conversational data under `GKDTrainer` — `apply_chat_template` fails because base-model tokenizers ship without a chat template, and a maintainer confirmed this is not Llama-specific but a base-vs-instruct mismatch [9].

## While it runs

- Signals: `GKDTrainer`'s docs list no dedicated logging guide analogous to trl's GRPO crucial-values page, so the JSD training loss itself is the signal available from its standard logging, and a zero loss should be read as the data-format degeneracy below rather than as convergence [5][8]. `DistillationTrainer`'s docs do carry a "Logged metrics" list: `num_tokens`, `step_time`, `completions/mean_length`, `completions/min_length`, `completions/max_length`, `completions/mean_terminated_length`, `completions/min_terminated_length`, `completions/max_terminated_length`, `completions/clipped_ratio`, `entropy`, plus tool-call metrics when tools are used [3].
- Published reference runs: both trl trainer documentation pages were checked and neither links a published loss/eval curve [3][5].
- Degeneracies and defaults: a dataset with a `prompt`-only column (no assistant-turn content) produces an identically-zero loss under `GKDTrainer`, because the collator masks every label position — this is a data-format bug in the caller's dataset, not a training failure, per a maintainer's diagnosis on a closed TRL issue [8]. Using a base (non-chat-template) model with conversational `messages` data raises a chat-template error under `GKDTrainer`'s SFT-style data pipeline [9]. `GKDTrainer`'s default $\beta=0.5$ and `DistillationTrainer`'s default $\beta=1.0$ diverge from each other and from the paper's task-dependent choices; carrying one trainer's default to the other silently changes which divergence is optimized [6].
- Named successors: none found among what was checked. Search performed: a GitHub search API call over `huggingface/trl` closed issues/PRs for "GKD" in the title reported `total_count: 56`, of which 20 items were actually returned by the API response; of those 20, the 4 items this card read in depth [6][7][8][9] are implementation fixes, doc-link fixes, and the `DistillationTrainer` migration path, not a new named method built to fix a documented GKD bias. The remaining 16 returned items and the 36 items outside the returned page were not read.
- Known failure modes: the paper reports no dedicated Limitations section; its Conclusion frames unexplored modalities (audio, video, text-to-image) as future work rather than a limitation of the method as published [1]. From closed TRL issues: (1) prompt-only data silently zeroes the loss [8]; (2) a user reported a `GKDTrainer` throughput regression between TRL v0.21.0 and v0.25.1 with `gradient_checkpointing=True` (TRL's default since v0.22.0, per the maintainer's own link into the source at commit `4708879`: https://github.com/huggingface/trl/blob/4708879046dde897bdb84667a97e781bfc1965d2/trl/trainer/base_config.py#L61-L66) and teacher/student on different GPUs; a non-maintainer proposed that the teacher's forward pass was being repeated during backward recomputation, but the maintainer's final reply explicitly refutes this and instead traces the regression to `torch.nn.DataParallel` auto-wrapping the student model whenever `n_gpu>1` is visible to the process [7]; (3) a user reported `GKDTrainer` not working well with the Llama series, which a maintainer resolved as a base-model/chat-template mismatch rather than a Llama- or GKD-specific defect [9].
- What the gain is - and is not: the paper's own framing is that GKD closes a train-inference distribution mismatch and lets the student learn from feedback on its own mistakes, improving over fixed-sequence KD on the tasks tested (summarization, translation, arithmetic reasoning, instruction-following) [1]; it is presented as a distillation method for transferring a teacher's capability into a smaller student, not as a method for exceeding teacher capability or adding new capabilities the teacher itself lacks.

## In industry practice

Thinking Machines Lab's "On-Policy Distillation" post is GKD's fully on-policy limit run with an external teacher, and it is the clearest public report of what the method buys [10]. The recipe is a Qwen3-8B-Base student against a Qwen3-32B teacher, minimizing reverse KL per token on the student's own samples. The student is first brought to 60% on AIME'24 by supervised fine-tuning on 400k OpenThoughts-3 prompts; 150 steps of on-policy distillation then take it to 70% [10].

The compute claim is the reason to care, and it needs one caveat. The post sets its result beside two rows it takes from the Qwen3 Technical Report's own comparison: supervised fine-tuning followed by RL reaches 67.6% on AIME'24 for about 17,920 GPU hours, while supervised fine-tuning followed by on-policy distillation reaches 74.4% for about 1,800 GPU hours [10]. That is roughly one tenth of the GPU hours at a higher score. Those two rows come from a cited table rather than from one controlled experiment the post ran itself, so read the ratio as the order of magnitude the practitioners report, not as a measured A/B. The post's own end-to-end run is the 60% to 70% move above.

The practical reading for this corpus: when a stronger open-weights model exists in the same family as your student, on-policy distillation is the cheaper route to its reasoning quality than running RL against a verifier, because every student token gets a dense teacher signal instead of one scalar reward per completion [10].
