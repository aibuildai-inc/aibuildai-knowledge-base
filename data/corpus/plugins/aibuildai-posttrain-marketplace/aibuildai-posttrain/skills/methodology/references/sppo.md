# SPPO

Iterative preference tuning that skips reward maximization: sample K responses per prompt, score every pair with a preference model, and regress the model's implicit log-probability-ratio for each response onto that response's estimated win-rate minus one half, repeating over rounds until the policy approximates the Nash equilibrium of the preference game.

**SPPO** (Self-Play Preference Optimization) is an iterative, on-policy RLHF method that casts language-model alignment as a two-player constant-sum game and seeks the Nash equilibrium of the pairwise preference distribution, rather than maximizing a scalar Bradley-Terry reward; it was introduced in "Self-Play Preference Optimization for Language Model Alignment" by Wu, Sun, Yuan, Ji, Yang, and Gu [1]. The paper frames RLHF as $\max_\pi \min_{\pi'} \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{P}(y \succ y' \mid x)]$ and derives a multiplicative-weight-update policy iteration whose closed-form update is approximated by a squared-error regression on log-probability ratios [1]. Its parent is DPO, "Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model" [2], from which SPPO keeps the reference-model log-ratio parameterization but replaces DPO's single-pair binary-preference loss with a squared-error fit toward the estimated win-rate itself, using multiple sampled responses per prompt at each round [1]. Three reasons the paper gives for the design: Bradley-Terry / Thurstone reward models assume transitive preferences, but human preferences can be intransitive, following the general-preference framing of Tversky (1969) [1]; DPO and IPO, which are not iterative by nature, "do not have provable guarantees that they can reach the Nash equilibrium," whereas SPPO's multiplicative-weight-update scheme provably converges to it at rate $O(1/\sqrt{T})$ [1]; and with scarce preference data (one pair per prompt), DPO only widens the winner-loser reward gap without any guarantee that the winner's own likelihood increases, a failure mode SPPO's loss avoids by separately anchoring the winner toward $+1/2$ and the loser toward $-1/2$ [1].

The paper's correct identity was confirmed against two name collisions: a LiFi/WiFi networking paper that also abbreviates to "sequential-proximal policy optimization," and an unrelated, uncited "S-SPPO: Semantic-Calibrated Self-Play Preference Optimization" paper; the confirmed paper is arXiv 2405.00675, verified as the top-cited match on an exact-title search (267 citations, 39 influential, per Semantic Scholar [3]) and is the paper the official `uclaml/SPPO` GitHub repository itself names and links [4]. Using Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 and UltraFeedback prompts, three rounds of SPPO raised the AlpacaEval 2.0 length-controlled win rate from a DPO-Iter3 baseline of 22.30% to 28.53% (SPPO Iter3), and the un-controlled win rate from 14.72% (base model) to 31.02% (Table 1) [1]. No landmark production system (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3) is documented as adopting SPPO; the top-cited papers that cite it (checked via the 20 highest-cited entries in a 100-paper Semantic Scholar citation pull) are themselves other self-play/RL-alignment research papers, not deployed systems [3]. Lineage: DPO (2023 [2]) -> SPPO (2024 [1]); no named successor method builds specifically on SPPO in the sources checked here.

**When to pick it**: choose SPPO when you can query a pairwise (not necessarily transitive) preference oracle on demand for freshly sampled completions and want iterative, self-play-style improvement with a convergence guarantee toward a preference-game equilibrium rather than a fixed reward maximum [1]. Prefer DPO [2] when you only have a static, pre-collected set of chosen/rejected pairs and no way to re-sample and re-score each round — DPO is offline and optimizes a single fixed dataset directly [5]. Prefer IPO [1][6] when you want a similarly iteration-capable, Nash-seeking method but are willing to accept its data-sparsity weakness (in Section 4.2 the paper argues SPPO fixes this by design, citing Pal et al., 2024 [1]). The nearest online neighbor for a non-pairwise setup is GRPO [7], which needs only a scalar reward per completion and a group of same-prompt samples, rather than SPPO's pairwise preference-oracle queries.

**Variant of**: DPO [2] (same reference-model log-ratio parameterization; different loss target).

**Data it needs**: prompts only, no pre-collected pairs — SPPO generates its own completions each round. Per prompt $x$, sample $K$ responses from the current policy (paper: $K=5$, top-$p=1.0$, temperature $1.0$) [1], query a pairwise preference oracle $\mathbb{P}(y_i \succ y_k \mid x)$ for all $\binom{K}{2}$ (or all $K^2$) pairs, and either keep all $K$ scored responses or (as the paper does for a fair comparison to 2-response iterative DPO) keep only the highest- and lowest-scoring response as $(x, y_w, y_l)$ triplets with an estimated win probability attached [1]. The paper's RL stage used ~60K UltraFeedback prompts split into three portions, one portion per iteration, with 20K prompts trained per iteration [1]. On-policy: every round resamples from the current policy snapshot; it cannot train on a static offline pair dataset the way DPO can, though the pairwise loss (Eq. 4.8) can be applied to a fixed hard-labeled pair as a degraded, non-self-play mode [1] (see How it works).

**Extra models**: a frozen reference model $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ (the policy the current round starts from) is required by the loss's log-ratio term, exactly as in DPO [1][2]. A preference model is also required to score sampled pairs — the paper uses PairRM, a 0.4B DeBERTa-V3 pairwise model [1] — but this is an oracle input to data construction, not a model trained jointly with the policy, and it is not a value network: SPPO has no value network or per-token critic [1]. verl's community recipe substitutes a scalar reward model plus a batch-level softmean baseline in place of PairRM (see Cost and How it works — this changes the algorithm's shape).

**Shipped by**: the paper's own reference implementation, `uclaml/SPPO`'s `SPPOTrainer` (subclass of a `Trainer`, `loss_type="sppo"` or `"sppo_single"`), 590 GitHub stars, Apache-2.0, last pushed 2025-01-23 [4]. trl's `DPOTrainer` supports `loss_type="sppo_hard"` as one of its documented, exported loss types [5] — an approximation only: trl's own docs state it approximates the algorithm "by employing hard label probabilities, assigning 1 to the winner and 0 to the loser," dropping the paper's soft win-rate estimate and its use of more than 2 responses per prompt [5]. verl has no SPPO support in its core package; a separately maintained community repository, `verl-project/verl-recipe`, ships an `sppo` recipe (`recipe/sppo`) built as a submodule against a pinned verl commit, but its actor implements a sequence-level regression toward a scalar reward-model score with a batch softmean baseline — mechanically different from both the paper's pairwise loss and trl's `sppo_hard` (see How it works and Cost) [8].

## How it works

Each round: sample $K$ responses per prompt from the current policy $\pi_t$; query the preference oracle for all pairwise comparisons; construct a dataset of prompt-response-probability triplets; fit the next policy by regression; repeat [1].

**The two-player game.** The paper frames alignment as $(\pi^*, \pi^{*\prime}) = \arg\max_\pi \min_{\pi'} \mathbb{E}_{x}[\mathbb{P}(\pi \succ \pi' \mid x)]$, a constant-sum game whose solution is the von Neumann winner / Nash equilibrium of the (possibly intransitive) preference distribution [1]. Solving this via a multiplicative-weight-update (Hedge-style [9]) policy iteration gives, per Eq. (4.1)-(4.2) of the paper:

$$ \pi_{t+1}(y \mid x) \propto \pi_t(y \mid x)\exp\big(\eta\, \mathbb{P}(y \succ \pi_t \mid x)\big), \qquad \pi_{t+1}(y \mid x) = \frac{1}{Z_{\pi_t}(x)}\pi_t(y \mid x)\exp\big(\eta\, \mathbb{P}(y \succ \pi_t \mid x)\big) $$

where $\mathbb{P}(y \succ \pi_t \mid x) = \mathbb{E}_{y' \sim \pi_t(\cdot\mid x)}[\mathbb{P}(y \succ y' \mid x)]$ is $y$'s win probability against the current policy's own output distribution, $\eta$ is a step size, and $Z_{\pi_t}(x)$ is a normalizer [1]. Theorem 4.1 gives $O(1/\sqrt{T})$ convergence to the equilibrium when $\eta = \Theta(1/\sqrt{T})$, with the proof built on Freund and Schapire's Hedge-algorithm bound [9] (Theorem 1 of that paper, per the SPPO paper's Appendix A) [1].

**From the update to a regression loss.** Taking logs of Eq. (4.2) gives $\log(\pi_{t+1}(y\mid x)/\pi_t(y\mid x)) = \eta\,\mathbb{P}(y\succ\pi_t\mid x) - \log Z_{\pi_t}(x)$ (Eq. 4.3) [1]. The paper approximates the intractable $\log Z_{\pi_t}(x)$ by the constant $\eta/2$ (justified, in a footnote, by the case where every pairwise win probability is 0 or 1 with equal chance as $K\to\infty$) [1], giving the training objective actually optimized (Eq. 4.6-4.7), fit by least squares over a sampled dataset $\mathcal{D}_t$:

$$ \min_\theta\ \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim\mathcal{D}_t}\Big[\Big(\log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y\mid x)}{\pi_t(y\mid x)} - \eta\big(\widehat{P}(y\succ\pi_t\mid x) - \tfrac12\big)\Big)^2\Big] $$

with the finite-sample win-rate estimate (Eq. 4.5) $\widehat{P}(y_i \succ \pi_t \mid x) = \frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K \mathbb{P}(y_i \succ y_k \mid x)$ [1].

**Pairwise loss actually used when only 2 responses are kept (Eq. 4.8)** — the form both the official repo and trl implement — for a triplet $(x, y_w, y_l)$:

$$ \ell_{\text{SPPO}}(x,y_w,y_l;\theta;\pi_{\text{ref}}) = \Big(\log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w\mid x)} - \eta\big(\mathbb{P}(y_w\succ y_l\mid x)-\tfrac12\big)\Big)^2 + \Big(\log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l\mid x)} - \eta\big(\mathbb{P}(y_w\prec y_l\mid x)-\tfrac12\big)\Big)^2 $$

where $\mathbb{P}(y_w \succ y_l\mid x)$ can be a soft probability in $[0,1]$ or a hard 0/1 label [1]. With a hard label and letting $a=\beta\log(\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)/\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w\mid x))$, $b=\beta\log(\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)/\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l\mid x))$ (the paper reuses DPO's $\beta$ notation here; the official repo's trainer docstring and a repo maintainer both state the code's convention $\beta = 1/\eta$ [4][12], though the paper's own text defines $a$, $b$, $c$ via $\beta$ without stating that relation itself [1]), the loss reduces to $\ell_{\text{SPPO}} = (a-1/2)^2 + (b+1/2)^2$ — the paper contrasts this directly against $\ell_{\text{DPO}}=-\log\sigma(a-b)$ (Eq. 4.9) and $\ell_{\text{IPO}}=[(a-b)-1]^2$ (Eq. 4.10) [1]: unlike DPO and IPO, which only constrain the *gap* $a-b$, SPPO's two independent squared terms separately pull $a$ toward $+1/2$ and $b$ toward $-1/2$, which the paper argues avoids DPO's data-sparsity failure of shrinking only the loser's likelihood while leaving the winner's roughly unchanged (citing Pal et al., 2024) [1]. The paper also notes SPPO "shares a similar spirit as" KTO's Eq. (4.11), $\ell_{\text{KTO}}=\sigma(-a+c)+\sigma(b-c)$ with $c=\beta\,\mathrm{KL}(\pi_\theta\|\pi_{\text{ref}})$, but KTO only pushes $a$ up and $b$ down without a fixed anchor point [1][10].

**Preference oracle used in the paper.** PairRM outputs a relative reward $s(y,y';x)$, converted to a probability via $\mathbb{P}(y\succ y'\mid x) = \exp(s(y,y';x)) / (1+\exp(s(y,y';x)))$; this is explicitly not required to be transitive, i.e. $s(y_1,y_2;x)+s(y_2,y_3;x)\neq s(y_1,y_3;x)$ in general [1]. Winner/loser selection in the paper uses $s_{\text{PairRM}}(y_i;x) = \frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K s(y_i,y_k;x)$, the response with highest/lowest average score against all $K$ samples [1]. A maintainer of the official repo confirmed a bug in an early release of this aggregation — averaging the raw score matrix rather than the win-rate matrix before taking the row mean — and stated the fix was expected to produce a "negligible" performance difference [11].

**Worked micro-example**, $K=2$, hard preference $y_w \succ y_l$, $\eta=1000$ (the paper's setting [1]; the official repo runs this as `BETA=0.001` under the code's $\beta=1/\eta$ convention [4][12]): suppose at initialization $\pi_\theta=\pi_{\text{ref}}$, so $a=b=0$. The loss gradient pushes $a$ toward $\eta(1-\tfrac12)=500$ and $b$ toward $\eta(0-\tfrac12)=-500$ in log-ratio-times-$\eta$ units — i.e. before any optimizer step the squared loss for the winner term alone is $(0-500)^2=250{,}000$, consistent with a maintainer of the official repo confirming that the model does start with a big loss precisely because $\eta=1/\beta=1000$ scales the target, and with one user in the same thread reporting a first-step loss of 139,721.65 [12]. Training gradually raises $\log(\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)/\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w\mid x))$ toward 0.5 (in $\beta$-scaled units) and lowers the loser's toward $-0.5$.

**Mini-batch ablation (Section 5.3).** With $K=2$, the paper uses a simplified win-rate estimator $\widehat{P}(y_w \succ \pi_t\mid x) = \tfrac12[\mathbb{P}(y_w\succ y_w\mid x)+\mathbb{P}(y_w\succ y_l\mid x)] = \tfrac12[\tfrac12+\mathbb{P}(y_w\succ y_l\mid x)]$; comparing $K=2$ to $K=5$ the paper reports SPPO's AlpacaEval performance is "robust to the noise in estimating" the win probability, and that $K=2$ produces a "reduced tendency to increase output length" relative to $K=5$ [1].

## Cost

**Theory, from the method's own math**: no value network is trained — SPPO drops PPO/DPO-style critics entirely [1]. Per round, generation cost scales with $K$ completions per prompt (paper: $K=5$) rather than the 1-2 needed by non-iterative DPO, and scoring cost scales with the number of pairwise oracle queries constructed from those $K$ samples — up to $O(K^2)$ if all pairs are scored [1]. One reference-model forward pass (no gradients) is needed per training example for the log-ratio term, identical in structure to DPO [1][2]. A naive reading might assume the preference oracle itself needs training each round; the paper's own recipe keeps PairRM frozen throughout and never fine-tunes it [1] — only the policy is updated.

**In practice, per framework**:
- Official `uclaml/SPPO` repo: PairRM (0.4B) is loaded once and frozen for scoring; generation uses vLLM at each iteration for the $K=5$ samples per prompt across 8×A100 GPUs in the paper's runs, and only the policy model and a frozen reference copy are held for the regression training step (`SPPOTrainer.sppo_loss`, `beta` parameter default 0.1 in the trainer signature, though the paper's actual runs used `BETA=0.001`) [1][4].
- trl `DPOTrainer` with `loss_type="sppo_hard"`: no separate preference-scoring pass is implemented by trl itself — it consumes a dataset of pre-formed chosen/rejected pairs like any other `DPOTrainer` loss type, so the extra generation-and-scoring loop that makes SPPO "self-play" in the paper is the caller's responsibility, not trl's [5]. Only a frozen reference model is held in addition to the trained policy, the same footprint as any other `DPOTrainer` run [5].
- verl-recipe `sppo` (community, not verl core): the actor holds a policy, an old-policy log-prob snapshot, and (only if `use_kl_loss=True`, which the shipped example script sets to `False`) a reference model for an added KL term [13][14]; a scalar reward model supplies `rewards` per sequence, replacing PairRM's pairwise scoring entirely — so this recipe's memory/compute shape is closer to a single-reward-model RL loop (like a reward-only GRPO variant) than to the paper's pairwise-oracle self-play loop [13][14]. Do not carry any of these three frameworks' costs to another — none implement the identical mechanism.

## How to use it

- Prompts only; no pre-built preference pairs are required upfront, since SPPO generates and scores its own comparison data every round [1]. The reference implementation's data pipeline uses UltraFeedback prompts split into three disjoint portions, training one portion per iteration to reduce overfitting, following the Snorkel iterative-DPO recipe [1].
- Reward/label convention: the *label* fed to the loss is a win-probability in $[0,1]$ (soft) or 0/1 (hard), not a scalar reward — trl's `sppo_hard` always treats it as hard 0/1 [5]; the official repo's `SPPOTrainer` supports both `"sppo"` (paired, uses separately estimated `chosen_probs_win`/`chosen_probs_lose`) and `"sppo_single"` (a single shared probability applied symmetrically to both responses) [4].
- Knobs, with each source's own value ("not stated" = the source was checked and does not give it; "not checked" = this card did not verify that source's key):

| knob | paper [1] | official repo (`uclaml/SPPO`, actual run script) [4] | trl `DPOTrainer` default [5] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\eta$ (win-rate scale) / $\beta=1/\eta$ | $\eta=1000$ | `BETA="0.001"` (pipeline.sh)$^\dagger$ | `beta` default 0.1 (DPOConfig; not SPPO-specific) |
| group size $K$ | 5 | not checked in run script (README describes generation via the repo's separate scripts, not captured in pipeline.sh) | not applicable — trl consumes pre-formed pairs, no $K$-sampling step |
| learning rate | $5.0\times10^{-7}$ | `LEARNING_RATE="5.0e-7"` | DPOConfig default `1e-6` (not SPPO-specific) |
| outer self-play iterations | 3 | not stated as a script argument (pipeline.sh is invoked once per outer iteration, with `--iter` distinguishing runs) | not applicable |
| `num_train_epochs` per iteration (LR-schedule length) | 18 | `NUM=18`, passed directly as `--num_train_epochs=$NUM` to the trainer script — matches the paper's own value | DPOConfig default `num_train_epochs=3.0` (not SPPO-specific) |
| global batch size | 64 | `BATCH_SIZE=8`, `ACCUMULATE=1` (per-device; global batch depends on GPU count, not stated in the script) | DPOConfig default `per_device_train_batch_size=8` (not SPPO-specific) |
| optimizer | not stated | `OPTIM="rmsprop"` | DPOConfig default `adamw_torch_fused` (not SPPO-specific) |

  $^\dagger$ The paper itself states only $\eta=1000$ (sppo_ar5iv.txt, Hyperparameter Tuning) [1]; $\beta=0.001$ is not a paper-stated quantity — it is the repo's own run-script value under the code's $\beta=1/\eta$ convention, stated in the trainer's docstring and confirmed by a maintainer [4][12].

  Reading the table: the official repo's own run script sets $\beta=0.001$ under the code's $\beta=1/\eta$ convention [4][12], far below trl's generic DPOConfig default of $\beta=0.1$ — a run started from trl's un-tuned default would apply a much weaker scaling of the target win-rate and would not reproduce the reference run's regime; trl's own docs give no SPPO-specific default, so this gap must be set deliberately [5].
- Trade-off: increasing $K$ gives a less noisy win-rate estimate per response but multiplies generation and oracle-query cost roughly quadratically if all pairs are scored, or linearly if only the best/worst of $K$ are kept (the paper's own choice, for comparability with 2-response iterative DPO) [1]; the paper's own $K=2$ vs $K=5$ ablation found performance "robust" to this choice while $K=2$ produced shorter, less length-inflated outputs [1].

## While it runs

- **Signals and their healthy shapes**: SPPO's own defining paper does not report a training-loss curve or intermediate loss/gradient dashboard; the closest first-hand operational signal is a maintainer's confirmation, in a GitHub issue, that the model is expected to start with a large loss because $\eta=1/\beta=1000$ scales the squared target (one user in that thread reported a first-step loss of 139,721.65) — a large initial loss is not itself a bug [12]. Because the pairwise loss (Eq. 4.8) reduces to trl's `sppo_hard` DPOTrainer loss when the label is hard, trl's generic `DPOTrainer` metrics apply if that trainer is used: `rewards/chosen` and `rewards/rejected` (the implicit $\beta\log(\pi_\theta/\pi_{\text{ref}})$ terms, i.e., $a$ and $b$ above), `rewards/margins`, and `rewards/accuracies` [5] — for SPPO specifically, `rewards/chosen` should trend toward $+1/2$ in $\beta$-scaled units and `rewards/rejected` toward $-1/2$, per the loss's own anchor points [1].
- **Published reference runs**: the paper's own Table 1 numbers are the reference curve — for Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 base -> SPPO Iter1/2/3, AlpacaEval 2.0 length-controlled win rate goes 17.11 -> 24.79 (+7.69) -> 26.89 (+2.10) -> 28.53, and raw win rate goes 14.72 -> 23.51 (+8.79) -> 27.62 (+4.11) -> 31.02, against DPO-Iter3's 22.30 LC / 23.39 raw and IPO-Iter3's 20.06 LC / 22.47 raw (Table 1) [1]. The official repo separately publishes post-paper checkpoints (Llama-3-8B-SPPO Iter1-3: LC win rate 31.73 -> 35.15 -> 38.77; Gemma-2-9B-It-SPPO Iter1-3: 48.70 -> 50.93 -> 53.27) — these are repo-only results, not reported in the arXiv paper body [4].
- **Degeneracies and defaults**: a documented real bug (not a hypothetical) — an early release of the official repo averaged the raw PairRM score matrix instead of the win-rate matrix before taking the per-response mean, which the maintainer confirmed was incorrect and expected to produce a negligible but nonzero performance shift once fixed [11]. Uniform PairRM scores across all $K$ samples for a prompt would make every $\widehat{P}(y_i\succ\pi_t\mid x)=1/2$, driving both loss terms toward pushing $a$ and $b$ to the same target — the paper does not discuss this case directly. The trl `sppo_hard` and verl-recipe implementations both silently diverge from the paper's algorithm shape (hard-label-only in trl; scalar-reward RL loop, not pairwise oracle, in verl-recipe) — a reader who assumes either reproduces the paper's soft win-rate self-play loop will not.
- **Named successors**: none found. Searched Semantic Scholar's citation list for arXiv 2405.00675 (100 entries fetched, top 20 by citing-paper citation count inspected) [3]; the highest-cited citing papers are other self-play/RL-alignment research works (e.g., "Language Self-Play For Data-Free Training," "Chasing Moving Targets with Online Self-Play Reinforcement Learning for Safer Language Models"), none of which name themselves as a direct successor or bias-fix to SPPO specifically.
- **Known failure modes**: from the paper's own Limitations section (Section 6): "approximating the optimal policy update via regression relies on the assumption that the model class is expressive enough and the input space is well covered by the generated data," the log-partition-factor approximation ($\log Z_{\pi_t}(x)\approx \eta/2$) "can become inaccurate if the preference model has different behavior," and all experiments were run on a single dataset (UltraFeedback) with a limited set of benchmarks due to compute constraints [1]. Separately, the paper's own analysis (Table 1 discussion) notes that gains do not hold uniformly across iterations for SPPO, DPO, and IPO alike on the Open LLM Leaderboard, which it attributes to an "alignment tax" (citing Askell et al., 2021) — aligning to PairRM preference can fail to improve, or even hurt, general-capability benchmark performance [1].
- **What the gain is - and is not**: the paper's own Table 1 and Figure 3 results show SPPO's gain is specifically in pairwise-judged response quality (AlpacaEval win rate against GPT-4-Turbo references, and PairRM-judged head-to-head win rate) [1]; the paper also notes that IPO can exceed SPPO in later iterations partly because IPO exploits PairRM's own bias toward longer outputs, while SPPO's gains come with comparatively less length inflation because of its "more robust regularization within a multiplicative weight update framework" [1]. The method is not shown to add capability beyond what the base policy and the preference oracle jointly define; on Open LLM Leaderboard's general-capability benchmarks, gains do not consistently hold across iterations [1].
