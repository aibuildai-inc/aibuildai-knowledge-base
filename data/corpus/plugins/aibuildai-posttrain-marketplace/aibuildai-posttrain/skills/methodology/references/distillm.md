# DistiLLM

Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03898 [1].

Knowledge distillation for LLMs with a skewed (reverse) KL loss in place of plain forward KL, plus an adaptive schedule that mostly trains on the fixed dataset and only occasionally injects fresh student-generated samples through a replay buffer, so most of an on-policy method's quality is kept at close to offline cost.

**DistiLLM** is a knowledge-distillation (KD) method for auto-regressive language models, introduced in "DistiLLM: Towards Streamlined Distillation for Large Language Models" as a KD framework combining a new skew-KLD objective with an adaptive off-policy training procedure [1]. Its parent is standard KD with the Kullback-Leibler divergence between a fixed teacher and a trainable student, introduced by Hinton et al. for compressing a neural network into a smaller one [2]; DistiLLM keeps the token-decomposed KLD form of that objective but skews it by mixing a small fraction α of the opposite distribution into the target before taking the divergence, and replaces continual on-policy sampling with a replay buffer whose fill rate is scheduled by validation loss [1]. The paper gives two reasons for departing from plain forward/reverse KLD-based KD: the asymmetric KLD can push the student to over-smooth ("mode averaging") or over-concentrate its output distribution, especially with limited student capacity, a failure mode the paper attributes to prior work on divergence choice [1]; and continual generation of student-generated outputs (SGOs) for on-policy training, as used by MiniLLM and GKD, is computationally expensive and can misguide the student when the teacher assigns spurious low loss to unfamiliar SGOs [1].

DistiLLM's authors report state-of-the-art results across instruction-following, summarization, and translation, with training speedups of 2.5-4.3x over MiniLLM and GKD-style KD (Fig. 2, Fig. 5, Fig. 7) [1]; on databricks-dolly-15k with a GPT-2 XL (1.5B) to GPT-2 (0.1B) teacher-student pair, skew-KLD alone (before adding the off-policy component) raised ROUGE-L on Dolly Eval from 23.52 (plain KLD) to 25.21 (skew RKLD, α=0.1) (Table 1) [1]. The same authors' own follow-up, DistiLLM-2, is the named successor: it applies distinct loss formulations to teacher- versus student-generated data via a contrastive objective, extending to preference alignment and vision-language settings [3]. Lineage: KD with KLD (Hinton et al., 2015 [2]) -> on-policy divergence-based KD, MiniLLM (2024 [4]) and GKD (2024 [5]) -> DistiLLM (2024 [1]) -> DistiLLM-2 (2025 [3]). The paper was confirmed by matching its exact title and arXiv ID (2402.03898) against the official repository's citation block, which credits the same four authors and ICML 2024 venue [6].

**When to pick it**: choose DistiLLM over plain forward-KLD KD (Hinton et al. [2]) when mode-averaging or a training/inference mismatch is limiting a distilled student and some extra generation cost is affordable; choose it over MiniLLM's policy-gradient on-policy approach [4] or GKD's continual on-policy generalized-JSD training [5] when that on-policy cost (up to 5.5x slower per the paper's own runtime comparison, Fig. 7 [1]) is not affordable, since DistiLLM's adaptive scheduler keeps the SGO-generation frequency small throughout training [1]. It is not a preference-optimization method: the paper states DistiLLM is designed for a supervised fine-tuning setup and leaves extension to human-preference optimization as future work [1].

**Variant of**: KD with KLD (Hinton et al., 2015) [2], skewing the divergence via the skew-divergence construction of Lee (2001) [1] and replacing MiniLLM/GKD's continual on-policy sampling with a scheduled, buffered off-policy variant [1].

**Data it needs**: a (source, target) sequence-pair dataset, i.e., prompts and reference completions, drawn from a fixed training set D (the paper's instruction-following runs use 14K training / 500 validation examples from databricks-dolly-15k, plus a language-modeling loss on OpenWebText) [1], evaluated against a frozen teacher model p(y|x); no separate reward or preference labels are required. Training is neither purely on-policy nor purely offline: each step samples one u ~ Unif(0,1) and compares it against two thresholds, λ_R (generation) and ϕ (buffer-use); because λ_R ≤ ϕ, whenever a step generates a fresh student completion into the replay buffer it also draws its training mini-batch from that buffer, and on other steps it falls back to the fixed dataset (Algorithm 1) [1].

**Extra models**: one frozen teacher model p(y|x), used both to score log-probabilities in the skew-KLD loss and (occasionally) to validate loss on held-out data for the adaptive scheduler [1]; no separate reward model or judge. No value network or critic is used (this is not an RL method). Cost details in Cost.

**Shipped by**: no training framework in this corpus ships a `DistiLLM` trainer — checked against trl's docs (generalized-JSD `GKDTrainer`, not skew-KLD [8]) and against verl's README, which mentions only generic "distillation," not DistiLLM by name [7]. The paper's own reference implementation is the research repository `jongwooko/distillm` (PyTorch, no license file in the repo as of the commit read). Its README originally pinned a specific HuggingFace Transformers commit "by following MiniLLM," but a later changelog entry in the same README states that local-transformers dependency was removed on 2024-08-12 so the code could work with recent LLMs — the pin is superseded, not currently binding [6]. Building it on top of an existing SFT trainer would mean adding a custom skew-KLD/skew-RKLD loss plus a validation-driven, buffer-backed sampling loop around generation — a new loss and a new sampling scheduler, not a drop-in loss swap.

## How it works

Each step: draw one u ~ Unif(0,1); if u < λ_R := ϕ(1 − t/T) (small, decreasing over training), generate a student output and store it in a replay buffer D_R; if the same u < ϕ (small early, growing over training), draw the training mini-batch from D_R instead of the fixed dataset D — since λ_R ≤ ϕ always, generation and buffer-use are gated by the same draw and buffer-use fires whenever generation does; then take a gradient step on the skew-(reverse-)KLD loss between teacher and student on that mini-batch (Algorithm 1) [1].

**Token-decomposed KLD** — the paper's starting point, exact under the assumption that the teacher's distribution matches its training data [1]:

$$ D_{KL}(p, q_\theta) = \mathbb{E}_x\!\left[\mathbb{E}_{y\sim p(\cdot|x)}\log\frac{p(y|x)}{q_\theta(y|x)}\right] \approx \frac{1}{|D|}\sum_{x\in D_X}\sum_{t}\sum_{y_t\in V} p(y_t|y_{<t},x)\log\frac{p(y_t|y_{<t},x)}{q_\theta(y_t|y_{<t},x)} $$

**Skew KLD (SKL)**, the loss family DistiLLM actually optimizes, with mixing parameter α ∈ [0,1] borrowed from Lee's skew divergence [1][9]:

$$ D^{(\alpha)}_{SKL}(p, q_\theta) = D_{KL}\big(p,\ \alpha p + (1-\alpha) q_\theta\big), \qquad D^{(\alpha)}_{SRKL}(p, q_\theta) = D_{KL}\big(q_\theta,\ (1-\alpha)p + \alpha q_\theta\big) $$

At α=0 both reduce to ordinary forward KLD / reverse KLD. The paper's Table 8 ablation across α ∈ {0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9} shows the two losses behave differently: for SKL, α=0.1 gives the best Dolly Eval ROUGE-L (24.80), and scores are 24.54, 24.55, 24.49, 24.27 at α=0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9 respectively — a small uptick from α=0.3 to α=0.5 before an overall decline to 24.27 at α=0.9; for SRKL, α=0.1 gives 25.21 but α=0.5 scores higher still, 25.92, before scores collapse to 21.41 and 18.20 at α=0.7 and α=0.9 — the paper itself describes both curves only as sharing "the concave form," not as monotonically degrading, and the paper's main results nonetheless use SRKL with α=0.1 rather than the Table-8-best α=0.5 (Table 8) [1]. The paper motivates the skew by a gradient analysis: the ordinary-KLD gradient is $-r_{p,q_\theta}\nabla_\theta q_\theta(y|x)$, whose coefficient $r_{p,q_\theta}=p/q_\theta$ can blow up when $q_\theta(y|x)\approx 0$; the SKL gradient is $-(1-\alpha)\,r_{p,\tilde q_\theta}\nabla_\theta q_\theta(y|x)$, replacing the ratio with $r_{p,\tilde q_\theta}$ against the mixture $\tilde q_\theta = \alpha p + (1-\alpha)q_\theta$ and scaling the whole coefficient down by $(1-\alpha)$; the mixture denominator stays bounded away from zero the way $q_\theta$ alone cannot (Eq. 6) [1]. Theorem 1 in the paper further bounds the mean-squared error of the empirical mini-batch SKL estimator relative to the true divergence; the paper reports that the relationship between this upper bound and α is convex (U-shaped) rather than monotonic, with the bound's own leading coefficient blowing up as α approaches either 0 or 1, so choosing α is a trade-off between the gradient scale and this L2-norm bound rather than a one-directional tightening (Fig. 3(d)) [1].

**Worked example.** Take a single token position with teacher probability p=0.9 for the correct token and student probability q_θ=0.05 for that same token (the mismatch case that causes forward-KLD gradient blowup). Ordinary forward-KLD's gradient coefficient is r = p/q_θ = 0.9/0.05 = 18. With α=0.1, SKL's mixture probability at that token is q̃_θ = 0.1(0.9)+0.9(0.05) = 0.135, giving a ratio r = p/q̃_θ = 0.9/0.135 ≈ 6.67; per Eq. 6 the full gradient coefficient also carries the (1-α)=0.9 factor, so the paper's actual coefficient is 0.9 × 6.67 ≈ 6.0, about a 3x reduction from the unskewed coefficient of 18 — this arithmetic follows directly from the paper's own gradient formula and is not taken from any framework implementation [1].

**Adaptive SGO scheduler.** The probability ϕ of drawing from replayed student-generated outputs is updated only at validation checkpoints, using validation loss L and a loss-tolerance hyperparameter ε (set to 0.1 in all experiments): if the new validation loss does not exceed the previous one by more than ε, ϕ is left unchanged; otherwise it is incremented by 1/N_ϕ, capped at 1.0, where N_ϕ (set to 10 in all experiments) is the number of stages [1]. The replay-generation probability is then λ_R := ϕ(1 − t/T), so it stays small throughout training even as ϕ grows, since the decreasing factor (1 − t/T) suppresses fresh generation late in training when ϕ is largest [1].

**Loss actually used in experiments.** The paper reports its main results using SRKL with α=0.1, off-policy replay buffer size 1000, and initial SGO probability 0 [1]; process-level or purely on-policy variants (as in MiniLLM/GKD) are compared as baselines but are not the shipped default.

## Cost

**Theory, from the method's own math:**
- Time: the skew-KLD loss itself needs only the teacher and student log-probabilities over the same batch of tokens — no extra forward pass beyond a standard KD step. The added cost is generation: with probability λ_R at each step, the student must autoregressively generate a full completion to add to the replay buffer, and the scheduler occasionally requires a validation-set forward pass through the student [1]. Because λ_R is designed to stay small throughout training (decreasing factor (1 − t/T) times the bounded ϕ), the paper's premise is that the amortized generation cost is much lower than a fully on-policy method that generates every step [1].
- Memory: one frozen teacher plus the trainable student, the minimum needed by any KLD-based KD method — no value network or reward model. The replay buffer itself holds up to `buffer size` (x, ỹ) text pairs (1000 in the paper's runs [1]), a storage cost, not a GPU-memory-during-forward-pass cost.
- A naive reading of Algorithm 1 might suggest generation happens on the same batch being trained on every step; the schedule's own math (λ_R small and decreasing) is what keeps this from being true in practice — see How to use it for the actual per-run generation frequency reported for GPT-2/OPT/OpenLLaMA families.

**In practice, per framework:**
- Neither trl [8] nor verl [7] documents a DistiLLM trainer, so there is no framework-level cost data to report; the only cost figures available are the paper's own Fig. 2 and Fig. 7 relative-runtime comparisons run on four A100 40GB GPUs (instruction-following) or four RTX 3090 GPUs (summarization/translation) [1].
- The reference implementation carries 7 open issues as of the commit read [6], including issue #18: an unresolved, unanswered report of a possible sign error in the paper's own Eq. 7 gradient formula for SRKL [10]. A separate, closed issue reported that the shipped `forward_kl`/`skewed_forward_kl` loss functions appear to omit a term implied by the textbook KLD definition; the maintainer replied that the term is constant with respect to the student and correctly dropped from the gradient, discussed further in How to use it [11].

## How to use it

- Data: (prompt, reference-completion) pairs from the target task, plus (in the paper's instruction-following setup) an auxiliary plain-text corpus for a language-modeling loss term, following the recipe of the MiniLLM paper it builds on [1][4].
- Loss/label conventions of the main implementation: the official repo computes skew-KLD/SKL over teacher and student token log-probabilities using masked, log-space arithmetic (`distillm/losses.py`); a closed issue questioned two points in that code — an apparently missing teacher-probability-weighted-log-teacher-probability term, and the subtraction order between teacher and mixed log-probabilities — and the repository owner replied that the teacher-probability term is constant with respect to the student's parameters and so is correctly excluded from the gradient, and that the subtraction order is correct because the final loss negates the computed product [11].
- Key knobs, framework default vs. paper value. Only one source (the paper itself) reports concrete numbers; no shipping framework exists to compare against, so this table has a single populated column and states that explicitly rather than leaving blanks unexplained:

| knob | paper's reported value [1] |
| --- | --- |
| skew parameter α | 0.1 (best in ablation, Table 8) |
| loss variant used in main results | SRKL (skew reverse KLD) |
| replay buffer size | 1000 |
| initial SGO probability ϕ₀ | 0.0 |
| number of scheduler stages N_ϕ | 10 |
| loss tolerance ε | 0.1 |
| instruction-following batch size / LR (≤1B student) | search over {8,16,32} batch, {5e-4,1e-4,5e-5} LR, 20 epochs |
| instruction-following batch size / LR (>1B student) | batch 8, search over {5e-5,1e-5,5e-6} LR, 10 epochs |
| terminal ϕ reached (GPT-2 family, Dolly) | 0.4 (Base), 0.4 (Medium), 0.5 (Large) |

  No framework default column exists because no framework ships this method (see Shipped by); a reader adopting DistiLLM by re-implementing it has only the paper's own values as an anchor.
- Trade-off a run designer faces: raising ϕ's growth rate (smaller N_ϕ) or the loss tolerance ε shifts training toward more replay-buffer use earlier, trading potential instability from imperfect SGOs against a lower training-inference mismatch. Table 2's Dolly-Eval ROUGE-L is the deciding comparison: for SRKL, On-policy scores 26.04, Mixed scores 26.01, and the paper's Adaptive schedule scores 26.37 — a small edge over both simpler baselines — while Fig. 7 reports the adaptive schedule needs substantially less relative training time than continual on-policy generation [1].
- Group/batch reuse is not a concept in this method — there is no group sampling as in GRPO-style RL; the closest analogous knob is replay buffer size, which trades sample-reuse efficiency against staleness of the buffered SGOs relative to the current student.

## While it runs

- Signals and healthy shapes: the paper's own diagnostic is validation loss trending upward under a fixed-dataset-only schedule, and the adaptive scheduler's job is to counteract that trend by increasing ϕ — Fig. 10 shows validation loss increasing without SGO use and being held down once the scheduler triggers additional SGO usage, a behavior the paper's figure caption calls "the core philosophy of our adaptive SGO scheduler" [1]. There is no framework logging convention to cite (see Shipped by), so this is the only first-hand signal available.
- Published reference runs: Table 1 (loss-function ablation) and Table 2 (off-policy ablation) in the paper are the closest thing to reference curves, reporting mean and standard deviation of ROUGE-L across five random seeds for each configuration on five instruction-following benchmarks [1]; no separate public logs (e.g., W&B) were found in the official repository.
- Degeneracies and defaults: with α=0 both SKL and SRKL collapse to ordinary KLD/RKLD, reintroducing the gradient-blowup and mode-averaging issues the method exists to fix [1]; a replay buffer probability ϕ stuck near 0 (loss tolerance ε set too high, or N_ϕ too large) makes DistiLLM behave like the fixed-dataset-only KD baseline rather than benefiting from SGOs at all [1].
- Named successor: DistiLLM-2 fixes the documented bias of applying one loss formulation uniformly to both teacher- and student-generated data, replacing it with a contrastive loss that treats the two data types differently [3].
- Known failure modes, from the paper's own limitations section (Appendix A) [1]: (1) the method's focus on (R)KLD still carries the mode-averaging and train-inference-mismatch weaknesses documented in prior work, only partially mitigated, not eliminated, by S(R)KL — the authors suggest interpolating with TVD- or EMD-based objectives as future work; (2) DistiLLM is designed for a supervised fine-tuning setup and is not extended to preference-optimization pipelines in the paper; (3) the method assumes the teacher and student share the same tokenizer, which the paper flags as a scope limitation. From the repository's own closed issue #5 [12]: asked to explain a discrepancy between a third party's reproduced teacher-model scores and the paper's reported scores, the maintainer attributed the gap to having used a different, unspecified snapshot of the MiniLLM SFT/teacher-training codebase than the one the reporter used, and did not resolve the discrepancy further — i.e., reproduction of the paper's exact numbers is sensitive to which upstream MiniLLM commit is used, a numerical trap not stated in the paper itself.
- What the gain is — and is not: the paper's own ablations (Table 1, Table 2) attribute DistiLLM's gains to two separable components — the skew-KLD loss improves ROUGE-L over plain KLD/RKLD even with the fixed-dataset-only schedule, and the adaptive off-policy component further improves scores close to a fully on-policy schedule while cutting training time 2.5-4.3x (Fig. 5, Fig. 7) [1]; the paper does not claim the method adds reasoning or generative capability beyond what a fully-trained on-policy KD baseline (e.g., MiniLLM, GKD) already reaches — its stated advantage is matching or exceeding that quality at lower training cost.
