# SPT

Item home: https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01658 (defining paper, arXiv:2409.01658)

Diagnose which small set of attention heads (under 5% of them) causally drives an unwanted behavior via path patching, then supervised-fine-tune only the input/output projections of those heads while freezing everything else, including all MLPs.

**SPT** (supervised pinpoint tuning) is a two-stage supervised fine-tuning method that first localizes the components of a transformer responsible for a specific behavior and then trains only those components, introduced by Chen et al. to fix sycophancy in chat models without the general-capability loss that full-parameter SFT causes [1]. The paper first uses path patching, a causal-intervention technique introduced by Wang et al. to find the circuit behind a specific model behavior [2], to rank every attention head in every layer by its direct effect on sycophantic output; it then takes the top-K heads and fine-tunes only their query/key/value/output projection matrices (query/output only for grouped-query-attention models, since keys and values are shared across a head group), freezing the rest of the network including all MLPs, using a plain supervised loss (Algorithm 2) [1]. The paper frames SPT as falling into the "structured selective PEFT" category of Ding et al.'s parameter-efficient-fine-tuning taxonomy, which splits PEFT into selective methods that fine-tune a subset of existing parameters and reparameterized methods like LoRA that train a low-rank re-parameterization [3][1]. The stated reasons for the design: full SFT degrades general capability while fixing the target behavior, and the paper's own diagnosis shows only a small, identifiable minority of heads (under 5%) drive the behavior, so most of the network never needs to move [1].

A Semantic Scholar citations query for arXiv:2409.01658 returned 69 citing papers as of this reading [4]; several other arXiv papers also use "SPT" for unrelated methods (spatial, spectral, soft, and semi-parametric prompt tuning), and this paper was confirmed as the intended match by being the top-cited candidate among that name collision. No adoption by a named production system was found in the paper or in a citation-graph search (below); the method has not been shown to generalize beyond the sycophancy and a preliminary arithmetic-reasoning experiment run by its own authors [1]. In the originating paper, on Llama-2-13B, SPT raised the "confidence" sycophancy metric from 0.08% to 71.92% (+71.84) and truthfulness from 18.89% to 86.72% (+67.83), while SFT reached a similar confidence gain (61.55%, +61.47) but with an 18x larger shift in the KL divergence between pre- and post-tuning output distributions (0.0476 vs. 0.0026) and a drop in general-task accuracy (StrategyQA -3.32, GSM8K -8.57) that SPT did not show (StrategyQA +1.09, GSM8K +1.59) [1, Table 1]. Lineage in one line: path patching for circuit discovery (Wang et al., 2022 [2]) + full-parameter SFT for sycophancy (Wei et al., 2023 [5]) -> SPT (Chen et al., 2024 [1]) -> no confirmed named successor as of this card (see While it runs).

**When to pick it**: pick SPT when a single, narrow behavior of an already-instruction-tuned chat model needs correcting (the paper's case is sycophancy) and you can build or reuse path-patching/circuit-analysis tooling to localize it first; the paper reports it matches or beats full SFT on the target metric while degrading general-task accuracy far less and shifting the output distribution far less (18x smaller KL on Llama-2-13B) [1]. Prefer full-parameter SFT, its parent comparison point, when no circuit-diagnosis step is available or the target behavior is broad rather than localized [1][5]. Prefer LoRA — a reparameterized-PEFT method that freezes the pretrained weights and injects trainable low-rank update matrices into each targeted weight matrix [6] — the nearest reparameterized-PEFT alternative, when you want a PEFT method with existing library support and don't need attention-head-level diagnosis; the paper found LoRA preserves general ability about as well as SPT but reaches lower sycophancy-fix scores (LoRA confidence 70.04 vs. SPT 71.92, truthfulness 79.66 vs. 86.72); combining LoRA with SPT's selected heads ("LoRA&SPT") pushes confidence to 86.33, the best of any setting in the table, but its truthfulness (86.21) is slightly below SPT alone (86.72), so the combination is not a strict win on every metric [1, Table 4]. Prefer DARE — a method that randomly drops most of an SFT model's delta parameters (the difference between the fine-tuned and base weights) and rescales the rest to approximate the original delta's effect [7] — the nearest selective-pruning alternative, only as a baseline to avoid: the paper found applying DARE at a 98.71% drop rate to an SFT model's delta parameters degrades general ability about as much as full SFT does, showing that identifying which parameters matter (SPT's diagnosis step), not merely reducing their count, is what protects general ability [1, Table 4].

**Variant of**: structured selective PEFT, per Ding et al.'s PEFT taxonomy [3], contrasted in the paper with full-parameter SFT as its main baseline and with reparameterized PEFT (LoRA [6]) as an orthogonal alternative it can be combined with [1].

**Data it needs**: multi-round question-answering transcripts built from existing QA datasets (the paper used subsets of MMLU, MATH, AQuA, and TriviaQA, sampled equally) reformatted into a challenge dialogue: the model answers, is told "I don't think that's right. Are you sure?", and the target completion has it stick to a correct first answer or correct a wrong one [1]. Columns needed: prompt/first-round question, first-round answer, challenge turn, target final-round answer — no reward model or preference pairs. Training is offline: the tuning data is fixed and collected once before training, and the loss is plain supervised cross-entropy on the target transcript (Algorithm 2), not sampled fresh from the policy during training [1]. Training scale: Llama-2-13B was tuned for 240 steps at batch size 32; Llama-2-7B/Mistral-7B similarly at 240 steps/batch 32; Llama-2-70B at 120 steps/batch 64 [1, Table 14].

**Extra models**: none — SPT trains one model with a plain supervised loss (Algorithm 2), and the path-patching diagnosis step runs forward passes with activation interventions on that same pre-tuning checkpoint rather than a second trained model [1][2]. No value network, reward model, or frozen reference model is part of the method's definition.

**Shipped by**: no packaged library implements SPT. The paper's own repository (`yellowtownhz/sycophancy-interpretability`, no license file declared in the repository metadata, 15 stars, not archived as of this reading) provides three script directories — `evaluation`, `path_patching`, and `pinpoint_tuning` — described as code for reproducing the paper, not a general-purpose trainer with a stable API [8]. Building it on top of an existing framework requires two distinct pieces: (1) a circuit-diagnosis tool that runs path patching / activation patching over attention heads to rank them by causal effect on a target behavior (the paper's own `path_patching` scripts, or a general activation-patching library), and (2) a custom supervised trainer that sets `requires_grad=False` on every parameter except the Q/K/V/O (or Q/O for grouped-query-attention models) projections of the selected top-K heads — a loss on top of an existing SFT trainer, not a new sampling loop, but the selective-freezing logic is not exposed by off-the-shelf trainers (trl, peft) and would need to be written by hand.

## How it works

The loop: (1) diagnose — rank every attention head by its direct causal effect on the target behavior using path patching, then verify the top-ranked heads with a knockout ablation; (2) freeze every parameter except the selected heads' projection matrices; (3) supervised-fine-tune on multi-round QA data with a plain loss [1].

**Path patching (diagnosis)** [2], as used in the paper [1]: a forward pass is run on a reference prompt to record activations, a second forward pass on a corrupted prompt patches in the reference activation at one attention head's output at a time, and the resulting change in the model's output logit for the sycophantic answer measures that head's direct effect; heads are ranked by this logit-difference magnitude, and the top-ranked heads are further validated by knocking them out (zeroing their contribution) and checking that the model's apologize-rate and post-challenge accuracy shift accordingly [1]. The paper's own diagnosis on Llama-2-13B found a small percentage of heads (about 4% by the paper's figure) have a noteworthy influence on the output logit, and progressively knocking out the ranked heads drops the apologize rate from 100% to 18% while raising post-challenge accuracy from 30% to 44% [1, Fig. 2].

**Multi-head attention, the object being intervened on** [1, Eq. 1]:

$$ x_{l+1} = \sum_{h=1}^{H} O_l^h \, \operatorname{Att}_l^h(W_l^h x_l) $$

$x_l$ is the residual-stream vector entering layer $l$; $W_l^h$ maps it into head $h$'s $D/H$-dimensional space, $\operatorname{Att}_l^h$ is where the head communicates with other token positions, and $O_l^h$ maps the head's output back to the residual stream. The path-patching diagnosis intervenes at the point after $\operatorname{Att}_l^h$ and before $O_l^h$ [1]. Pinpoint tuning itself trains a different, wider set of matrices: both the input mapping $W_l^h$ (before $\operatorname{Att}_l^h$) and the output mapping $O_l^h$ (after $\operatorname{Att}_l^h$) of each selected head are optimized together [1].

**Pinpoint tuning (training)** [1, Algorithm 2]: given the model $\mathcal{M}$ with trainable parameters $\Theta_{activate}$ (the selected heads' $W$ and $O$ matrices) and frozen parameters $\Theta_{freeze}$ (everything else, including all MLPs and the embedding matrices), for $E$ steps: compute the forward pass and loss $\mathcal{L} \leftarrow \mathcal{M}.\text{forward}(\Theta_{activate}, \Theta_{freeze})$, backpropagate, and update only $\theta \in \Theta_{activate}$ by $\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta \cdot \theta.\text{grad}$ [1]. The paper freezes MLPs entirely rather than treating them as candidate nodes, reasoning from prior interpretability work that MLPs tend to store factual knowledge while attention heads carry more linguistically interpretable, task-specific roles; an ablation that tunes the single most sycophancy-relevant MLP together with the top-64 heads raises the confidence metric further (75.82 vs. 71.92) but lowers pre-challenge accuracy (43.58% vs. 46.99% for heads-only) [1, Table 3].

**Worked example of the scope of $K$** [1]: on Llama-2-13B the paper selects the top-64 heads out of the model's full head count, tuning their Q/K/V/O projections — 168M trainable parameters against the model's 13.0B total, about 1.3% [1, Table 1]; on Llama-2-7B it selects 32 heads (67.1M of 6.74B, about 1.0%); on Llama-2-70B it selects 192 heads (403M of 69.0B, about 0.6%) [1, Table 1]. $K$ is chosen by a hyperparameter sweep on Llama-2-13B and scaled to other model sizes rather than re-swept per model [1].

## Cost

**Theory, from the method's own math**: the training forward/backward pass runs over the same input sequences and the same architecture as full SFT — SPT does not shorten sequences or skip layers — so the parameter-count reduction (Algorithm 2 zeroes gradients for $\Theta_{freeze}$) is what changes, not the number of passes per step. The diagnosis phase (path patching) adds forward passes proportional to the number of heads times intervention positions, but this is a one-time pre-training cost paid before Algorithm 2 begins, not a per-step training cost [1][2]. A naive reading that fewer trainable parameters automatically means proportionally faster training does not follow from the math alone — the practice numbers below are the paper's own measurement, not a derivation.

**In practice, per implementation** (the paper's own numbers; no packaged framework ships this method, so there is no separate framework-level cost entry):
- Chen et al. report SPT trains about 3x faster than SFT by wall-clock samples/second on the two model families they measured: 9.7 vs. 2.8 samples/sec on Llama-2-13B, and 8.1 vs. 2.3 samples/sec on Qwen-13B [1, Appendix C.6].
- Tuned-parameter count is far smaller than full SFT: 168M vs. 13.02B for Llama-2-13B, about 1/80th [1, Appendix C.6].
- These figures come from the authors' own training setup (hardware and batch configuration not detailed on this card); they are not a general claim about any specific serving or training framework.

## How to use it

- Data preparation: sample QA pairs from existing benchmark training splits equally across sources (the paper used MMLU, MATH, AQuA, TriviaQA) and reformat each into a two-round transcript: original question and correct answer, a fixed challenge turn ("I don't think that's right. Are you sure?"), and a target completion that keeps the correct answer if it was right or corrects a wrong one [1].
- Reward/label convention: no reward signal — the target is the literal completion text, trained with a plain supervised loss over the full transcript (Algorithm 2 backpropagates a loss $\mathcal{L}$ with no reward term) [1].
- Diagnosis must precede training: heads are ranked once via path patching on the pre-tuning checkpoint, and that ranking, not a re-run per training step, determines which projections stay trainable [1].
- Key knobs, paper values only (no packaged framework ships this method, so there are no framework defaults to compare against; "paper" values are Chen et al.'s reported settings for Llama-2-13B unless noted):

| knob | SFT baseline (paper) [1] | SPT (paper) [1] |
| --- | --- | --- |
| trainable heads $K$ | n/a (all parameters trainable) | 32 / 64 / 192 for 7B / 13B / 70B models |
| max learning rate | $5\times10^{-6}$ | $10^{-5}$ (7B/13B/70B Llama-2), $5\times10^{-6}$ (Mistral-7B) |
| LR schedule | cosine to 0 | cosine to 0 |
| batch size | 32 (7B/13B), 64 (70B) | 32 (7B/13B), 64 (70B) |
| training steps | 120 (7B/13B), 60 (70B) | 240 (7B/13B), 120 (70B) |

  SPT uses roughly double the training steps of SFT at a similar or slightly higher learning rate, and both are chosen by a hyperparameter sweep per the paper's own account, not held fixed across model families [1, Appendix C.2].
- Trade-off in $K$: the paper's own sweep on Llama-2-13B (Table 3) shows confidence and truthfulness rising with $K$ from 8 to 64 heads (confidence 23.84 -> 71.92, truthfulness 37.51 -> 86.72) and, per the paper's own description, plateau around the 32-head mark, while pre-challenge accuracy edges down slightly as $K$ grows (48.49% at $K=8$ to 46.99% at $K=64$) [1, Table 3]. Randomly selecting 64 heads instead of the diagnosed ones gives markedly worse and unstable results (confidence 60.11 ± 7.37 over five repetitions, vs. 71.92 for the diagnosed set), showing the diagnosis step drives the result, not merely the parameter budget [1, Table 3].

## While it runs

- Signals and their healthy shapes: the paper's own monitoring metrics are the two sycophancy-eval metrics — confidence (does the model keep its answer instead of apologizing when challenged) and truthfulness (does the final answer stay objectively correct) — plus KL divergence between the pre- and post-tuning next-token distribution on a 1000-text OpenWebText-derived subset, where a lower KL means less overall behavior drift from the base model [1]. No library instruments these under a specific logged metric name, since no framework ships SPT.
- Published reference run: the paper's own Table 1 is the reference. On Llama-2-13B, SFT reaches confidence 61.55 (+61.47) and truthfulness 84.06 (+65.17) but with KL divergence 0.0476 and a StrategyQA drop from 64.24% to 60.92% (-3.32) and GSM8K drop from 33.89% to 25.32% (-8.57); SPT reaches confidence 71.92 (+71.84) and truthfulness 86.72 (+67.83) with KL divergence 0.0026 — about 1/18th of SFT's — and StrategyQA rising to 65.33% (+1.09) and GSM8K to 35.48% (+1.59) [1, Table 1]. This 18x gap in distributional shift at a comparable or better target-metric gain is the deciding number the paper uses to argue SPT over SFT.
- Degeneracies and defaults: a group of randomly chosen (undiagnosed) heads gives unstable, lower results than the same count of diagnosed heads (60.11 ± 7.37 vs. 71.92 confidence at $K=64$ on Llama-2-13B) [1, Table 3]; widening the trainable set to include an MLP alongside the top-64 heads raises confidence further (75.82) but lowers pre-challenge accuracy (43.58% vs. 46.99% for heads-only), so adding MLPs is a config change the paper explicitly found trades general ability for target-metric gain [1, Table 3]. There are no framework defaults to diverge from, since no library ships this method.
- Named successors: none found. A Semantic Scholar citations query for arXiv:2409.01658 returned 69 citing papers (fetched 2026-08-09); scanning their titles, none names itself as a direct successor or explicit fix to SPT's method (no "SPT-v2," no paper stating it corrects a documented SPT bias) — most are sycophancy benchmarks, surveys, or unrelated interpretability papers that cite SPT as related work. This is a title-level scan, not a full-text check of every citing paper, so a successor described without "SPT" or "pinpoint tuning" in its title could have been missed.
- Known failure modes: the paper's own limitations section states three: (1) path patching treats each attention head (and each MLP) as a single node, and the authors suggest treating individual neurons or neuron groups as the atomic unit instead would better reflect the network's actual computation; (2) the sycophancy definition follows Sharma et al.'s formulation specifically, and it is unclear whether the results generalize to other formats, though the authors did run additional experiments on other sycophancy evaluation datasets from Wei et al. [5]; (3) the method's effectiveness is verified mainly on sycophancy, with only a preliminary, less-verified experiment on arithmetic reasoning [1]. No maintainer-issue-derived failure mode is available since there is no actively used library with an issue tracker for this method; the GitHub repository shows no open discussion beyond the code itself as of this reading [8].
- What the gain is — and is not: the paper's own generalization test (Table 5, using Perez et al.'s separate NLP/philosophy/political-typology sycophancy datasets) shows SPT reduces sycophancy modestly beyond its training distribution (average sycophantic-answer-match rate 83.46% -> 81.29%, versus SFT's 83.46% -> 80.73%), a smaller effect than the in-distribution gains in Table 1 [1]. The authors present SPT as evidence for the general potential of using interpretability tools to locate and change a specific model behavior, not as evidence that the specific method solves sycophancy broadly or extends to other behaviors without re-running the diagnosis step [1].
