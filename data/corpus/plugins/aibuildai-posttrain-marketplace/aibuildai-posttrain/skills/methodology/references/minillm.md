# MiniLLM

Knowledge distillation where the student samples its own responses on-policy and is pushed to raise the teacher's log-probability on them, minimizing reverse KLD instead of the forward KLD used by standard KD.

**MiniLLM** is a white-box knowledge-distillation method for compressing a large teacher language model into a smaller student, introduced in "MiniLLM: On-Policy Distillation of Large Language Models" as an alternative to standard KD's forward-KLD objective [1]. Its parent is standard word-level KD, which trains the student on the teacher's token distribution over a fixed dataset by minimizing forward KLD $\operatorname{KL}[p||q_\theta]$ [1]; MiniLLM instead minimizes reverse KLD $\operatorname{KL}[q_\theta||p]$, and because that objective requires expectations over the student's own samples, the paper derives an on-policy policy-gradient training procedure, similar in structure to RLHF, that samples from the student during training rather than only reading from a fixed corpus [1]. The paper gives two reasons for the switch: forward KLD forces the student to cover every mode of the teacher's output distribution, which a lower-capacity student cannot do and which causes it to place probability mass on the teacher's void regions (producing low-quality text), while reverse KLD lets the student seek the teacher's major modes within its own capacity [1]; and because only the last steps of an on-policy rollout carry direct feedback, naive policy gradient on this objective has high variance and reward-hacks toward short, degenerate strings unless several stabilizing tricks are added [1].

The paper reports RL with MiniLLM lifting GPT-2 125M from an SFT Rouge-L of 23.3 to 24.6 on DollyEval and OPT 1.3B from 26.0 to 26.7, with consistent gains over SFT, word-level KD, and SeqKD baselines across GPT-2 (120M-1.5B), OPT (1.3B-13B), and LLaMA (7B, teacher 13B) families (Table 1) [1]. The reverse-KLD-with-self-generated-samples idea was picked up as "On-Policy Distillation" by DeepMind's GKD, published the same month, which frames it as training the student on its own rollouts scored by the teacher and shows gains on summarization, translation, and reasoning tasks [2]; GKD's exact title, "On-Policy Distillation of Language Models: Learning from Self-Generated Mistakes," was confirmed on the arXiv abs page [2]. Lineage: word-level KD -> MiniLLM (2023, on-policy + reverse KLD) [1] -> parallel/contemporary GKD (2023) [2] -> DPKD, which replaces MiniLLM's RL step with a DPO-style pairwise loss over teacher/student generations (2024) [3].

**When to pick it**: pick MiniLLM when you have a strong white-box teacher (its own logits are readable) and want a student that avoids the teacher's low-quality long-tail generations, at the cost of running an RL-style training loop with online sampling [1]. Prefer plain word-level KD [1] when a single forward/backward pass per batch (no generation loop) is acceptable and the student is close in capacity to the teacher. Prefer SeqKD [1] (train on decoded teacher outputs) when you cannot backprop through the teacher's logits or only have API access. Nearest online neighbor is GKD [2]: GKD generalizes the same self-generated-rollout idea to a mixed on/off-policy schedule and lets the practitioner choose a JSD interpolation between forward and reverse KLD per token, rather than committing to full-trajectory reverse KLD with MiniLLM's variance-reduction machinery [2].

**Variant of**: word-level (forward-KLD) knowledge distillation [1].

**Data it needs**: a conditional generation dataset $\mathcal{D}$ of prompts with ground-truth responses (the paper used instruction-response pairs, e.g. Dolly, with 3-epoch SFT initialization on the same data) [1], plus an optional plain-text corpus $\mathcal{D}_{\text{PT}}$ used only for an auxiliary language-modeling loss that preserves general-NLP-benchmark performance; the paper uses OpenWebText for $\mathcal{D}_{\text{PT}}$ when distilling the GPT-2 family and the RoBERTa training corpus for the other model families [1]. No preference pairs and no separate reward model are required; the reward signal is the teacher's own per-token log-probability on student-sampled tokens [1]. On-policy: each training step samples fresh responses from a mixture of the student and the teacher (see How it works), not from a static pre-collected set [1]. Training scale in the paper: minibatch size 64, 256 sampled sequences collected per outer step, 4 inner epochs of policy optimization per collected batch, 5000 total steps, max length 512 [1].

**Extra models**: a frozen teacher model, held only for forward passes to score student-sampled tokens and to contribute to the teacher-mixed sampling distribution [1]; no value network and no separate reward model. trl's `MiniLLMTrainer` follows this: it takes a `teacher_model` argument loaded via `teacher_model_init_kwargs`, and it is built on the GRPO trainer infrastructure, so it also accepts an optional `reward_funcs` argument for combining the reverse-KL signal with extra scalar rewards [4]; details in Cost.

**Shipped by**: trl, `trl.experimental.minillm.MiniLLMTrainer` with `trl.experimental.minillm.MiniLLMConfig` [4]. The module sits under `trl.experimental`, whose package docstring calls it an "unstable or incubating" submodule where any trainer "may change (or be removed) in any release without deprecation" [5] — treat it as unsupported/experimental, not a stable trainer. No other framework in this corpus ships it. The original authors' own implementation is a from-scratch DeepSpeed/Megatron-style codebase (custom fork of `transformers`) rather than a plugin to an existing trainer, released at `microsoft/LMOps/minillm` [6].

## How it works

Each step: sample a mini-batch of prompts, roll out responses from a teacher-student mixture distribution, score each generated token against the frozen teacher, turn those scores into a policy-gradient update on the student, and add an auxiliary language-modeling loss on plain text [1].

**The base objective** minimizes reverse KLD between student $q_\theta$ and teacher $p$ [1, Eq. 1]:

$$ \theta = \arg\min_\theta \operatorname{KL}[q_\theta \| p] = \arg\min_\theta \left[-\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim p_{\bm{x}},\, \bm{y}\sim q_\theta} \log \frac{p(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}{q_\theta(\bm{y}|\bm{x})}\right] $$

Because $\bm{y}$ is sampled from $q_\theta$ itself, the paper applies the policy gradient theorem [1, Eq. 2]:

$$ \nabla \mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim p_{\bm{x}},\,\bm{y}\sim q_\theta(\cdot|\bm{x})} \sum_{t=1}^{T} (R_t - 1)\, \nabla \log q_\theta(y_t|\bm{y}_{<t}, \bm{x}), \qquad R_t = \sum_{t'=t}^{T} \log \frac{p(y_{t'}|\bm{y}_{<t'},\bm{x})}{q_\theta(y_{t'}|\bm{y}_{<t'},\bm{x})} $$

$R_t$ accumulates the per-token log-ratio $r_{t'} = \log\big(p(y_{t'}|\cdot)/q_\theta(y_{t'}|\cdot)\big)$ from position $t$ to the end of the sequence, so it is exactly the teacher-vs-student advantage of the remaining generation [1].

**Three stabilizers**, each addressing a documented failure of the raw policy gradient above [1]:

- Single-step decomposition rewrites the gradient so the immediate-step term $\mathbb{E}_{y_t\sim q_\theta(t)}[r_t]$ is computed exactly by summing over the vocabulary (not by Monte Carlo), because errors in early tokens otherwise accumulate through $R_t$ and dominate the training variance [1, Eq. 3].
- Teacher-mixed sampling replaces sampling purely from $q_\theta$ with sampling from a per-token mixture $\tilde p(y_t|\cdot) = \alpha\, p(y_t|\cdot) + (1-\alpha)\, q_\theta(y_t|\cdot)$ [1, Eq. 4], correcting the resulting gradient with an importance weight $w_t \approx q_\theta(y_t|\cdot)/\tilde p(y_t|\cdot)$ [1, Eq. 5], because sampling purely on-policy from a weak student lets it reward-hack toward repeated or degenerate strings that the teacher happens to score highly [1]. The paper fixes $\alpha=0.2$ for all main experiments, and its Appendix C.4 ablation of $\alpha$ from 0 (student-only) to 1 (teacher-only) shows this is the value that works across model families and sizes [1].
- Length normalization rescales the tail sum into an average, $R^{\text{Norm}}_{t+1} = \frac{1}{T-t-1}\sum_{t'=t+1}^{T} \log\big(p(y_{t'}|\cdot)/q_\theta(y_{t'}|\cdot)\big)$ [1, Eq. 6], because the un-normalized sum favors short sequences and drives the student toward empty responses [1].

**The combined gradient** used in training is $(\nabla\mathcal{L})_{\text{Single}} + (\nabla\mathcal{L})_{\text{Long}}^{\text{Norm}} + \nabla\mathcal{L}_{\text{PT}}$, where $(\nabla\mathcal{L})_{\text{Long}}^{\text{Norm}}$ additionally clips the importance ratio $\rho_t(\theta) = q_\theta(y_t|\cdot)/\tilde p(y_t|\cdot)$ to $[1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon]$ before taking a $\min$, in the same shape as PPO's clipped surrogate [7], and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{PT}} = -\mathbb{E}_{\bm{d}\sim\mathcal{D}_{\text{PT}}}\log q_\theta(\bm{d})$ is a plain language-modeling loss on the plain-text corpus, included to preserve performance on canonical NLP benchmarks [1, Algorithm 1]. Worked micro-example: for one generated token with teacher probability $p=0.8$ and student probability $q_\theta=0.2$ on the realized token, the per-token log-ratio is $r_t = \log(0.8/0.2) = \log 4 \approx 1.386$ — a positive signal that pushes the student to raise its probability on that token; if instead $q_\theta=0.9 > p=0.8$, $r_t = \log(0.8/0.9) \approx -0.118$, a small negative signal, since the student already over-favors that token relative to the teacher (arithmetic is this card's own, illustrating the formula, not taken from either the paper or trl).

trl's `MiniLLMConfig` exposes each stabilizer as a toggle inherited from the same objective: `rkl_advantage` (default `True`) turns the long-horizon reverse-KL advantage term on or off, `single_step_decomposition` (default `True`) turns the exact single-step term on or off, and `gamma` (default `0.0`) is a discount factor applied to future-step rewards that the original paper's equations do not include as a free hyperparameter [4]. trl's docs state that setting `rkl_advantage=False, single_step_decomposition=True` reduces $L_{\text{MiniLLM}}$ to the reverse-KLD version of the GKD loss, and setting `rkl_advantage=True, single_step_decomposition=False, gamma=False` reduces it to the plain on-policy KD loss used by Tinker [4]. Length normalization, load-bearing in the paper's own ablation (see While it runs), is a named `MiniLLMConfig` toggle, `length_normalization` (default `True`), though this card did not verify its implementation matches Eq. 6 exactly [4]. Teacher-mixed sampling has no corresponding `MiniLLMConfig` field in the fetched docs [4].

## Cost

**Theory, from the method's own math**: every training step needs one teacher forward pass and one student forward pass over every sampled token (to compute $r_t$ at each position), plus a student backward pass; the teacher never receives gradients so it needs no optimizer state [1]. The single-step term additionally sums over the full vocabulary at every position rather than using the sampled token alone, so its cost scales with vocabulary size per token, not just sequence length [1, Eq. 3]. A naive reading says this needs the same models-in-memory as a KL-regularized RLHF setup (policy + frozen reference), i.e. one extra frozen model beyond the student, since no value network appears anywhere in the derivation [1] — the practice layer below shows what the shipping trainer actually adds on top (a GRPO-style rollout stack).

**In practice, per framework**: trl's `MiniLLMTrainer` is built directly on `GRPOTrainer`'s machinery, inheriting its rollout and batching controls (`max_prompt_length` default 512, `num_generations` default 8, `max_completion_length` default 256) and its optional vLLM-backed generation path (`use_vllm`) [4]. Because it subclasses the GRPO stack, it also carries GRPO's `beta` field, which trl's GRPO docs describe as the KL coefficient against a reference model and note that at its default of `0.0` the reference model is not loaded at all [8] — a separate knob from MiniLLM's own `rkl_advantage`/`gamma` terms. Its default `loss_type` is `"dapo"` [4] — inherited defaults that are not part of MiniLLM's own definition in the paper and should not be read as the paper's choices.

## How to use it

- Prompts: an instruction/response dataset for the target task; the paper's own runs used Dolly-style instruction data with the ground-truth response reused only for the SFT initialization phase, not for the MiniLLM phase itself [1].
- Reward: none in the classic scalar-reward sense — the signal is the teacher's own token-level log-probabilities on student-generated text, read directly off the teacher model, so no separate reward-model training is needed [1]. trl's trainer additionally accepts `reward_funcs` (inherited from GRPO) for mixing in extra scalar signals, which is a framework extension beyond the paper's definition [4].
- Two-phase procedure, as run in the paper: Phase 1 fine-tunes the student on $\mathcal{D}$ for 3 epochs and keeps the checkpoint with lowest validation loss as the RL starting point; Phase 2 runs Algorithm 1 from that checkpoint [1].
- Key knobs, paper values as anchors against trl's shipped defaults:

| knob | paper [1] | trl `MiniLLMConfig` / inherited default [4] |
| --- | --- | --- |
| teacher mix-in $\alpha$ | 0.2 (swept 0.0-1.0) | not exposed as a separate `MiniLLMConfig` field in the fetched docs |
| clip range $\epsilon$ | 0.2 | `epsilon` 0.2 (inherited GRPO field)$^\dagger$ |
| learning rate (Phase 2) | 5e-6 | `learning_rate` 1e-6 (inherited) |
| minibatch / rollout size | mini-batch 64, 256 sequences collected per outer step, 4 inner epochs | `num_generations` 8, `num_iterations` 1 (inherited GRPO fields; not the same batching shape) |
| max response length | 512 | `max_completion_length` 256 |
| sampling temperature | 1 (training-time sampling and evaluation) | `kd_temperature` 1.0 |
| single-step decomposition | on (proposed as essential, Table 4) | `single_step_decomposition` True |
| reverse-KL advantage term | on (proposed as essential, Table 4) | `rkl_advantage` True |
| discount factor $\gamma$ | not a parameter of the paper's objective | `gamma` 0.0 |

  $^\dagger$ Not the same quantity: the paper's $\epsilon$ clips $\rho_t(\theta)=q_\theta(y_t|\cdot)/\tilde p(y_t|\cdot)$, the ratio of the student's probability to the teacher-mixed sampling distribution [1, Algorithm 1]; trl's inherited GRPO `epsilon` clips $\pi_\theta(o_{i,t})/\pi_{\theta_{old}}(o_{i,t})$, the ratio of the current policy to the last rollout snapshot, and the fetched MiniLLM docs give no description of `epsilon` beyond the bare inherited default [4][8]. The two clip ranges act on differently-defined ratios and should not be read as the same knob despite sharing a value.

  The mismatch to flag: trl's defaults come from its GRPO parent class and were not tuned for MiniLLM's own reported setup — the batching shape (256 sequences / 4 inner epochs per step) and learning rate (5e-6) in the paper have no matching defaults in the fetched trl config, so a run started from trl's defaults alone does not reproduce the paper's training recipe.
- Group-size / batch-reuse trade-off: the paper collects a fixed set of 256 sampled sequences and reuses them for 4 inner policy-optimization epochs before resampling, the same batch-reuse pattern PPO-style RLHF uses to amortize the cost of generation against several gradient steps [1].

## While it runs

- Signals and their healthy shapes: the paper's own diagnostic is the reverse KLD between student and teacher over training (Figure 8), which decreases in all ablation variants, including broken ones — a decreasing reverse-KLD curve alone does not confirm healthy training, because degenerate repeated-token outputs can also drive it down [1].
- Published reference run: the paper's own ablation (Table 4), distilling GPT-2-125M from GPT-2-1.5B, is the closest thing to a reference curve in this source: full MiniLLM reaches validation Rouge-L 27.4 / test (Dolly) Rouge-L 24.6; removing length normalization collapses it to 17.4 / 14.7; removing teacher-mixed sampling gives 22.3 / 20.4; removing single-step decomposition gives 27.0 / 23.7 [1, Table 4]. The 10-point drop from removing length normalization is the largest single effect in the ablation and is the number that shows the method fails without it.
- Degeneracies and defaults: without teacher-mixed sampling or length normalization, the paper reports the student quickly learns to generate repeated, short, or meaningless strings that still score highly under the teacher — the paper's own named instance of reward hacking in this setting [1]. trl's GRPO-inherited `beta` defaults to `0.0`, at which no reference model is loaded and no KL penalty is applied unless set explicitly [8], and this is a separate knob from MiniLLM's own `rkl_advantage` term that should not be conflated with it.
- Named successors: DPKD replaces the RL optimization step with a direct preference-style loss between teacher and student generations, removing the on-policy sampling loop [3]. GKD generalizes the self-generated-rollout idea to a tunable on/off-policy mixture and a choice of forward/reverse/JSD divergence per token [2].
- Known failure modes: the paper's own limitations are reward hacking toward degenerate short/repeated outputs when the stabilizers are removed (Section 3.4) [1], and a documented diversity trade-off from mode-seeking reverse-KLD optimization, which the paper argues is limited in practice (distinct 4-gram proportion and language-modeling loss are preserved in their own diversity analysis, Table 7 in the source's numbering) [1]. A GitHub issues search on `repo:huggingface/trl MiniLLM` (29 results, checked 2026-09-03) surfaces three maintainer-relevant findings on trl's `MiniLLMTrainer`. First, closed issue #4562: TRL member qgallouedec states on the record that GKD and MiniLLM are "formulated around token-level KL divergence between teacher and student logits, which intrinsically requires a shared vocabulary," calling this "the loss definition" rather than a fixable bug, and closes the issue by directing cross-tokenizer use cases to the separate experimental `GOLDTrainer` instead [9] — MiniLLM in trl requires teacher and student to share a tokenizer/vocabulary. Second, open issue #6626: a reported and independently reproduced bug in `MiniLLMTrainer._compute_advantage`, where the discount `gamma` is applied by absolute sequence position instead of by distance from each token, so every advantage is scaled by an extra factor of `gamma^t`; with `length_normalization=True` this factor cancels analytically but `gamma**t` underflows to exactly `0.0` in float32 on long sequences, silently producing NaN advantages — one commenter reproduced non-finite advantages starting at position 987 of a 1024-token sequence with `gamma=0.9` [10]. Third, open issue #7024: in the same trainer, right-padding is not masked out before the advantage sum, so padded positions' teacher/student log-prob differences leak into every real token's advantage, with a worked example showing padded-vs-correctly-masked advantages of `[6.5, 5.0, 4.0, 2.0]` versus `[2.5, 1.0, 0.0, 0.0]` for the same four-token, two-pad completion [11]. The latter two are open, unresolved-by-maintainer issues at the time of this search, but both are numerical traps directly bearing on the `gamma` and `length_normalization` knobs discussed above in How to use it.
- What the gain is - and is not: the paper's own analysis attributes MiniLLM's gains to precision and calibration — lower exposure bias, better calibration, and higher Rouge-L especially on longer responses — not to new capability beyond what the teacher already expresses; on short-response prompts, where the student can already cover the teacher's modes, reverse-KLD and forward-KLD (standard KD) perform similarly [1]. The paper explicitly frames this as a mode-seeking student reproducing the teacher's major modes, not surpassing the teacher's own distribution [1].
