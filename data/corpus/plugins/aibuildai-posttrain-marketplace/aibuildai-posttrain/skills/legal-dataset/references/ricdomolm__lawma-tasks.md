# ricdomolm/lawma-tasks

260 legal classification tasks built from the U.S. Supreme Court Database and the Songer Court of Appeals Database - 692,483 full-opinion multiple-choice questions with train, val and test splits, whose test split is the source of the CaselawQA benchmark.

**ricdomolm/lawma-tasks** holds "the legal classification tasks from Lawma" [1], the tasks introduced in "Lawma: The Power of Specialization for Legal Annotation" by Dominguez-Olmedo et al. [2]. Each task asks a question a political scientist would code about a court opinion - which agency acted, what issue area, which way the decision went - with the whole opinion as context and an answer drawn from the database's codebook [1][3]. 37 tasks come from the Supreme Court Database (`sc_*`) and 223 from the Songer Court of Appeals Database (`songer_*`) [4]. It lives at https://huggingface.co/datasets/ricdomolm/lawma-tasks .

**Hold out every `test` split: CaselawQA (`ricdomolm/caselawqa-8k`) is 10,000 questions "randomly selected from the test sets of the Lawma tasks".**

**Use it for**: SFT for long-context legal classification: read a full opinion, answer a multiple-choice question. It is the largest labelled U.S. legal task set here and the one Lawma's specialised models were trained on. The raw columns need a prompt template; `ricdomolm/lawma-instructions` is the same material already rendered as instruction/output text.

**Licence**: MIT (`license: mit` in the card and the Hub tag) [5]. Ungated [5]. The one catch: none stated - the opinions are U.S. court documents and the labels come from academic databases the card links [1].

**Shape**: 692,483 rows across 260 configs, each with `train` / `val` / `test`: 484,786 / 69,633 / 138,064 rows in total [4]; five columns (`opinion`, `instruction`, `question`, `choices`, `answer`) [6].

**Hold out**: every config's `test` split (138,064 rows) - it is where CaselawQA's questions were drawn from [7]. Keep `val` for model selection.

**Origin**: opinions written by U.S. judges; labels coded by the Supreme Court Database and Songer project researchers, not generated by a model [1][2]. Hub API at the check date: `downloads` 2,459, `downloadsAllTime` 47,451, `likes` 2 [5].

**Trained-on-by**: the Lawma paper fine-tunes Llama 3 8B and 70B on these tasks [2]. No model on the Hub declares the dataset through its dataset tag [8].

**Introduced by**: [2] (Dominguez-Olmedo et al.). The card's citation block uses the paper's earlier title, "Lawma: The Power of Specialization for Legal Tasks" [1]; the live abs page now reads "... for Legal Annotation" [2].

## Quality

- Labels come from two expert-coded political-science databases, not from a model [1]. The instructions quote the codebooks at length - row 0's instruction runs to several hundred characters before the question [3].
- Label spaces can be very large: `sc_adminaction` offers 124 agencies as choices in row 0, and its answer is index 116 ("State Agency") [3].
- The same opinion appears across many tasks, one question each; a mix drawn from several configs repeats long opinions many times, which inflates token counts without adding documents.
- No source states a measured duplicate rate.

## Load it

Train on `train`, select on `val`, hold out `test`, and pin the revision:

```python
import datasets

REV = "818f6cd30d4328cf03d7778ae2b159b46e0321db"  # main at the check date
train = datasets.load_dataset("ricdomolm/lawma-tasks", "sc_adminaction", revision=REV, split="train")  # 1,689 rows
val = datasets.load_dataset("ricdomolm/lawma-tasks", "sc_adminaction", revision=REV, split="val")      # 237 rows
test = datasets.load_dataset("ricdomolm/lawma-tasks", "sc_adminaction", revision=REV, split="test")    # 527 rows - hold out
```

**Trap**: `answer` is a *list* of indices into `choices`, not a string: row 0 of `sc_adminaction/train` has `"answer": [116]` [3]. A template that renders `answer` directly writes "[116]" as the target. Map the index to its choice (or to a letter) yourself, and handle multi-label rows where the list has more than one index.

## Appendix: screening record

### Screening verdict

Usable as SFT data for long-context legal classification: train on `train`, hold out `test`, which is CaselawQA's source. Labels are expert-coded; the `answer` field needs mapping before it can be a text target.

### The screening row

The row's own note: "SCDB + Songer coded variables over full opinions; test splits feed CaselawQA." The row carries no flag.
