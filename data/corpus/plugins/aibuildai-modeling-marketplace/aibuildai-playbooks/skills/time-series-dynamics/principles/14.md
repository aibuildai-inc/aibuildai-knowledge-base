## Principle 14: Project Predictions onto the Feasible Output Space

*Consensus: 4/5 competitions applied important post-processing constraints; the unconstrained Jane Street target was the main exception.*

After prediction, enforce only constraints that are true by definition or strongly validated: nonnegativity, quantile order, monotone cumulative paths, discrete measurement grids, target masks, algebraic updates, and stable long-horizon tails. Perform projection in the correct target space and precision.

**Why this matters:** Generic regressors can create impossible outputs even when their average error is low. Constraint projection removes error without needing more data: COVID quantiles had to be ordered, cumulative forecasts should not fall, web counts were nonnegative integers, and climate submission weights and deterministic tendencies required exact handling.

**When to use:** Use when the output support or relation is specified by measurement, physics, metric, or target definition. Validate smoothing and tail damping separately because they are assumptions, not hard constraints.

**When NOT to use:** Do not enforce visual smoothness on genuinely jumpy markets or events, and do not force cumulative monotonicity when the scored target is daily incidence rather than cumulative count. Hard rounding may hurt continuous targets unless the labels are genuinely quantized.

**How to reason about it:**
Separate hard constraints from soft priors. Apply hard projection unconditionally; tune soft smoothing, clipping, decay, and fallback thresholds on rolling-origin OOF predictions. Check that operations do not change masked columns, break quantile calibration, or lose precision during normalization inversion.
