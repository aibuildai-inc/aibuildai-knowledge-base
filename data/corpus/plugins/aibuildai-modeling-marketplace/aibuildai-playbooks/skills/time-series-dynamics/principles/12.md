## Principle 12: Ensemble Distinct Error Modes, Not Just More Models

*Consensus: 5/5 competitions used seed, fold, checkpoint, architecture, horizon, or mechanism/model blending; gains often exceeded gains from another small architecture tweak.*

Combine models whose residuals differ across seeds, architectures, feature scales, training windows, mechanisms, or regimes. Start with simple averaging or medians, and learn weights only on deployment-faithful out-of-fold predictions.

**Why this matters:** Dynamic tasks have high variance and regime-dependent errors. Checkpoint and seed averaging stabilize noisy training, while architecture or mechanism diversity reduces systematic errors; weaker individual climate and market models still improved blends because their mistakes differed.

**When to use:** Use when out-of-fold residual correlation is below one and inference constraints permit multiple models. Medians are attractive under occasional extreme failures; means are efficient for roughly symmetric errors; constrained stacking is useful with reliable OOF predictions.

**When NOT to use:** Do not blend near-duplicates merely to increase model count, and do not fit many unconstrained weights to a small or mismatched validation set. The LEAP winner used equal weights partly to reduce leaderboard overfit, while a hill-climbed LEAP ensemble was supported by a highly correlated temporal holdout.

**How to reason about it:**
Build a residual-correlation matrix by horizon, target, and regime. Add a model when its blend improvement is robust across folds, not only when its standalone score is high. Prefer diversity from meaningful choices—mechanistic versus learned, local versus global, recent versus long-history—then test equal weights before optimization.
