## Principle 8: Match the Training Distribution to Recency, Season, and Data Scale

*Consensus: 4/5 competitions reported major gains from full-data scaling, random temporal windows, recent-only sampling, or time-decayed weighting.*

Choose how much history to train on by balancing sample size against distribution shift. Use randomized valid windows to increase coverage in stationary systems, but prioritize recent or season-matched data when the generating process evolves.

**Why this matters:** More samples reduce variance only when they represent the deployment law. Full low-resolution climate data materially improved emulation, random web windows acted as augmentation, while old Jane Street periods added no value after the daily structure changed; COVID tree models upweighted recent observations because interventions and reporting evolved rapidly.

**When to use:** Use all available compatible data for stable simulators and repeated physical systems. Use recency filters, time decay, or season-matched sampling for markets, epidemics, and other drifting populations.

**When NOT to use:** Do not discard old data solely because it is old if it contains rare regimes that may recur. Conversely, do not assume data volume compensates for shift: climate validation became optimistic when near-duplicate space-time samples surrounded the holdout, and Jane Street found no gain from early unstable periods.

**How to reason about it:**
Plot feature, target, missingness, and entity-coverage drift by time. Evaluate nested training windows under the same future fold. IF longer history improves all relevant regimes, keep it; IF it hurts recent/gap folds, use decay or a cutoff; IF rare regimes disappear under recency filtering, mix a recent stream with a stratified historical stream.
