## Principle 10: Regularize with Valid Invariances and Robust Representations

*Consensus: 3/5 competitions explicitly benefited from log/robust transforms, soft clipping, or feature coarsening.*

Regularize according to transformations the system can plausibly tolerate, and compress extreme values without destroying ordering. Treat every augmentation as a hypothesis about the data-generating process, not as a generic deep-learning recipe.

**Why this matters:** Heavy tails and scale differences destabilize optimization, while carefully chosen perturbations force the model to learn shared dynamics instead of memorizing regimes. Climate solutions used multiple normalizations and soft clipping; web models used log1p; and a COVID solution rounded/coarsened features to reduce overfitting.

**When to use:** Use monotone transforms for multiplicative heavy-tailed values, soft clipping for rare extremes, and mixup/masking when interpolations or missing static attributes are meaningful. Multiple representations are useful when both absolute level and within-profile shape matter.

**When NOT to use:** Do not shuffle or reverse sequences if direction and causality are essential, and do not mix entities whose targets do not interpolate physically. Hard clipping can erase rare but important events; one LEAP solution therefore used smooth logarithmic compression rather than truncation.

**How to reason about it:**
For each transform ask what is invariant: scale, offset, small timing jitter, entity type, or local ordering. Validate on extreme and ordinary regimes separately. Prefer reversible transforms and preserve raw scale as an additional feature when normalization removes information.

def soft_compress(x, cutoff):
    # Continuous, monotone compression; customize cutoff from train tails.
    return torch.where(x.abs() <= cutoff, x,
        x.sign() * (cutoff + torch.log1p(x.abs() - cutoff)))
