## Principle 4: Combine Learned Models with Mechanistic Identities and Regime Routing

*Consensus: 3/5 competitions had decisive gains from exact formulas, stable baselines, or regime-dependent blending; the pattern was strongest in climate, web, and epidemic tasks.*

Search for deterministic relationships, conservation/update equations, discrete controllers, and simple baselines, then use them as features, constraints, replacements, or routing experts. Let the learned model handle only the residual or the cases where the mechanism is incomplete.

**Why this matters:** A flexible model spends capacity approximating known structure and may violate it out of distribution. An atmospheric update identity replaced difficult target levels; and low-volume or strongly seasonal web series were better served by specialized baselines than by one universal extrapolator.

**When to use:** Use when values lie on a discrete lattice, metadata identifies physical regimes, a simulator/controller equation is documented, targets obey an algebraic update, or baseline error varies systematically with volume, seasonality, or maturity.

**When NOT to use:** Do not impose a brittle mechanism merely because the domain is physical. Epidemiological compartment models were difficult to fit reliably under reporting bias. If residual checks show systematic violations, retain the mechanism as an expert or feature rather than forcing all predictions through it.

**How to reason about it:**
Fit the proposed identity on training episodes and inspect residuals by regime. IF residuals are near numerical tolerance and required inputs are available, use exact replacement; IF the mechanism is approximate, predict its residual; IF different experts dominate in observable regions, learn or validate a gate; ELSE keep the unconstrained model. Never infer unavailable test labels while identifying a regime.

```python
base = mechanistic_predict(x, params)       # Customize known update/controller
match = mechanism_residual(x, base) < tolerance
ml = learned_model(features)
# Exact replacement only where the mechanism is identifiable and validated.
pred = np.where(match, base, ml)
# Approximate alternative: pred = base + residual_model(features)
```
