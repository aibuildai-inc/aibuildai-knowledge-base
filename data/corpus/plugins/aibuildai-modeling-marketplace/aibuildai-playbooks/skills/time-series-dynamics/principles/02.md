## Principle 2: Preserve Natural Axes and Sequence Boundaries

*Consensus: 5/5 competitions used meaningful grouping or ordering; several explicitly built recurrent, convolutional, attention, or state-space processing around it.*

Reshape flat tables into their true units—episodes, instruments by day, article histories, locations, or atmospheric columns—and process the meaningful ordered axis without crossing group boundaries. Carry static entity or regime attributes alongside dynamic features.

**Why this matters:** Dynamics are expressed through transitions within a unit, while accidental transitions between units are noise or leakage. Local convolutions capture nearby interactions, recurrent/state-space blocks maintain evolving state, attention captures longer interactions, and static context distinguishes systems that respond differently to the same trajectory.

**When to use:** Use sequence/profile models when there are repeated observations per entity, fixed-length episodes, or an ordered physical grid. This is especially valuable when adjacent levels or timesteps have strong local dependence.

**When NOT to use:** Do not assume that every ordinal ID is a uniformly sampled physical time. Jane Street explicitly had irregular time intervals, while some COVID and web approaches succeeded with window summaries rather than raw long sequences. If ordering carries little incremental signal, pooled tabular models with grouped lag features can be faster and more stable.

**How to reason about it:**
Verify boundaries first, then test the incremental value of axis-aware processing against a tabular baseline. Choose local convolution for short-range smooth interactions, recurrence/state-space layers for evolving state, and explicit long lags or attention only when distant dependence survives validation. Encode static attributes as embeddings, one-hot vectors, or conditioning layers rather than repeating independent models unnecessarily.
