## Principle 7: Align Target Parameterization and Loss with the Metric

*Consensus: 5/5 competitions changed the loss, target representation, masking, or weighting to better reflect the evaluator.*

Optimize a stable surrogate that preserves the metric’s priorities, and parameterize targets in a space where the dynamics and error geometry are learnable. Apply official sample weights and score masks during both training diagnostics and validation.

**Why this matters:** MSE overemphasizes outliers for MAE-, SMAPE-, or heavy-tailed tasks; raw counts make multiplicative errors dominate; and an unweighted aggregate can spend capacity on unscored or low-value outputs. Top solutions used MAE/Huber/SmoothL1, log transforms, pinball loss, weighted zero-mean R², target-specific masking, and robust growth-rate parameterizations.

**When to use:** Use metric-aware objectives whenever the official score is asymmetric, weighted, scale-sensitive, quantile-based, or masks part of the target. Robust losses are especially useful under spikes, fat tails, and mixed target scales.

**When NOT to use:** Do not copy the metric formula blindly if it is numerically unstable for gradient training. Direct SMAPE is problematic near zero, and R² itself can be awkward as a minibatch objective. Use a smooth surrogate, but verify rank correlation with the exact metric on full validation folds.

**How to reason about it:**
Decompose the metric into transformation, sample/target weighting, asymmetry, and aggregation. Decide whether to model levels, log-levels, increments, rates, residuals, or quantiles. Compare candidate losses using exact out-of-fold scoring, including per-target diagnostics; mask targets whose official weight is zero.

def weighted_pinball(y, qhat, tau, weight):
    err = y - qhat
    return (weight * torch.maximum(tau * err, (tau - 1) * err)).mean()
