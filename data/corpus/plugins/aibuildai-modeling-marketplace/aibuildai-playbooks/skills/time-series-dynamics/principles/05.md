## Principle 5: Pool Across Series, but Preserve Entity and Regime Context

*Consensus: Most source competitions benefited from global models trained across many series; local/statistical experts remained useful for sparse or exceptional regimes.*

Prefer a pooled model when individual histories are short or there are many related entities, but supply entity metadata, scale, regime, and cross-sectional context so pooling does not erase heterogeneity. Retain local or rule-based fallbacks for entities with enough distinctive history or too little usable signal.

**Why this matters:** Pooling multiplies the number of training transitions and lets the model learn shared response laws. However, identical recent trajectories can imply different futures for different physical regimes, traffic classes, market symbols, climates, or epidemic stages, so conditioning and normalization are essential.

**When to use:** Use for large panels, repeated experiments, or systems sharing a response law. It is especially useful for cold-start and low-data entities, including future symbols or small locations.

**When NOT to use:** Avoid blind pooling when entities follow unrelated mechanisms or when an identity feature lets the model memorize training entities that will not recur. A per-series median was difficult to beat for low-volume web pages, and top COVID solutions manually or algorithmically separated large, mature locations from tiny or early-stage ones.

**How to reason about it:**
Compare three baselines: local, fully pooled, and pooled-with-context. Normalize dynamic magnitudes without discarding absolute scale; add scale back as a feature. Evaluate by entity regime and unseen-entity subsets, not only aggregate score. Route to a local baseline when the pooled model’s conditional error is predictably worse.
