## Principle 9: Use Auxiliary Targets to Teach Structure, Not Just Add Tasks

*Consensus: 2/5 competitions—LEAP and Jane Street—reported clear gains from derivative, related-responder, confidence, or group-specific auxiliary objectives.*

Add auxiliary targets that encode a real relationship: adjacent-level differences, response derivatives, alternate temporal aggregates, regime/location context, or prediction error. Keep the scored target primary and remove auxiliaries that introduce negative transfer.

**Why this matters:** A pointwise loss does not tell the model that neighboring outputs should have the right slope or that several responders are noisy aggregates of one latent process. Structural auxiliaries provide denser supervision, improve internal representations, and can expose when jointly trained target groups begin to interfere.

**When to use:** Use when auxiliary labels are available only during training but are causally related to the scored target, or when they can be deterministically derived from labels. Validate both the joint model and target-group fine-tuning.

**When NOT to use:** Do not add loosely related targets merely because they exist. LEAP solutions observed late-stage interference between target groups and recovered performance by group fine-tuning; the Jane Street online update used only the scored responder because auxiliary labels were not needed for adaptation.

**How to reason about it:**
State the shared latent quantity each auxiliary target measures. Measure scored-target gain, gradient conflict, and subgroup effects. IF joint training helps early but plateaus, fine-tune target groups or the scored head; IF an auxiliary requires unavailable or prohibited test information at inference, use it only as a training loss and discard its output.

# Structural derivative along the true ordered axis.
