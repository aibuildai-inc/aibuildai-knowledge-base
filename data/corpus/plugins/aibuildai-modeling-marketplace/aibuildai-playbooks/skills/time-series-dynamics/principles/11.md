## Principle 11: Choose Direct, Recursive, or Autoregressive Decoding by Horizon Stability

*Consensus: 3/3 long-horizon forecasting competitions made this choice explicit: web traffic and both COVID formulations used different direct, recursive, or sequence-decoder strategies.*

Use recursive decoding when feedback between predicted states is essential and validation shows stable rollouts; use horizon-specific direct models when recursive errors compound; use an autoregressive sequence decoder when jointly modeling the path adds value. The choice should vary with horizon and signal-to-noise ratio.

**Why this matters:** A one-step model may be excellent yet drift catastrophically after feeding back its own errors. Direct models avoid feedback but duplicate models and may produce an incoherent path. Autoregressive decoders learn exposure to their own predictions, but are harder to train and can still accumulate error.

**When to use:** Use recursive models for state equations or short horizons with stable dynamics; direct models for noisy long horizons and cheap models; sequence decoders when cross-horizon dependence is strong and enough data exists. Consider a hybrid: detailed short-horizon forecasts followed by a conservative long-horizon tail.

**When NOT to use:** Do not recurse by default because the training API exposes lag features. A top COVID neural solution switched to one model per horizon after earlier recursive forecasts diverged. Conversely, direct models are wasteful for very long horizons and can violate smoothness unless coupled or post-processed.

**How to reason about it:**
Backtest error as a function of horizon, not just its mean. IF recursive error accelerates faster than direct-model variance, switch to direct or hybrid prediction; IF direct paths are incoherent, add shared parameters, path losses, or smoothing; IF future exogenous inputs are known, condition every horizon on them rather than predicting them.
