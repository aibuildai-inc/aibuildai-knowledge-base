## Principle 12: Validate That Your CV Score Aligns With Leaderboard

*Consensus: Universal concern. M5 3rd explicitly selected models by past 14 periods. Optiver said 'CV aligns with LB very well'. All top solutions monitor CV/LB gap.*

If your local cross-validation score doesn't correlate with leaderboard score, your CV is wrong. This usually means: (1) CV doesn't match test time period, (2) leakage in CV, (3) different distributions, or (4) overfitting to public LB. Fix CV before optimizing models.

**Why this matters:** Bad CV makes you optimize the wrong thing. You waste time on improvements that don't generalize. The validation-LB gap is your primary debugging signal—if it's large or doesn't correlate, stop and fix validation before doing anything else. Good CV lets you iterate offline.

**When to use:** Check CV/LB alignment after every submission, especially early. If gap > 0.01 (for normalized metrics), investigate. If CV improvements don't transfer to LB, your CV is unreliable. Particularly critical when test period is different from validation period (different months, different market regime).

**When NOT to use:** Small gaps (<0.005) are normal—don't over-optimize. Public LB might be unreliable (small or unrepresentative)—trust CV more than public LB, but watch for divergence. Private LB is the real test.

**How to reason about it:**
Debug: (1) Check time periods: does validation match test chronologically? (2) Check distributions: plot feature histograms for train/val/test. (3) Check for leakage: can you predict val/test membership? (4) Check metric computation: are you computing it exactly as the competition does? (5) Check for overfitting: if LB is worse than CV, you're overfitting—reduce model complexity. Keep a log: submission, CV score, LB score. If they don't correlate, CV is wrong.
