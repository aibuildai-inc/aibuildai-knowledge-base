## Principle 9: Build Ensembles Across Diverse Architectures and Training Schemes

*Consensus: Optiver 1st combined CatBoost + GRU + Transformer. M5 3rd used DeepAR. Every top solution ensembles.*

Ensemble models with different inductive biases: GBDT (feature interactions) + RNN (sequential patterns) + CNN (local patterns). Also vary training: different seeds, different feature sets, different CV folds. Blend with simple weighted average or stacking.

**Why this matters:** Diverse models make uncorrelated errors—ensemble reduces variance and captures different aspects of the data. A GBDT might nail weekly seasonality while an LSTM captures rare event patterns. The 1-2% boost from ensembling is often the difference between gold and silver medals.

**When to use:** Always ensemble your top-K models in final submission. Most effective when: (1) models use different architectures, (2) models trained on different data subsets/features, (3) base models have CV > threshold but differ in predictions. For large datasets, ensemble more models; for small, ensemble 3-5 to avoid overfitting.

**When NOT to use:** Don't ensemble models that are too similar (same architecture, same features, only seed differs)—diminishing returns. Don't ensemble if base models are bad—garbage ensemble is still garbage. Don't over-weight complex models in blend—simple models often generalize better.

**How to reason about it:**
Good ensemble = low correlation between errors. Check: do your models disagree on hard examples? Train diversity: (1) architecture diversity (GBDT + neural net), (2) feature diversity (drop 20% of features), (3) sample diversity (bootstrap, different CV folds), (4) hyperparameter diversity (depth, learning rate). Blend weights: start with equal weights, optimize on validation set. For time series, weighted average often beats stacking (less overfitting).
