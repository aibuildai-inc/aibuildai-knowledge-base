# 30th Place Approach and Lessons Learned

Competition: web-traffic-time-series-forecasting
Rank: #30
Source: https://www.kaggle.com/c/web-traffic-time-series-forecasting/discussion/43569

Thanks to those who have shared their approaches and made useful comments in the discussion. I learnt a lot from some of those and they have inspired me to push on with Deep Learning. 

As far as what I learned, I found a lot of value in setting up a CV framework that reflected the nature of the competition. For the prediction period I set up 6 consecutive folds of 61 days each test data. This covered a period of 366 days which was as close to a year as I could get it. This was an attempt to cover seasonality at least to a degree. I also paid close attention to the number of days gap between the train and test period as it was possible to model much more accurately where the gap was lower. So for each test period my CV was set up with a gap of a few days (based on what the competition organisers were indicating that gap would be) with the training period started prior to that gap of days. So for example if I had a fold with test data starting on 1st May I made a 10 day gap so that I would have the train data end on 20th Apr (for the same year obviously). 

I mainly used the median approach and did not find an elegant way to improve on it. I did however find a simple way to improve on it which worked for me in this competition though it may not generalise very well. I initially had the idea that internet use globally is generally growing. Then I considered that the median actually exists at a point on a range of quantiles or percentiles. So I tried treating that as a parameter. If internet use is growing then perhaps a percentile slightly above 50 might be a better option. On the first level predictions this did not lead to a significant improvement and I decided that the median was the best option. But when taking a second level - the so called "median of medians" I found that increasing the percentile actually lead to a worse score. However, just to be thorough I tried lowering the percentile and to my initial surprise I found that this lead to an improvement. Depending on the CV fold I was able to improve by around 0.2 to 1.0 using percentiles approximately in the range 27.5 to 37.5 where there were around 8 or 9 first level medians. Because of the short deadline at the end of the competition and because I had this idea quite late I was not able to test it very thoroughly. At that point I did more EDA across the aggregate dataset - I probably should have done that first! This showed that the seasonal increase from summer to fall/winter was stronger in 2015 than in 2016. It also showed that the seasonal decrease from the beginning to the middle of 2017 was more severe than in 2016. This led me to the conclusion that many of the sites in the dataset (which was selected at a specific point in time) were based around specific events where interest would peak at a certain point and then reduce dramatically. I recall one example site for a big sporting event in 2016. Clearly the interest in that would build up through 2015 to the event itself but then decline afterwards dramatically. Using percentiles at the second level was not the only difference between my two submissions but I estimate that the improvement in the competition was fairly substantial at around 0.4 or maybe more. For others who used a median based approach your mileage may vary...

For those who find the top solutions a bit intimidating then perhaps there are some ideas here that are a bit more approachable. I've learned a lot from Kaggle and hope to repay some of that by helping others. Two takeaways points are 1) CV is always important, just find a way to do it that suits your data and 2) think about the problem from a practical point of view - the median approach assumed static internet use but was that accurate? As it turned out it wasn't totally accurate. Investigating this led me to a conclusion that was the opposite of what I expected but did eventually lead to an improvement in my score. This would not have happened if I had not thought about the problem and questioned some basic assumptions. 

I am aware that the winning models are way more advanced than I what I did, but what I have described was enough for 30th place and a silver medal. Good luck with your next competition!
