# #8: Nine essays are enough

Competition: llms-you-cant-please-them-all
Rank: #8
Source: https://www.kaggle.com/c/llms-you-cant-please-them-all/discussion/566333

One of the best-kept secrets in this competition is that you don't need a thousand different essays. If you repeat the same nine essays over and over, the similarity score is near 1/9, which is way below the required 0.2. Working with fixed essays simplifies the game massively: The publicly available schemes which combine a hundred random words with a trigger phrase exhibit high variance because some sequences of random words are good and others aren't. By submitting combinations of only three fixed essays one can easily discern the good ones.

Considering that all judges construct their llm prompts as a function of the topic and the fixed essay (`prompt = f(topic, essay)`), the scores still can vary depending on the topic. It's possible that the same essay gives 990 for 97 % of the topics and 992 for the other 3 %. My strategy to reduce the dependence on the topic was to inject fixed override topics into some essays. If the essay repeats a fixed topic three times, the llm can get confused and forget the judge's real topic which is contained in the prompt only once.
