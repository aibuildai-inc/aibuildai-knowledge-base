### Introduction

In this section, I describe the overview and details of my solution (klog).


### Overview (What Worked)

My core idea was that the score could be improved by following three steps:
1. Arrange trees in a regular tiling structure  
2. Add the remaining trees around the tiling  
3. Compress the entire configuration as much as possible  
This approach is similar to how objects are packed in the real world when trying to fit them into a box as efficiently as possible.

I mainly focused on improving the score for large values of N.  
However, this solution did not work well for smaller N in many cases, and those cases were significantly improved thanks to my teammate’s approach.

Based on this idea, I divided my solution into the following two parts.


#### Tiling Part

First, I constructed a square-based tiling of trees.  
Then, I treated the entire square tiling as a single object and placed additional trees around it using **Sparrow**.

Compared to placing trees randomly around the tiling, using Sparrow was clearly more efficient and resulted in much better placement quality.

#### Simulated Annealing (SA) Part

To further improve the solutions generated by the tiling and Sparrow steps, I applied Simulated Annealing.  
Although I experimented with several custom SA implementations, I most often relied on a publicly available SA notebook.
[santa-2025-ensemble-sa-greedy-backtracking-tes](https://www.kaggle.com/code/blueshyy/santa-2025-ensemble-sa-greedy-backtracking-tes)

(To: @blueshyy , @wyz114514 Thank you for sharing such a high-quality SA implementation.)

One key feature of this SA is the introduction of a gravity-like force toward the center, which biases transitions toward more compact configurations.  This design made it easier to achieve score improvements in practice.
For detailed explanations, I strongly recommend checking the linked notebook and giving it an upvote.


### What Did Not Work

I also explored the following ideas, but was not able to successfully implement them.

- Non-square tiling structures
  Looking at higher-ranking solutions, many teams established strategies that used diagonally oriented tilings and then added trees around them.　Unfortunately, I was not able to develop this approach to a sufficient level.

- Optimizing the placement of surrounding trees
  In our case, the surrounding trees were added in a mostly random manner.  
  In contrast, top teams designed more sophisticated methods to explicitly optimize this placement.
