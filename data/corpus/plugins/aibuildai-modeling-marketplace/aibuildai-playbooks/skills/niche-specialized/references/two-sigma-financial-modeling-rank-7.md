# E2, Public LB #15, Private LB #8

Competition: two-sigma-financial-modeling
Rank: #7
Source: https://www.kaggle.com/c/two-sigma-financial-modeling/discussion/29793

This was actually our first Kaggle competition and therefore we are not really disappointed to miss the price by smallest margin. I should also add that, we have spent majority of the competition time to learn the basics of the new Kaggle environment and especially to setup ours and only heavily concentrated solving the problem at hand in the last few weeks. That being said, we both previously spent some healthy amounts of time on machine learning during our PhD studies and later in our professional lives. One of us also spent majority of the last decade developing and trading quantitative strategies. Therefore we were probably not the most naive and uninitiated when it comes to solving a data science problem in financial time series domain. 

So how we attacked it? Looking at the characteristics of the y's to be forecasted, it was clear that some heavy normalization was in place from the beginning. After playing with a few commonly employed methods to normalize time-series returns, like using rolling volatility (aka standard deviations) or cross-sectional normalizations, we came to the conclusion that it was a mixture and not straightforward to reverse-engineer. Adding to this the opaque nature of other indicators, i.e. we didn't know what those are, if they were also normalized, frequencies they were operating etc., the more traditional approaches were out of the window. And like quite a few other successful participants did, we also decided to start with a generic learner therefore. We selected XGB, due to its robustness in terms of feature handling and the wide model complexity range it can target out of the box. The efficient and fast implementation that yielded to test even larger models quickly was certainly another point which helped us to decide to start here instead of some more universal function approximators like neural networks for example. 

One of the intuitions we had early was to train a relatively strong model and overfit to the training and validation data to some degree, with the hope of almost guaranteeing not to miss any genuinely useful feature. As the signal to noise ratio in financial time series is famously low, every bit of useful information counts. We also thought that a strong learner would have a higher chance to capture a potentially leaking (it is hard to fully normalize things if you are doing normalizations across different dimensions at the same time as we hypothesized) return based information. The nature of financial data makes it hard to learn a massively wrongly biased model once enough data and regularization employed (typically you may not learn anything useful though) and thus we want to control at least the model variance risk, which we believe at the end proven itself useful by providing stable scores by public and private leaderboards for our submissions.

After some feature engineering via adding first and second order differences as well as some interaction terms and cross sectionally normalized forms of input features, we played with various cross-validation scenarios and hand-picked a largish set of features that gave us first good score in public LB by a single XGB model.

As the next step, since we already had an overfitted model we want to extend the ensemble with a very simple heuristic based approach. Here domain knowledge what to expect from financial time series certainly helped. At the end it was a model which was switching between some weak form of trend and mean reversion depending on the return dispersion at each timestamp. 

As the final addition, we came up with a model which bets only rarely to capture inflection points from market extremes. By forecasting a reversal after some extreme risk normalized performance of the complete universe,  this sub-strategy aimed to deliver a positively skewed return profile. One can think this strategy as an option bought for the cheap. That model helped in training and public leaderboard scoring significantly but contributed only marginally in the final private set. 

Those 3 models had different strengths and weaknesses and their ensemble proven to be a quite successful strategy with a stable public/private LB characteristic. The complete script took around 30 minutes to run, by majority of the computation time being spent on XGB model. We are not planning to make our code public at this moment but we hope to be able to contribute to the discussion of this very interesting and fun competition by this writing.
