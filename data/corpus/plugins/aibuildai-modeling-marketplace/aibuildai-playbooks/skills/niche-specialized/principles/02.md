## Principle 2: Exploit Mathematical or Physical Structure Directly

*Consensus: Universal in optimization competitions (all 11 Santa puzzles analyzed), physics (TrackML 1st/2nd/3rd), and explicit in adversarial challenges. Even ML-heavy winners (audio, fraud) decomposed their pipelines into structure-aware stages.*

Find the fundamental domain constraint or decomposition that reduces problem complexity by orders of magnitude. For puzzles: move sequences that preserve structure (3-rotations). For physics: geometric constraints (helix fitting, cluster decomposition). For fraud: business process structure (client lifecycle).

**Why this matters:** Brute-force search or pure ML hits exponential walls. Santa 2023's winner found 3-rotation sequences that swap only 3 pieces while leaving others fixed—this mathematical insight reduced a 10^100+ state space to tractable subproblems. TrackML's winner used helix fitting and outlier probability modeling instead of pure learning, cutting compute needs by 100x while achieving better accuracy. Structure exploitation makes previously intractable problems solvable.

**When to use:** When the problem has known invariants (permutation parity, conservation laws, geometric constraints), when brute-force enumeration is infeasible, when domain literature describes structural properties, or when simple moves/operations have complex global effects (puzzles, particle trajectories, chemical reactions).

**When NOT to use:** When the domain is truly unstructured (raw perceptual tasks like image aesthetics, sentiment), when exploiting structure requires inaccessible domain expertise and the competition timeline is short, or when structure-agnostic ML already achieves near-perfect scores (rare in niche competitions).

**How to reason about it:**
Ask: What is conserved or invariant? What decomposes the state space into independent subproblems? What constraints MUST be satisfied (parity, continuity, feasibility)? Read domain literature for established decompositions. For optimization: Can you solve special cases first, then extend? For physics: What are the fundamental trajectories/shapes? For business: What is the causal process? Implement structure-aware stages BEFORE end-to-end learning.
