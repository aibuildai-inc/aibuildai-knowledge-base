## Principle 3: Validate Against the Actual Test Condition, Not Convenience Proxies

*Consensus: Explicitly mentioned in 8/10 top solutions across all domains. BirdCLEF winners all used sliced-then-max validation, fraud winners validated on unseen clients.*

Your validation split and metric must mirror the exact inference scenario, including data format, aggregation strategy, and distribution shift. Do not validate on a random split if test has entity-based holdout. Do not validate on full clips if inference uses sliding windows.

**Why this matters:** Validation that doesn't match test conditions gives misleading signals, encourages overfitting to training distribution, and causes shakeup. BirdCLEF inference scored 5-second windows with max-pooling across a soundscape; winners who validated by randomly splitting full clips got false confidence. Fraud winners who used time-based CV (not client-based) overfit to training clients. The gap between convenience CV and real test explains most major shakeups.

**When to use:** Always. This is not optional. Before writing your first model, understand the test format: How is data grouped? What aggregation happens at scoring time? What distribution shift exists between train and test?

**When NOT to use:** Never skip this. Even in simple IID tasks, you still validate the same way you score.

**How to reason about it:**
Read the evaluation section carefully. If test data is grouped (clients, soundscapes, events): validate on unseen groups, not random rows. If scoring involves aggregation (max, voting, averaging over time/space): replicate that aggregation in validation. If there's a stated distribution shift (new species, new time period, new clients): split validation to simulate it. Build your validation ONCE, early, and trust it over public LB.

```python
# BirdCLEF example: slice train clips to match test inference\ndef validate_birdclef(model, val_soundscapes):\n    all_preds = []\n    for soundscape in val_soundscapes:\n        # Slice into 5-second windows just like test\n        windows = slice_audio(soundscape, window_size=5, hop=5)\n        window_preds = [model(w) for w in windows]\n        # Max-pool across windows (matches scoring logic)\n        soundscape_pred = np.max(window_preds, axis=0)\n        all_preds.append(soundscape_pred)\n    return compute_metric(all_preds, val_labels)
```
