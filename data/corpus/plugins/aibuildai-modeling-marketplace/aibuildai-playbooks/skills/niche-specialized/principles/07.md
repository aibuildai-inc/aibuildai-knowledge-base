## Principle 7: Use Domain-Aware Augmentation, Not Generic Transforms

*Consensus: Present in most audio solutions (mixup, background noise), 3/3 image-based niche tasks (particle detection used geometric transforms respecting detector symmetry), and even fraud (time-jittering within client sessions).*

Augmentations should reflect realistic variation in the domain, not arbitrary perturbations. For audio: mix waveforms and add environmental noise. For particle physics: rotate within detector symmetry groups. For fraud: jitter timestamps within plausible windows.

**Why this matters:** Generic augmentations (random crops, color jitter, flips) often violate domain invariants and inject noise. Domain-aware augmentations expose the model to realistic variation it will see at test time. BirdCLEF winners mixed waveforms and added environmental background noise from past competitions, simulating the acoustic conditions in test soundscapes. Random pitch-shifting or time-stretching DIDN'T work because they violated biological constraints on bird calls.

**When to use:** When you have domain knowledge about realistic variation (environmental noise, sensor perturbations, user behavior patterns), when baseline augmentations fail to improve or actively hurt performance, or when test data includes known sources of variation (different recording conditions, different sensor calibrations, different time periods).

**When NOT to use:** When domain knowledge is unavailable and you're doing pure exploration, when the domain is so controlled that all test variation is already in training (rare), or when augmentations introduce label noise (e.g., pitch-shifting a short spoken utterance changes its identity).

**How to reason about it:**
Ask: What varies in the real world but is missing from my training data? What perturbations preserve the label? What transforms would a domain expert recognize as realistic? For audio: add noise, not pitch-shift (preserves identity). For physics: apply detector symmetries, not random rotations. For fraud: jitter times, not shuffle transaction order. Test each augmentation in isolation; drop anything that hurts validation.
