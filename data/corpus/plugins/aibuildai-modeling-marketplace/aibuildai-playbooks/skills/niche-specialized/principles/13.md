## Principle 13: Invest in Problem Understanding Before Scaling Compute

*Consensus: Stated or implied by 9/10 top solutions. TrackML explicitly noted compute didn't help; fraud winners spent weeks on EDA before modeling; audio winners spent time on class analysis and threshold strategy.*

Allocate significant time to understanding the data, evaluation metric, and domain structure before training large models or ensembles. The highest-ROI activities are often non-ML: EDA, metric analysis, validation design, and reading domain literature.

**Why this matters:** Misunderstanding the problem leads to wasted compute on the wrong objective. Winners spent days or weeks on: understanding client vs transaction (fraud), analyzing per-class distributions (audio), studying permutation group theory (Santa), reading particle physics papers (TrackML). This upfront investment paid off 10x by preventing dead ends. Conversely, jumping to modeling without understanding leads to overfitting, poor validation, and misaligned objectives.

**When to use:** At the start of every competition, before writing your first model. When stuck or plateauing (return to EDA). When validation and LB diverge (re-examine your understanding of the evaluation).

**When NOT to use:** Never skip this entirely. Even in familiar domains, each competition has unique quirks.

**How to reason about it:**
Spend the first 20% of competition time on: (1) EDA—plot distributions, check for leakage, identify entity structure, (2) read evaluation carefully—what is actually being scored? what aggregation happens? (3) read domain literature or past solutions—what structure exists? (4) design validation—does it match test conditions? Only then start modeling. If you find yourself training 10 models with no improvement, STOP and return to understanding.
