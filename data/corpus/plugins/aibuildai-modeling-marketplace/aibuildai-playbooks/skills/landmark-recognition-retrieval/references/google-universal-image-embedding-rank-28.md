# 28th Place Solution

Competition: google-universal-image-embedding
Rank: #28
Source: https://www.kaggle.com/c/google-universal-image-embedding/discussion/359618

First of all we would like to thank Kaggle / Google for a very interresting competition. Also many thanks to my teammate @mightyrains for the excellent cooperation and teamwork.

**Our solution**
Our solution consisted of Tensorflow training pipeline that ran on the Kaggle TPU's. We also used Colab Pro additionally because of the Kaggle 20 hours TPU limit. This proved to be a very good combination todo many experiments. 

We used the CLIP-ViT-H-14 model as backbone. As projection layer we use a 64 dense units linear layer that was trained with ArcFace (s = 30, m = 0.30). Augmentation consisted of the usual stuff like: Horizontal/vertical flips, rotations and randomly modifying saturation, brightness, hue and contrast.

The majority of training runs consisted of 30 to 40 epochs where each epoch consisted of 20% of all training data.

For datasets the core consisted of Products10K data and the Google Landmarks data. In our final solution we added some small datasets such as the Food Recognition, Stanford Cars dataset and the Best Artsworks of All Time. Looking at the private LB the Products10K and Landmarks on itself already seem to give a very good result. Depending on the training run the additional datasets could give a slight increase or decrease of the results. This is likely caused by the randomness in each training run.

For the Tensorflow CLIP VIT H-14 model we ran into the issue eventually that after submission it would error on compute resources used. We solved this in a simple way by exporting the weights from the projection layer and importing these into a similar setup PyTorch model that we used for Inference and submission.

We looked at setting up a complete training pipeline in PyTorch but with the easy way we could use the Tensorflow/Pytorch combination we now had we decided to keep it that way.

Our selected solutions scored 0.653 on the Private LB. Our best solution on the Private LB scored 0.658. 

**What didn't work / didn't seem to work / partially failed ;-)**
1. We had a hard time getting a good local validation strategy to work. There was to little consistency with the LB score. Because of little time available in the end we didn't further invest time in it.
2. We tried various datasets like Omni Benchmark and webscraped images. With embeddings generated by a base CLIP model we tried to create clusters of images on which to further train a CLIP model with the idea to further increase performance. In general this didn't work…although it is interresting to note that performance also not really dropped. Using clean and clear datasets with proper labels was the best.
3. We did some tests with ensembles of models. This didn't seem to work on LB….with hindsight this did work however. In the next competition this will defintely get more focus.
4. Early in the competition we already did experiments with also training the backbone. At that moment it didn't give us proper results so we didn't follow up on that. Reading the various other solutions where this was applied succesfully this is something we will retry with a few Late Submissions.

Overall we can look back at a very exciting and challenging competition which we enjoyed very much.
