## Principle 5: Freeze Backbone and Train Head First, Then Unfreeze with Stratified Learning Rates

*Consensus: Universal Embedding 1st/2nd/5th place, multiple landmark solutions. Standard for fine-tuning pretrained models.*

When fine-tuning a strong pretrained model (especially CLIP), first freeze the backbone and train only the metric learning head for 1-3 epochs, then unfreeze the backbone and train with backbone_lr = head_lr × 0.001 to 0.01. Use strong regularization (weight_decay=0.1) on backbone parameters.

**Why this matters:** Pretrained models from LAION-2B already encode powerful generic visual features. Immediate end-to-end training with high learning rates destroys these features, causing catastrophic forgetting. Training the head first allows it to align with the pretrained feature space. Stratified learning rates (much lower for backbone) let you adapt the pretrained features slightly while preserving their representational power. Without this, you often see training score improve but validation score collapse.

**When to use:** When using pretrained models from large-scale pretraining (CLIP, models trained on ImageNet-22K). When the pretrained model alone (frozen) already achieves >50% of your target metric—this signals the features are valuable. When you observe validation performance dropping when you unfreeze the backbone with normal learning rates.

**When NOT to use:** When using ImageNet-1K pretrained models on very different domains (medical, satellite), full fine-tuning often works better. When your dataset is much larger than pretraining data (rarely the case). When pretrained features are weak (frozen model scores <20% of target), you may need full training. Early competitions (2019) often trained from scratch on landmarks because strong vision-language pretraining didn't exist yet.

**How to reason about it:**
1. Test frozen backbone performance: train only the head and measure validation mAP. 2. If frozen score > 0.5 × target, use stratified LRs. 3. Start with lr_ratio = 0.01 (backbone_lr = head_lr × 0.01). 4. If backbone_lr = 0.001 × head_lr works better (2nd place universal embedding), the pretrained features are very strong. 5. Monitor both training and validation—if train goes up but validation down, lower the ratio further or increase weight_decay.

```python
# Stage 1: Freeze backbone, train head only
for param in model.backbone.parameters():
    param.requires_grad = False
for param in model.head.parameters():
    param.requires_grad = True

optimizer = torch.optim.AdamW(model.head.parameters(), lr=1e-3)
train(model, train_loader, epochs=2)

# Stage 2: Unfreeze backbone with stratified learning rates
for param in model.backbone.parameters():
    param.requires_grad = True

backbone_params = list(model.backbone.parameters())
head_params = list(model.head.parameters())

optimizer = torch.optim.AdamW([
    {'params': backbone_params, 'lr': 1e-5, 'weight_decay': 0.1},  # lr_ratio = 0.01
    {'params': head_params, 'lr': 1e-3, 'weight_decay': 0.01}
])

# Alternative: even lower ratio for very strong pretrained models
# {'params': backbone_params, 'lr': 1e-6, 'weight_decay': 0.1}  # ratio = 0.001
```
