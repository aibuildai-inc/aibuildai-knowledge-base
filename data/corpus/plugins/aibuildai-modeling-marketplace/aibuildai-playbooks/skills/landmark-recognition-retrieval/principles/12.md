## Principle 12: For Image Matching (SfM), Use Sparse Local Features with Match Filtering Over Dense Matchers

*Consensus: Top 3 image matching challenge 2024 all used ALIKED+LightGlue; dense matchers underperformed.*

For structure-from-motion tasks requiring pose estimation, use sparse keypoint detectors (ALIKED, SuperPoint) with learned matchers (LightGlue) on high-resolution images. Filter matches by thresholds on match count and use rotation-invariant matching. Dense matchers (LoFTR, DKM, RoMa) underperform due to poor repeatability across viewpoints.

**Why this matters:** SfM requires accurate correspondences across wide baselines and viewpoint changes. Sparse detectors find repeatable keypoints that can be matched across views. Dense matchers produce many correspondences but with low repeatability—the same pixel may not be detected in different views, breaking the fundamental assumption of multi-view geometry. Sparse features with geometric verification (RANSAC) are more robust.

**When to use:** When the task requires multi-view geometry: 3D reconstruction, camera pose estimation, structure-from-motion. When images have wide baselines, scale changes, or rotation. When you need repeatable features across viewpoint changes.

**When NOT to use:** For pure retrieval tasks where you only need similarity, not correspondences—use global descriptors instead (faster and more accurate). When images are nearly planar and baseline is small, dense matchers can work. When objects are textureless or transparent, feature detection fails; see transparent scene handling in 1st place IMC 2024.

**How to reason about it:**
1. Identify your task: need correspondences (SfM) or just similarity (retrieval)? 2. If SfM, start with ALIKED+LightGlue at high resolution (1280-2048px). 3. Extract keypoints on full image AND cropped overlapping regions (DBSCAN clustering to find dense regions). 4. Match with rotation TTA: rotate one image by 0°, 90°, 180°, 270° and keep rotation with most matches. 5. Filter by match count threshold (>30 for single detector, >100 for ensemble). 6. Apply RANSAC for geometric verification. 7. If scenes are transparent/textureless, use direct pose estimation with image ordering (TSP on optical flow).
