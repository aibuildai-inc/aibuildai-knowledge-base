## Principle 6: Pretrain Transformers on Pseudolabeled Domain Data When Foundation Models Underperform

*Consensus: 3/5 transformer solutions added domain pretraining; using only HuggingFace weights was suboptimal*

When your molecule type is underrepresented in public pretrained models (polymers, specialized proteins), generate pseudolabels on a large unlabeled dataset using your best ensemble, then pretrain a transformer on a ranking or regression task using those pseudolabels before finetuning on real labels.

**Why this matters:** Foundation models are pretrained on common molecules (PubChem, UniProt). If your domain differs (synthetic polymers, engineered proteins, RNA), transfer is weak. The 1st-place polymer solution pretrained on 50k pseudolabeled PI1M polymers using a ranking task, improving 0.004 LB over directly finetuning HuggingFace weights. This outperformed even polymer-specific PolyBERT, suggesting task-specific pretraining beats domain-specific pretraining when domains align poorly.

**When to use:** When: (1) you have >10k unlabeled molecules similar to your test set, (2) public pretrained models are not from your domain (e.g., PolyBERT for polymers but you have a narrow subtype), (3) you can generate reasonable pseudolabels (ensemble of diverse models with CV correlation >0.85). Pretrain on ranking task (pairwise comparison: which molecule has higher property?) because it's robust to label noise.

**When NOT to use:** Do NOT pretrain if your labeled data is large (>20k) - just train from scratch or finetune. Do NOT pretrain on poor pseudolabels (CV <0.8) - it degrades rather than helps. Do NOT use regression pretraining if labels are very noisy (ranking is more robust). The BELKA 1st-place tried SMILES→ECFP pretraining but it hurt - likely because ECFP prediction is too different from binding prediction.

**How to reason about it:**
Step 1: Train diverse ensemble on your small labeled set (transformers + GBDTs + 3D models). Step 2: Generate pseudolabels on large unlabeled set using ensemble. Step 3: Create ranking pairs: FOR each molecule pair (i,j), create training sample (SMILES_i, SMILES_j, label=1 if pseudo_i > pseudo_j else 0). Ignore pairs where |pseudo_i - pseudo_j| < threshold to reduce noise. Step 4: Pretrain transformer on binary classification of ranking pairs (multi-task across all properties). Step 5: Finetune on real labeled data. VERIFY: does pretrained model beat direct finetuning on your CV?
