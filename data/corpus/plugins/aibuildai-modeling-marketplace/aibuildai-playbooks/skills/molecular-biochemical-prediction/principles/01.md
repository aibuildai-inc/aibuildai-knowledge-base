## Principle 1: Choose Molecular Representation by Data Availability and Property Type

*Consensus: All top solutions prioritize this decision and match the representation to their data regime*

The optimal molecular representation depends on whether you have abundant unlabeled molecules for pretraining, need 3D information, or can rely on sequence alone. SMILES-based transformers dominate when pretrained models exist; graph methods win when 3D geometry matters and you lack pretraining data.

**Why this matters:** Different properties depend on different structural features. Geometry-dependent properties need precise 3D coordinates; binding affinity benefits from learned chemical semantics; stability prediction needs both sequence context and local 3D neighborhoods. The wrong representation cannot learn the right features no matter how much you tune.

**When to use:** Use SMILES + pretrained transformers (ChemBERTa, ModernBERT, ESM2) when: (1) pretrained models exist for your molecule type, (2) you have <10k labeled samples, (3) the property correlates with chemical function not just geometry. Use graph neural networks when: (1) you need rotational invariance, (2) 3D coordinates are available, (3) local geometric patterns matter (bond angles, distances). Use 3D models (Uni-Mol) when: (1) conformational flexibility matters, (2) you can generate or have 3D structures, (3) dataset is large enough (>5k).

**When NOT to use:** Do NOT use pretrained transformers if the domain vocabulary differs drastically (e.g., polymer SMILES with custom notation not in pretraining). Do NOT use pure graph methods if long-range sequence context matters (RNA secondary structure). Do NOT use 3D models on small datasets (<1k samples) - they overfit. The 1st-place polymer solution found general-purpose ModernBERT beat chemistry-specific ChemBERTa, suggesting domain pretraining helps less than model scale when transfer is good.

**How to reason about it:**
Ask: (1) Is there a pretrained model for molecules like mine? (2) Does my property depend on 3D geometry or chemical semantics? (3) How much labeled data do I have? IF pretrained model exists AND <10k samples → transformer. IF 3D coordinates available AND geometry-dependent property → GNN or 3D model. IF >50k samples AND no good pretrained model → train graph model from scratch. THEN consider hybrid: combine multiple representations (SMILES transformer + fingerprints + 3D descriptors) and let the model learn their relative importance.
