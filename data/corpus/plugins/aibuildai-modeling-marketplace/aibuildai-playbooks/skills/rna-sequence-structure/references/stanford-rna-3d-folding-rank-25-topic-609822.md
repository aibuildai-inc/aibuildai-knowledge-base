# My 25th Place Solution – Stanford RNA 3D Folding

First of all, I would like to thank Kaggle and the competition organizers for hosting such an exciting and challenging event.  
I finished 29th on the public leaderboard before the release of the hidden test set and 25th on the final private leaderboard.  
In this post, I would like to briefly share my approach.

---

## Overall Strategy
Since I participated solo with limited compute resources, time, and skills, I did not perform any fine-tuning.
Instead, my strategy focused on **how to select the most accurate structures** from multiple predictions generated by existing models.  

When the leaderboard was refreshed midway through the competition, my ranking remained relatively stable. This gave me confidence that this selection-based approach had some potential.

---

## Models Used
For the final submissions, I relied on **Proteinx**, **DRfold2**, and **Boltz**.  
Here is how I used each model:

---

### DRfold2
After the first leaderboard refresh, I identified 20 prediction sets generated with the `cfg_97` configuration that showed stable and relatively high scores.  
Due to inference time constraints, I reduced this to 12 sets for further processing.  

For each set:
- I performed clustering on the predicted structures.
- Within each cluster, I selected the structure with the lowest energy score as the representative.  
- Then, I calculated TM-scores between representatives.  

Finally, I included:
1. The representative with the **lowest energy**  
2. The representative **most structurally different** from it (lowest TM-score)  

The goal was to maintain diversity in the final predictions rather than relying solely on the lowest-energy structure.

---

### Boltz
For Boltz, I set `diffusion_samples=3` to generate three candidate structures.  
Among them, I selected the final prediction based on:
1. **complex_plddt** (highest priority)
2. **confidence_score** (tie-breaker when pLDDT was identical)  

I remember that using this selection approach led to a noticeable improvement in my scores.  

I repeated this process with two different seeds and selected the best structure from each.

---

### Proteinx
For Proteinx, I set `--sample_diffusion.N_sample=3` to generate three structures.  
Since pLDDT scores are available in the JSON output, I simply selected the **highest-scoring** structure for the final submission.
