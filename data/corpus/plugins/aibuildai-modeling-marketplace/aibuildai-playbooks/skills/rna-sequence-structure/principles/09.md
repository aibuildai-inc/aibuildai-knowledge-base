## Principle 9: Use Pretraining and Curriculum to Match the Real Generalization Gap

*Consensus: Multiple high-ranking solutions in every task family used pretrained RNA encoders, autoencoding/MLM, fine-tuned structure models, or length/quality curricula, but several writeups also reported neutral pretraining—making selection conditional rather than universal.*

Pretrain or fine-tune for the missing capability: sequence understanding, long-length exposure, RNA-specific representation, or 3D generation. Stage training from abundant/easy/noisy examples to scarce/hard/clean examples when direct optimization is unstable.

**Why this matters:** RNA labels are expensive, but unlabeled sequences and general biomolecular models are abundant. Pretraining helps only if its representation transfers to the target distribution; a curriculum can prevent noisy or long structures from destabilizing early training while still exposing the model before inference.

**When to use:** Use RNA language models or masked-token training when labeled data are small, and fine-tune pretrained 3D models when compute permits. Use length curricula when memory and optimization difficulty grow steeply with sequence length.

**When NOT to use:** Do not spend the budget on generic MLM when a strong pair prior and large supervised dataset already dominate; top Ribonanza teams reported mixed or negligible external-MLM gains. Do not fine-tune on temporally illegal structures or narrow public-target proxies that inflate leaderboard performance.

**How to reason about it:**
Define the hoped-for transfer before training. If the goal is length robustness, test a long holdout; if remote homology, test low-identity clusters; if 3D accuracy, test temporally legal structures. Stop when pretraining improves only the easy split or removes candidate diversity.

```python
stages = [
    {"data": unlabeled_rna, "objective": "masked_token", "steps": pretrain_steps},
    {"data": broad_labeled, "objective": "target_loss", "max_len": short_first},
    {"data": clean_labeled, "objective": "target_loss", "max_len": full_length},
]
for stage in stages:
    train_stage(model, **stage)  # validate on the intended shift after every stage
```
