## Principle 10: Treat Pseudo-Labels as Noisy Measurements, Not Ground Truth

*Consensus: 3/5 top Ribonanza solutions used confidence-filtered pseudo or corrected labels; other strong teams found unfiltered pseudo-labeling neutral or harmful.*

Generate pseudo-labels out of fold, keep only positions or structures with strong ensemble agreement, and return to real labels in a final fine-tuning stage.

**Why this matters:** Pseudo-labeling can expose models to hidden lengths and sequence families, but it also amplifies shared model bias and leaks folds if labels come from an ensemble containing the student’s validation fold. Confidence filtering and real-label recovery reduce this confirmation loop.

**When to use:** Use it when the unlabeled set represents a clear covariate shift, teacher ensembles are diverse, and per-position uncertainty or inter-model variance can be estimated.

**When NOT to use:** Avoid it when teachers are highly correlated, CV becomes contaminated, or a pseudo-labeled model only reproduces the ensemble without adding diversity. The Ribonanza winner found a stronger single model but no final ensemble gain, illustrating that pseudo-labeling is not automatically additive.

**How to reason about it:**
Build foldwise teacher predictions, estimate uncertainty, mask rather than force uncertain entries, mix pseudo-data briefly, and verify gains on untouched folds. Track both accuracy and prediction correlation: a small score gain can be harmful if it collapses ensemble diversity.

```python
teacher_mean = teacher_preds.mean(axis=0)
teacher_std = teacher_preds.std(axis=0)
confident = teacher_std < np.quantile(teacher_std, 0.75)
pseudo_target = np.where(confident, teacher_mean, np.nan)

# Safer schedule: real -> real+pseudo at low LR -> real-only recovery.
train(model, real_data)
train(model, mix(real_data, pseudo_target), lr=base_lr * 0.1)
train(model, real_data, lr=base_lr * 0.01)
```
