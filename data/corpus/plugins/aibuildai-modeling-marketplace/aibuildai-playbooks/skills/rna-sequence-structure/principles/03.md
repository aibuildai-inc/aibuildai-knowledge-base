## Principle 3: Use a Template–Neural Cascade for 3D Prediction

*Consensus: 8/9 reviewed leading 3D solutions used template-based modeling directly or used predicted structures as templates for neural refinement; the main alternative was an ensemble of fine-tuned neural predictors.*

Search known structures first, transfer coordinates through an RNA-aware alignment when a credible template exists, and reserve neural de novo prediction or template-conditioned refinement for missing, uncertain, or novel regions. A strong neural prediction can itself become a template for a second model.

**Why this matters:** RNA 3D data are scarce, while structural folds may remain conserved beyond obvious sequence identity. Templates provide a high-precision global scaffold; neural models fill the remote-homology and template-free gap, and refinement combines complementary inductive biases.

**When to use:** Use the cascade when a temporally legal PDB/template database is available, especially for medium or long RNAs with detectable homologs. Use embedding-based search, secondary-structure-aware alignment, or standard sequence alignment according to validation evidence.

**When NOT to use:** Do not let weak templates dominate short template-free targets or sequences with low alignment coverage. In that regime, allocate more predictions to independent neural models. Conversely, do not replace a near-identical template with a stochastic de novo prediction merely because the latter is more sophisticated.

**How to reason about it:**
Estimate template confidence from identity, coverage, alignment score, chain compatibility, and release date. High confidence implies direct transfer plus light refinement; medium confidence implies transfer plus neural refinement; low confidence implies neural generation with, at most, a weak template prior.

```python
def build_3d_candidates(query, template_db, neural_models):
    hits = search_templates(query, template_db)  # identity, coverage, chain match, date legality
    best = hits[0] if hits else None
    if best and best.confidence > 0.8:
        return transfer_and_refine(query, hits[:3], strength="light")
    if best and best.confidence > 0.5:
        seeds = transfer_and_refine(query, hits[:2], strength="moderate")
        return seeds + condition_neural_models(query, seeds, neural_models)
    return [m.predict(query) for m in neural_models]
```
