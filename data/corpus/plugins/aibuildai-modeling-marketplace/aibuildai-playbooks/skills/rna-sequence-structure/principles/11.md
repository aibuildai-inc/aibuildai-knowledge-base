## Principle 11: Optimize the Five 3D Outputs as a Portfolio

*Consensus: Every reviewed 3D solution was shaped by best-of-five scoring; top approaches mixed templates, models, seeds, MSA settings, or refinements rather than submitting five near-duplicates.*

Under best-of-five TM-score, allocate slots jointly to quality and structural diversity. Use template confidence, sequence length, and model applicability to decide how many slots go to TBM, de novo neural models, and template-conditioned refinements.

**Why this matters:** The metric takes a maximum, so the marginal value of a candidate depends on what is already in the set. A slightly weaker but genuinely different fold can increase expected score more than another high-confidence duplicate.

**When to use:** Use portfolio selection whenever evaluation chooses the best among multiple submitted conformations or references. Structural clustering, pairwise TM-distance, and model-family diversity are useful selection signals.

**When NOT to use:** Do not maximize diversity blindly: arbitrary hinge/noise perturbations can consume slots without representing plausible conformations. For a near-exact template, several template-derived candidates may be safer than forced neural diversity.

**How to reason about it:**
Estimate candidate quality and pairwise redundancy. Preserve at least one strongest candidate, then add candidates by marginal expected coverage. Increase neural/model-family diversity as template confidence falls; increase template allocation as identity and coverage rise.

```python
def select_portfolio(candidates, k=5, diversity_weight=0.25):
    chosen = [max(candidates, key=lambda c: c.quality)]
    while len(chosen) < k:
        def utility(c):
            novelty = min(structure_distance(c, x) for x in chosen)
            return c.quality + diversity_weight * novelty
        remaining = [c for c in candidates if c not in chosen]
        chosen.append(max(remaining, key=utility))
    return chosen
```
