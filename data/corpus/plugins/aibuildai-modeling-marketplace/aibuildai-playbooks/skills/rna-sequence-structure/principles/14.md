## Principle 14: Ensemble Profile Predictions by Error and Complementarity

*Consensus: Most Ribonanza writeups used multi-model blending, often across architectures, folds, feature sets, targets, or augmentations.*

For reactivity-profile tasks, average or robustly blend calibrated out-of-fold models, emphasizing complementary error patterns rather than collecting many replicas. Sequence-only and structure-aware models can be useful partners even when one is weaker alone.

**Why this matters:** MAE and RMSE benefit from variance reduction, while assay noise makes seed-level variance substantial. Architectural diversity captures different views of RNA: sequence-only models can resist folding-prior errors, while pair-aware models capture long-range protection effects.

**When to use:** Use blending after obtaining trustworthy out-of-fold predictions for every candidate. Include cross-target models when experimental outputs are correlated, as long as the submitted heads remain calibrated to their own metrics.

**When NOT to use:** Do not transfer this logic directly to 3D coordinates: coordinate averaging can destroy a fold, whereas scalar averaging remains in a valid output space. Avoid adding pseudo-labeled models that raise correlation without lowering ensemble error.

**How to reason about it:**
Measure each model’s OOF score, residual correlation by length and structural regime, and calibration near target bounds. Start with a simple mean, then optimize conservative nonnegative weights; use trimmed or median-like blends only if outlier predictions are common.

```python
def blend_profiles(oof_preds, test_preds, y, metric):
    # Fit weights only on untouched OOF predictions; constrain for robustness.
    w = fit_nonnegative_weights(oof_preds, y, metric=metric, sum_to_one=True)
    keep = prune_redundant_models(oof_preds, w, max_residual_corr=0.995)
    w = w[keep] / w[keep].sum()
    pred = sum(wi * test_preds[i] for wi, i in zip(w, keep))
    return np.clip(pred, 0.0, 1.0)  # customize bounds to the competition metric
```
