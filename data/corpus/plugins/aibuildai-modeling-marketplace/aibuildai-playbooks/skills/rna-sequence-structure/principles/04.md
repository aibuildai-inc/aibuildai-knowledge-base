## Principle 4: Design Positional Information to Extrapolate Beyond Training Lengths

*Consensus: 5/5 top Ribonanza solutions explicitly replaced or avoided fixed absolute position embeddings; length shift was also central in both 3D competitions.*

Prefer relative position bias, ALiBi-like schemes, clipped relative distances, or local convolutions over learned absolute position tables when test RNAs are longer than training RNAs. Preserve explicit end or chain-boundary information if terminal context matters.

**Why this matters:** An absolute embedding can bind a biochemical pattern to a training index and has undefined behavior at unseen positions. Relative geometry expresses reusable relationships such as separation along the backbone and therefore extrapolates more naturally.

**When to use:** Use extrapolating position schemes whenever train/test length distributions differ or long hidden targets are expected.

**When NOT to use:** Fixed absolute positions can be acceptable when sequence lengths and assay layout are genuinely fixed and index-specific technical effects dominate. Sliding windows are not a universal fix: they erase long-range pairings and performed poorly when the full RNA context determined the target.

**How to reason about it:**
Create a length holdout before selecting the positional design. Compare performance not only by sequence length but also by distance from sequence ends and by long-range contact separation. Prefer the simplest scheme whose degradation remains smooth beyond the training range.

```python
def relative_bucket(i, j, max_dist=64):
    # Signed clipped distance; replace with logarithmic buckets for very long RNA.
    d = (j - i).clamp(-max_dist, max_dist)
    return d + max_dist
```
