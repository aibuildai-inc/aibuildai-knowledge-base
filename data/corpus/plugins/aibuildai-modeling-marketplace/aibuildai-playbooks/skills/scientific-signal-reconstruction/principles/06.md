## Principle 6: Make Validation Reproduce Acquisition Groups, Difficulty Strata, and Scientific Checks

*Consensus: Both competitions required metric-specific validation; top writeups used large held-out subsets, family-wise scores, or supplied control datasets.*

Split on the highest-level unit that can share generation conditions, and report both the official aggregate and conditional metrics. Reproduce all hard scientific validity checks locally, and generate out-of-fold predictions for every learned stacker, router, or correction stage.

**Why this matters:** Random sample splits can leak simulator templates, file-level conditions, repeated scenes, or acquisition batches. Aggregate scores can also hide catastrophic failure on a rare regime. In constrained science tasks, a high predictive score is irrelevant if the submission fails agreement or decorrelation tests.

**When to use:** Always. Group by source file, simulation seed, or acquisition batch as appropriate; stratify diagnostics by family, noise level, and confidence.

**When NOT to use:** Do not make so many tiny folds that each fold misses rare regimes or becomes prohibitively expensive. For massive datasets, one or two large, representative held-out groups can be more reliable than conventional small random folds.

**How to reason about it:**
Build a validation table with rows for overall metric, each physical regime, each difficulty quantile, simulator-versus-real controls, and every submission gate. Require improvements to survive more than one group or seed, especially for routing and post-processing.
