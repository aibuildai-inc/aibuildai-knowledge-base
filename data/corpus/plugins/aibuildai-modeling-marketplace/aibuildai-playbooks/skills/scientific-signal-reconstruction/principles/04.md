## Principle 4: Promote a Trusted Forward Operator to a First-Class Model Component

*Consensus: 13/15 waveform writeups referenced forward modeling, simulation, or generated data; it was central to all top-6 waveform solutions. This is specialized but decisive when a usable operator exists.*

When a known forward map converts latent states into measurements, use it for synthetic pairing, cycle consistency, residual features, pseudo-label adaptation, or differentiable test-time refinement. A learned inverse provides a strong initialization; the operator then checks whether that estimate could have produced the observed signal.

**Why this matters:** Supervised inversion alone can exploit dataset shortcuts and remains underconstrained. The forward residual supplies sample-specific physical information and allows unlabeled test measurements to participate without inventing target labels independently of the acquisition process.

**When to use:** Use when the operator is sufficiently faithful, differentiable or cheaply callable, and fast enough for the intended role. Start with offline generation; progress to residual-conditioned correction or optimization only if the extra complexity produces robust validation gains.

**When NOT to use:** Do not make a slow or mismatched simulator the training bottleneck. If the simulator omits real noise, calibration effects, or implementation quirks, aggressive cycle fitting can move predictions away from truth. In that case use it as a weak regularizer or diagnostic rather than an oracle.

**How to reason about it:**
Apply a hierarchy: (1) compare simulated and real measurements, (2) augment latent states and generate paired observations, (3) train a second stage on forward residuals, (4) attempt differentiable refinement from a learned initialization. Stop at the simplest level that improves held-out real data.

# Generic residual-correction pattern; customize F, normalizations, and loss.

    y0 = inverse_model(x)
    x0 = forward_operator(y0)
