## Principle 13: Ensemble for Error Diversity, Then Learn Routing Only if Disagreement Is Predictable

*Consensus: Ensembling, blending, stacking, or model selection is common among the source writeups; the largest gains came from family-aware waveform blends.*

Select ensemble members by out-of-fold error complementarity, not standalone rank or model count. Begin with metric-aware linear blending; use a small selector or stacker only when sample features and model confidence reliably predict which member is better.

**Why this matters:** Architectures fail differently: convolutional decoders, global transformers, and physics refiners have distinct biases. However, correlated checkpoints add little, and flexible meta-models can overfit subtle validation structure.

**When to use:** Use when residual correlations are low, models disagree substantially, and validation is large enough to fit weights. Predicted confidence, regime probability, and inter-model disagreement can support routing.

**When NOT to use:** Skip a large ensemble when one model dominates every stratum or runtime is constrained. One waveform solution saw its ensembling gain saturate and concluded that further effort belonged in single-model quality.

**How to reason about it:**
Measure the oracle-selector gain and pairwise residual correlation. Add the model with the best marginal improvement, refit weights on out-of-fold predictions under the exact metric, and validate the whole selection procedure on a second holdout. Prefer a shallow router with large leaves or strong regularization.
