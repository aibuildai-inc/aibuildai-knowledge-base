## Principle 9: Audit Simulator Fidelity and Keep an Anchor to Real Measurements

*Consensus: Both competitions exposed simulator or acquisition-domain artifacts: waveform dtype/boundary mismatches, and Flavours of Physics real-versus-simulated agreement checks.*

Measure the domain gap between simulated and observed data at every stage, and retain real training examples or control samples as an anchor. Treat suspiciously predictive generation artifacts as risks, not free signal.

**Why this matters:** A high-capacity inverse can learn simulator fingerprints instead of physics. Small implementation details such as precision, boundary rows, or source conventions can dominate at competition scale and fail under true deployment or a shifted hidden set.

**When to use:** Use whenever any labels, signals, or rare classes are simulated, pseudo-labeled, or forward-generated. Compare low-level statistics, model embeddings, residual spectra, and prediction distributions between domains.

**When NOT to use:** A full simulator-domain audit is less important when all training and test data are real measurements with matched acquisition, though train/test acquisition shifts still deserve checks.

**How to reason about it:**
Train a domain classifier between real and simulated measurements. If it succeeds, inspect which features drive separation and whether the reconstruction model uses them. Mix synthetic data with real anchors, downweight mismatched regions, or improve the simulator. For scientific classifiers, directly include agreement and nuisance-correlation diagnostics in model selection.

# A simple domain-gap diagnostic.
X = np.concatenate([real_features, simulated_features])
domain = np.r_[np.zeros(len(real_features)), np.ones(len(simulated_features))]
auc = cross_validated_domain_auc(X, domain)
if auc > 0.7:  # customize threshold and inspect feature attribution
    investigate_generation_artifacts()
    synthetic_weight *= 0.5
