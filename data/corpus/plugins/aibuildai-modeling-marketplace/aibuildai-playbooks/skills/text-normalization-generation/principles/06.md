## Principle 6: Apply Two-Stage Training When Distribution Shift Exists

*Consensus: Several feedback-prize-english-language-learning solutions pretrained on pseudo-labeled data from earlier Feedback competitions, then fine-tuned on the target set (one gained 0.002-0.005 CV); a Deep Past translation solution found continual pretraining then fine-tuning consistently beat direct fine-tuning*

When training data contains both a broader or older distribution (e.g., an earlier related corpus, or pseudo-labels from a previous competition) and a target-distribution subset, use two-stage training: Stage 1 pretrain on old data (+ optional external data), Stage 2 finetune on new data. Evaluate on new-distribution-only CV, save best model from stage 2.

**Why this matters:** Models trained jointly on old + new data compromise—they average the two distributions and underperform on new. One feedback-prize-english-language-learning top solution used two rounds of pseudo-labeling: stage 1 trained on pseudo-labeled data from an earlier, related competition, and stage 2 fine-tuned on the actual train set with a smaller learning rate, improving CV by 0.002-0.005. Pretrain → finetune lets the model learn general writing-quality understanding from abundant related data, then specialize to target criteria with scarce new data.

**When to use:** Use when: (a) Train data has identifiable subsets from different distributions (different time periods, corpora, or scoring criteria), (b) Test distribution matches the smaller, newer subset, (c) You've verified via EDA that score distributions differ across subsets. Example: a large pseudo-labeled corpus from an earlier competition plus a smaller target train set → pretrain on the corpus, finetune on the target set.

**When NOT to use:** Skip two-stage when: (a) No distribution shift exists (same corpus, time period, rubric), (b) Subsets are too small for meaningful pretraining (e.g., 500 old + 500 new → just use standard CV), (c) You lack compute for two training passes.

**How to reason about it:**
Step 1: Partition data into old and new subsets. Step 2: Pretrain on old data for 2-4 epochs with standard CV on old data. Step 3: Load best pretrain checkpoint, finetune on new data for 2-3 epochs, evaluate on new-data-only CV. Step 4: Compare against single-stage baseline (train on all data jointly)—two-stage should improve CV on new distribution. Advanced: Use soft labels from stage 1 OOF predictions on new data during stage 2 to blend old and new knowledge.

```python
# Stage 1: Pretrain on old distribution
model.fit(old_train_data, epochs=3, lr=5e-6, eval_on=old_val_data)
best_pretrain_ckpt = save_best_model(model)

# Stage 2: Finetune on new distribution
model.load(best_pretrain_ckpt)
model.fit(new_train_data, epochs=2, lr=2e-6, eval_on=new_val_data)
# Evaluate on new_val_data only, ignore old data performance
```
