## Principle 2: Diagnose Train-Test Distribution Shift Early via Prompt/Score Analysis

*Consensus: Several commonlit-evaluate-student-summaries top solutions analyzed prompt-level differences between training, public, and private data*

Writing assessment competitions often have different prompts, time periods, or scoring criteria between train and test. Analyzing prompt diversity, score distributions per prompt, and metadata (grade level, corpus source) reveals whether test distribution matches train—critical for choosing CV strategy and deciding whether to apply domain adaptation.

**Why this matters:** Train data with only a handful of prompts but a hidden test set drawn from unseen prompts creates massive domain shift. Models that memorize prompt-specific patterns fail. One commonlit-evaluate-student-summaries top solution found that prompt text lengths and the number of summaries per prompt differed sharply between the training/public data and the private data, which caused a shake-down for many teams. Similarly, when training data mixes corpora (for example, labels from an earlier related competition and the target data), even good models underperform on the target. Pretraining on the broader data and then fine-tuning on the target data is a common fix.

**When to use:** Always check: (a) Number of unique prompts/topics in train vs. expected in test, (b) Score distribution per prompt—do same prompts have different distributions in different data sources? (c) Temporal metadata—were train and test essays written in different eras? (d) Source corpus overlap—does test come from a different dataset than train?

**When NOT to use:** Skip detailed distribution analysis when: (a) Task is a deterministic text transformation with no subjective scoring, (b) Train and test come from same time period and corpus with confirmed distribution match, (c) Competition explicitly states same distribution.

**How to reason about it:**
Step 1: Plot score distributions per prompt/topic. If same prompt has different distributions in different subsets → scoring criteria changed. Step 2: Check prompt overlap—if train has prompts A,B,C,D and test has 100+ unseen prompts → generalization to new topics is critical. Step 3: If distribution shift exists: use two-stage training (pretrain on the broader distribution, finetune on the target distribution) or focus CV on the smaller, newer-distribution subset and treat old data as auxiliary. Step 4: For CV, stratify by the shift dimension (prompt_id + score, not just score alone).
