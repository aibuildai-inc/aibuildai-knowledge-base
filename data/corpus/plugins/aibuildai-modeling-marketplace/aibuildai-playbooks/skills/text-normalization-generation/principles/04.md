## Principle 4: Use DeBERTa as Default Backbone for Writing Assessment

*Consensus: Most writing assessment solutions use DeBERTa (v2/v3, base/large/xlarge), including nearly all top-5 solutions*

For writing assessment tasks, DeBERTa (especially v3-large) consistently outperforms RoBERTa, BERT, ELECTRA, and ALBERT in both single-model performance and ensemble contribution. DeBERTa-v3-base offers a strong speed-accuracy tradeoff; DeBERTa-v3-large is the top choice when compute allows.

**Why this matters:** DeBERTa's disentangled attention and enhanced mask decoder make it superior for understanding nuanced text, critical for evaluating writing quality. Across competitions, RoBERTa-large typically scores 0.005-0.01 worse than DeBERTa-large. Trying other backbones wastes time—multiple top solutions report 'other models did not improve ensemble.'

**When to use:** Default to DeBERTa-v3-large for accuracy-focused tracks. Use DeBERTa-v3-base when: (a) runtime is constrained (efficiency track), (b) you need to ensemble 10+ models within time limits. Use DeBERTa-v2-xlarge only if you have proven it beats v3-large on your specific task (rare).

**When NOT to use:** Avoid DeBERTa for: (a) rule-based transformation steps (unnecessary where rules are exact), (b) when you have < 8GB GPU RAM and can't fit even base model, (c) non-English tasks where a language-specific model exists (though DeBERTa multilingual can work).

**How to reason about it:**
Start with DeBERTa-v3-base if exploring or under time pressure. Once you have a working pipeline, upgrade to DeBERTa-v3-large—it typically gains 0.01-0.02. For ensemble diversity, vary: (a) pooling strategy (CLS, mean, attention, LSTM), (b) max length (768, 1024, 1536), (c) layer freezing, (d) auxiliary losses—not the backbone. Adding non-DeBERTa models (ELECTRA, RoBERTa) for diversity rarely helps unless they use fundamentally different inputs or architectures.
