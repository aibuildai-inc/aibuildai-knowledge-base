## Principle 14: Use MLM Pretraining on Domain Data to Adapt Backbones

*Consensus: Several solutions use MLM or Span MLM on essay data before fine-tuning*

Before fine-tuning on the target task, continue pretraining the transformer backbone with Masked Language Modeling (MLM) on domain-specific data (student essays, prior competition data). Use higher masking rate (40-50% vs. standard 15%) and span masking (mask contiguous 3-15 tokens) for better adaptation.

**Why this matters:** Pretrained models are trained on formal text (Wikipedia, books). Student essays have informal language, grammar errors, and domain-specific vocabulary. MLM adaptation teaches the model this distribution. The 3rd-place effectiveness solution used Span MLM with 40% masking on 15k essays and gained 0.02-0.03. Custom tokens (e.g., [LEAD], [POSITION]) also get trained during MLM so they're not random embeddings during fine-tuning.

**When to use:** Use when: (a) Domain data is available (prior competition essays, public corpora), (b) Target domain differs from pretraining corpus (student writing vs. Wikipedia), (c) You have compute for extra pretraining (typically 1-2 epochs on domain data). Use span masking when average text length > 100 tokens (captures phrasal patterns).

**When NOT to use:** Skip MLM when: (a) Domain data < 5k samples (insufficient for meaningful adaptation), (b) Target domain is similar to pretraining corpus (formal essays, news articles), (c) Time-constrained and direct fine-tuning works well.

**How to reason about it:**
Collect domain data: prior competition essays, public datasets (Persuade corpus, Feedback Prize), LLM-generated essays. Decide masking strategy: 15% (standard) or 40-50% (aggressive, better for small domain data). Use span masking (mask 3-15 contiguous tokens) to learn phrasal patterns. Pretrain for 1-2 epochs on domain data, save checkpoint. Fine-tune from this checkpoint on target task. Validate: does MLM pretraining improve CV? Typical gain: 0.01-0.03.
