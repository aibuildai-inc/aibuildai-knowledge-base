## Principle 3: Establish CV-LB Correlation Before Trusting Optimization

*Consensus: Several top linking-writing-processes-to-writing-quality solutions emphasize trusting a well-correlated CV over the public leaderboard as critical*

Before optimizing hyperparameters or ensemble weights, verify that cross-validation improvements translate to leaderboard improvements. Use stratified k-fold on the distribution shift dimension (e.g., prompt_id + score). Track every experiment's CV vs. LB—if correlation breaks, your CV strategy is wrong and optimization will overfit.

**Why this matters:** Without CV-LB correlation, you're optimizing a proxy that doesn't predict test performance. The 1st-place linking-writing-processes-to-writing-quality solution showed the public LB score distribution matched the train set and trusted CV throughout, even when its LB scores ran consistently worse than CV—this let it avoid overfitting to the public leaderboard. Broken correlation means your folds don't represent test distribution, often due to improper stratification or data leakage.

**When to use:** Always for writing assessment tasks. Establish correlation in first week: train 3-5 models with different approaches, submit each, plot CV vs. LB. If Pearson correlation < 0.8, revisit fold strategy. For deterministic transformation tasks with a clear train-test match, CV alone may suffice.

**When NOT to use:** Don't obsess over perfect correlation when: (a) LB is < 1% of data (too noisy), (b) Task is deterministic rule-based, (c) You have confirmed no distribution shift and standard k-fold works.

**How to reason about it:**
Questions to ask: (1) Do my folds stratify on the right dimensions? For writing assessment tasks, stratify by prompt_id + score, not score alone—otherwise a fold may lack a critical prompt. (2) Is there data leakage? Same text appearing in train and validation breaks correlation. (3) Is LB size adequate? If LB is < 1%, random noise dominates. (4) Are my models diverse enough to test correlation? If all models are near-identical, correlation can be false. Fix: Use MultilabelStratifiedKFold on [prompt_id, score]. If shift exists, create folds within each distribution subset separately.
