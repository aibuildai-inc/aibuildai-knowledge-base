## Principle 7: Ensemble Diverse Models: Different Backbones, Resolutions, and Pretraining Sources

*Consensus: 1st place Happy Whale ensembled around 50 models; most other top solutions also ensembled models with different backbones or input resolutions.*

Fine-grained features are subtle and different architectures capture different patterns. Ensemble models trained with different backbones (CNN vs Transformer), resolutions (512 vs 768 vs 1024), and pretraining (ImageNet vs domain-specific) to maximize diversity.

**Why this matters:** A single model may latch onto spurious correlations (background, lighting). Ensembling diverse models averages out these biases. CNNs excel at local textures; Transformers capture global structure. High-resolution models see fine details; lower-resolution models are more robust to noise. Different pretraining initializes feature detectors differently.

**When to use:** Always ensemble for final submissions. Train 3-10 models varying: backbone (EfficientNet, ResNet, ViT, Swin), resolution (384, 512, 768, 1024), pretraining source (ImageNet, NoisyStudent, a domain-specific checkpoint). Weight ensemble by validation performance or optimize weights with a held-out set.

**When NOT to use:** Do not ensemble near-identical models (same backbone, same resolution, different random seeds) unless you have >20 of them (marginal gains). Do not ensemble models trained on different data splits without accounting for overfitting (e.g., k-fold models should predict on their out-of-fold test samples for diversity).

**How to reason about it:**
Train your best single model. Then vary one dimension at a time: (1) change backbone (if EfficientNet-B5, try ResNet101, ViT-B/16, Swin-L), (2) change resolution (if 512, try 768 and 1024), (3) change pretraining (if ImageNet, try NoisyStudent or a domain-specific checkpoint). For each variation, train until convergence (20-50 epochs) and validate. Keep models with validation score within 1-2% of the best (discard clearly worse ones). At inference, average predictions (for classification) or concatenate embeddings (for metric learning) from all kept models. Optionally, optimize ensemble weights with scipy.optimize on a held-out set.

# Train diverse models

# Inference: ensemble predictions
def ensemble_predict(models, test_loader):
    all_preds = []
    for model in models:
        model.eval()
        preds = model.predict(test_loader)  # (N, n_classes)
        all_preds.append(preds)
    # Simple averaging
    ensemble_preds = torch.stack(all_preds).mean(dim=0)
    return ensemble_preds
