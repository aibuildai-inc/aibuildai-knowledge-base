## Principle 13: Choose Test-Time Augmentation Based on Medical Imaging Physics

*Consensus: 30+ solutions used TTA; specific augmentations varied by modality*

Apply test-time augmentation (TTA) selectively based on imaging physics and anatomy. Horizontal flip is usually safe for roughly left-right symmetric anatomy such as head and chest CT slices; vertical flip rarely makes sense for anatomical images. Full rotation and flips are valid for microscopy and histopathology but not for anatomy with an intrinsic orientation.

**Why this matters:** Medical images have anatomical constraints that natural images don't. Flipping a CT slice vertically creates an impossible anatomy, and flipping an image whose labels name the left or right side silently swaps the answer. Physics-invalid augmentations add noise instead of averaging out variance.

**When to use:** Use horizontal flip for roughly symmetric anatomy (head and chest CT slices). Use 90-degree rotations and flips for rotation-invariant images (histopathology tiles, cellular microscopy). Avoid orientation-changing TTA where left and right or top and bottom carry diagnostic meaning, or swap the side-specific outputs after flipping.

**When NOT to use:** When anatomy has intrinsic orientation that shouldn't be violated. When inference time is severely constrained and single-pass prediction suffices. When validation shows TTA doesn't help; one skin-lesion (ISIC 2024) winning solution found that averaging predictions over augmented samples gave no gain.

**How to reason about it:**
Identify anatomical constraints: Is anatomy roughly symmetric across the midline? → Horizontal flip valid. Is orientation arbitrary (sample on slide, cells in a well)? → Full rotation valid. Does gravity or patient position matter? → Vertical flip invalid. Do labels name a side? → Flip only with a matching swap of the side-specific outputs. For histopathology and cellular microscopy: horizontal and vertical flips plus 90-degree rotations (8-way TTA). For CT slices: horizontal flip only. Average predictions: simple mean usually works; no need for complex aggregation.

```python
# TTA by modality
# Histopathology / cellular microscopy: flips and 90-degree rotations
tta_transforms = [
    lambda x: x,  # Original
    lambda x: torch.flip(x, dims=[-1]),  # H-flip
    lambda x: torch.flip(x, dims=[-2]),  # V-flip
    lambda x: torch.flip(x, dims=[-1, -2]),  # H+V flip
    lambda x: torch.rot90(x, k=1, dims=[-2, -1]),  # Rotate 90
    lambda x: torch.rot90(x, k=2, dims=[-2, -1]),  # Rotate 180
    lambda x: torch.rot90(x, k=3, dims=[-2, -1]),  # Rotate 270
]
preds = [model(aug(image)) for aug in tta_transforms]
final_pred = torch.stack(preds).mean(dim=0)

# CT slice: H-flip only (V-flip is anatomically invalid)
pred_orig = model(image)
pred_hflip = model(torch.flip(image, dims=[-1]))
final_pred = (pred_orig + pred_hflip) / 2

# Side-specific labels: flip, then swap the left and right outputs back
pred_flip = swap_left_right(model(torch.flip(image, dims=[-1])))
final_pred = (model(image) + pred_flip) / 2
```
