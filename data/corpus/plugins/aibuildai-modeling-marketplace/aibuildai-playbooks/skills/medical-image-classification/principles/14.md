## Principle 14: Train for Fewer Epochs with Heavy Regularization to Combat Overfitting

*Consensus: Several solutions trained for only a few epochs, and some reported that longer training made results worse*

Medical datasets are small and models overfit quickly. Use aggressive regularization (high classifier dropout, stochastic depth, heavy augmentation) and train for only a few epochs during fine-tuning. Avoid early stopping which can itself overfit to validation set.

**Why this matters:** Small medical datasets (5k-50k images, often with <100 positive cases) cannot support long training. Models memorize training set and degrade on validation after 5-10 epochs. Heavy regularization + short training prevents overfitting while still learning. Fixed epoch counts avoid validation set overfitting from early stopping.

**When to use:** When training set is <20k images. When validation score peaks early then degrades. When using strong pretrained backbones that need minimal adaptation. For fine-tuning stages (stage 2 and stage 3) after pretraining.

**When NOT to use:** When training from scratch (may need 20-50 epochs). When dataset is large (>100k varied samples). When pretraining on external data (stage 1 can use 15-30 epochs). When validation is stable or still improving after 10 epochs.

**How to reason about it:**
Regularization strategy: Use high dropout in the classifier head and stochastic depth (drop_path) in the backbone, and tune both on validation. Apply heavy augmentation (see modality-specific guidelines). Use small weight decay (1e-4). Training schedule: For fine-tuning, use cosine annealing or one-cycle LR over 3-5 fixed epochs. For pretraining, 15-30 epochs with LR warmup. Epoch selection: Either use fixed epochs or snapshot ensembling (average multiple checkpoints) rather than selecting single best epoch on validation. Multiple seeds: Train 2-3 models with different seeds to capture variance since training is short.
