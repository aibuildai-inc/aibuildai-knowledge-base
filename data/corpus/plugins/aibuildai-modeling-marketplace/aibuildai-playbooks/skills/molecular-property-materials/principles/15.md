## Principle 15: Calibrate Predictions Only After Diagnosing a Reproducible Shift

*Consensus: 9/12 polymer writeups discussed post-processing, calibration, bias, shifts, or rescaling.*

Use OOF residuals, overlapping sources, adversarial validation, and controlled probes to test for unit, protocol, or acquisition shifts. Apply the simplest defensible per-target correction, and preserve an uncalibrated fallback when the hidden distribution is uncertain.

**Why this matters:** A constant or linear bias can dominate MAE even when ranking is excellent, as seen for polymer Tg, but leaderboard-tuned offsets can overfit a small public sample. When a shift comes from a unit or protocol difference in a known external source, correcting that source's labels before training may be safer than shifting outputs; when grouped OOF shows no bias, calibration may be unnecessary.

**When to use:** Use when residuals show systematic intercept or slope error across folds or trusted overlap, source protocols imply a unit transformation, or adversarial diagnostics reveal a target-specific shift that survives uncertainty analysis.

**When NOT to use:** Do not infer a shift from a few leaderboard moves or force predictions to match the training mean. If grouped OOF is calibrated and test covariates appear stable, prefer raw predictions or ordinary OOF calibration.

**How to reason about it:**
Fit calibration only on OOF predictions; test intercept-only, affine, monotonic, and no-correction models. Estimate uncertainty and perform sensitivity analysis across plausible test mixtures. Separate model calibration from speculative test-distribution correction, and submit or retain a no-shift hedge.
