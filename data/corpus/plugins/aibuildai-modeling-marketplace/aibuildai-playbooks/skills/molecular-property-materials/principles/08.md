## Principle 8: Reconcile External Data Before Scaling Its Volume

*Consensus: 8/12 polymer writeups used or discussed external datasets.*

Treat each external source as a separate measurement domain. Canonicalize and deduplicate structures, inspect overlap, estimate source-specific label offsets or transforms, filter failures, and tune source weights using validation only on the competition domain.

**Why this matters:** The same nominal property can differ with units, simulation protocol, chain length, temperature, or measurement method, and raw volume may teach the wrong domain more strongly than the desired chemistry.

**When to use:** Use when a target is data-starved, source provenance is known, licensing permits it, and overlap or a trusted teacher permits source calibration. External unlabeled molecules are useful for representation or ranking pretraining even when their absolute labels are unreliable.

**When NOT to use:** Do not merge sources blindly. Several solutions reported limited or negative gains from raw external data or attempted rescaling; a clean in-domain representation can beat more data.

**How to reason about it:**
For each source, answer: Are units and protocols aligned? Are duplicate structures consistently labeled? Does source identity remain predictable after preprocessing? Does adding the source improve competition-domain OOF error? Use rescaling, filtering, weighting, or feature-level stacking according to the failure mechanism.
