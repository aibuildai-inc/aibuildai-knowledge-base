## Principle 10: Add Auxiliary Supervision Only When It Encodes the Same Mechanism

*Consensus: Polymer writeups reported gains from ranking pretraining on pseudolabels and from simulation-derived features stacked into a tabular model, while contrastive pretraining did not help; reported gains were highly conditional.*

Use simulated physical quantities, ranking objectives, masked representations, or confident pseudolabels to shape the representation—not merely to add losses. Verify that the auxiliary signal is available without leakage and shares causal structure with the final target.

**Why this matters:** Ranking pseudolabels can tolerate noisy absolute polymer labels, letting a model learn from weaker supervision without discarding it. Unrelated auxiliary tasks consume capacity and may propagate teacher or domain errors.

**When to use:** Use when the auxiliary quantity shares a physical mechanism with the target, expands coverage with lower-noise comparisons, or directly addresses an observed error mode. Generate all stacked or teacher features out of fold.

**When NOT to use:** Do not assume multitask learning is automatically regularizing. Contrastive pretraining on subgraphs did not help one polymer GNN, and a winning solution that stacked simulation-derived predictions measured only a small CV gain from them.

**How to reason about it:**
Specify the mechanism in one sentence: “This auxiliary target helps because…”. Then compare target-only, auxiliary-only pretraining, and joint training; inspect per-target gradients and teacher confidence. Reject the auxiliary task if its gain disappears under leakage-safe OOF evaluation.
