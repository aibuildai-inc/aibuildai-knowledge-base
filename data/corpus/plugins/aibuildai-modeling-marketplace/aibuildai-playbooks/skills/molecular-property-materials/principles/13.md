## Principle 13: Ensemble Orthogonal Error Sources, Not Merely More Seeds

*Consensus: 9/12 polymer writeups mentioned ensembling, blending, or stacking.*

Combine models whose representations, folds, feature sets, or inductive biases produce complementary residuals. Estimate weights from out-of-fold predictions at the metric’s natural grouping level rather than from leaderboard probing.

**Why this matters:** Molecular errors are structured: graph and descriptor models fail on different chemotypes, and string, graph, and 3D models see different information about the same molecule. Diversity can produce much larger gains than small improvements to one already-correlated model.

**When to use:** Use after obtaining leakage-safe OOF predictions and measuring residual correlation. Allow weights to differ by target property; one polymer solution fit a separate model-weight set for each target.

**When NOT to use:** Do not blend weak, highly correlated models just to increase ensemble size, and do not train a stacker on in-sample predictions. Some polymer model families added no value to a stronger AutoGluon ensemble.

**How to reason about it:**
Compute per-target residual correlation and marginal ensemble gain. Prefer architectural and representation diversity first, then fold/seed diversity. Constrain weights, regularize the blender, and verify every weight under OOF or nested CV.
