## Principle 7: Validate by the Deployment Unit, Chemical Neighborhood, and Error Slice

*Consensus: 8/12 polymer writeups explicitly discussed folds, validation, grouping, clustering, similarity, or deduplication.*

Build validation around the independent deployment unit and plausible novelty level: split polymer data by canonical structure or similarity cluster, and remove near-duplicates of validation molecules from training. Report per-target and difficulty-slice metrics, not only one mean.

**Why this matters:** Random rows can place the same molecule, a near-duplicate monomer, an alternate spelling of the same SMILES, or a structural family in both train and validation. This inflates scores and can reverse model rankings, especially when test labels are sparse or structurally shifted.

**When to use:** Always. Add similarity-aware validation when external datasets, repeated structures, randomized SMILES, or multiple labels per molecule are present.

**When NOT to use:** Extremely conservative scaffold or Tanimoto splits can underestimate performance if the hidden test is IID. Keep both an IID grouped split and a novelty-stress split rather than replacing one unknown bias with another.

**How to reason about it:**
Define three views: deployment-group CV, chemical-novelty CV, and shift/adversarial diagnostics. Prefer changes that improve the first and do not collapse on the second. Stratify errors by target, label availability, molecule size, data source, and parse or conformer status.
