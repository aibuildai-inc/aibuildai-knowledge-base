## Principle 11: Optimize the Metric’s Statistical Functional, Not a Convenient Proxy

*Consensus: Polymer solutions trained or selected models on the weighted multi-property MAE, and several replaced MSE with a metric-matched, Huber, or quantile objective.*

Mirror target weights and missing-label masks in training and model selection, then choose a loss whose optimum matches the metric’s statistical functional. For MAE-like metrics, robust or quantile objectives and median aggregation are often more coherent than MSE.

**Why this matters:** Scale and frequency differences can let common targets dominate gradients even when the official score reweights targets. MAE rewards conditional medians, so MSE training and mean aggregation optimize a different functional from the one that is scored.

**When to use:** Always compute the exact competition metric out of fold. Use per-target normalization or weighting when the official score reweights targets by scale or label count.

**When NOT to use:** Do not copy the metric formula blindly into minibatch training if it creates unstable gradients or unstable per-target normalization in small batches. Use a stable surrogate such as Huber when needed, but checkpoint and tune on the exact metric.

**How to reason about it:**
Separate optimization from selection: pick a stable differentiable loss, calculate the exact metric at epoch end, and inspect each group’s contribution. Compare mean versus median prediction aggregation and target scaling under fixed folds.

```python
def masked_weighted_mae(pred, y, mask, target_weight):
    err = (pred - y).abs() * mask
    per_target = err.sum(0) / mask.sum(0).clamp_min(1)
    return (per_target * target_weight).sum()
```
