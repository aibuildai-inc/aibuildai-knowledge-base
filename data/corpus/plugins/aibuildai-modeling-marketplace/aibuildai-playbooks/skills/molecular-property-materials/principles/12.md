## Principle 12: Use Curriculum and Scaling Only After the Data Path Is Stable

*Consensus: A recurring pattern across the top solutions: polymer winners used staged pretraining and fine-tuning schedules.*

First establish a fast, trustworthy representation and validation loop; then scale model width, depth, epochs, or pretraining data in stages. Reuse checkpoints when changing only the data mix or the specialization of the target.

**Why this matters:** Molecular models can be compute-heavy, and representation bugs or invalid preprocessing are more damaging than insufficient capacity. Curriculum learning lets the model acquire broad syntax or chemistry cheaply before expensive target-specific refinement.

**When to use:** Use when deep attention, 3D conformers, molecular simulation, or large external corpora make full-fidelity experiments slow. It is especially useful for language models moving from unlabeled or pseudolabeled pretraining to in-domain regression.

**When NOT to use:** Do not assume larger is better. Polymer results were mixed: the winning string model scored worse with larger variants, while one 3D-model solution gained from a larger model until memory ran out. If learning curves saturate or CV variance dominates, invest in data quality and ensembling instead.

**How to reason about it:**
Scale one axis at a time and plot error against compute. Continue only if gains persist on grouped OOF and hard slices. Freeze components temporarily to test new heads, but perform an end-to-end fine-tuning stage before final inference.
