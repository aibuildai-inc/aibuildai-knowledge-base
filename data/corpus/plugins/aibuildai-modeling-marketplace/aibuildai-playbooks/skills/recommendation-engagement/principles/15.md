## Principle 15: Validate Model Selection Against Business Metrics, Not Just AUC

*Consensus: KKBox #1 raised the question of AUC vs. user-level metrics (GAUC). Implicit in most engagement tasks.*

AUC measures global ranking but may not reflect business value. For recommendation, top-K precision matters more. For user engagement, per-user AUC (GAUC) is more relevant than pooled AUC. Validate that your metric aligns with the task.

**Why this matters:** A model with 0.80 AUC might rank high-activity users perfectly but fail on low-activity users, yielding poor recommendations for most users. AUC treats all users equally in aggregate; GAUC computes per-user AUC and averages, ensuring fairness. Similarly, optimizing logloss can hurt top-1 precision. Misaligned metrics lead to models that score well but fail in production.

**When to use:** For recommendation systems (use precision@K, recall@K, NDCG, GAUC instead of or alongside AUC). For engagement prediction where per-user performance varies widely. When the competition metric is a proxy for business value—sanity-check that your model serves the real goal.

**When NOT to use:** When the competition metric is AUC and that's the only thing you're evaluated on—optimize it. When you lack the data to compute alternative metrics (need user-level labels for GAUC). When business metric computation is expensive and you're in rapid iteration mode—use AUC for speed, check business metric on final models only.

**How to reason about it:**
Read the evaluation metric carefully. If it's AUC but the task is 'recommend top 10 items to each user', compute precision@10 offline. If users have varying activity levels (some have 1000 items, some have 5), compute per-user AUC and average (GAUC). Compare model rankings: does the best AUC model also win on GAUC/precision? If not, investigate. Choose the model that aligns with deployment goals.

```python
# GAUC: per-user AUC, then average\nfrom sklearn.metrics import roc_auc_score\ndef gauc(y_true, y_pred, user_ids):\n    user_aucs = []\n    for user in user_ids.unique():\n        mask = user_ids == user\n        if y_true[mask].nunique() > 1:  # need both classes\n            user_aucs.append(roc_auc_score(y_true[mask], y_pred[mask]))\n    return np.mean(user_aucs)\n\n# Precision@K\ndef precision_at_k(y_true, y_pred, k=10):\n    top_k_idx = np.argsort(y_pred)[-k:]\n    return y_true[top_k_idx].sum() / k
```
