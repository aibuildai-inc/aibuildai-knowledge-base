## Principle 2: Apply Negative Downsampling for Extreme Imbalance (>99:1)

*Consensus: TalkingData #1 (99.8% negatives downsampled 1:1), multiple ad fraud/CTR solutions. Not used in balanced problems like Home Credit.*

When positive class is <1%, downsample negatives to 1:1 or 1:5 ratio during training, keeping all positives. Use bagging over multiple random samples of negatives.

**Why this matters:** Training on 99.8% negatives wastes compute on redundant easy negatives while the model underfits rare positives. Downsampling to 1:1 lets you use hundreds more features (training time drops 100×) and prevents the model from learning 'always predict 0'. Bagging 5-10 models on different negative samples recovers the information discarded by sampling.

**When to use:** When positive rate <1% (CTR, ad fraud, rare conversions) AND you have sufficient positive examples (>10k). When training time limits feature exploration. When single model overfits to always predicting the majority class.

**When NOT to use:** When classes are 10:90 or more balanced—standard class weights suffice. When positive examples are <5k (downsampling leaves too little data). When using FFM (already handles imbalance well). When inference requires calibrated probabilities (downsampling skews them; recalibrate post-hoc).

**How to reason about it:**
Check positive rate. If <0.5%, try 1:1 downsampling: keep all positives, randomly sample equal negatives. Train 5 models with different random seeds for negative sampling. Average predictions. Compare single full-data model vs. 5-model bagged ensemble on held-out AUC. If bagged wins by >0.001, keep it.

```python
# Downsample negatives, bag multiple models\npos = train[train.target == 1]\nneg = train[train.target == 0]\n\nmodels = []\nfor seed in range(5):\n    neg_sample = neg.sample(n=len(pos), random_state=seed)\n    balanced = pd.concat([pos, neg_sample])\n    model = lgb.train(params, lgb.Dataset(balanced[features], balanced.target))\n    models.append(model)\n\n# Average predictions\npreds = np.mean([m.predict(test[features]) for m in models], axis=0)
```
