## Principle 9: Ensemble GBM and Neural Networks for Complementary Patterns

*Consensus: KKBox #1 (0.6 LGBM + 0.4 NN), Home Credit #1 (GBM + NN stacking), TalkingData #1 (7 GBM + 1 NN blend). Standard 2-model diversity approach.*

GBMs and neural networks learn different patterns: GBMs capture head flow (frequent patterns via greedy splits), NNs explore tail flow (rare combinations via gradient descent across all parameters). Blend them 60:40 to 70:30 (GBM:NN).

**Why this matters:** A single model type has blind spots. GBMs greedily optimize splits on frequent patterns, leaving rare cases underfit. NNs explore broad feature combinations, fitting tail flow better but adding noise to head flow. Their predictions correlate ~0.90-0.92 (high but not identical). Ensembling recovers 0.005-0.01 AUC over the best single model.

**When to use:** When you have a strong GBM baseline (top 10% LB). When you have sufficient data for NNs (>100k rows). When single-model improvements plateau. When GBM and NN predictions correlate 0.85-0.95 (complementary but not redundant).

**When NOT to use:** When you have <50k rows (NNs overfit). When GBM alone achieves top-tier performance and NN is >0.01 AUC behind (NN adds noise). When correlation >0.97 (models are redundant). When compute budget limits NN tuning (a bad NN hurts the blend).

**How to reason about it:**
Train best GBM. Train NN (see field-aware architecture principle). Compare single-model AUCs. Check prediction correlation. If GBM is 0.800 and NN is 0.790 with correlation 0.92, blend them: try 0.7×GBM + 0.3×NN. If blend >0.801, keep it. Tune blend weights via CV grid search.
