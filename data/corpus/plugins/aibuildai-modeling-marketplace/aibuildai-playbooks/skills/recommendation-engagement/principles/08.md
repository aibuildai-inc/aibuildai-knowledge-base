## Principle 8: Detect and Exploit Train/Test Distribution Differences

*Consensus: Santander #1 (identified real vs. fake test samples to enable >0.92 LB), Home Credit #1 (0.98 AUC train/test classifier), Outbrain split present/future test.*

Train a classifier to distinguish train from test. High AUC (>0.7) reveals distributional shift or data leaks. Use adversarial validation to reweight samples or split test into subgroups requiring different models.

**Why this matters:** If a model can predict train vs. test with >70% accuracy, the distributions differ—features or sampling changed. This can indicate: (1) temporal drift (test is future data), (2) synthetic/corrupted test samples, (3) data leakage. Identifying the discriminative features guides strategy: remove them (if leakage), model them separately (if real shift), or split test into subsets (present vs. future).

**When to use:** When you suspect train/test come from different distributions. When LB and CV diverge unexpectedly. When organizers augmented test data with synthetic samples. Early in competition to diagnose data issues. To create sample weights (downweight train samples that look like test).

**When NOT to use:** When train and test are from identical distributions (adversarial AUC ~0.5)—no action needed. When the leak is the entire competition (e.g., duplicate rows)—exploitation is banned. When you've already split test by time or other metadata—adversarial validation is redundant.

**How to reason about it:**
Create binary target: train=0, test=1. Train GBM on all features. Check AUC. If <0.6, distributions are similar. If 0.6-0.8, moderate shift—inspect feature importances to find discriminative features. If >0.8, severe shift or leak—investigate top features. Use findings to: (1) remove leak features, (2) create separate models for distribution subgroups, or (3) reweight training samples.
