## Models

The solution is a blend of:
1. [LightGBM](https://www.kaggle.com/lucaskg/20th-solution-part-1-lgb-no-aug-92153) whose CV is 0.9215, private LB 0.92033. We then use [Jiwei's great augmentation ](https://www.kaggle.com/jiweiliu/lgb-2-leaves-augment) method to boost the score up further.
2. NN, which is essential to blending and our overall success. My teammate [@Strideradu](https://www.kaggle.com/strideradu) explains his [NN framework](https://www.kaggle.com/c/santander-customer-transaction-prediction/discussion/89070#514156) in more details.
3. [Here](https://www.kaggle.com/c/santander-customer-transaction-prediction/discussion/88922#latest-513657) is the detail of how we implemented the augmentation scheme.

Finally, the two models are stacked through [a weighted AUC optimization](https://www.kaggle.com/lucaskg/20th-solution-part-2-auc-weight-optimization). 

## Features

1. Value counts: the frequency of unique values reveals a lot of information. For some variables like `var_108` and `var_12`, values with high counts tend to have higher probability of positive target.

2. Multiplication and division between raw feature and its corresponding value count. E.g., `df['var_0']/df['var_0_count']` and `df['var_0']*df['var_0_count']`, which disperse the distribution of different values with the same counts. 

3. Features grouped by `count==1/!1`. We created features like rank ratio (`x.rank()/x.rank().max()`) and zscore (`(x-x.mean())/x.std()`) for values whose count equals 1 or greater than 1, respectively. It turns out that `count==1` is very special. Simulated data (generated by some distribution and then rounded to 4 decimals) should have very low PDF towards the mode of the overall distribution of the variable. However, this is not the case for many variables (will upload figures later). The zscore kind of normalizes the distribution so that we can compare the edges of distributions for `count==1/!1` altogether.
