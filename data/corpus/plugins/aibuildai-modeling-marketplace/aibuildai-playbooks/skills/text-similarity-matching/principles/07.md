## Principle 7: Combine Deep Embeddings with TF-IDF for Diversity

*Consensus: 16/21 Shopee solutions, multiple Quora solutions*

Ensemble neural text embeddings (BERT, RoBERTa) with classical TF-IDF vectors. TF-IDF provides strong baselines and adds diversity to ensembles.

**Why this matters:** Neural models capture semantics but may miss exact keyword matches. TF-IDF excels at lexical overlap and is fast. Their errors are uncorrelated, so ensembling yields significant gains (typical +0.01-0.02). TF-IDF also stabilizes when neural models overfit.

**When to use:** Text matching tasks where exact word overlap matters (product titles, ad text, short phrases). When ensembling multiple models for final submission. When you want a fast, strong baseline before investing in deep models.

**When NOT to use:** When text is highly paraphrased or multilingual with little lexical overlap (neural models dominate). If text is very short (<5 tokens), TF-IDF may be too sparse. Skip if adding TF-IDF to ensemble shows no CV gain.

**How to reason about it:**
Train TF-IDF with n-grams (1-2 or 1-3) on the text field. Compute cosine similarity matrix. Run kNN or threshold-based matching. For ensembling: treat TF-IDF similarities as another modality and union/vote with neural similarities, or include TF-IDF distance as a feature in stage-2 classifier.

```python
from sklearn.feature_extraction.text import TfidfVectorizer
import cupy as cp  # or numpy

# Train TF-IDF
tfidf = TfidfVectorizer(min_df=2, max_df=0.9, ngram_range=(1,2), max_features=10000)
tfidf_matrix = tfidf.fit_transform(df['text'])  # sparse

# Compute cosine similarity (use GPU via cuML for speed)
tfidf_matrix_gpu = cp.sparse.csr_matrix(tfidf_matrix)
tfidf_sim = (tfidf_matrix_gpu @ tfidf_matrix_gpu.T).toarray()

# Ensemble with neural embeddings
final_sim = 0.6 * neural_sim + 0.4 * tfidf_sim  # tune weights by CV
```
