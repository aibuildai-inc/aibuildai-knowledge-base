## Principle 10: Train on Full Data Without Folds for Final Submission If Convergence Is Stable

*Consensus: Several source competitions; especially those with <10 volumes/patients*

After validating hyperparameters on folds, retrain final models on 100% of training data (no validation holdout) to maximize performance when training data is limited and convergence is stable.

**Why this matters:** Medical datasets are small (often <500 images or <10 3D volumes). Holding out 20% for validation means 20% less data for the final model. If convergence is stable (validation loss plateaus smoothly without wild fluctuations), you can safely use all data for the final model, gaining a consistent score boost.

**When to use:** When: (a) total training data is <500 images or <20 volumes, (b) validation curves show smooth convergence without overfitting spikes, (c) you've already validated hyperparameters on folds and won't change them. Use early stopping based on epochs (fixed schedule, e.g., 30 epochs) or LB feedback, not validation loss.

**When NOT to use:** Skip if: (a) convergence is unstable (validation loss oscillates or diverges), (b) you're still tuning hyperparameters (you need validation for feedback), (c) dataset is large enough (>1000 images) that the 20% holdout doesn't matter.

**How to reason about it:**
1. Train 5-fold CV, monitor validation loss curves. 2. Check if all folds converge smoothly by epoch 20-30 without overfitting. 3. If yes: average the optimal epoch across folds (e.g., fold 1 peaks at epoch 25, fold 2 at 28 → use 26-27). 4. Retrain on 100% data for that many epochs with the same hyperparameters. 5. Validate on public LB: if score drops, revert to averaging fold predictions. 6. For ensemble, train multiple full-data models with different seeds or augmentation strengths.
