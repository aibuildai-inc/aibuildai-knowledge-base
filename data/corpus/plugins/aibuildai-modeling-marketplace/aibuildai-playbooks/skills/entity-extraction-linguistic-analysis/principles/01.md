## Principle 1: Choose Prediction Granularity Based on Annotation Boundary Alignment

*Consensus: Seen across competitions - NBME (1st, 3rd, 11th, 18th, 19th) mapped token predictions to characters and repaired whitespace offsets; Feedback Prize (4th, 11th) blended different tokenizers at character level; token-level with boundary repair in others*

Decide whether to predict at token-level or character-level based on whether entity boundaries align with tokenizer output. Misaligned boundaries create an unlearnable target for token classifiers.

**Why this matters:** When annotations split tokens (e.g., a word directly after a quote or bracket shifts the offset by one character, or boundaries fall on whitespace), a token-level model cannot learn the correct span because the target is outside its prediction space. Character-level prediction or aggressive post-processing is required.

**When to use:** Use character-level prediction when: (1) annotations frequently fall mid-token (check via tokenizer offset mapping), (2) whitespace/punctuation boundaries are noisy, (3) the task allows partial-word extraction (noisy labels), (4) you blend models with different tokenizers (map each to character probabilities first). Use token-level when annotations respect word boundaries and the domain has clear tokenization rules (clinical notes with medical terms, structured PII).

**When NOT to use:** Avoid character-level when: (1) entities are always full words/phrases (PII names, dataset titles), (2) the tokenizer naturally aligns with annotations (subword tokenization handles the domain well), (3) inference speed is critical (character models are slower). Top NBME solutions kept token-level models but mapped their probabilities to characters and repaired whitespace offsets; PII's 1st place used token-level with whitespace post-processing because names are whole words.

**How to reason about it:**
Check annotation-tokenizer alignment: for 100 train samples, tokenize and map annotation spans to token offsets. If >5% of spans split tokens or start/end mid-token, character-level prediction or boundary repair is needed. Character-level: map each token probability to the characters in its offset range, average character probabilities across models (this also blends models with different tokenizers), then threshold characters into spans. Token-level: predict on tokens, then post-process to expand/contract spans to character boundaries based on whitespace/punctuation rules.

```python
# Map token probabilities to characters with the tokenizer's offset mapping
def token_to_char_probs(text, offsets, token_probs):
    char_probs = np.zeros(len(text))
    for (start, end), prob in zip(offsets, token_probs):
        char_probs[start:end] = prob
    return char_probs
```
