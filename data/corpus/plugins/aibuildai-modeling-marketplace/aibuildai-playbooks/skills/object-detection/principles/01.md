## Principle 1: Choose Detector Architecture Based on Object Size and Aspect Ratio Distribution

*Consensus: Top solutions consistently consider object characteristics when selecting models. YOLO variants dominate among top-3 solutions, but anchor-free methods (CenterNet, FCOS) excel for extreme aspect ratios.*

Select your base detector architecture by analyzing the distribution of object sizes and aspect ratios in your training data, not by model popularity or benchmark performance on other datasets.

**Why this matters:** Default anchor configurations in anchor-based detectors (Faster R-CNN, YOLO, EfficientDet) are optimized for COCO-like distributions. Datasets with unusual object characteristics (very small, elongated, or clustered objects) require either anchor-free architectures or extensive anchor tuning. A mismatch causes poor recall on edge cases and wasted training capacity.

**When to use:** Always perform this analysis before model selection. Critical when objects are predominantly small (<32px), have extreme aspect ratios (>3:1 or <1:3), or when the data differs substantially from COCO (e.g., medical scans, satellite imagery, microscopy).

**When NOT to use:** Skip detailed anchor analysis only if your objects closely match COCO statistics (mixed sizes, roughly square aspect ratios 1:1 to 2:1). Even then, validate IoU distributions during training—poor anchors show up as boxes that don't tighten during training.

**How to reason about it:**
Plot object width/height distributions and aspect ratios. If most objects are <50px: try anchor-free (CenterNet, FCOS) or YOLO with modified anchors. If aspect ratios are bimodal or highly skewed: test anchor-free first, or run k-means clustering on your boxes to generate custom anchors. If objects cluster densely: increase anchor density or use NMS-free architectures. Validate by checking training loss curves—boxes should converge quickly if anchors match well.
