## Principle 2: Invest in Domain-Specific Pre-training or Data When Base Models Plateau

*Consensus: Critical for LLM Science (Wikipedia contexts, generated STEM questions), Eedi and MAP (synthetic math data)*

Pre-trained models on general corpora often plateau quickly on domain-specific QA tasks. The winning move is either domain-adaptive pre-training (continue MLM on domain text) or injecting domain-specific training data that bridges the distribution gap.

**Why this matters:** Base BERT/RoBERTa models trained on general text (Wikipedia, books) lack domain vocabulary, reasoning patterns, and knowledge. Continued pre-training on in-domain text and external training data from a similar distribution are the two usual fixes. For LLM Science, properly parsed Wikipedia with formulas, and fine-tuning on tens of thousands of generated STEM questions with retrieved contexts (21st place used a public 60k set plus 39k questions of its own). For Eedi, synthetic math misconception data was the difference between top 10 and winning.

**When to use:** When initial models with standard pre-trained weights plateau and error analysis shows domain-specific failures: missing technical terms, wrong domain reasoning, lack of subject knowledge. Especially for: technical domains (math, science), specialized question types (misconceptions, multi-hop reasoning).

**When NOT to use:** Skip if the domain is general web text and data is tiny (< 1k samples) - you'll overfit the domain data. Skip if a domain-specific model already exists and works (e.g., don't re-train on legal text if Legal-BERT exists). For Home Depot product search, domain pre-training wasn't a pattern because the task was feature-engineering driven.

**How to reason about it:**
Check if existing domain models exist first (Hugging Face Hub by domain keywords). If not: (1) Can you collect domain text for MLM? Aim for 10M+ tokens. (2) Can you get domain-specific QA pairs? Public datasets from a similar domain can transfer. (3) Can you generate synthetic data that's verifiable? For Eedi, LLM-generated math MCQs with human/LLM filtering. Trade-off: pre-training cost (days on GPU) vs adding external data (hours of curation).
