## Principle 4: Measure CV-LB Agreement Before Trusting Either on Small or Noisy Data

*Consensus: Mixed in the source writeups: Eedi solutions saw CV gains that did not reach the LB, while a MAP solution with noisy labels chose its submission by out-of-fold score instead of the close public LB*

When training data is small or known to be noisy (inconsistent labels, annotator disagreement), local cross-validation can decorrelate from the leaderboard, and the public LB can also mislead. Measure how well the two agree before you let either one choose models.

**Why this matters:** CV guides your decisions. Wrong CV → wrong decisions. With a small, noisy training set, each validation fold can be too small to rank models reliably. A public LB can be just as noisy: in Eedi, one top solution found that hard example mining improved CV but not the LB, and another tuned model merging until CV and public LB both improved after 4 merges, while the best private score came from 3 merges. In MAP, whose labels were noisy, a top solution chose its submission by out-of-fold score because public LB scores were too close to separate.

**When to use:** When: (1) Training data is small relative to the public test set. (2) Training labels are known noisy (acknowledged in competition description, annotator disagreement, found inconsistencies). (3) Early experiments show CV and LB moving in different directions. (4) The public LB is compressed, so many submissions score almost the same.

**When NOT to use:** Don't abandon CV entirely for large data (> 10k train samples) or when public test is known to be easy/different (organizers warn about distribution shift). Don't trust public LB for tuning if you have < 5 submissions remaining. Don't skip CV for architectural experiments (still need local signal for fast iteration).

**How to reason about it:**
Calculate CV-LB correlation early (first 10 submissions). If the rank correlation between your CV scores and public LB scores across models is low, find out why before trusting either: check the fold design (Principle 10), the label noise, and the size of the public test set. Strategy: use CV for fast iteration (filtering obviously bad ideas); use the public LB as a second check, not as a tuning target. Maintain diversity in final submissions to hedge against shake-up. For post-processing: if CV and LB disagree, tune on LB but validate the direction makes sense (don't blindly overfit).

```python
# Tracking CV vs LB correlation
import pandas as pd
from scipy.stats import spearmanr

experiments = pd.DataFrame({
    'model': ['model_a', 'model_b', 'model_c', ...],
    'local_cv': [0.756, 0.762, 0.759, ...],
    'public_lb': [0.723, 0.735, 0.729, ...]
})

corr, p_value = spearmanr(experiments['local_cv'], experiments['public_lb'])
print(f'CV-LB correlation: {corr:.3f}')  
if corr < 0.7:
    print('WARNING: Low correlation, check the fold design and label noise before trusting either')
```
