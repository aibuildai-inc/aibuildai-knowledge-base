## Principle 10: Validate with GroupKFold on the Right Grouping Variable

*Consensus: Critical decision affects CV-LB correlation*

The grouping variable for cross-validation must match what creates dependence in your data. Group by question_id if same question appears with different answers; by subject/construct if you want to test generalization to unseen topics; by user_id for personalized tasks.

**Why this matters:** Wrong grouping creates leakage and overly optimistic CV. For Eedi, grouping by question_id (ensuring same question's different answers stay together) gave optimistic CV; grouping by construct_id (broader category) better matched LB. The choice determines what generalization you're testing: seen questions with new answers, vs new questions entirely, vs new topics.

**When to use:** Default to grouping by the natural question identifier (question_id) if each question can have multiple samples (different answers, different contexts). Switch to broader grouping (subject_id, construct_id) if: (1) CV is much better than LB and you suspect train-test distribution shift. (2) Test data contains new categories not in train. (3) Competition emphasizes generalization to 'new questions/topics'.

**When NOT to use:** Don't use random splits when questions can repeat (creates leakage). Don't group by very broad categories if it makes folds unbalanced (one fold has 80% of data). Don't over-engineer complex stratification if simple GroupKFold gives good CV-LB correlation.

**How to reason about it:**
Identify dependence structure: which samples share information that shouldn't leak across train/val? Common structures: (1) Same question, different answers → group by question_id. (2) Questions from same topic → group by topic/construct for OOD testing. (3) User behavior → group by user_id. Test correlation: try different grouping strategies, track CV vs LB correlation over 10 submissions. Highest correlation = correct grouping.
