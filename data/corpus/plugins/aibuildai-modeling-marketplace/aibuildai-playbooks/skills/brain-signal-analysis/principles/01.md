## Principle 1: Choose Signal Representation Based on Data Modality and Task Horizon

*Consensus: Parkinson's FOG solutions modeled accelerometer data as 1D sequences or patches, with the 6th-place team adding spectrogram and wavelet models; seizure prediction solutions turned EEG into hand-crafted band-power and correlation features. The choice depends on data modality and on whether the task requires fine temporal resolution or frequency patterns.*

Brain and body signals can be modeled as raw 1D sequences, as hand-crafted spectral features, or as time-frequency images such as spectrograms and wavelet scalograms. The representation decides which models fit, how much data they need, and what temporal resolution survives.

**Why this matters:** Hand-crafted spectral features (log power in frequency bands from an FFT or a Welch estimate, channel correlation matrices and their eigenvalues) compress a long multi-channel clip into a few hundred numbers. That suits small datasets and tree, linear, or nearest-neighbor models, and it is what the seizure prediction solutions did. 1D sequence models (GRU, 1D-CNN, transformers over patches) keep the full time resolution that event boundaries need, which is why the FOG solutions used them. Spectrogram and wavelet images reveal frequency structure but compress time; in the FOG 6th-place solution they worked best as additional ensemble members next to 1D models.

**When to use:** Use spectral features when one label covers a long window and frequency content separates the classes, as in seizure prediction from EEG clips. Use 1D sequences or patches when the task needs sub-second timing, as in detecting FOG event boundaries in accelerometer data. Add spectrogram or wavelet models as extra ensemble members when compute allows, because they err on different events than 1D models.

**When NOT to use:** Do NOT reshape a low-dimensional accelerometer signal into a fake image: reshaping a (N, 3) signal into (N//10, 10, 3) discards the natural time structure. Do NOT train a large sequence model from scratch on a small EEG dataset when band-power features and a simple model already separate the classes. Top Parkinson's FOG solutions (accelerometer) used 1D sequences or patches; top seizure prediction solutions (EEG) used hand-crafted band-power and correlation features with tree, linear, or KNN models.

**How to reason about it:**
Ask: (1) How long is the window one label covers, and how many labeled windows exist? (2) Does frequency content or precise timing separate the classes? (3) How much compute is available for extra models? IF long windows + frequency separates the classes + few labels → spectral features with a tree or linear model. IF event boundaries matter → 1D sequences or patches with a GRU or transformer. IF budget allows → add spectrogram or wavelet models to the ensemble.
