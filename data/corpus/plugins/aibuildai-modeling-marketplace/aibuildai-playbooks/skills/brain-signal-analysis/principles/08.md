## Principle 8: Handle Variable Sequence Lengths with Patches or Adaptive Chunking

*Consensus: Parkinson's FOG competition (variable 5k-200k steps): 4/5 solutions chunked sequences into fixed-length windows with overlap. Transformer solutions (2/5) used patch-based encoding to reduce sequence length.*

Accelerometer datasets have sequences ranging from 5,000 to 200,000 timesteps. Transformers scale quadratically with sequence length; RNNs lose long-range information. Chunking (split into fixed-length segments with overlap, predict on each, merge) handles variable length. Patches (reshape sequence into non-overlapping blocks, treat as tokens) reduce length by patch_size.

**Why this matters:** Training on full 200k-step sequences is infeasible (memory, compute). Training on random 5k crops loses context for long events. Solution: train on fixed short chunks (1000-5000 steps, memory-efficient, faster iteration), infer on long chunks (15k-30k steps, more context), overlap predictions by 50% and average. Patches: reshape (15552, 3) accelerometer into (864, 54) by grouping 18 consecutive steps, reducing transformer attention cost by 18². One solution reduced labels the same way (max-pool over patch window) so predictions are at patch resolution, then tile back to original resolution for submission.

**When to use:** Use chunking when: (1) sequences vary widely in length, (2) you train RNNs or 1D CNNs that can handle any length but you want fixed batch sizes, (3) labels are dense (every timestep has a label). Use patches when: (1) you use transformers (quadratic cost in length), (2) patch-resolution labels are acceptable (coarser predictions), (3) you can tile predictions back to full resolution for submission.

**When NOT to use:** Do NOT chunk if your task requires full-sequence context (e.g., classifying entire series as one label)—use hierarchical models (chunk embeddings → sequence-level transformer). Do NOT use patches if you need sub-patch-resolution predictions (e.g., detecting event boundaries to ±1 timestep). Do NOT train on short chunks and infer on full sequences if the model was designed for fixed length (e.g., fixed positional encoding)—retrain or use relative position encoding.

**How to reason about it:**
For variable-length sequences: (1) Decide on model architecture: if transformer, use patches (reduce length by 10-20x). If RNN/1D CNN, use chunking (split into fixed-length segments). (2) Training: use short chunks (1000-5000 for memory/speed). (3) Inference: use long chunks (15k-30k for context). (4) Overlap: shift chunks by chunk_length // 2, predict on center 50% of each chunk, average overlaps. (5) For patches: reduce labels by max-pooling over patch window, predict at patch resolution, tile back to full resolution.
