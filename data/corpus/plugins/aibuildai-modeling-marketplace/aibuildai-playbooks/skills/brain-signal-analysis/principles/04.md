## Principle 4: Cross-Validate by Patient/Subject and Recording Sequence

*Consensus: Several Parkinson's FOG solutions used GroupKFold or StratifiedGroupKFold with Subject as the group; Melbourne seizure prediction solutions kept each 1-hour sequence in one fold, and one found that ignoring sequences made local AUC about 0.1 higher than the leaderboard.*

Brain signals are highly subject-specific. A patient's baseline EEG or gait pattern is more similar to their own seizure/FOG than to another patient's normal state. Splitting folds randomly by sample or by time leaks patient-specific patterns into validation. Consecutive clips cut from one recording are also correlated, so they must stay in the same fold.

**Why this matters:** Random splitting: a model learns patient A's baseline in training, sees patient A's seizures in validation, and achieves falsely high CV by memorizing patient-specific features (electrode impedance, baseline amplitude) rather than generalizing to seizure patterns. Grouping by patient forces the model to generalize across subjects. The same leak happens inside one patient when adjacent clips of one sequence land in different folds: a Melbourne seizure prediction writeup reported local CV between 0.82 and 0.92 that did not track the leaderboard when files were treated individually.

**When to use:** Always group CV folds by patient/subject ID in brain signal tasks. Always keep clips from one continuous recording or sequence in the same fold.

**When NOT to use:** Do NOT group by patient if patients contribute only 1 sample each (no leak possible, but rare). Do NOT group by patient when the task trains one model per patient and the test set contains the same patients—then group by recording sequence inside each patient instead.

**How to reason about it:**
Step 1: Check if dataset has patient/subject IDs. If yes, use StratifiedGroupKFold with group=patient_id. Step 2: Check if samples are cut from longer recordings. If yes, assign a sequence ID and group by it as well. Step 3: Compare CV with the public leaderboard; if CV is much higher and does not track it, look for a remaining leak in the split.

```python
from sklearn.model_selection import StratifiedGroupKFold

# Group by patient (or by recording sequence), stratify by target class
skf = StratifiedGroupKFold(n_splits=5)
for fold, (train_idx, val_idx) in enumerate(skf.split(X, y, groups=group_id)):
    # No subject or sequence appears on both sides of the split
    assert set(group_id[train_idx]).isdisjoint(group_id[val_idx])
```
