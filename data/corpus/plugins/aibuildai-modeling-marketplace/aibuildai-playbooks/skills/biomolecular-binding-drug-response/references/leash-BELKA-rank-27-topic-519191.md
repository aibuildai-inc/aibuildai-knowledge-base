We adopted different strategies for shared and nonshared targets.

# 1. Nonshared Target Part

## CV Strategy
The split was made based on building blocks, as we saw in this helpful [notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/thedrcat/belka-split-cv-like-the-host). Each building block was sorted and then systematically sampled to avoid unbalanced sampling.

## Model
We used [ChemBERTa-10M-MTR](https://www.kaggle.com/code/tetsuya3510/leash-bio-chemberta-baseline) as our base model for each protein
## Training
### Fine-tuning: 
- For all proteins: We used 4M sampling for fine-tuning each model.
- We fine-tuned each model using 4M samples for the respective protein.
- For sEH Triazine core: We used a mixed dataset, with half the data from external sources and half from the regular training data.
- For sEH NonTriazine core: We used a dataset trained exclusively on [external data containing various cores](https://chemrxiv.org/engage/chemrxiv/article-details/6438943f08c86922ffeffe57). This approach was chosen to potentially improve predictions for unknown cores.
- Overfitting Prevention: To mitigate overfitting, we froze the weights of the first three layers of the ChemBERTa model.

## Ensemble Method
- For most proteins: We tested three ensemble methods - simple average, CV-weighted average, and CV-weighted average to the power of 5. We chose the CV-weighted average to the power of 5 as it yielded the highest LB score.
| Ensemble Method | Public | Private |
|---|---|---|
| Simple Average | 0.486 | 0.272 |
| CV-weighted Average | 0.488 | 0.271 |
| CV-weighted Average^5 | 0.496 | 0.266 |

- For sEH NonTriazine core: We used a simple average ensemble of the three runs with different seeds.

Performance: The final Public LB score (mask share) for our nonshared target models was 0.173.

# 2. Shared Target Part
For shared targets, we used a 1DCNN model, improving upon the Kaggle [public notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/ahmedelfazouan/belka-1dcnn-starter-with-all-data).

## CV Strategy
We used 5-fold split generated by StratifiedKFold. All training data was used. 
## Model
- Increased number of filters (32 to 64, doubling in each layer)
- Enhanced convolution layers (padding='same', varied kernel sizes)
- Added BatchNormalization
- Introduced shortcut connections and Attention mechanism
- Adjusted Dropout rate (0.1 to 0.2)
- Modified fully connected layers


## Training
- Increased number of epochs (20 to 30)
- Reduced patience in ReduceLROnPlateau (5 to 3)
- Allowed slight overfitting

These improvements aimed to intentionally overfit the model slightly, enabling it to learn shared target data more deeply and improve scores. This strategy allowed the model to capture more detailed features of the training data, leading to improved predictive performance.

## Score
We compared the LB scores of fold0 out of 15 folds to evaluate our model improvements. The results are shown in the table below:
| Model | Public | Private |
| --- | --- |
| Original notebook model | 0.359 | 0.217 |
| Improved model | 0.387 | 0.220 |


These results demonstrate the effectiveness of our improvement strategy. By increasing model complexity and allowing deeper learning, we significantly improved our score on the public leaderboard.
After confirming the improvement through this single fold comparison, we proceeded with our final strategy:
- We created a new model with 5 folds, separate from the 15-fold model used for comparison.
- We applied our improved model architecture to all 5 folds.
- Finally, we created an average ensemble using these 5 folds to generate our final predictions.

The final performance of our 5-fold ensemble 1DCNN model on the Public LB (mask nonshare) was 0.347.
This approach allowed us to leverage the benefits of our model improvements while also utilizing ensemble techniques to further enhance our predictions.
