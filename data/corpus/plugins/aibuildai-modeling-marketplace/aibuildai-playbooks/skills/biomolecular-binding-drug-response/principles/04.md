## Principle 4: Share Statistical Strength Across Related Targets, but Test the Output Parameterization

*Consensus: At least 5/7 BELKA solutions explicitly predicted all three proteins jointly. Most MoA neural solutions used joint multi-label heads, while the 8th-place solution showed that label-powerset multiclass prediction was a useful low-correlation alternative.*

Use a shared encoder with multi-target outputs when targets arise from the same molecule or assay profile. If label combinations are highly structured, test auxiliary target heads, correlated-label transfer, or a label-powerset model as a complement rather than assuming  independent binary models are optimal.

**Why this matters:** Rare targets cannot individually estimate robust representations, but related targets share chemical motifs and biological response pathways. Joint learning pools evidence; alternative output parameterizations can expose label co-occurrence structure and generate ensemble diversity, though they may constrain unseen combinations.

**When to use:** Use shared multi-label heads when examples have labels for several proteins or mechanisms and the targets show reusable signal. Consider a label-powerset branch when the number of observed label combinations is far smaller than all possible combinations and a standard multi-label model remains in the blend.

**When NOT to use:** Train separate target models when labels come from materially different assays, have incompatible missingness, or target-specific CV shows strong negative transfer. Do not rely solely on label powersets when novel target combinations are plausible, because a softmax over observed combinations cannot represent arbitrary new combinations.

**How to reason about it:**
Measure label co-occurrence and target-wise residual correlation. Compare joint versus separate heads per target, not only by the mean metric. If a structured-output model loses slightly alone but has distinct errors, retain it only if OOF blending confirms a net gain.

import torch
import torch.nn as nn

class JointPanelModel(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, encoder, hidden_dim, n_targets):
        super().__init__()
        self.encoder = encoder
        self.head = nn.Linear(hidden_dim, n_targets)

    def forward(self, x):
        return self.head(self.encoder(x))  # BCEWithLogitsLoss over available labels
