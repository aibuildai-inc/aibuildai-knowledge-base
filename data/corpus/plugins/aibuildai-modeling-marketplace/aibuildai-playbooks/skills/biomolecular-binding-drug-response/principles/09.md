## Principle 9: Treat Fold and Seed Variation as a Diagnostic, Not Just Bagging Material

*Consensus: 5/7 BELKA solutions averaged folds, seeds, epochs, or checkpoints; BELKA writeups repeatedly emphasized severe non-shared variance. At least 11/14 MoA solutions used multiple folds, seeds, or both.*

Repeat the experiment across identity-aware folds and seeds, then summarize mean, dispersion, and target/regime breakdowns. Average models when variation is irreducible, but investigate whether unstable folds have missing positives, shifted prevalence, or different chemical similarity.

**Why this matters:** Sparse labels and grouped splits can make a fold qualitatively different rather than merely noisy. Blind averaging hides validation defects; diagnosing the source reveals whether to redesign folds, weight predictions by training support, or simplify the model.

**When to use:** Use repeated folds/seeds when positives are scarce, folds differ in molecular similarity, or small training changes cause large score swings. Maintain OOF predictions for every run so comparisons and blends use the same rows.

**When NOT to use:** Do not spend the full budget bagging a model whose mean grouped-CV performance is poor. On extremely large datasets, BELKA teams used different folds for different architectures to obtain diversity at feasible cost instead of training every model on every fold.

**How to reason about it:**
For each candidate, inspect score mean, standard deviation, worst fold, and per-target positive support. A robust model with slightly lower mean can be preferable to a brittle leader. If a fold lacks positives for a target, repair the split or downweight that fold–target prediction based on training support rather than pretending it is comparable.

```python
import pandas as pd

records = []
for fold in folds:
    for seed in seeds:
        pred = fit_predict_oof(fold, seed)
        records.append(score_by_target_and_regime(pred, fold.valid_y))

report = pd.concat(records)
summary = report.groupby(["model", "regime"]).agg(
    mean_score=("score", "mean"),
    std_score=("score", "std"),
    worst_score=("score", "min"),
)
print(summary)
```
