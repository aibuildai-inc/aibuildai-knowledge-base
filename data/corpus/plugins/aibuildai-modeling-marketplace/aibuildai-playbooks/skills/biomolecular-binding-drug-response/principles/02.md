## Principle 2: Make Validation Withhold the Causal Identity That Will Be Novel

*Consensus: 6/7 BELKA writeups explicitly used or analyzed building-block-aware non-shared validation. 7/14 MoA writeups used, compared, or discussed drug-group-aware validation; several others retained multilabel-stratified CV because it aligned better with their observed leaderboard regime.*

Split by the entity whose reuse would create shortcut learning: building block, scaffold, compound identity, experimental batch, or another repeated unit. Preserve target prevalence where possible, and report performance separately for seen-identity and unseen-identity strata.

**Why this matters:** Random row splits leak reusable chemical components or repeated drug signatures into validation. Such leakage measures interpolation or memorization, whereas a deployment set containing new chemistry requires extrapolation; one aggregate CV score can hide opposite behavior in these regimes.

**When to use:** Use grouped or similarity-separated validation when test molecules contain unseen building blocks/scaffolds, when multiple rows share a drug identity, or when batch effects are plausible. For mixed tests, create both an interpolation split and an extrapolation split.

**When NOT to use:** Do not automatically prefer the hardest possible group split. BELKA solutions found highly dissimilar splits noisy or over-pessimistic, and some MoA teams found ordinary multilabel stratification better aligned with the actual test mixture. In those cases, keep both diagnostics and weight them according to an evidence-based estimate of the test composition.

**How to reason about it:**
Define candidate leakage units, quantify train–test overlap for each, and reproduce the expected novelty distribution. Check fold target counts and similarity distributions—not just fold sizes. Compare model rankings across random, grouped, and similarity-separated CV; instability is evidence that the validation regime, not a small hyperparameter difference, dominates the decision.

```python
import numpy as np
from sklearn.model_selection import GroupKFold

# Customize groups: drug_id, scaffold_id, or an exclusive building-block cluster.
groups = df[novelty_group_col].to_numpy()
cv = GroupKFold(n_splits=5)

for fold, (tr, va) in enumerate(cv.split(df, y=None, groups=groups)):
    assert not set(groups[tr]) & set(groups[va])
    # For multilabel data, also inspect per-target positives in each fold.
    positives = y[va].sum(axis=0)
    print(fold, positives.min(), np.median(positives))
```
