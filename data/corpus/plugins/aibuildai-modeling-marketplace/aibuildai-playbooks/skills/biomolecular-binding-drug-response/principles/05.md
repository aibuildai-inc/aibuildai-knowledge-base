## Principle 5: Use Auxiliary Supervision to Teach the Encoder Before Rare Labels

*Consensus: 4/7 BELKA writeups used or investigated molecular pretraining, including the winner’s MLM plus SMILES-to-ECFP stages. At least 7/14 MoA writeups used non-scored targets for pretraining, transfer, stacking, or auxiliary prediction.*

When scored positives are sparse, pretrain on an abundant task that is mechanistically adjacent: masked molecular tokens and descriptor/fingerprint prediction for molecules, or non-scored biological responses for assay profiles. Transfer the encoder, then fine-tune conservatively on scored targets.

**Why this matters:** Auxiliary tasks constrain representation learning with far more signal than rare endpoint labels. Fingerprint/descriptor prediction teaches molecular topology, while non-scored responses teach biological axes shared with scored mechanisms; both reduce reliance on accidental correlations in scarce positives.

**When to use:** Use auxiliary learning when unlabeled molecules, test covariates, chemical descriptors, or non-scored assay labels are available and can be consumed without target leakage. Favor auxiliary targets correlated with the scored task and evaluate gains specifically on unseen identities.

**When NOT to use:** Do not assume more pretraining data or longer pretraining is automatically better. BELKA reported that a much larger generic ZINC pretraining corpus did not help, and MoA solutions found irrelevant non-scored targets or excessive pretraining could hurt. Skip auxiliary stages when they add no OOF gain or shift the encoder away from the endpoint.

**How to reason about it:**
Choose an auxiliary task by asking what invariant it teaches, whether that invariant survives the test shift, and whether it is learnable at the available scale. Compare no pretraining, frozen transfer, and full fine-tuning. Use masked losses for partially labeled external data and prevent target labels from entering unsupervised preprocessing.

```python
# Stage 1: auxiliary representation learning
aux_logits = model.aux_head(model.encoder(x))
aux_loss = auxiliary_loss(aux_logits, aux_targets)
aux_loss.backward()

# Stage 2: replace the head and fine-tune on scored labels.
# label_mask supports external rows that label only some targets.
logits = model.scored_head(model.encoder(x))
raw = torch.nn.functional.binary_cross_entropy_with_logits(
    logits, scored_targets, reduction="none"
)
loss = (raw * label_mask).sum() / label_mask.sum().clamp_min(1)
```
