## Principle 11: Post-process According to Metric Geometry and Hard Biological Constraints

*Consensus: BELKA’s ranking metric motivated rank-based or protein-specific blending in several solutions, though only one writeup explicitly used rank ensembling. At least 6/14 MoA writeups explicitly used clipping, smoothing, class-wise blending, control handling, or confidence-aware post-processing; this principle is included because the two metrics demand opposite behavior.*

For average precision, prioritize stable within-target ordering and consider rank harmonization when model scales differ. For log loss, preserve calibrated probabilities, use restrained OOF-validated clipping or calibration, and enforce only genuinely deterministic constraints such as guaranteed null-control outputs.

**Why this matters:** Average precision is invariant to monotone score transforms within an evaluated group, so scale disagreement can be removed without losing rank information. Log loss is a strictly proper scoring rule: rank transforms discard probability meaning, while a few overconfident errors can dominate the score.

**When to use:** Use rank averaging for ranking metrics when component models have comparable ordering quality but incompatible score scales. Use probability averaging, mild clipping, and OOF calibration for log loss. Set outputs to exact structural values only when the data definition guarantees them—for example, controls defined to have no scored mechanisms.

**When NOT to use:** Do not rank-transform a log-loss submission, hard-threshold probabilities, or use aggressive class-specific corrections learned on the public leaderboard. Under AP, avoid global ranking across groups if the metric ranks separately by protein or split. Do not force control-like samples to zero merely because they look similar to controls.

**How to reason about it:**
Reproduce the metric exactly, including its grouping. Ask whether a transformation preserves what the metric rewards. Validate post-processing on OOF predictions with the same grouping and prevalence, report per-target changes, and reject gains driven by a handful of unstable classes.
