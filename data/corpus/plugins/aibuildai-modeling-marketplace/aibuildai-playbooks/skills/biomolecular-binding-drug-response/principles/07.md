## Principle 7: Handle Imbalance Without Changing What Validation Means

*Consensus: 6/7 BELKA writeups explicitly sampled negatives, used focal/weighted losses, or discussed sub-1% positives. At least 10/14 MoA writeups used label smoothing, target weighting, stratification, or another imbalance-aware device.*

Use negative subsampling, target weighting, or focal-style objectives to make training computationally effective, but evaluate on the natural prevalence and competition metric. For multi-label panels, inspect target-wise positive counts because one global weighting rule can destabilize the rarest outputs.

**Why this matters:** With very rare binders or mechanisms, uniform mini-batches spend most updates modeling easy negatives. Rebalancing improves gradient signal, but it changes the effective prior and can produce overconfident or miscalibrated predictions—especially damaging under log loss.

**When to use:** Subsample easy negatives in massive binding datasets, retain all positives, and preserve representative validation prevalence. For small multi-label assay data, use multilabel stratification, mild smoothed class weighting, or label smoothing when target-wise OOF results justify it.

**When NOT to use:** Do not evaluate on balanced validation data or blindly use extreme inverse-frequency weights. Some MoA writeups found class-balanced or focal losses ineffective, and weighted-loss CV gains did not always transfer to the leaderboard. If probability calibration is central, prefer mild weighting and calibrate only with leakage-free OOF predictions.

**How to reason about it:**
Separate optimization distribution from evaluation distribution. Track per-target AP/log loss, prediction mean versus label prior, and calibration after any sampling change. Increase rebalancing only until rare-target learning improves without causing unstable fold-to-fold confidence.

```python
from torch.utils.data import WeightedRandomSampler

# Training-only sampler; validation remains untouched.
weights = is_positive * positive_sampling_weight + (~is_positive) * 1.0
sampler = WeightedRandomSampler(weights.double(), len(weights), replacement=True)

# Mild target weighting, capped to avoid exploding rare-label gradients.
pos_weight = (n_negative / n_positive.clamp_min(1)).sqrt().clamp(max=max_weight)
criterion = torch.nn.BCEWithLogitsLoss(pos_weight=pos_weight)
```
