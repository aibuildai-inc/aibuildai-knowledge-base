## Principle 14: Train with FP16 for Efficiency, But Verify Train-Inference Consistency

*Consensus: Several competitions used FP16. ASL fingerspelling 1st: 'Important to train with mixed precision to leverage fp16 inference without performance drop.' MABe 5th: FP16 training + inference.*

Use mixed-precision (FP16) training to speed up training and reduce memory, enabling deeper models and larger batches. Ensure inference also runs in FP16 to avoid train-test mismatch.

**Why this matters:** FP16 training is 1.5-2x faster and uses half the memory, allowing larger models or batch sizes within GPU/TPU constraints. For competitions with model size limits (TFLite 40MB), FP16 models are half the size. However, if you train in FP32 and infer in FP16, numerical precision differences cause a performance drop (~0.01). Training in FP16 and inferring in FP16 avoids this mismatch.

**When to use:** Always, unless you encounter training instability (NaNs). Use automatic mixed precision (AMP in PyTorch, mixed_precision policy in TensorFlow). For TFLite submissions, export models as FP16 (half the file size). Verify that training loss doesn't NaN; if it does, scale gradients or reduce learning rate. For techniques like AWP that can cause NaNs with FP16, either skip AWP or tune its hyperparameters.

**When NOT to use:** Do not use FP16 if it causes NaN losses or gradients (tune gradient scaling first). Do not mix FP32 training and FP16 inference — either both FP32 or both FP16. Some operations (BatchNorm, LayerNorm) may need FP32 accumulation even in FP16 mode; use AMP which handles this automatically.

**How to reason about it:**
Plan to use FP16 from the start. Enable mixed precision in your training framework (PyTorch: torch.cuda.amp, TensorFlow: policy = mixed_precision.Policy('mixed_float16')). Train a small model to verify no NaNs. If NaNs appear, check: (1) learning rate too high, (2) gradient explosion (clip gradients), (3) operations that need FP32 (AMP should handle this). For inference, export model as FP16 (TFLite: converter.optimizations = [tf.lite.Optimize.DEFAULT]). Verify CV on FP16 inference matches FP16 training. If there's a mismatch, re-train in FP16.

```python
# PyTorch: Mixed precision training
from torch.cuda.amp import autocast, GradScaler
scaler = GradScaler()

for x, y in dataloader:
    optimizer.zero_grad()
    with autocast():  # FP16 forward
        logits = model(x)
        loss = criterion(logits, y)
    scaler.scale(loss).backward()  # Scaled backward
    scaler.step(optimizer)
    scaler.update()

# TensorFlow: Mixed precision policy
from tensorflow.keras.mixed_precision import Policy
policy = Policy('mixed_float16')
tf.keras.mixed_precision.set_global_policy(policy)

# TFLite: Export as FP16
converter = tf.lite.TFLiteConverter.from_keras_model(model)
converter.optimizations = [tf.lite.Optimize.DEFAULT]
converter.target_spec.supported_types = [tf.float16]
tflite_model = converter.convert()
```
