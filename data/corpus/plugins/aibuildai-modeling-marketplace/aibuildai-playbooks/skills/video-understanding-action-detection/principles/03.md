## Principle 3: Design Cross-Validation to Match Test-Set Domain Shift

*Consensus: 5/7 competitions used stratified CV by a grouping variable. ASL: by participant_id (different signers). MABe: by lab_id. YouTube8M: video-level pre-train, segment-level fine-tune.*

Split validation folds by the grouping variable that represents domain shift in the test set (participant, game, lab, video) rather than random splitting, and ensure CV tracks LB throughout training.

**Why this matters:** Random splits leak information: if the same participant/game/lab appears in both train and validation, the model learns that specific domain rather than generalizing. This causes CV-LB divergence and overfitting. Proper grouping forces the model to generalize to unseen domains. ASL 1st place: 'nearly perfect correlation between CV and public LB' with participant-based folds.

**When to use:** Always, when the test set contains unseen levels of a grouping variable. Common groups: participant/signer ID (ASL), game/match ID (sports), lab/institution (scientific data), video ID (temporal tasks). Use stratified k-fold on the grouping variable. Verify CV-LB correlation early — if they diverge, your split is wrong. For temporal localization, maintain video-level split but evaluate segment-level.

**When NOT to use:** Do not use grouping splits when test data comes from the same distribution as train (rare). Do not split by label or action type — that tests memorization, not generalization. When the test set is very small, local CV becomes less stable and may not perfectly track LB due to variance, but grouping still reduces overfitting.

**How to reason about it:**
Ask: What varies between train and test that my model must generalize across? (new signers, new games, new labs, new videos). That variable becomes your grouping key. Use StratifiedKFold or StratifiedGroupKFold on it. If multiple grouping levels exist (e.g., lab + video), split by the higher level. For small test sets, expect some CV-LB noise but maintain the grouping principle. Track CV vs public LB in early submissions — if correlation is poor, revisit your split.
