## Principle 4: Apply Modality-Specific Augmentations Aggressively for Regularization

*Consensus: All top solutions used heavy augmentation. ASL fingerspelling 1st: CutMix +0.005, FingerDropout +0.005, temporal stretch. MABe 2nd: Gaussian noise, time stretching, Mixup.*

Augmentations must match the data modality: temporal resampling and masking for sequences, spatial affine for images, invariance-preserving transforms for pose data. Apply them with high probability (50-80%) to enable deeper models and longer training.

**Why this matters:** Training from scratch or fine-tuning with limited data causes overfitting, especially with deep models and 300+ epochs. Augmentation acts as regularization, allowing larger models and longer training to improve generalization. The right augmentations also teach invariances: time stretch makes the model robust to signing speed, CutMix teaches partial sequence reasoning, spatial affine handles camera angle variation.

**When to use:** Always. For landmark/pose sequences: (1) temporal — random resample (0.5x-1.5x speed), time shift, frame masking/dropout, (2) spatial — affine (scale, rotate, shear, translate), landmark dropout (fingers, body parts), flip. For video: (1) spatial — random crop, color jitter, cutout, (2) temporal — frame sampling strategy, (3) Mixup (for images/sequences, not transformers). For both: (3) domain-specific — e.g., finger tree rotation for hands, swap endzone/sideline views for multi-view video. Use 50-80% application probability for core augmentations.

**When NOT to use:** Do not break semantic meaning: don't flip when left/right hand matters (unless you also flip the label), don't time-reverse for causal tasks. Do not use augmentations that the model easily learns to ignore or that hurt CV. ASL 1st place: Mixup didn't work for transformers because defining labels for mixed-length sequences is unclear. Test each augmentation's impact via ablation.

**How to reason about it:**
For your modality, list invariances the model should learn. For landmarks: speed, translation, scale, rotation, hand choice (flip), missing data (dropout). For video: viewpoint, lighting, crop. Design augmentations that enforce these. Start with standard augmentations (resample, affine, flip), measure CV, then add domain-specific ones (finger dropout, CutMix) with ablation. If the model overfits (CV-LB gap > 0.02), increase augmentation probability or add new augmentations. Balance: too little → overfit, too much → underfit or slow convergence.
