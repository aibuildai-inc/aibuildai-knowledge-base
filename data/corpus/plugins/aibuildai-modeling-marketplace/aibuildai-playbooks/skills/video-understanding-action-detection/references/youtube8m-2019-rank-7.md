My approach is fairly simple. First, train a video-level model on the frame-level dataset. Then fine-tune the model on the segments dataset to create segment classifiers. There are two types of the segment classifiers — context-aware and context-agnostic. The final predictions come from simply averaging the probabilities (I did not try other averaging methods) generated by these classifiers (with roughly 1:1 mix of the two types). 

## Video-level model

Only some subsets of the frame-level training and validation datasets were used to train the base video-level model. They add up to just under 1TB. I’ll provide a list of the tfrecord files used in the code repository.

Only videos tagged with the 1000 relevant labels were used in training. This was mainly to speed up training. I did not try training with the full set of labels. 

A random sample of 150 frames are drawn from a video when its length is larger than that.

I had two video-level models that were good enough at the end of the competition:

- A NeXtVLAD model with `n_clusters = 64` and `n_groups = 16`. Trained 200k steps with batch size 48 and slanted triangular learning rates (`max_lr = 3e-4`). Training took almost 15 hours with a single P100 GPU.
- A context-gated dbof model with `frame_dim = 4096` and `video_dim = 2048`. Trained 100k steps with batch size 128 and slanted triangular learning rates (`max_lr = 3e-4`). Training took 13 hours with a single T4 GPU (this is a rough estimate because the instance was preempted several times). 

## Segment classification model

The segments dataset was split into 8 folds (by tfrecord files). 

In addition to the 5 seconds/frames labeled by human, the previous and later _n_ frames are also included to the training example. So for a labeled segment start at time t, the actual segment fed to the model starts from _t-n_ and ends at _t+4+n_. I found _n=3_ works best in early experiments and stuck with it. This particular choice probably caused some overfitting to the leaderboard. 

The context-agnostic classifiers are just the video-level models fine-tuned on the segments datasets. 

A context-aware classifier consists of three parts: a video encoder, a segment encoder, and a fully connected classifier. A video encoder is created by removing the final logistic (mixture-of-experts) layer of a video-level model. I tried different ways of creating segment encoders, and found that using the same method as video encoder seemed to create the fewest complications. The encoded vectors are concatenated and fed to the fully connected classifier (one linear layer plus ReLU activation on top of the mixture-of-experts classifier).

The first 6 segments and the last 2 segments in the video features are dropped before being fed to the video encoder to reduce noise (probably should have also done this when training video-level models). Like in the training of video-level models, 100 ~ 200 random samples are drawn when the video length is larger than that.

The weights of the video encoder are frozen when training (that is, they are not being updated). I tried fine-tuning the video encoder but did not find significant benefit from doing so. 

The learning rate of the segment encoder is set to be half the learning rate of the fully connected classifier (a.k.a. discriminative learning rates).

I also did some negative label mining to speed up the convergence.

## Inference

Just like in the training of segment classifiers, the first 6 segments and the last 2 segments in a video are skipped, and the segment starts from _t-n_ and ends at _t+4+n_ for a segment starts at time _t_ on paper. 

The probabilities were discretized from 0. ~ 1.0 to 0 ~ 9999 and put into 10000 buckets. Regular pruning is performed to reduce memory usage. The most probable 100,000 segments for each class were put into the final submission.

## Implementation

I use PyTorch in the whole pipeline. The data is directly loaded from tfrecord files. This means that the data loading might have been sub-optimal. I did find a very small amount of examples have video features of length 0. Those examples are discarded in both training and inference. I considered converting tfrecord files to other format to speed up the loading and avoid grouping segments from the same video in the same batch, but decided not to for better flexibility.

The NeXtVLAD ,context-gated DBoF, and mixture-of-experts models are re-implemented in PyTorch with some small modifications.
