## Principle 5: Exploit Metadata and Hard Biological Constraints Deliberately

*Consensus: 6/6 families used metadata or task structure, including antibody identity, plate candidate sets, sites, organs, pixel size, WSI origin, glomeruli, or image type.*

Use metadata to define candidate labels, routing, auxiliary targets, grouping, exclusions, or retrieval—not merely as extra scalar features. Separate hard constraints guaranteed by the data-generation process from correlations that may shift.

**Why this matters:** Experimental design can reduce an ambiguous visual problem to a constrained assignment problem. Anatomical metadata can prevent known false positives, while auxiliary organ or scale prediction can force representations to encode useful context.

**When to use:** Use plate-specific candidate masks and one-to-one assignment when guaranteed; aggregate replicate sites; exclude predictions in explicitly forbidden regions; route TMA versus WSI; and consider metric-learning retrieval when an identity such as antibody or perturbation produces repeated morphology.

**When NOT to use:** Do not hard-code frequency patterns, hospital-label correlations, or public-test quirks unless they are guaranteed for the hidden test. Metadata can become a shortcut and harm generalization when collection policy changes.

**How to reason about it:**
Classify each metadata relationship as invariant, likely stable, or incidental. Enforce invariants as hard decoding constraints, use likely-stable variables for routing or auxiliary learning, and use incidental variables only for grouped validation and error analysis.
