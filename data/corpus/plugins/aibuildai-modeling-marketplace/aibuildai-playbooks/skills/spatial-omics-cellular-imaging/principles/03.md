## Principle 3: Decompose Large Fields Without Throwing Away Context

*Consensus: 5/6 families made decomposition central; the remaining perturbation task still aggregated multiple sites rather than relying on a single view.*

Use cell masks, tissue masks, padded tiles, or learned region selection to reduce large images to meaningful instances. Preserve enough surrounding context for membrane, neighborhood, vessel, and tissue-architecture cues.

**Why this matters:** Uniform resizing wastes capacity on background and destroys fine structure. Conversely, tight crops can remove relational evidence, create boundary artifacts, and split objects across tile edges.

**When to use:** Use cell crops for single-cell outputs, tissue-filtered tiles for WSI, overlapping or neighbor-padded tiles for segmentation, and top-k or attention selection when only a small fraction of a slide is diagnostic. Route WSI and TMA separately if they differ in scale and composition.

**When NOT to use:** Whole-image inference can be preferable when the field fits memory, global arrangement is essential, or tile selection is less reliable than the downstream model. Avoid hard top-k selection early in training if the selector has weak labels and may systematically miss rare morphology.

**How to reason about it:**
Measure foreground fraction, object size relative to image size, and GPU/I/O cost. Compare three representations: full image, tight ROI, and ROI plus context. For tiled inference, validate overlap, edge weighting, and whether neighboring tiles are available.
