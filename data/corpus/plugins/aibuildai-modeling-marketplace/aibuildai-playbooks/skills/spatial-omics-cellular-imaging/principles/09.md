## Principle 9: Optimize Rare Classes and Open-Set Behavior for the Actual Metric

*Consensus: 5/6 families had severe imbalance, rare morphology, or an explicit unseen/outlier class; leading solutions used weighted sampling/losses, targeted mining, metric learning, or synthetic negatives.*

Separate representation learning from decision policy for rare labels. Balance training enough to expose rare morphology, then calibrate class-specific thresholds, retrieval distances, or rejection rules against macro F1, balanced accuracy, or AP.

**Why this matters:** Overall loss and accuracy are dominated by common classes, whereas macro metrics give rare classes equal influence. Open-set classes are harder: the model must estimate unfamiliarity even when no labeled training example exists.

**When to use:** Use multilabel-stratified folds, clipped class weights, focal loss, targeted oversampling, rare-instance mining, or synthetic 'Other' bags when validation supports them. Metric-learning distance is useful when known classes form coherent morphological neighborhoods.

**When NOT to use:** Do not assume inverse-frequency weighting is always beneficial; it can overfit mislabeled rare examples. In assignment-constrained multiclass tasks, global decoding may solve imbalance more safely than aggressive resampling. Do not define 'Other' as merely low softmax unless calibrated on realistic negatives.

**How to reason about it:**
Track per-class precision-recall curves and confidence distributions by domain. Distinguish 'rare but known' from 'unknown.' For known rare classes, improve exposure and calibration; for unknowns, collect heterogeneous negatives or use retrieval/energy scores and validate rejection separately.
