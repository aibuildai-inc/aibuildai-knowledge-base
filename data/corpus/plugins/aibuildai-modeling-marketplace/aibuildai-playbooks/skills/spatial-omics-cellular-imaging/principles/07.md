## Principle 7: Bridge Weak Labels With Multi-Instance Consistency

*Consensus: 2/6 families were intrinsically weakly supervised, and leading solutions in both repeatedly used CAM, MIL, dual-level heads, or selected-instance learning; related ideas also appeared in noisy segmentation data.*

When only a bag is labeled, jointly reason about bag and instance predictions rather than copying the bag label to every cell or tile. Combine global evidence with local evidence and explicitly encourage coverage when the target may occur in several instances.

**Why this matters:** Naively assigning a slide or image label to every patch creates systematic false positives. Standard CAM tends to focus on the single most discriminative region, causing low recall when many cells must be labeled.

**When to use:** Use attention MIL for slide labels, cell-aware CAM for image-to-cell transfer, a dual head with a strong bag loss and lower-weight uncertain instance loss, or teacher-generated soft instance labels. Product-like fusion is useful when both bag presence and local compatibility must be true.

**When NOT to use:** Do not add MIL when dense or instance labels are already trustworthy. Attention scores are not automatically calibrated instance labels, and hard pseudo-labels from an immature teacher can amplify localization errors.

**How to reason about it:**
Determine the bag-label semantics: 'at least one instance,' 'many instances,' or 'all instances.' Choose max/noisy-OR, attention pooling, mean pooling, or coverage regularization accordingly. Validate both bag accuracy and instance localization on a small strongly labeled audit set.
