## Principle 6: Validate on the Acquisition Hierarchy, Not Random Images

*Consensus: 6/6 families emphasized split design; top writeups grouped by WSI, experiment, patient/source, antibody or duplicate identity, and stratified rare labels or organs.*

Construct validation folds at the highest level that can leak style or biology: patient, WSI, experiment, plate, antibody, or near-duplicate group. Make at least one validation view resemble the hidden test's source and annotation quality.

**Why this matters:** Tiles, cells, sites, and images from one specimen are highly correlated. Random splitting estimates interpolation within a specimen or batch, not generalization to unseen acquisition units, and can reverse model rankings.

**When to use:** Use leave-WSI/source-out validation for histology, experiment-level folds for perturbation screens, patient grouping for clinical slides, and duplicate-aware multilabel stratification for HPA. Report scores by domain, organ, image type, and rare class.

**When NOT to use:** A strict leave-domain-out split may be too small or pessimistic if the deployment test contains the same domains. In that case maintain both an in-domain fold for optimization and a stress-test fold for model selection.

**How to reason about it:**
Write down the test-generation process and draw its hierarchy. Split at the node that differs between train and test. Check whether a model can infer fold membership from pixels; if so, random CV is likely optimistic. Prefer a lower but credible score over a high score driven by shared source artifacts.
