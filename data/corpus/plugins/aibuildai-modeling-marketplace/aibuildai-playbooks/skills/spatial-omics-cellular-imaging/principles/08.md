## Principle 8: Treat Label Quality, External Data, and Pseudo-Labels as One Lifecycle

*Consensus: 6/6 families used at least one of external images, controls, manual relabeling, unannotated images, pseudo-labeling, or mined instances; gains depended strongly on filtering and domain match.*

Expand data only after auditing provenance, duplicates, label semantics, stain/channel compatibility, and annotation quality. Generate soft pseudo-labels with out-of-fold or ensemble teachers, preserve source identity, and retrain progressively rather than merging all data at equal weight.

**Why this matters:** Additional biomedical images increase morphological coverage but often differ in protocol or ontology. High-volume noisy data can dominate scarce expert labels, while carefully selected pseudo-labels can adapt a model to the target domain and recover rare examples.

**When to use:** Use external data when it adds missing domains, rare classes, or outliers; unlabeled target-style images when domain shift is severe; and manual annotation where a small high-quality set resolves the dominant error mode. Fine-tuning on the clean target distribution after broad pretraining is often effective.

**When NOT to use:** Skip external data when labels do not map cleanly, source shortcuts are obvious, or quality cannot be validated. Stop iterative pseudo-labeling when confidence margins shrink, disagreement rises, or grouped validation degrades.

**How to reason about it:**
For every source, ask: Does it add biology or only volume? Does its label definition match? Can source be predicted trivially? Use a staged experiment: clean data baseline → source added at low weight → target-domain fine-tune. Retain only sources that improve leave-source-out validation.
