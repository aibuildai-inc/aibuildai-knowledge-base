## Principle 10: Stitch Tiles with Edge-Aware Weighting and Recover Missing Context with Views

*Consensus: 5/5 competitions used large crops, tiled inference, multiscale inference, or TTA; all 3 volumetric competitions explicitly addressed sliding-window edges or multiview aggregation.*

Infer with overlapping tiles and down-weight unreliable tile borders before merging. Add flips, scales, or orthogonal views only when they provide complementary context, and aggregate predictions in a common coordinate system.

**Why this matters:** Padding and truncated context make edge predictions less reliable, creating seams, duplicate peaks, and broken vessels. Overlap with central or Gaussian weighting suppresses these artifacts, while multiview inference lets a 2D/2.5D model approximate volumetric evidence.

**When to use:** Use whenever the full image or volume does not fit, when training crops are smaller than test fields, or when long structures cross tile boundaries. Orthogonal views are especially useful for 3D structures whose visibility varies by orientation.

**When NOT to use:** Do not maximize overlap and TTA blindly: runtime spent on nearly identical predictions may be better spent on higher resolution or model diversity. Multiview predictions can hurt when one axis has very poor resolution or the model was not trained for that view.

**How to reason about it:**
Measure seam and border errors separately. Increase overlap until decoded metrics plateau, then compare edge cropping, triangular/Gaussian weights, and larger inference patches. Benchmark each TTA/view for marginal gain per minute under the notebook limit.

```python
def weighted_stitch(volume, model, windows, weight_map):
    pred_sum = torch.zeros_like(volume, dtype=torch.float32)
    weight_sum = torch.zeros_like(volume, dtype=torch.float32)
    for slc in windows:
        logits = model(volume[slc])
        pred_sum[slc] += logits * weight_map
        weight_sum[slc] += weight_map
    return pred_sum / weight_sum.clamp_min(1e-6)
# weight_map should peak centrally and approach zero near patch borders.
```
