## Principle 13: Ensemble at the Earliest Compatible Representation

*Consensus: 4/5 competitions had top-tier probability, box, mask, fold, seed, or architecture ensembles; single models also placed first or near first in DSB 2018, BYU, and SenNet, showing that ensembling is conditional rather than mandatory.*

Combine calibrated continuous outputs before discrete decoding when models predict compatible fields; otherwise fuse boxes, peaks, or masks with geometry-aware clustering. Spend the inference budget on genuinely different errors, not many correlated checkpoints.

**Why this matters:** Early averaging reduces variance and preserves uncertainty for thresholding, whereas combining already thresholded objects discards information. However, logits from different losses or architectures can have incompatible scales, and a large ensemble may force lower resolution or weaker overlap.

**When to use:** Ensemble folds, seeds, architectures, dimensionalities, or target formulations when OOF residuals are complementary. Average logits or probabilities for aligned semantic/heatmap models; use WBF, NMS, mask voting, or distance clustering for heterogeneous detectors.

**When NOT to use:** Skip the ensemble when models make the same errors, calibration mismatch cannot be corrected, or runtime trade-offs require sacrificing resolution. A robust single model can beat a public-LB-optimized blend, as several private leaderboard results demonstrated.

**How to reason about it:**
Measure pairwise error correlation and marginal OOF gain. Normalize or calibrate outputs before blending, rerun threshold selection after every blend, and compare gain per inference minute against alternatives such as larger input scale, more overlap, or one useful TTA.

def rank_match(source, reference):
    # Useful only when two heatmaps have similar spatial meaning but different scales.
    order_s = source.flatten().argsort()
    sorted_r = reference.flatten().sort().values
    out = torch.empty_like(source.flatten())
    out[order_s] = sorted_r
    return out.view_as(source)
