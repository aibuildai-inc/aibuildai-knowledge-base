## Principle 9: Augment Acquisition Nuisances While Preserving Biological Semantics

*Consensus: 5/5 competitions relied heavily on augmentation, especially with few labeled specimens; geometric symmetries, scale variation, intensity shifts, blur/noise, and MixUp/CutMix appeared repeatedly.*

Use augmentations to model plausible changes in orientation, magnification, contrast, reconstruction quality, and local composition. Transform coordinates, masks, flows, and physical uncertainty consistently, and prefer domain-relevant perturbations over an indiscriminate long list.

**Why this matters:** A model can memorize specimen texture or scanner statistics long before it learns morphology. Plausible augmentation expands the acquisition distribution, but invalid transforms alter biological geometry or erase the weak signal the model is meant to detect.

**When to use:** Use flips and right-angle rotations when orientation is arbitrary, scale/spacing augmentation when magnification shifts, and contrast/noise/blur when acquisition quality varies. MixUp or CutMix can regularize tiny datasets if label composition remains valid.

**When NOT to use:** Do not rotate across axes with incompatible spacing without resampling, use flips when an anatomical direction is meaningful, or apply heavy blur/noise merely because it is available. Several writeups found particular noise, zoom, affine, or MixUp settings harmful outside their domain.

**How to reason about it:**
For every transform ask: could the imaging process or sample orientation create this change, and can labels be transformed exactly? Start with true symmetries, then add measured intensity and scale shifts. Ablate augmentation families on held-out acquisition domains and inspect small-object recall.

```python
# Pattern only: customize ranges from observed acquisition variation.
augment = Compose([
    RandomAxisFlip(valid_axes=(0, 1, 2)),
    RandomRotate90(valid_planes=((1, 2),)),
    RandomScale(scale_range=(0.8, 1.2), transform_points=True),
    RandomIntensityShift(std_fraction=0.15),
    OneOf([GaussianNoise(), SimulateLowResolution()], p=0.2),
])
```
