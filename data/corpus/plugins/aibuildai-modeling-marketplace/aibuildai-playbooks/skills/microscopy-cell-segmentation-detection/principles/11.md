## Principle 11: Decode Probabilities with the Topology Assumed by the Task

*Consensus: 5/5 competitions used task-specific decoding: connected components or local maxima for particles, watershed/flows or mask NMS for cells, and connectivity/morphology constraints for vessels.*

Post-process only with constraints that follow from the target topology. Convert point heatmaps through local maxima or component centroids, split cells with flows or watershed, enforce non-overlap by confidence ordering, and remove disconnected vessel fragments only when anatomy and field coverage justify it.

**Why this matters:** The network predicts local evidence, but the metric scores objects or surfaces after a discrete conversion. Correct decoding resolves duplicates, merges, overlap violations, and isolated false positives; incorrect topology rules can delete valid predictions and dominate the final score.

**When to use:** Use class-specific connected-component size filters for blob masks, NMS with a physical distance for point peaks, score-ordered overlap resolution for instance masks, and conservative 3D connectivity filters for continuous vascular trees.

**When NOT to use:** Do not assume every small component is false, every vessel belongs to one visible component, or every nearby point is a duplicate. Aggressive kidney masks and largest-component filters produced severe public/private failures when the test crop contained disconnected branches or shifted anatomy.

**How to reason about it:**
Write down the invariants guaranteed by the submission and biology: allowed overlap, expected object count, minimum physical size, minimum separation, and whether structures can leave the field of view. Tune each post-processing parameter on OOF predictions, and run ablations on small objects, border objects, and sparse domains.

```python
def decode_point_heatmap(prob, threshold, min_distance_vox):
    pooled = torch.nn.functional.max_pool3d(
        prob[None, None], kernel_size=2 * min_distance_vox + 1,
        stride=1, padding=min_distance_vox
    )[0, 0]
    peaks = (prob == pooled) & (prob >= threshold)
    return torch.argwhere(peaks)
```
