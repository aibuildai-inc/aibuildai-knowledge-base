## Principle 6: Treat Annotation Quality and Domain Match as Model Components

*Consensus: 4/5 competitions benefited materially from corrected labels, carefully staged pseudo-labels, or aligned external data; CZII top teams often found synthetic or supplemental data neutral or harmful, making the condition itself important.*

Audit labels and acquisition preprocessing before scaling the model. Add external or unlabeled data only after matching intensity, scale, and annotation policy, and use model-assisted review or staged pseudo-labeling to repair sparse and missing annotations.

**Why this matters:** A localization model trained on missing positives is explicitly taught that real objects are background. Sparse vessel labels create false-negative supervision, while external datasets with different mask boundaries or filtering create systematic target conflict. Correcting these errors can outperform architectural changes.

**When to use:** Use when visual review reveals missing objects, duplicate masks, sparse labels, or when high-recall OOF predictions expose consistent annotation gaps. For pseudo-labels, begin with domains where the teacher is reliable and expand iteratively; preserve soft probabilities when uncertainty matters.

**When NOT to use:** Do not pseudo-label an entire foreign domain in one pass or assume more data is always better. Skip external data when its reconstruction, voxel scale, cell type, or annotation convention cannot be aligned; several top solutions reported synthetic/external data failures for exactly this reason.

**How to reason about it:**
Audit a stratified sample of positives, negatives, borders, and high-loss cases. Compare external and competition intensity statistics, object sizes, and annotation rules. Train a clean baseline, generate low-threshold candidates, review disagreements, and measure the gain on untouched grouped folds. Track label provenance and sampling weights so uncertain pseudo-labels cannot dominate real dense labels.
