# Overview
Our solution is basically an AlphaZero-like policy/value-dual network + MCTS. We built the agent through several months of iterations of pretraining with behavioral cloning, model training with Deep Reinforcement Learning, MCTS improvement, benchmark evaluation, and episode generation.

[schematic illustration of our approach]

# 1. Behavioral Cloning
We collected high quality episodes and did pretraining using these. The episodes we used were those of the top agents we [scraped](https://www.kaggle.com/robga/simulations-episode-scraper-match-downloader), the best agents of our team with enhanced search using GPUs, and those generated by the agent evaluation process. In particular, episodes from the top teams were continuously collected and repeatedly injected. Since I thought that RL by self-play was itself a hate approach to its past self, I thought that RL by imitating my opponent would be advantageous in adapting to the meta-game. In addition to the 17 features in the [Public HandyRL notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/yuricat/smart-geese-trained-by-reinforcement-learning), we used floodfill and 2 food-related features. These were based on observations of our lost episodes, and the goal was to block the bad paths. The feature generation process was accelerated by numba.jit.

# 2. Reinforcement Learning
For RL, we rely heavily on a great library, [HandyRL](https://github.com/DeNA/HandyRL). Because we wanted agents to learn both the long-term big picture of the game and short-term efficiency, we ramped up and down the forward_steps and changed the gamma accordingly, it's not clear that it worked though. For the target, we used UPGO for most of the training and switched to V-trace at the end for re-training. This is because using UPGO is biased and distorts the distribution, so calibration was necessary.

# 3. MCTS
It is based on [sazuma's excellent public notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/shoheiazuma/alphageese-baseline), but with many improvements. The improvements are shown in the figure above, but mainly the approach is to handle the output value more accurately. Also, by correctly treating step199 as a final state, we were able to get a much bigger boost than I expected. This is because step 199 does not require inference by the model, so the search for steps 199 (also 198 and previous steps) can be increased significantly.

# 4. Evaluation
The evaluation was very difficult because it takes a very long time to play a game with MCTS. Another difficulty was that the initial evaluation of LB was unstable and convergence was slow. The evaluation was done in two stages: in the first stage, 224 matches were played against our standard agent (~LB1200), and the agent with the good result was added to the match pool and the match was repeated to evaluate the rating by TrueSkill. The purpose of the TrueSkill rating is to evaluate robustness by exposing our agent to diverse strategies. The evaluation results were displayed on Slack as LB in the team. At the same time, one of the purposes of this process was to collect episodes with MCTS through a huge number of matches.

# 5. Episodes Collection
In addition to scraping from meta-kaggle and generating episodes during evaluation, we also generated episodes with MCTS using the GPUs. The GPU-enabled agent was stronger in the benchmark results due to a much larger number of searches, and pretraining with that episode boosted the LB rating by about 20-30.
