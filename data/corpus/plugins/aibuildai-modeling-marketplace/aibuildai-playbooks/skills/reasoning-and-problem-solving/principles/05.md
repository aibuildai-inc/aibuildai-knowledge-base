## Principle 5: Generate Synthetic Training Data When Real Data is Scarce

*Consensus: 5/6 top math solutions, 3/3 top LLM prompt recovery solutions, 2/2 top ARC solutions*

Use strong models (GPT-4, Gemini) to generate diverse training data with verifiable correctness. Math competitions generated code-integrated solutions with GPT-4; LLM prompt recovery generated prompt variations; ARC generated new tasks following the same patterns.

**Why this matters:** Reasoning tasks often have limited training data but the solution space is well-defined. Generated data allows covering more of the distribution. AIMO 2nd place generated 12 solutions per problem with models at different training stages to get diverse correct and incorrect examples. Quality matters more than quantity - filtering for correctness and diversity is critical.

**When to use:** When training data is limited (<10k examples), when you can verify generated solutions programmatically or manually, when strong generative models are available, and when the task structure is compositional or rule-based.

**When NOT to use:** When you have abundant real data, when verification is unreliable, when generated data distribution differs significantly from test, or when the task requires domain knowledge stronger models don't have.

**How to reason about it:**
Generation strategy: (1) Prompt strong model for solutions, (2) Execute/verify to filter correct ones, (3) Generate variations (paraphrases, different approaches), (4) Balance positive/negative examples. For math: generate at different reasoning depths. For ARC: apply problem transformations (rotation + padding). For prompt recovery: cluster and balance by cluster. Always validate: does synthetic data improve validation performance?
