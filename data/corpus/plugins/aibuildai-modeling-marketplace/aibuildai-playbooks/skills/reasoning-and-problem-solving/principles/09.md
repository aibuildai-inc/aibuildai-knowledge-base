## Principle 9: For Multi-Agent Games, Bootstrap from Strong Agents Then Self-Play

*Consensus: 4/4 top Hungry Geese solutions, 3/3 top Lux AI solutions using neural nets*

Start with imitation learning or behavioral cloning from replays of top leaderboard agents, then improve via self-play RL. Download replays from strong agents, train a policy to predict their moves (behavioral cloning), optionally add MCTS or other search, then fine-tune via self-play.

**Why this matters:** Pure self-play from random initialization is very slow to learn basic competence in complex games. Bootstrapping from strong agents provides a much better starting point. Goosebumps 2nd place: behavioral cloning on top-agent replays got a strong baseline, MCTS made it competitive, opponent unpredictability modeling made it robust. Lux AI 1st place: self-play from scratch worked but only with reward shaping for the first 20M steps.

**When to use:** When strong agents exist on the leaderboard and you can download their replays, when the game is complex enough that random self-play would be too slow, and when you have the compute budget for self-play after bootstrapping.

**When NOT to use:** When you're the first to the leaderboard and no strong agents exist yet, when replay format doesn't include enough information to reconstruct the game state, or when imitation would copy the weaknesses of existing agents without a way to improve beyond them.

**How to reason about it:**
Data selection: Download replays where the weakest agent's rating is above a threshold (increase threshold over time as agents improve). This filters out low-quality data. Training: Predict moves of all agents simultaneously (don't distinguish between good and bad moves within a game). Add entropy penalty to policy loss to encourage exploration. Architecture: Use translation-equivariant models for board games. Then add MCTS or search on top of the cloned policy. Finally, fine-tune via self-play or against a league of past versions to prevent strategic collapse.
