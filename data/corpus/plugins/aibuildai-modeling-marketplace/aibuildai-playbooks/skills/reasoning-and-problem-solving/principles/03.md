## Principle 3: Scale Inference-Time Compute Over Training Compute

*Consensus: 5/5 top math solutions, 4/4 top ARC solutions using test-time adaptation, 6/6 top game solutions using search*

For reasoning tasks, generating multiple solution candidates at inference and selecting the best via voting, verification, or search often yields larger gains than training a bigger model. Math competitions use 32-96 samples with majority voting; ARC uses test-time fine-tuning; games use MCTS with 20-400k rollouts per move.

**Why this matters:** Reasoning problems have high solution diversity and verification is often cheaper than generation. Generating 48 candidates and voting (AIMO 1st place) improved accuracy more than 10x model size increase. Test-time fine-tuning improved ARC from 11→33 solved tasks. The model doesn't need to be right on the first try if you can verify and retry.

**When to use:** When you can verify solutions (math via code execution, games via rollout), when there's sufficient inference-time budget (ARC allows 12 hours), or when test instances are novel and training data doesn't cover them.

**When NOT to use:** When inference time is severely limited (<1 second per instance), when verification is unreliable or expensive, or when the test distribution exactly matches training (rare for reasoning tasks).

**How to reason about it:**
Budget inference time: small model with 50 samples often beats large model with 1 sample. For verification: use reward models (math), self-consistency voting, or adversarial checking. For search: MCTS when the state space is large, beam search when it's structured, SAT solvers when it's constraint-based. Always measure: does doubling candidates improve score more than doubling model size?
