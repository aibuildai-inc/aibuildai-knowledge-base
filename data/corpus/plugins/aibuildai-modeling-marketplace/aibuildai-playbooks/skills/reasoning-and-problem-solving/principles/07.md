## Principle 7: Train Separate Verification or Reward Models for Weighted Voting

*Consensus: 4/5 top math solutions, 2/2 top ARC solutions attempted (with mixed success)*

When generating multiple solution candidates, train a separate model to score their quality rather than using uniform voting. Math competitions used outcome reward models (ORM) trained to distinguish correct from incorrect solutions, using the scores as weights in geometric-mean voting.

**Why this matters:** Not all generated solutions are equally likely to be correct. A reward model can learn to detect subtle correctness signals that simple majority voting misses. AIMO 2nd place improved from 2/10 to 4/10 on validation by using reward-weighted voting instead of uniform voting. The key is training the reward model on diverse correct and incorrect examples.

**When to use:** When you generate many candidates (>10) and verification is not binary or deterministic, when you can create a dataset of correct vs incorrect solutions, and when the reward model can learn meaningful quality signals beyond surface features.

**When NOT to use:** When verification is perfect and deterministic (just filter to verified solutions), when you have too few candidates to benefit from weighting, or when the reward model overfits to spurious correlations (e.g., solution length).

**How to reason about it:**
Data collection: Generate solutions with models at different capability levels (interpolate checkpoints, vary training epochs) to get diverse correct and incorrect examples. Balance positive/negative labels ~1:1 per problem. Training: Use sequence classification on problem + solution. Aggregation: Use geometric mean × count instead of sum to prevent over-optimization from one high score. Validate: Does the reward model improve validation accuracy vs. uniform voting?

```python
# Weighted geometric mean voting
def weighted_vote(answers, scores):
    vote_dict = {}
    for ans, score in zip(answers, scores):
        if ans not in vote_dict: vote_dict[ans] = []
        vote_dict[ans].append(score)
    best_ans, best_weight = None, -1
    for ans, score_list in vote_dict.items():
        weight = len(score_list) * (prod(score_list) ** (1/len(score_list)))  # Geometric mean × N
        if weight > best_weight:
            best_ans, best_weight = ans, weight
    return best_ans
```
