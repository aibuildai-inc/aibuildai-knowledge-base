## Principle 13: Optimize Mean/Template Solutions for Underspecified Problems

*Consensus: 4/4 top LLM Prompt Recovery solutions*

For problems where the solution space is vast and underspecified (many valid answers), optimize a mean solution on a representative subset, then refine per-cluster or per-instance. LLM Prompt Recovery 3rd place: beam search over word combinations to find the mean prompt that maximizes score on a subsampled dataset, then use clustering to select per-cluster mean prompts.

**Why this matters:** When there's no single correct answer and the metric rewards similarity to ground truth, a well-optimized mean solution can score surprisingly well by capturing common patterns. Prompt recovery: the mean prompt scored 0.69 LB alone, approaching pure model predictions. The insight: discrete optimization over token combinations is feasible when the vocabulary is bounded and you can evaluate on a proxy dataset.

**When to use:** When solutions have high variance but cluster around common patterns, when you can define a representative validation set, and when the evaluation metric rewards overlap or similarity rather than exact match.

**When NOT to use:** When solutions are truly diverse with no common structure, when the metric heavily penalizes generic solutions, or when instance-specific prediction is required.

**How to reason about it:**
Validation set creation: Subsample or generate data that matches the test distribution. Use public leaderboard feedback to validate the match. Mean optimization: Beam search or L-BFGS over token embeddings to maximize score. Use a bounded vocabulary (e.g., most common 2000 words). Clustering: If a global mean plateaus, cluster the data (by embeddings or features) and optimize separate mean solutions per cluster. Use a classifier to route test instances to clusters. Hybrid: Combine the mean with model predictions, adding unique words from predictions to the mean.
