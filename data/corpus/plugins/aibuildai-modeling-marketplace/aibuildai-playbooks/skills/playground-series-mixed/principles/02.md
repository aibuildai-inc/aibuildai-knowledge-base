## Principle 2: Trust Cross-Validation Over Public Leaderboard

*Consensus: Emphasized in 20+ writeups, including multiple 1st place solutions. The TPS Jan-2022 winner had public LB rank 306 but won on private LB by trusting CV.*

Use local cross-validation as your primary decision-making metric. Avoid optimizing for public leaderboard scores, which may evaluate on a non-representative subset or incentivize overfitting.

**Why this matters:** Public/private leaderboard splits often differ in distribution (e.g., time series competitions where public=Q1, private=Q2-Q4 with all major holidays). Optimizing for public LB leads to overfitting temporal or distributional artifacts. The 2nd place cat-in-the-dat solution explicitly states: 'I learned to trust my CV, and only trust my CV' after shaking up from 170th to 10th in a prior competition.

**When to use:** Always. For time series, use GroupKFold with time-based splits. For imbalanced classification, use StratifiedKFold. Run 5-10 folds and trust the mean CV score. Make decisions (feature selection, hyperparameters, model selection) based on CV delta, not LB movement.

**When NOT to use:** You still need 1-2 LB submissions to verify your CV isn't completely broken (e.g., data leakage). Use LB as a sanity check that CV and LB are correlated, but don't optimize for it.

**How to reason about it:**
For every decision, ask: Did this improve my CV score? By how much? Is the improvement consistent across multiple random seeds? If public LB disagrees with CV, investigate why (e.g., distribution shift) but don't abandon CV. The TPS Jan-2022 winner evaluated SMAPE separately for Jan-Mar vs Apr-Dec in CV, optimizing for Apr-Dec since that's where holidays (and private LB) were.

from sklearn.model_selection import StratifiedKFold, cross_val_score
import numpy as np

# Stratified CV for classification
skf = StratifiedKFold(n_splits=5, shuffle=True, random_state=42)
cv_scores = cross_val_score(model, X, y, cv=skf, scoring='roc_auc')
print(f'CV AUC: {cv_scores.mean():.5f} +/- {cv_scores.std():.5f}')
