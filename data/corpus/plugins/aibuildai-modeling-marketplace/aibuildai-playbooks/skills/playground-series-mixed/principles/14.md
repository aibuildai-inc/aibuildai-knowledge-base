## Principle 14: Consider AutoML for Fast Competitive Baselines

*Consensus: 4+ solutions used AutoGluon successfully, including a 1st place win. Multiple solutions used H2O, LightAutoML.*

AutoML frameworks (AutoGluon, LightAutoML, FLAML) can produce strong baselines quickly by automating model selection, hyperparameter tuning, and ensembling. Use as a starting point or ensemble component.

**Why this matters:** AutoML handles the tedious work of model selection and hyperparameter tuning. The playground-series-s3e1 winner used only AutoGluon with minimal feature engineering and won. AutoGluon trains multiple architectures (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, RF, NeuralNets), tunes hyperparameters, and stacks them. For time-constrained competitions, AutoML provides a strong baseline in 1-2 hours.

**When to use:** Use AutoGluon as an initial baseline to understand problem difficulty. If it achieves competitive CV scores, incorporate its predictions into your ensemble. If it underperforms, investigate why (e.g., it missed domain-specific features). AutoML is especially valuable for: (1) quick prototyping, (2) learning which model families work, (3) generating diverse base models for stacking.

**When NOT to use:** AutoML may underfit domain-specific patterns (e.g., time series with complex seasonality) that require manual feature engineering. The TPS May-2021 winner: 'Don't get me wrong those [AutoML results] are very helpful but in this competition they did not help my local cv so I did not use them at the end.' Don't blindly trust AutoML if CV doesn't validate its performance.

**How to reason about it:**
Run AutoGluon with time_limit=3600 (1 hour) as a first step. Check its CV score vs your manual models. If AutoGluon is competitive, use its predictions as a base model in your ensemble. If it's weak, investigate: did it miss important features? Does the problem require domain expertise? Use AutoML as a sanity check and ensemble component, not the sole solution.

from autogluon.tabular import TabularPredictor

# Quick AutoGluon baseline
