## Principle 13: Train Coarse-to-Fine or Use Progressive Resizing for Efficiency

*Consensus: Seen in top Bengali and ECG solutions. Train at low resolution for many epochs, then fine-tune at higher resolution (Bengali 7th: 128 then 224; ECG 7th: five size stages).*

Start training at lower resolution for faster iteration and broader exploration, then fine-tune at higher resolution for final detail. Reduces training time while maintaining final quality.

**Why this matters:** Training at high resolution (512+) is 4-16× slower than low resolution (128). Starting low lets you iterate augmentations, architectures, and hyperparameters faster. Fine-tuning at high resolution captures fine details for final boost. Bengali 7th place: 128→224 gave faster training and maybe better generalization.

**When to use:** When training time is a bottleneck, when you need to iterate hyperparameters quickly, when fine details matter for final accuracy but coarse features suffice for early training, or when GPU memory limits high-resolution batch size.

**When NOT to use:** When low-resolution training harms generalization (some features only visible at high resolution), when you have ample compute and prefer single-stage, or when resolution shift introduces distribution mismatch that fine-tuning can't recover from.

**How to reason about it:**
Stage 1: Train at a low resolution for many epochs (Bengali 7th: about 100 epochs at 128px). Tune augmentations, architecture, loss. Stage 2: Load best weights, fine-tune at the target resolution with lower LR. For very wide or long inputs, raise the size in several stages (ECG 7th: 20 epochs per stage, from 224x896 up to 336x3584). Optionally train the last few epochs with no augmentation. Validate that final high-res model beats single-stage high-res training in accuracy and total time.

```python
# Progressive resizing
# Stage 1: Train at 128x128
model = Model(backbone='resnet50')
train(model, data, input_size=128, epochs=100, lr=1e-3)

# Stage 2: Fine-tune at 224x224
# Load weights from stage 1
model.load_state_dict(torch.load('model_128.pth'))
train(model, data, input_size=224, epochs=20, lr=1e-4)

# Optional stage 3: Final fine-tune at 224 without augmentation
train(model, data, input_size=224, epochs=5, lr=1e-5, augmentation=None)

# Benefits: ~3x faster total training time, similar final accuracy
```
