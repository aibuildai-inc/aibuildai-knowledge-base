## Principle 11: Enforce Global Consistency, but Re-Verify Inferred Relations

*Consensus: Cross-demonstration consistency was central to at least 10/12 ARC approaches; graph consistency or traversal appeared in at least 11/16 substantive Foursquare solutions.*

Use structural constraints after local scoring: one program should explain every demonstration, and an entity match should be symmetric and approximately transitive. When closure creates a relation that was never scored directly, verify it or require stronger path evidence before adding it.

**Why this matters:** Local decisions ignore shared context. Intersecting programs across demonstrations removes accidental fits, while graph paths can recover missed edges; however, one false positive can contaminate an entire connected component if transitive closure is applied blindly.

**When to use:** Use exact cross-example consistency when labels are reliable and the rule should be shared. Use union-find, shortest paths, message passing, or cluster-average linkage when the target is genuinely an equivalence relation.

**When NOT to use:** Avoid full closure for asymmetric, temporal, hierarchical, or context-dependent relations. Even for equivalence matching, use conservative graph logic when pair scores are poorly calibrated or large clusters contain common generic names.

**How to reason about it:**
Identify the algebraic properties of the target relation—symmetry, transitivity, equivariance, cardinality—and enforce only those justified by the task. Raise evidence requirements with path length or cluster size, and score newly induced edges where feasible.

graph = build_symmetric_graph(pair_scores)
