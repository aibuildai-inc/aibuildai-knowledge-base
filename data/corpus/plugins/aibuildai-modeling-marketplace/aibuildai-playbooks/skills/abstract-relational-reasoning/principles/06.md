## Principle 6: Match Model Capacity to the Amount and Kind of Supervision

*Consensus: 10/12 ARC writeups favored DSLs, simple trees, or explicit operations; 14/16 substantive Foursquare writeups used learned pair models after blocking.*

Under tiny per-task supervision, prefer interpretable compositional primitives, low-depth search, or low-capacity trees whose bias is the main source of generalization. With abundant cross-entity labels, use stronger GBDTs, language models, or metric learning—but only after constraining their candidate universe.

**Why this matters:** Capacity that is appropriate globally may be inappropriate locally. In ARC, CNN image generation commonly failed because three to five examples cannot identify a large function; in Foursquare, rich learned similarities improved discrimination because millions of records supplied repeated evidence about noise patterns.

**When to use:** Use symbolic or compact learners when exact consistency and extrapolation to unseen symbols matter more than fitting average patterns. Use learned encoders and pair classifiers when noise mechanisms recur across many labeled groups.

**When NOT to use:** Do not interpret this as “symbolic for images, neural for text.” A pixel tree can solve local transformations, and a learned metric can aid program proposal if meta-training is sufficient. The relevant variable is effective supervision per degree of freedom.

**How to reason about it:**
Estimate how many independent relation groups supervise each model parameter or primitive. If many hypotheses fit all demonstrations, reduce capacity or strengthen priors; if a bounded candidate set still contains subtle, repeated ambiguities, increase verifier capacity.

```python
if supervision.per_task_is_tiny:
    verifier = CompositionalSearch(max_depth=depth_budget, typed=True)
elif supervision.independent_groups_is_large:
    verifier = PairEnsemble([GBDT(), TextPairEncoder()])
else:
    verifier = HybridRanker(symbolic_features=True, learned_embedding=True)
```
