## Principle 7: Validate on Unseen Relation Groups and the Real Candidate Universe

*Consensus: Group-aware validation was explicitly discussed in at least 8/16 substantive Foursquare writeups; leading ARC writeups also measured train/evaluation transfer and observed distribution shift.*

Split by the latent relation group—not by rows or generated pairs—and reproduce deployment-scale candidate competition. For few-shot tasks, validate on entirely held-out tasks; for entity matching, keep every record of a POI in one fold and preserve realistic database size and density.

**Why this matters:** Pair-level random splits leak entity identity and make nearly duplicate records appear on both sides. Retrieval quality also degrades as the candidate universe grows, so evaluating a small validation database can overstate both recall and calibration.

**When to use:** Always use group-disjoint validation when rows share entities, tasks, scenes, users, or templates. Recreate inference-time candidate generation inside each fold rather than evaluating a classifier on a fixed, unrealistically easy pair table.

**When NOT to use:** A row split is acceptable only when observations are demonstrably independent and no latent group contributes to multiple rows. If hidden groups cannot be identified, use conservative temporal, geographic, or source-based splits as proxies.

**How to reason about it:**
Ask what unit must generalize at test time. Then verify that no representation, target encoding, candidate-generation model, or post-processing statistic was fitted using the held-out unit. Compare metrics at several validation-universe sizes to detect scale sensitivity.

for train_groups, valid_groups in group_split(groups, n_splits=K):

    valid = data[data.group.isin(valid_groups)]
