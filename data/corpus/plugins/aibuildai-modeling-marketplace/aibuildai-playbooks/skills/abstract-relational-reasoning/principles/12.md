## Principle 12: Use the Output Budget for Complementary Hypotheses

*Consensus: Complementary solver components were combined in at least 8/12 ARC writeups and multiple leading Foursquare teams blended retrieval views or model families; the benefit was strongest when errors were non-overlapping.*

When multiple outputs or model components are allowed, select hypotheses by expected coverage and error diversity rather than taking near-duplicates with the highest individual scores. Diversity may come from different representations, routing branches, proposal channels, or inductive biases.

**Why this matters:** ARC's top-three metric directly rewards covering distinct plausible programs. In entity matching, model or retrieval diversity improves robustness only when components recover different positives or reject different false positives; correlated ensembles mostly add cost.

**When to use:** Use diverse top-k prediction when demonstrations underdetermine the rule, and blend models when out-of-fold residuals are meaningfully different. Team or solver merges are especially valuable when solved-instance overlap is low.

**When NOT to use:** Do not force diversity that produces implausible outputs, and do not average scores with incompatible calibration. If one model dominates every subgroup, spend the budget on its uncertainty estimates or proposal expansion instead.

**How to reason about it:**
Cluster candidate outputs by structural similarity, estimate correctness from held-out tasks, and select representatives that maximize expected union coverage subject to quality. For model ensembles, inspect residual correlation and subgroup wins rather than leaderboard deltas alone.

```python
selected = []
while len(selected) < output_budget:
    candidate = max(pool, key=lambda c: quality(c) + diversity_weight * novelty(c, selected))
    selected.append(candidate)
    pool.remove(candidate)
return selected
```
