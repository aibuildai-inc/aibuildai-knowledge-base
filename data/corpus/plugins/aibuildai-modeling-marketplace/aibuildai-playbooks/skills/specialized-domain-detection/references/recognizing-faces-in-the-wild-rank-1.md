# 1st place solution - few notes

Competition: recognizing-faces-in-the-wild
Rank: #1
Source: https://www.kaggle.com/c/recognizing-faces-in-the-wild/discussion/103670#latest-605952

Let me start expressing all the happiness for the results achieved. This was my first competition and I really learned a lot from all the discussions and in particular from the public kernels. 
My final model combined the results of 37 different submission files (a special thanks to @vaishvik25 for opening my mind to the power of model ensemble)
7 of the 37 submission files are coming from public kernels (see the list of reference at the end) and the others 30 are generated by me. 
My understanding is that the ensemble of different model outputs work well if two conditions are met:
each model output is good (I considered a minimum score of 0.82 as reasonable threshold) and all models are (more or less) independent.
The requirement of independence is probably the most difficult to achieve.
How to get independent solutions?

I made use of 3 different aspects to generate my 30 models
1)
In the excellent discussion thread https://www.kaggle.com/c/recognizing-faces-in-the-wild/discussion/92454#latest-594706  it was suggested to concatenate quadratic combinations of VGG feature output (e.g. x1^2-x2^2 and (x1-x2)^2). I could not understand or find a reason for this particular choice and therefore I started to explore the different combinations (e.g. x1+x2 and x1-x2 or sqrt(x1) + sqrt(x2) and sqrt(x1) - sqrt(x2)).
Surprisingly concatenating different combinations of features lead to models that are not fully correlated. And most of them are pretty accurate.    
2) 
For each model xx based on VGGFace there is a similar model yy based on Facenet. For both models the score is similar but the two results are pretty much unrelated. As a further step VGGFace and Facenet features can be combined together in the same model to generate a third model zz.
As example my single best scoring model (0.893 on public leaderboard) https://www.kaggle.com/mattemilio/smile-best-who-smile-last  concatenate together combinations of features of VGGFace and Facenet
3)
Changing the structure of the FC layers after concatenation (number of layers, size, dropout rate)

How to combine different solutions?
I tried both a simple average of all model outputs and weighted sum approaches (giving more weight to solutions that have higher score and are more uncorrelated. The second approach is slightly better but the difference is really minor (maybe in the noise)

**Personal conclusions**
The most important lesson that I learned from this competition is that few good models combined together are better than a single excellent model.
I would be also curious to see the achievable limit of kinship recognition. Ensembling more models was constantly improving the score and I could not see a saturation yet.
