## Principle 8: Stack Diverse Models with a Second-Level Meta-Learner

*Consensus: Camera ID 1st/10th (52-model blend, XGBoost stacker), audio tagging 4th (meta-learning ensemble), passenger screening 1st/4th (gradient-boosted models trained on out-of-fold or holdout scores)*

Train a diverse ensemble of first-level models (different architectures, input representations, augmentations), then train a second-level model (XGBoost, LightGBM, or MLP) on their holdout predictions to learn optimal blending weights.

**Why this matters:** Specialized signals are multifaceted: spatial models capture different patterns than frequency or noise-feature models, small models learn different features than large models, and different augmentations expose different failure modes. A simple average assumes all models are equally reliable, but a meta-learner can upweight models that complement each other and downweight redundant ones. The camera model ID 10th place turned to a second-layer XGBoost model after plain blending of its CNNs stopped improving; the stack of 9 first-level models (6 image CNNs and 3 models on sensor-noise features) was its best submission (0.9877 public, 0.9866 private).

**When to use:** When you have diverse first-level models (different domains, architectures, crop sizes) and a holdout set large enough to train a second-level model without overfitting (thousands of samples). The more diversity in your ensemble, the more a meta-learner can exploit it. The camera ID 1st place used 52 models with XGBoost/LightGBM/Keras stackers.

**When NOT to use:** When your models are homogeneous (all EfficientNet-B7 with similar augmentations), stacking won't help much over averaging. Also skip if your holdout is too small (<1000 samples) or you lack time to train the stacker. The power line 6th place could not build a stable stack and used the arithmetic mean; the deepfake 5th place found a learned fusion of its 3D and 2D models did worse than a simple 50:50 pooling; the faces 1st place found weighted blending only marginally better than a simple average.

**How to reason about it:**
Measure the diversity of your models: compute pairwise correlation of their predictions. If correlation <0.9, stacking can help. The faces 1st place kept models that each scored well and were as independent of each other as possible. Train your first-level models on a K-fold split, collect out-of-fold predictions, then train XGBoost/LightGBM on those predictions as features. Validate the stacker with nested cross-validation to avoid overfitting. If stacking does not beat averaging by more than the fold-to-fold noise, it's not worth the complexity.
