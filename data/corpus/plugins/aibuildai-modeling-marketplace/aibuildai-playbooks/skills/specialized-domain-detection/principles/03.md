## Principle 3: Use Larger Crops to Capture Contextual Information

*Consensus: Deepfake 1st/2nd (face crops with a large margin around the face), camera model ID 3rd (train on small patches, fine-tune on larger ones); camera model ID 1st/5th found small crops nearly as accurate and much faster*

Give the model enough spatial context around the signal—a larger crop, or a wider margin around the region of interest—because the evidence often lies in how a region relates to its surroundings, not in the region alone.

**Why this matters:** Many specialized signals are only interpretable in context. In deepfake detection, a tight face crop hides the boundary where the swapped face is blended into the frame and the difference between how the face and its surroundings were encoded; the 2nd place found a margin of 0.5-0.7 of the face size worked significantly better than no margin, and the 1st place added 30% of the face size on each side. Similarly, camera fingerprints and forgery boundaries are more distinct when the model sees surrounding regions. Larger crops also reduce edge artifacts from tiling.

**When to use:** When the evidence forms coherent structures across space or depends on the boundary between a region and its surroundings: face manipulations (blending extends beyond tight crops), forgeries (copy-paste regions are large), camera fingerprints (more pixels give a stronger noise estimate). The deepfake 2nd place raised its face margin step by step (0.05 → 0.1 → 0.5 → 0.7), and its public score improved at each step.

**When NOT to use:** When your signal is purely local (single-pixel sensor defects) or when larger crops don't fit in memory and the training time penalty is prohibitive. The camera model ID 1st place called its full-size 512 crops its biggest flaw: smaller crops train faster, allow more TTA options and gave almost the same accuracy; the 5th place trained on 224 crops with 8-minute epochs. Some deepfake teams also kept margins small (27th) or inputs small (37th) on purpose, expecting better robustness to unseen manipulations, so test whether crop size actually bottlenecks you.

**How to reason about it:**
Ablate crop size (or margin) on a validation fold: train one model at 128×128, one at 256×256, one at 512×512. If performance improves monotonically and wall-clock time stays acceptable, scale up. A cheap path is the camera model ID 3rd place's recipe: train on small patches, then fine-tune on larger ones. Check visual outputs where you can: do small-crop predictions look noisy/patchy while large-crop predictions are smooth and coherent? That's your signal that context matters. For video or volumetric data, the same logic applies to the time or Z axis: more frames or slices = more context.

```python
# Face crop with a margin around the detected box
def crop_with_margin(frame, box, margin=0.5):
    """box = (x1, y1, x2, y2) from a face detector.
    New crop size = face size * (1 + margin), centered on the face."""
    x1, y1, x2, y2 = box
    w, h = x2 - x1, y2 - y1
    pad_w, pad_h = margin * w / 2, margin * h / 2
    x1 = max(0, int(x1 - pad_w))
    y1 = max(0, int(y1 - pad_h))
    x2 = min(frame.shape[1], int(x2 + pad_w))
    y2 = min(frame.shape[0], int(y2 + pad_h))
    return frame[y1:y2, x1:x2]
```
