## Principle 6: Trust Local Cross-Validation Over Public Leaderboard

*Consensus: Camera model ID 1st/3rd (tuning to the public LB overfit; trust your validation), power line 9th/38th (picked the stable or conservative model over the best public score), scientific image forgery 1st (built a clean validation set)*

When the public test set is small or unrepresentative, your local validation is a more reliable signal for model selection than the public leaderboard. Choose models and ensembles based on CV, not LB.

**Why this matters:** Specialized domain competitions often have tiny or skewed public test sets because the data is expensive to collect or annotate. In camera model ID, the 1st place forced every class to be equally represented with the Hungarian algorithm and scored 0.991 public but 0.985 private, while its hold-out blend scored 0.986 public and 0.989 private; the 9th place saw class balancing help the public LB and later overfit. In power line fault detection, train and test distributions differed: the 9th place kept a conservative model that was only modest on the public LB, and the 38th place picked the model whose CV and public scores agreed (0.659 and 0.652), which then scored 0.667 private. Public LB optimization is direct overfitting when the set is unrepresentative.

**When to use:** Always in specialized domains where the public test set is <500 samples, or when you know it's drawn from a different distribution than private (different cameras, samples, video sources). Also when the task has high variance (small test sets have high variance) or you suspect the organizers deliberately made public/private different to prevent LB probing.

**When NOT to use:** When public and private test sets are known to be drawn identically and the public set is large (thousands of samples). Also, if your local CV is unstable or you have severe train/test distribution shift and no good validation strategy, the LB becomes your only signal—but this is a red flag that your CV split is wrong. The deepfake team that finished 2nd on the public LB chose the LB over its uncorrelated CV and finished 30th private, so a distribution shift is a reason to fix validation (the forgery detection 1st place re-annotated its validation pairs), not to chase the LB.

**How to reason about it:**
Check the public LB size and your model's variance on it. If the public set is <500 samples and your submissions' scores vary by >2% despite nearly identical models, the LB is noisy. Compare your local CV ranking of models to their public LB ranking; if they disagree (model A better CV, model B better LB), trust CV. The camera model ID 1st place's two final submissions show the pattern: the class-equalized one led on public (0.991 vs 0.986) and lost on private (0.985 vs 0.989). Set up a rigorous local validation (stratified, grouped, or holdout mirroring test conditions) and use it as your north star.
