# Solution Summary
I generated approximately 10.6 million images from around 8.6 million prompts. Various methods were utilized to accelerate the image generation process. These images and prompts were used to train ViT-Large, ConvNeXt-XXLarge, and BLIP-2 models. Modifications were made to these models, such as removing the Large Language Model (LLM) component of BLIP-2 and employing LORA. My final submission involved a weighted ensemble of embeddings from the three aforementioned model types.

# Datasets
In this competition, having diverse and high-quality data is important. To achieve this, I first utilized DiffusionDB_2M and prioritized datasets relevant to image captioning tasks, including COCO Captions, VizWiz, Open Image, ADE20K, Flickr30K, and TextCaps. Duplicates with a similarity score >0.8 were removed. Finally, prompts generated by ChatGPT were also added into the dataset with stricter rules, resulting in a dataset of around 2 million high-quality prompts that we refer to as "PROMPTS_HQ" In the following text. For every prompt, I generated 2 images using different random seeds. 

COYO-700M is a large-scale dataset that contains 747M image-text pairs, I attempted to select and add some of them to the PROMPT_HQ dataset, it did not improve the score. Therefore, I utilized this dataset for pretraining and found it to be highly effective(+0.008~0.01). From this big dataset, I selected approximately 6.6 million samples that met specific criteria, such as ensuring the similarity between the image and text was greater than 0.3 and removing duplicates. I then utilized the text as prompts, feeding them into Stable Diffusion.This dataset is referred to as "PROMPTS_LQ". 

# Image Generation
Generating 10.6M images using Stable Diffusion is not an easy task for me, generating an image using scripts provided is about 15s, to speed up the generation, many measures were taken:
1.XFormer, 10s/img
2.FP16, 5s/img 
3.The output image size from 768x768 to 512x512 and the steps from 50 to 25, 2s/img

# Models
In the early stages, I spent several days designing models for this task by understanding and applying the mathematical principles of stable diffusion, which motivated me to enter this competition. Unfortunately, I did not found efficient and effective way. Therefore, I used vision models which were supervised using target vectors produced by the all-MiniLM-L6-v2 model.

My final submission included the ViT-Large, ConvNeXt-XXLarge, and BLIP-2 models. Although I also tried SWIN, DinoV2, and other models, they did not demonstrate an advantage within the limited time and resources available.

The Large Language Model(LLM) component in BLIP-2 was removed, not solely due to efficiency concerns, but primarily because both grammar and sequence of prompts in this task were unimportant or even a little harmful if supervised by the them. However, if time permits, the characteristics of the prompts and dedicated losses designed for them may bring improvements.

LORA was adopted to finetune the models, I used LORA for the bottom layers and it bring some improvement.

To my surprise, adding another large fully connected layer before the output layer brought significant improvement to the CLIP model.
```python
ebd_dim = 1024
fc_dim = 16 * 1024
self.head = nn.Sequential(
  nn.Linear(ebd_dim, fc_dim),
  nn.BatchNorm1d(fc_dim),
  nn.ReLU(),
  nn.Linear(fc_dim, 384),
)
```
I trained my models in two stages: first, pretraining on PROMPTS_LQ, and then finetuning them on PROMPTS_HQ.

When training, images from the same prompt were randomly selected once per epoch. Other options were also tested, such as training different models and ensembling, but they performed nearly the same.

I found that using larger image sizes led to higher scores, but I did not attempt to use sizes beyond 336x336 as I believed computation resources should be invested in other more important options.

No augmentation was used when training CLIP models and default augmentation was used when training BLIP-2 model.
