## Principle 9: For Image-to-Text or Cross-Modal Tasks, Leverage Pretrained CLIP Embeddings

*Consensus: 4/4 relevant competitions - Stable Diffusion ALL top solutions used CLIP ViT models, Open Problems used pretrained embeddings for sentence similarity*

CLIP models are pretrained on 400M-2B image-text pairs to align vision and language in a shared embedding space. For any task involving images and text (captioning, retrieval, prompt prediction), CLIP embeddings provide a strong initialization that is hard to beat from scratch.

**Why this matters:** Stable Diffusion 1st place: CLIP ViT-Large, ConvNeXt-XXL, and BLIP-2 (all CLIP-based) achieved 0.64-0.65 individually and 0.66 ensembled. Training from scratch on 10M generated images wouldn't reach this. CLIP's pretraining on massive diverse image-text data captures the prompt vocabulary and visual concepts that Stable Diffusion uses. Finetuning CLIP is faster and better than training from scratch.

**When to use:** Task involves both images and text (captioning, VQA, prompt inversion, retrieval). You have access to pretrained CLIP weights (open-source, widely available). Evaluation metric is embedding-based (cosine similarity) or you predict text from images.

**When NOT to use:** Task is pure vision (image classification without text) - CLIP may still work but is not strictly necessary. Task is pure NLP (no images). Domain is far from CLIP's training data (medical imaging, satellite) - CLIP may transfer poorly.

**How to reason about it:**
If the task says 'predict text from image' or 'match image to text', start with CLIP. Load a pretrained CLIP vision encoder (ViT-L, ViT-H, ConvNeXt). Option 1: Use CLIP embeddings as features for a downstream model. Option 2: Finetune the CLIP encoder (linear probing first, then full finetuning). Option 3: Ensemble multiple CLIP models (different architectures). Compare against training from scratch: CLIP should give +0.05-0.10.
