## Principle 11: Ensemble OOF-Verified Error Modes, Not Model Names

*Consensus: 12/13 top-eight solutions used averaging, seed bagging, weighted blending, stacking, or pseudolabel ensembles; the main exception achieved seventh with a single factorized model.*

Retain models that either improve the hard-fold score or make materially different errors, then determine simple nonnegative weights from out-of-fold predictions. Diversity can come from assay transforms, target rank, architecture, loss, training window, or entity representation—not just different random seeds.

**Why this matters:** Wide-output single-cell models have high variance, and different representations preserve different biological programs. Averaging reduces seed variance, while blending structurally distinct models can reduce bias; unconstrained stacking on a small number of experimental groups can instead learn fold artifacts.

**When to use:** Ensemble after every candidate has aligned OOF predictions from the same test-like folds. Seed-average strong neural models first, then blend a compact set of low-correlated model families or representations. Use leakage-safe OOF features for stacking.

**When NOT to use:** Do not weight by public leaderboard score, average many near-duplicates, or fit a flexible meta-model on mismatched folds. Several writeups preferred coarse weights or simple averages because more optimized blending overfit local CV; non-neural models that were uniformly worse and highly correlated added no value.

**How to reason about it:**
For each candidate, record hard-fold score, subgroup score, prediction variance, and residual correlation with the current ensemble. Add it only if the blended OOF score improves across plausible shifts. Prefer a broad plateau of good weights to a sharp optimum, which signals unstable ensemble fitting.
