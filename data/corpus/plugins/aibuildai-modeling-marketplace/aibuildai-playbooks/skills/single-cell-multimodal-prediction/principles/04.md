## Principle 4: Combine Global Low-Rank State with Modality-Specific Direct Features

*Consensus: All 8 top multimodal writeups used or discussed dimensional reduction; all 8 retained selected direct genes for CITEseq or explicitly reported that direct features did not help Multiome.*

Represent global cell state with PCA, truncated SVD, LSI, or a validated denoising encoder, then optionally append a small set of raw features with direct biological or empirical links to the targets. Decide this separately for each modality pair.

**Why this matters:** Low-rank projections denoise correlated programs and make hundreds of thousands of features computationally manageable, but they can dilute a small number of strong one-to-one links such as a transcript associated with a surface protein. In ATAC-to-RNA, accessibility is distributed across many regulatory regions, so individual raw peaks are less reliably transferable.

**When to use:** Append direct features when names, pathways, fold-stable correlations, or tree importance identify reproducible links—especially RNA-to-protein prediction. Select correlations independently within training folds or require that a feature rank highly across several batches.

**When NOT to use:** Skip direct features when the source-to-target relationship is diffuse or selection is unstable under domain shift. Multiple Multiome solutions reported no gain or deterioration from important raw peaks and instead relied on LSI/SVD cell-state features.

**How to reason about it:**
Evaluate three blocks independently: low-rank state, direct features, and their concatenation. Require selected direct features to improve more than one donor/day fold. If they help random CV but fail future-day CV, they are probably encoding batch-specific associations.
