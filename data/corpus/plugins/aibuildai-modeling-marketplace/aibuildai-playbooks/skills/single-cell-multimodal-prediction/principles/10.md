## Principle 10: Bias Training Toward the Deployment Domain Without Throwing Away Signal

*Consensus: Only about 3/13 top-eight solutions explicitly used recency-only training, batch fine-tuning, or domain weighting, but the gains were material when temporal or cell-type drift was strong.*

When hard-fold diagnostics reveal systematic drift, compare full-data training with recency weighting, domain-similarity weighting, sample weighting, or fine-tuning on the closest observed subgroup. Preserve a full-data model as a complementary hypothesis rather than assuming less data is universally better.

**Why this matters:** Older days or biologically distant cell types can increase sample size while biasing the learned mapping away from the deployment distribution. Conversely, aggressive filtering raises variance and can remove transferable compound or pathway effects.

**When to use:** Use domain-biased training when errors worsen monotonically with temporal distance, an adversarial classifier separates train from test, or response distances show that some source cell types are closer to the target cells. One top Multiome solution gained strongly by training on the latest day despite a worse public score.

**When NOT to use:** Do not discard data based only on leaderboard probing or an adversarial AUC. High train/test separability can coexist with useful predictive features, and one multimodal writeup saw performance fall after removing features merely to confuse the domain classifier.

**How to reason about it:**
Estimate score versus source distance and data volume. Train full, weighted, and nearest-domain variants under identical hard folds. If the nearest-domain model wins only on the latest fold, blend it with the full model; if gains are consistent, use it more heavily. Keep weighting rules determined from metadata or OOF behavior, never private leaderboard feedback.
