## Principle 1: Classify the Missingness Geometry Before Choosing a Model

*Consensus: 2/2 competitions required different solution families; all 13 top-eight writeups reflected this distinction in their representations or model design.*

First determine whether each row contains a measured molecular modality that must be translated into another modality, or only identifiers such as cell type and compound whose response profile must be inferred. Treat the former as high-dimensional supervised translation and the latter as structured tensor or recommender-style completion.

**Why this matters:** The observable input determines where predictive information can come from. Paired-modality rows carry a noisy but cell-specific signal, whereas a cell-type–compound tuple contains almost no numeric information unless the model learns effects shared across cells, compounds, and genes.

**When to use:** Always perform this classification. Indicators of paired translation are sparse matrices with cells as rows and molecular features as columns; indicators of structured completion are repeated categorical entities with an entire gene-response vector as the target.

**When NOT to use:** Do not force both regimes into one generic tabular recipe. The multimodal competition showed that methods transferable from CITEseq to Multiome could still fail because RNA-to-protein and ATAC-to-RNA have different sparsity and dimensionality; perturbation prediction required yet another approach.

**How to reason about it:**
Ask: (1) What is observed at inference—molecular measurements or entity IDs? (2) Is the missing axis a modality, future time, donor, cell type, compound, or interaction? (3) Can the test entities be represented from training alone? If measured features exist, prioritize denoising and translation. If only entities exist, prioritize factorization, target-derived priors, and external entity descriptors.

def choose_problem_family(X, entity_cols, molecular_cols):
    # Customize thresholds to the dataset schema.
    if len(molecular_cols) > 100 and X[molecular_cols].shape[1] >> len(entity_cols):
        return "paired_modality_translation"
    return "structured_interaction_completion"
