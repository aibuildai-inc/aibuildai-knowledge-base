## Principle 5: Turn Sparse Entity Labels into Interaction Priors

*Consensus: 5/5 top-eight perturbation solutions learned categorical/entity representations; 3/5 explicitly used target means, medians, standard deviations, or quantiles as input features.*

When inference provides only `(cell_type, compound)`, enrich each entity with leakage-safe response summaries or learned latent factors. Useful alternatives include separate cell and compound embeddings, gene-aware tensor factorization, target means/medians/quantiles, and chemical descriptors derived from SMILES.

**Why this matters:** One-hot labels cannot share statistical strength among related entities. Marginal response profiles estimate a compound's common effect and a cell type's susceptibility, while latent factors learn low-dimensional interactions; chemical structure can additionally represent compounds that are poorly supported or unseen.

**When to use:** Use response summaries when each entity appears in enough training combinations, and compute them out of fold with smoothing. Use learned embeddings or tensor factorization when the response table has repeated cell–compound–gene structure. Test fingerprints or pretrained molecular embeddings when chemical similarity is likely to transfer.

**When NOT to use:** Do not assume richer external descriptions are automatically better. The winning perturbation solution benefited from ChemBERTa, but natural-language biological descriptions hurt; other top solutions reported that SMILES sequence models or large pretrained gene models failed. If every test compound is already heavily observed in other cells, learned response priors can dominate chemical descriptors.

**How to reason about it:**
Start with the additive hypothesis `response = compound prior + cell prior + interaction residual`. Measure how much variance each prior explains out of fold. Add structure embeddings only if they improve compounds with weak support, not merely the average random fold. Use leave-one-out or fold-local aggregation to prevent a row's target from entering its own features.
