## Principle 8: Align Both Loss and Target Space with the Evaluation Metric

*Consensus: At least 8/13 top-eight solutions explicitly used correlation loss, MRRMSE, Huber/MAE mixtures, or another metric-aware objective rather than relying only on raw MSE.*

Derive what the metric ignores and what it amplifies, then choose the training loss and target parameterization accordingly. For rowwise Pearson correlation, shape and centering matter more than scale; for mean rowwise RMSE, every row receives equal weight while large gene errors dominate within a row.

**Why this matters:** A numerically convenient loss can optimize the wrong geometry. Pearson scoring is invariant to positive affine transforms of each row, whereas MSE penalizes them; signed `-log10(p)` targets are heavy-tailed and can be easier to model after an invertible transform or with robust losses.

**When to use:** Use correlation or cosine components for Pearson tasks, often blended with MSE for stable magnitude learning. Use direct rowwise RMSE or a robust MSE/MAE/LogCosh mixture for perturbation profiles. Evaluate target transforms—such as standardization or approximate t-score inversion—by transforming predictions back before scoring.

**When NOT to use:** Do not blindly optimize a complicated loss if it destabilizes gradients or worsens hard-fold ranking. Several strong systems retained MSE for latent Multiome targets, and one top perturbation solution found Huber inferior. A simple surrogate is acceptable when its OOF ordering agrees with the true metric.

**How to reason about it:**
Write the metric per sample and inspect its gradient emphasis. Determine whether errors in mean, variance, sign, or extreme genes are most costly. Compare candidate losses under the real metric, per subgroup, and in original target space. Keep auxiliary losses only when each changes useful error modes.

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def row_corr_loss(pred, target, eps=1e-6):
    pred = pred - pred.mean(dim=1, keepdim=True)
    target = target - target.mean(dim=1, keepdim=True)
    corr = F.cosine_similarity(pred, target, dim=1, eps=eps)
    return 1.0 - corr.mean()

def row_rmse_loss(pred, target, eps=1e-8):
    return torch.sqrt(((pred - target) ** 2).mean(dim=1) + eps).mean()

```
