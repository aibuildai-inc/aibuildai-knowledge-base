## Principle 12: Postprocess Only Along Proven Metric Invariances

*Consensus: At least 4/13 top-eight solutions explicitly used row normalization, zero-target restoration, clipping, or calibrated output scaling; leaderboard-only scaling produced a notable public-to-private drop.*

Apply deterministic postprocessing only when it follows from the metric or target support and is confirmed with OOF predictions. Examples include row centering/scaling before Pearson ensembles, restoring structurally zero outputs, and cautiously clipping extrapolative perturbation predictions to fold-derived bounds.

**Why this matters:** For Pearson correlation, models with identical directional information but different row scales can combine poorly unless normalized. For RMSE, global scaling and clipping directly change error and can help a biased model, but they are highly distribution-dependent and easy to overfit to a public leaderboard.

**When to use:** Normalize each model's rows before blending in correlation tasks when OOF tests confirm the gain. Reinstate outputs known to be structurally constant after excluding them from training. Estimate any scale, shift, or clipping bounds exclusively from cross-fitted predictions and inspect subgroup stability.

**When NOT to use:** Do not use public-leaderboard probing to choose multipliers or segment-specific weights. In the perturbation competition, a factor of 1.2 greatly improved the public score but finished much lower privately. Avoid clipping when genuine test responses may exceed the training range or when it merely hides underfitting.

**How to reason about it:**
Separate metric invariance from calibration. If a transform should leave the metric unchanged for a single row, it may be safe before ensembling but still needs numerical checks. If it changes the metric, learn it as a cross-validated calibration parameter and require improvement on every relevant fold.
