## Principle 7: Choose Spectrogram vs Tabular Features Based on Data Scale and Model Type

*Consensus: Most earthquake solutions used hand-crafted tabular features; the 19th-place team added STFT-spectrogram 2D-CNNs and 1D-CNNs only as ensemble members*

For seismic time-series, you can either: (a) convert signals to spectrograms (STFT or mel) and use CNNs, or (b) extract tabular features and use GBDTs. Spectrograms need enough independent samples to train a CNN; tabular features work with small datasets and tree models. Hybrid approaches ensemble both.

**Why this matters:** The choice determines your feature space and model family. Spectrograms preserve full time-frequency structure but require CNNs and more data. Tabular features compress the signal into statistics but lose fine-grained patterns. Neither dominates universally: on small datasets with few independent events, tabular models usually win, and spectrogram CNNs add value mainly as diverse ensemble members.

**When to use:** **Spectrograms** when: (1) you have many independent training samples, (2) you can use pretrained CNNs, (3) time-frequency localization matters (specific frequency bands at specific times), (4) you have GPU compute. **Tabular** when: (1) you have few independent samples or events, (2) you want fast iteration (GBDTs train in minutes), (3) interpretability matters, (4) you have domain knowledge for feature engineering. **Hybrid**: Always try ensembling both - they capture different information.

**When NOT to use:** Don't use spectrograms for: (1) very short signals (<1000 points) where you lose resolution, (2) non-stationary signals where frequency content is meaningless. Don't use pure tabular for: (1) very long signals (>100k points) where dimensionality reduction loses too much, (2) image-like data where spectrograms don't make sense.

**How to reason about it:**
**Decision tree**: 1. How many independent samples (events, not overlapping windows)? Many → spectrograms become viable. Few → tabular safer. 2. Do you have domain knowledge of important frequency bands? Yes → can engineer targeted tabular features. No → spectrograms let the CNN learn. 3. Do you have pretrained CNNs for your domain? Yes → spectrograms easier. 4. How fast do you need to iterate? Fast → tabular (train in seconds). 5. **Hybrid strategy**: Compute spectrograms → fine-tune one or more pretrained CNN backbones → ensemble. In parallel, extract tsfresh/librosa features → train XGBoost/LightGBM/CatBoost → ensemble. Final blend: choose the weights on out-of-fold predictions, not by a fixed ratio.

    features['mean'] = np.mean(signal)
    features['std'] = np.std(signal)
    features['kurtosis'] = scipy.stats.kurtosis(signal)
