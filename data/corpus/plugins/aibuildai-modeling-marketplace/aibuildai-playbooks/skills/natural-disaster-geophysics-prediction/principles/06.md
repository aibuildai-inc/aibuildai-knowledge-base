## Principle 6: Leverage Physics Simulators for Data Augmentation and Post-Processing

*Consensus: 8/10 waveform inversion solutions, 1/1 ClimSim top solution (forward model to refine predictions)*

When you have access to a differentiable forward physics model (seismic wave propagation, atmospheric physics), use it to: (1) generate unlimited synthetic training data, (2) refine predictions by optimizing them to match observed data through the forward model. This is test-time optimization grounded in physics.

**Why this matters:** Physics-based competitions have an exploitable structure: the forward process (velocity → seismic waveform, atmospheric state → fluxes) is known and often fast to compute. You can invert limited real data into a larger synthetic dataset, or refine neural network predictions by checking they satisfy physical constraints. This combines data-driven learning with domain knowledge.

**When to use:** When: (1) a fast forward simulator is available (runtime <1s per sample), (2) it's differentiable or you can use gradient-free optimization, (3) you're solving an inverse problem (recover cause from effect), (4) your neural network predictions have systematic physics violations. Most impactful in geophysics (waveform inversion, climate emulation) and fluid dynamics.

**When NOT to use:** Skip if: (1) no simulator exists or it's too slow (>10s per sample), (2) the forward model is approximate and introduces its own errors, (3) you already have >1M real training samples (diminishing returns), (4) the task is not an inverse problem (e.g., earthquake time prediction has no simple forward model).

**How to reason about it:**
**Data augmentation path**: 1. Train a generator (GAN or heuristic) to create synthetic velocity models. 2. Run forward simulator on each to generate synthetic seismic data. 3. Pretrain your inverse model on 10x synthetic data, then fine-tune on real data. **Post-processing path**: 1. Neural network predicts velocity from seismic. 2. Run forward model on predicted velocity → predicted seismic. 3. Compute loss between predicted seismic and true seismic. 4. Backprop through differentiable forward model to refine velocity prediction. Iterate 3-5 times. 5. This is test-time training: each test sample gets individually optimized. **Hybrid**: Use synthetic data for pretraining, then physics-based refinement at test time.
