## Principle 10: Implement High-Frequency Validation During Training

*Consensus: 3/15 solutions mentioned validating every 10% of an epoch for large datasets*

For large datasets (100k+ samples) or unstable tasks, validate every 10-20% of an epoch rather than once per epoch. Save the checkpoint with the best validation score, not the final epoch checkpoint. For small datasets, validate after every epoch.

**Why this matters:** Sentiment/toxicity models often peak mid-epoch and then overfit. Waiting a full epoch to validate can miss the optimal checkpoint. The 9th place solution noted: 'typically the model is trained for 1-2 epochs with validation at least every 10% batches.' High-frequency validation catches the peak before degradation.

**When to use:** When training on large datasets (>50k samples per epoch) or when you observe training loss decreasing but validation loss oscillating. Essential for fine-tuning on noisy, multi-annotator data.

**When NOT to use:** When validation is expensive (e.g., requires inference on 100k samples) and would slow training by >30%. When training on very small datasets (<5k samples) where each epoch is already fast.

**How to reason about it:**
Frequency choice: plot validation score vs training step for a pilot run. If score peaks and declines within an epoch, add intra-epoch validation. Schedule: for 100k-sample dataset with batch size 64, one epoch = ~1500 steps; validate every 150-300 steps (10-20% of epoch). Checkpoint strategy: always keep the best validation checkpoint; optionally keep the last N checkpoints for ensembling.

```python
# High-frequency validation
from transformers import Trainer, TrainingArguments

total_steps = len(train_dataloader) * num_epochs
eval_steps = total_steps // 20  # validate 20 times total, not just per epoch

training_args = TrainingArguments(
    output_dir='./results',
    num_train_epochs=2,
    per_device_train_batch_size=16,
    evaluation_strategy='steps',
    eval_steps=eval_steps,
    save_strategy='steps',
    save_steps=eval_steps,
    load_best_model_at_end=True,
    metric_for_best_model='eval_accuracy',
)
```
