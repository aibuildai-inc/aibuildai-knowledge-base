## Principle 15: Create Pairwise Training Data from Multi-Label Annotations

*Consensus: 5/5 solutions that used Ruddit or Jigsaw data for ranking tasks created pairwise comparisons*

When your task is ranking but your training data has absolute labels (toxicity scores, sentiment categories), convert it to pairwise comparisons: for each pair of samples where label_A < label_B, create a training instance (A, B, target=1) for MarginRankingLoss. Filter to pairs with sufficient gap (e.g., |score_A - score_B| > threshold) to avoid noisy comparisons.

**Why this matters:** Ranking models learn from relative comparisons, not absolute scores. Pairwise data is more robust to label noise (annotators may disagree on whether text is 4/10 or 5/10 toxic, but agree that A < B). The 5th place solution created pairs from Ruddit's (A, B, C, D) tuples, generating all 6 comparisons; filtered versions often work better than exhaustive pairing.

**When to use:** When your evaluation is ranking-based but training data has scores or categories. When you have multi-level ordinal labels (e.g., Ruddit's 4-level offensiveness). When your dataset has high annotator disagreement on absolute values but agreement on relative order.

**When NOT to use:** When you already have explicit pairwise annotations (no conversion needed). When your dataset is tiny (<1k samples) — exhaustive pairing creates too many similar instances. When labels have no meaningful order (nominal categories).

**How to reason about it:**
Pairing strategy: for N items with scores, exhaustive pairing gives N*(N-1)/2 pairs (quadratic). For large N (>10k), sample pairs: either uniform random or importance-sample pairs with moderate score gaps (filter |score_diff| < 0.1 and > 2.0). Margin: set MarginRankingLoss margin proportional to the score gap (margin = k * |score_A - score_B|, k=0.5 is common).

```python
# Create pairwise data from absolute labels
import itertools

def create_pairs(df, score_col='toxicity', min_gap=0.1, max_pairs_per_item=10):
    pairs = []
    for i, row_i in df.iterrows():
        # Sample negative pairs (less toxic)
        candidates = df[df[score_col] < row_i[score_col] - min_gap]
        if len(candidates) > max_pairs_per_item:
            candidates = candidates.sample(max_pairs_per_item)
        
        for j, row_j in candidates.iterrows():
            pairs.append({
                'less_toxic': row_j['text'],
                'more_toxic': row_i['text'],
                'score_gap': row_i[score_col] - row_j[score_col]
            })
```
