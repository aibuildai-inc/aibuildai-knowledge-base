## Principle 11: Apply Layerwise Learning Rate Decay for Transformer Fine-Tuning

*Consensus: 5/15 solutions mentioned layerwise LR, with typical ranges 2e-5 (backbone) to 1e-3 (head)*

When fine-tuning transformers, use a lower learning rate for early layers (close to pre-trained weights) and higher learning rate for later layers and the task-specific head. Typical ratio: 2e-5 for BERT layers, 1e-4 to 1e-3 for the final classification/regression head.

**Why this matters:** Early transformer layers encode general linguistic features (syntax, common words) that transfer across tasks. Later layers and the head are task-specific and need more adaptation. Uniform LR either undertunes the head (too low) or destroys pre-trained features (too high). Layerwise decay balances both needs.

**When to use:** Always when fine-tuning transformers on domain-specific tasks. Especially important when your task is far from the pre-training objective (e.g., toxicity vs next-sentence prediction).

**When NOT to use:** When training from scratch (no pre-trained weights). When your task is extremely similar to pre-training (e.g., fine-tuning a toxicity model on another toxicity dataset with the same label scheme) — uniform LR can work.

**How to reason about it:**
Layer assignment: typically 12 layers (BERT-base) or 24 (BERT-large). Assign LR decay: layer 0 gets base_lr × decay^11, layer 11 gets base_lr × decay^0. Common decay factor: 0.95 or 0.9. Head LR: 10-50× the base_lr (e.g., base=2e-5, head=1e-3). Scheduler: combine with warmup (5-10% of steps) and cosine annealing.

```python
# Layerwise learning rate decay
def get_optimizer_grouped_parameters(model, base_lr=2e-5, head_lr=1e-3, decay=0.95):
    layers = [model.embeddings] + list(model.encoder.layer)
    param_groups = []
    
    for i, layer in enumerate(layers):
        lr = base_lr * (decay ** (len(layers) - 1 - i))
        param_groups.append({
            'params': layer.parameters(),
            'lr': lr
        })
    
    # Task head gets higher LR
    param_groups.append({
        'params': model.classifier.parameters(),
        'lr': head_lr
    })
    
    return param_groups

from torch.optim import AdamW
optimizer = AdamW(get_optimizer_grouped_parameters(model))
```
