## Principle 11: Ensemble Through Architectural and Process Diversity, Not Just Seeds

*Consensus: Top solutions in most source competitions ensemble, best gains from diverse approaches*

Don't just average models with different random seeds. Maximize diversity through different architectures (LSTM + BERT + LightGBM), different preprocessing (with/without punctuation, different tokenizers), different training frameworks (PyTorch + TensorFlow), and even different developers' codebases. Diversity beats marginal accuracy.

**Why this matters:** The 1st place multilingual solution attributed their +0.0019 merger gain (larger than typical) to training diversity: one teammate used PyTorch on local GPUs, the other TensorFlow on Kaggle TPUs, starting from different public kernels. Their models made different errors, so ensembling worked better than two models from the same codebase.

**When to use:** Always, for any competition or production system where accuracy matters more than serving latency. When you've plateaued with single models. When you have team members with different frameworks/approaches. When CV shows high variance across folds (diverse models stabilize predictions).

**When NOT to use:** When you have strict inference latency requirements (< 100ms) and can't run multiple models. When model size is constrained (edge deployment). When your models are already highly correlated (same architecture, same data, different seeds) - diversity gain is minimal. Consider knowledge distillation to compress ensembles for production.

**How to reason about it:**
Measure pairwise prediction correlation between models. If correlation > 0.95, they're too similar; find more diverse architectures or data. If correlation < 0.7, they're adding unique signal. The best ensemble weights aren't uniform and aren't just proportional to individual CV scores - use a small validation set to optimize blend weights. Rank averaging often beats probability averaging for diverse models (handles different calibration).
