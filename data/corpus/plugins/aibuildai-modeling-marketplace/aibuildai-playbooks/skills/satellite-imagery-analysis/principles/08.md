## Principle 8: Use Cross-Validation with Spatial or Temporal Splits to Match Test Distribution

*Consensus: Most solutions used K-fold CV; two top ship-detection solutions built leak-free folds by grouping overlapping images*

Random K-fold on satellite data can leak information if samples from the same acquisition are split across folds. Use spatial folds (group by tile/region), temporal folds (group by date), or stratified folds (group by a predictive metadata value) to create realistic validation.

**Why this matters:** Samples from the same acquisition or group look alike and often share labels, so splitting a group across folds leaks label information. The ship detection 9th-place grouped overlapping images into friend circles and split by groups, not individual images. Random folds inflate local CV and don't reflect true generalization.

**When to use:** Use spatial/temporal folds when: images come from tiled acquisitions, there are geographic/temporal metadata, or when you find suspiciously high correlation between nearby samples. Use stratified folds when metadata (acquisition date, region) is predictive.

**When NOT to use:** If images are truly independent (different sensors, different years, random global sample), random K-fold is fine. If your dataset is small (<1000 images), the overhead of grouping may not be worth it.

**How to reason about it:**
Ask: (1) Do samples share metadata (location, time, acquisition ID)? If yes, group by it. (2) Are nearby samples correlated? If yes, use spatial folds. (3) Is there a train-test distribution shift (e.g., train from region A, test from region B)? If yes, make validation match test. (4) Check: does local CV correlate with LB? If not, your folds may be leaking.
