## Principle 9: Ensemble Diverse Models with Low Correlation to Maximize Gains

*Consensus: Most top solutions ensembled several diverse models*

Ensembling multiple models improves robustness and score, but gains saturate. Maximize diversity by varying: encoder architecture (ResNet vs EfficientNet vs SE-ResNeXt), input size (512 vs 768), augmentation strength, loss function, or even band selection. Average predictions at the probability level, not after thresholding.

**Why this matters:** The 1st-place cloud solution ensembled 3 seg-with-cls + 9 seg-only models, gaining +0.015 over single model. Correlation between models determines diminishing returns—adding a 10th ResNet34 helps less than adding one EfficientNet.

**When to use:** Always ensemble at least 3-5 diverse models for final submission. Vary architecture families (ResNet, EfficientNet, SE-ResNeXt), not just depth (ResNet34 vs ResNet50). For very competitive leaderboards, ensemble 10-20 models with different training setups.

**When NOT to use:** Don't ensemble if: you only have one model and no time to train more, models are too correlated (same architecture, same folds), or inference time is critical. The speed prize winner skipped ensembling to maximize throughput.

**How to reason about it:**
Steps: (1) Train 3-5 models with different encoders (e.g., ResNet34, EfficientNet-B1, ResNeXt101). (2) Check correlation: if pairwise correlation >0.95, models are too similar. (3) Ensemble by averaging probabilities (not logits, not binary masks). (4) Optionally: tune weighted average (not always equal weights). (5) For large ensembles (>10 models), use stacking: train a simple model (XGBoost, linear) on model predictions as features.

```python
# Simple ensemble: average probabilities
preds = [model1(x), model2(x), model3(x)]
pred_ensemble = torch.stack(preds).mean(dim=0)

# Weighted ensemble (tune weights on validation)
weights = [0.5, 0.3, 0.2]  # sum to 1
pred_ensemble = sum(w * p for w, p in zip(weights, preds))

# Check correlation
from scipy.stats import pearsonr
corr = pearsonr(pred1.flatten(), pred2.flatten())[0]
if corr > 0.95:
    print('Models too similar, add more diversity')

# Stacking: second-level model on predictions
X_meta = np.column_stack([p.flatten() for p in preds])
meta_model = xgboost.XGBClassifier()
meta_model.fit(X_meta_train, y_train)
```
