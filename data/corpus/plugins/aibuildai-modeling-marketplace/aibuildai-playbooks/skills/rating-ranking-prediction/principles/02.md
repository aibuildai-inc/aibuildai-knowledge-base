## Principle 2: Identify and Respect Data Structure for Cross-Validation

*Consensus: Top solutions in temporal competitions use time-aware CV; most in grouped data use group-aware splits*

Rating/ranking tasks often have inherent structure: temporal sequences (credit statements over months), groups (tournament seasons, users), or hierarchies. Your CV strategy must respect this structure to avoid leakage and produce reliable validation scores.

**Why this matters:** Wrong CV strategy produces optimistic scores that don't generalize. Time-series leakage (using future to predict past) and group leakage (same entity in train and validation) are the two most common causes of train-test mismatch. AmEx (monthly statements), March Madness (seasons), Mercari (none) require different strategies.

**When to use:** Immediately after understanding the data. Check: (1) Is there a time dimension? Use time-aware splits (e.g., leave-one-season-out for tournaments). (2) Are there groups? Use GroupKFold or StratifiedGroupKFold (e.g., by user_id, prompt_id). (3) Neither? Standard StratifiedKFold is safe.

**When NOT to use:** If the data is truly i.i.d. (independent, identically distributed) with no temporal or group structure, standard stratified CV is simpler and sufficient. Don't overcomplicate purely cross-sectional tabular data.

**How to reason about it:**
Draw a timeline or dependency graph. Can the model see 'future' information during training? Are multiple rows from the same entity? If yes to either: use structure-aware CV. For temporal data: fit on [0, T-k], validate on [T-k, T], test on [T, T+n]. For grouped data: entire groups go into one fold. For temporal drift: validate on the most recent period that resembles the test set. Check CV-LB correlation: if StratifiedKFold CV is 0.85 but LB is 0.78, you have leakage—switch strategies.
