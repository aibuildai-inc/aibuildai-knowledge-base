## Principle 10: Integrate Domain-Specific External Features and Rankings

*Consensus: March Madness top solutions use ELO ratings, Nate Silver rankings; tournament solutions universally use external rankings*

For domains with established rating systems (sports, games, competitions), incorporate external rankings (ELO, Sagarin, KenPom, Nate Silver) as features. These compress decades of domain knowledge into a single number and are highly predictive.

**Why this matters:** Domain experts have solved part of the problem already. ELO ratings for basketball, chess engines for chess positions, and price indices for real estate are powerful features. March Madness 1st place: Nate Silver ratings as main feature, converting rating differences to win probabilities with a simple formula. Reinventing these from raw box scores is inefficient.

**When to use:** When the domain has established rating systems or when external data is allowed. Check competition rules for data restrictions. Use for sports (ELO, team rankings), games (chess engines, MCTS evaluations), prices (inflation indices, market trends). Combine external features with your own feature engineering.

**When NOT to use:** If external data is prohibited or unavailable. Don't blindly trust external features—validate their predictive power on your CV. If the domain is novel (no prior art) or if external rankings are outdated, build features from scratch instead.

**How to reason about it:**
Search for domain-specific ratings: what do experts use? For sports: ELO, Sagarin, RPI, AP polls. For prices: inflation-adjusted indices, market comparables. For games: engine evaluations. Integrate: (1) as raw features (team_a_elo, team_b_elo), (2) as differences (elo_diff = team_a_elo - team_b_elo), (3) as probabilities (convert elo_diff to win_prob via logistic function). Validate: does adding external features improve CV? If not, they may be noisy or stale.
