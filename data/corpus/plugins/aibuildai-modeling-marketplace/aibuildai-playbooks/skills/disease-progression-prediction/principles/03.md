## Principle 3: Identify Hidden Subpopulations Through EDA

*Consensus: 5th place Parkinson's solution found control groups or patient strata*

Medical datasets often contain unlabeled control groups or patient subpopulations with fundamentally different progressions. Finding these groups through visit patterns, baseline measurements, or test coverage provides step-function CV gains.

**Why this matters:** A mixed model predicting both healthy controls and diseased patients averages their trajectories. Separating them allows different progression curves. Controls may have annual-only visits and skip certain tests.

**When to use:** When EDA shows multimodal distributions in outcomes, visit schedules, or test availability. When certain patients have systematically missing measurements that aren't random.

**When NOT to use:** When all patients follow identical protocols and outcome distributions are unimodal. Don't force artificial clusters when natural variation explains the data.

**How to reason about it:**
Plot outcome over time per patient, colored by first visit month or test availability. Do distinct trajectory clusters emerge? Check if patients with first visit at month 0 vs month 12 differ systematically. If a subgroup has lower scores and different visit/test patterns, hypothesize control group. Create indicator features.

# Identify potential control group
df['first_nonzero_visit'] = df.groupby('patient_id')['visit_month'].apply(
    lambda x: x[x > 0].min() if (x > 0).any() else 0

# Patients whose first visit is month 12+ may be controls
df['is_control'] = (df['first_nonzero_visit'] >= 12).astype(int)
