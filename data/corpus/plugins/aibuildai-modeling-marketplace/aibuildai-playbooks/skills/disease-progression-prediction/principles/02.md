## Principle 2: Mine Temporal Metadata Before Complex Features

*Consensus: Top 18/18 Parkinson's gold solutions used visit date features; 1st place ignored all biomarker data*

Patient visit timing (which months they attended, gaps between visits, first non-zero visit) often contains stronger signal than expensive medical measurements because visit frequency correlates with disease severity.

**Why this matters:** Medical staff schedule follow-ups based on observed severity—patients with worse symptoms visit more frequently. This encodes clinical judgment as a feature. Visit patterns are also robust: boolean indicators are less noisy than continuous biomarkers.

**When to use:** When your data includes visit schedules, appointment dates, or any temporal pattern. If patients with different disease trajectories have different visit cadences, this is your strongest signal.

**When NOT to use:** When visit schedules are fixed protocol (everyone visits month 0, 6, 12 regardless of condition) or when temporal data is censored in ways that differ between train and test.

**How to reason about it:**
Plot UPDRS/outcome by visit month, stratified by visit frequency. Do high-frequency visitors have consistently different outcomes? Create boolean features: did patient visit on month M? Count non-annual visits. Check if first non-zero visit month separates control from disease groups. If these beat your biomarker model, trust them.

```python
# Extract visit pattern features
for month in [6, 12, 18, 24, 36, 48]:
    # 3-state: visited=1, didn't visit=0, unknown=-1
    df[f'v{month}'] = -1  # default unknown
    df.loc[df['visit_month'] == month, f'v{month}'] = 1
    df.loc[(df['visit_month'] != month) & 
           (df['visit_month'] > month), f'v{month}'] = 0

# Count non-annual visits
df['non_annual_visits'] = ((df['visit_month'] % 12 != 0) & 
                           (df['visit_month'] > 0)).sum()
```
