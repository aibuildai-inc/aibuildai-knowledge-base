## Principle 1: Optimize the Reconstructed Partition, Not Individual Hit Links

*Consensus: 6/6 solutions ultimately reconstructed complete tracks; the winning solution explicitly measured score after every stage.*

Start from the exact scoring rule and treat the output as a constrained partition of hits, not as independent pair classifications. Track-level purity and particle-level coverage thresholds can make a locally reasonable edge or hit assignment worth zero after grouping.

**Why this matters:** In TrackML, a candidate scores only when the matched particle supplies a strict majority of the candidate and the candidate contains a strict majority of that particle's hits. False additions can destroy purity, while fragmented predictions can fail coverage; endpoint and high-momentum hits also carry unequal weight. This creates asymmetric costs that ordinary clustering accuracy does not represent.

**When to use:** Always when the metric matches whole predicted trajectories to true trajectories, especially when it has majority, overlap, minimum-length, or per-hit weighting rules.

**When NOT to use:** Do not transfer TrackML's exact majority thresholds to tasks scored by edge F1, pointwise accuracy, or continuous parameter error. In those tasks, derive the analogous decision objective from their own matching and aggregation rules.

**How to reason about it:**
Write the official scorer first. For every proposed operation—merge, split, extend, or delete—ask: can it move a candidate across a purity or coverage boundary, and which hit weights are at risk? Validate both final score and decomposed quantities such as weighted recoverable coverage, candidate purity, fragmentation, and contamination.

def track_is_valid(pred_hits, true_particle, truth_by_hit):
    # Replace with the competition's exact matching rules.
    overlap = sum(truth_by_hit[h] == true_particle for h in pred_hits)
    purity = overlap / len(pred_hits)
    coverage = overlap / true_particle.n_hits
    return purity > 0.5 and coverage > 0.5
