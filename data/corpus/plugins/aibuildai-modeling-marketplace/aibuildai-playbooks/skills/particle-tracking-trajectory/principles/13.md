## Principle 13: Instrument Stagewise Oracle Recall and Runtime Before Tuning Details

*Consensus: The winner explicitly built load/score checkpoints and upper-bound metrics after every stage; at least 4/6 writeups reported stage gains or runtimes, making this a high-value winning practice despite limited explicit documentation.*

At each pipeline boundary, record actual score, best possible score recoverable from current candidates, candidate count, memory, and latency. Optimize the stage that destroys the most recoverable score or consumes the dominant compute, rather than tuning the easiest visible parameter.

**Why this matters:** Final score alone cannot distinguish failed seeding from bad assignment. Oracle candidate evaluation exposes whether truth is still present but poorly selected, while stage serialization makes expensive pipelines debuggable and supports controlled ablations.

**When to use:** Use throughout development, especially for multi-hour events, multi-pass clustering, or pipelines with millions of candidates. Weight recall with the official metric so diagnostics prioritize consequential trajectories and endpoints.

**When NOT to use:** Do not compute an exact combinatorial oracle when it is itself intractable; use optimistic per-truth coverage bounds or sampled events. Avoid relying on truth-only diagnostics at inference—these are development instruments, not production features.

**How to reason about it:**
After each stage ask: what fraction of weighted truth is represented by at least one viable candidate, what fraction can survive metric thresholds, and how much did the stage cost? If oracle recall is low, fix proposal or gates; if high but final score is low, fix ranking and assignment; if both are high, target recovery and runtime.

audit = []
for name, stage in pipeline:
    state = stage(state)
    audit.append({
        "stage": name,
        "official_score": score_if_assignable(state, truth),
        "oracle_weighted_recall": optimistic_recoverable_score(state, truth),
        "n_candidates": len(state.candidates),
        "memory_mb": measure_memory(),

    })
