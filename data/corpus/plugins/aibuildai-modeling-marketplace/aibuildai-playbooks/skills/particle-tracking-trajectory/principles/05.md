## Principle 5: Branch the Seeding Strategy on Origin and Trajectory Regime

*Consensus: 4/6 explicitly exploited proximity to the interaction vertex or z-axis; rank 3 then ran separate searches for non-vertex and lower-momentum tracks.*

Use strong origin constraints to clean the dominant easy population, but create separate passes for displaced, short, curling, low-momentum, or boundary-crossing trajectories. A prior should reduce search, not silently define what can exist.

**Why this matters:** A common-origin constraint sharply reduces seed combinations and stabilizes helix parameterization. However, trajectories originating away from that region violate the transform and are systematically lost; low-momentum tracks also have larger curvature and less stable extrapolation.

**When to use:** Apply a vertex-first pass when training data or domain knowledge shows a large concentrated origin population. Follow it with relaxed three-hit or outside-in seeding on residual hits when displaced or unusual trajectories contribute meaningful score.

**When NOT to use:** Skip the vertex-constrained pass if origins are broadly distributed, the source moves, or the target population is specifically secondary trajectories. A single unconstrained pass may suffice in low-occupancy data.

**How to reason about it:**
Estimate the origin distribution and score mass by origin distance, momentum, length, and detector region. Order passes by expected score recovered per unit compute. Tight early passes should claim only high-confidence tracks so residual passes are not poisoned.

```python
passes = [
    {"name": "vertex_high_momentum", "seed_size": 2, "origin_gate": "tight"},
    {"name": "vertex_low_momentum",  "seed_size": 3, "origin_gate": "moderate"},
    {"name": "displaced_or_outside_in", "seed_size": 3, "origin_gate": None},
]
for cfg in passes:
    candidates = search(unclaimed_hits, **cfg)
    claim_high_confidence(candidates)
```
