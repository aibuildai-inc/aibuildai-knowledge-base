## Principle 6: Prefer Locally Updated Motion Models When Global Physics Is Misspecified

*Consensus: 4/6 explicitly improved candidates with curve fitting, local three-hit helices, z-axis constraints, or magnetic-field corrections; ranks 1 and 3 emphasized local propagation.*

Fit the simplest motion model that predicts the next detector intersection, and update it locally as new hits arrive. Add field- or region-dependent corrections only when residual diagnostics show systematic drift.

**Why this matters:** A perfect helix assumes a sufficiently uniform field and ideal motion. Real trajectories experience field variation, scattering, energy loss, and detector transitions; a model fitted to the most recent hits can absorb local behavior without requiring a fully calibrated global simulator.

**When to use:** Use local three-point curvature or short-window fitting when measurements are precise and deviations change along the path. Use explicit field corrections when residuals vary smoothly with position, layer, or angle and the correction transfers across events.

**When NOT to use:** Do not use a three-hit fit when spatial resolution is too poor or points are nearly degenerate; a longer robust fit or probabilistic state estimator may be safer. Avoid hand-built corrections when residuals are random, event-specific, or already modeled by an accurate field map and Kalman-style propagator.

**How to reason about it:**
Plot extrapolation residuals against z, radius, angle, layer transitions, and candidate length. If residual bias is local and systematic, choose between sliding-window refits and a calibrated physical correction. Keep uncertainty gates wider where scattering or model error grows.
