## Principle 12: Ensemble Complementary Search Passes, Not Redundant Copies

*Consensus: 3/6 clearly combined multiple passes or models: rank 3 searched detector regions and regimes sequentially, rank 7 merged long parameter sweeps, and rank 9 merged a specialized inner-detector model with broader models.*

Run specialized reconstructors for different detector regions, origin assumptions, curvature ranges, or precision–recall settings, then merge candidates through overlap and quality rules. Diversity should target documented failure modes rather than repeat the same errors at higher cost.

**Why this matters:** One parameterization rarely behaves uniformly across field regions and trajectory populations. A conservative inner-region model can exploit near-ideal helices, while broader or outside-in passes recover displaced and distorted tracks; candidate-level merging preserves structure better than arbitrary label voting.

**When to use:** Use specialized passes when residual analysis shows distinct regimes and each pass adds unique weighted coverage. It is also useful when a fast strict pass can reduce occupancy before an expensive relaxed pass.

**When NOT to use:** Do not ensemble highly correlated runs whose marginal coverage is negligible, especially under throughput constraints. Avoid merging solely by track IDs, which are arbitrary across reconstructors; compare hit overlap and physical compatibility.

**How to reason about it:**
Measure each pass's unique oracle contribution, runtime, and conflict rate. Merge strongly overlapping compatible candidates, choose among conflicting candidates by calibrated quality, and refit unions before acceptance. Stop adding passes when marginal score per compute becomes poor.

all_candidates = []
for model in [inner_precise, global_balanced, displaced_outside_in]:
