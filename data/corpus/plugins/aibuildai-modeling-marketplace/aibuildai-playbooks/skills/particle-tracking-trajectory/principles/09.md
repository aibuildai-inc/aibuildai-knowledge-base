## Principle 9: Rank Candidates by Detector Coverage and Path Plausibility

*Consensus: 5/6 explicitly ranked or filtered complete candidates; rank 9 found distinct volume coverage and learned layer-sequence plausibility more effective than raw hit count.*

Score a candidate using independent detector-surface coverage, fit consistency, path topology, and accidental-hit likelihood—not merely the number of included hits. Treat duplicate measurements on one surface separately from evidence that the trajectory traversed another surface.

**Why this matters:** A contaminated or duplicated candidate can have many hits without representing a coherent particle. Distinct-surface coverage is harder to obtain by chance, and valid layer sequences encode detector reachability; both align better with trajectory completeness and majority-based metrics.

**When to use:** Use topology-aware quality whenever detector surfaces are known, duplicate hits can occur, or tracks cross different regions. Learn transition or subsequence priors when training and test geometries match.

**When NOT to use:** Avoid rigid sequence priors when sensors are frequently inactive, trajectories legitimately skip surfaces, or geometry changes between train and test. In such cases use a reachability model that marginalizes missing measurements rather than declaring skips impossible.

**How to reason about it:**
Separate evidence into coverage, residual fit, topology, and contamination. Check whether each score increases true-track precision at fixed recoverable coverage. Normalize for candidate length so long accidental chains do not win automatically.

```python
def candidate_quality(track, topology_model, density_model):
    surfaces = deduplicate_by_surface(track.hits)
    coverage = len(surfaces)
    fit_term = -robust_fit_loss(track)
    path_term = topology_model.log_probability([h.surface_id for h in surfaces])
    chance_term = -density_model.expected_accidentals(track)
    return 1.0 * coverage + 0.5 * fit_term + 0.5 * path_term + chance_term
```
