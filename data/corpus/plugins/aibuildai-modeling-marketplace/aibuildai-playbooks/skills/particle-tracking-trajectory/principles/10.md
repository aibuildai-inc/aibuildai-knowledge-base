## Principle 10: Resolve Shared Hits Globally and Peel Conservatively

*Consensus: 4/6 explicitly described best-candidate conflict resolution followed by hit removal; several others merged or reconstructed tracks from overlapping candidate evidence.*

Generate candidates permissively, then impose the one-hit-to-one-track constraint in a dedicated assignment stage. Iteratively claim only high-quality tracks, update conflicting candidates, and rerun relaxed searches on residual hits.

**Why this matters:** Independent candidate generation naturally produces massive overlap. Prematurely assigning hits during growth makes early mistakes irreversible, while greedy removal without score updates can leave stale candidate qualities after shared hits disappear.

**When to use:** Use iterative peeling when one dominant easy population can be reconstructed reliably and removing it materially reduces occupancy. Dynamic priority queues are effective when candidate scores can be updated cheaply after conflicts.

**When NOT to use:** Avoid aggressive peeling when candidate confidence is poorly calibrated or tracks frequently cross with ambiguous shared measurements; a global set-packing, matching, or probabilistic assignment may be safer. Do not remove low-confidence claims merely to simplify the next pass.

**How to reason about it:**
Compare greedy peeling with stronger assignment on small events where exact optimization is tractable. Define what happens when a candidate loses hits: refit, rescore, split, or discard. Maintain a residual pool for unclaimed hits and noise so every hit need not contaminate a physical track.
