## Principle 8: Calibrate Gates to Local Occupancy and Model Uncertainty

*Consensus: 4/6 made this explicit through outlier-density thresholds or adaptive clustering widths; the winner modeled chance outliers rather than relying on an unavailable exact inlier-noise model.*

Set search radii, clustering epsilon, and acceptance thresholds from expected accidental occupancy and local prediction uncertainty. A fixed geometric distance has different meaning across dense and sparse detector regions.

**Why this matters:** The expected number of random hits inside a gate grows with local hit density and gate area. Density-calibrated thresholds keep contamination approximately stable across surfaces, while uncertainty-aware gates avoid rejecting trajectories in regions with larger scattering or field mismatch.

**When to use:** Use density normalization in heterogeneous occupancy, anisotropic modules, or multi-region detectors. It is especially useful when the inlier error distribution is difficult to model but the background hit density is measurable.

**When NOT to use:** A global gate may be adequate when density and resolution are nearly uniform. Do not estimate background from a region contaminated by strong coherent structures without robust smoothing or held-out calibration.

**How to reason about it:**
Estimate background density by surface and local coordinates, then choose a tolerated expected outlier count. Combine it with a covariance or empirical residual ellipse. Validate threshold stability by region and trajectory regime rather than tuning one event-wide epsilon.

```python
def gate_radius(local_density, expected_random=0.05, sigma_floor=0.0):
    # For an isotropic 2D gate: E[random] ≈ density * pi * r^2.
    density_radius = np.sqrt(expected_random / (np.pi * max(local_density, 1e-12)))
    return max(density_radius, sigma_floor)

# For anisotropic surfaces, use an ellipse and integrate density over its area.
```
