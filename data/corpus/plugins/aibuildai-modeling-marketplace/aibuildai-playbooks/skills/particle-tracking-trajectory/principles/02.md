## Principle 2: Choose a Reconstruction Paradigm from Geometry, Occupancy, and Compute

*Consensus: 6/6 used a structured decomposition, but in three methodological clusters: local geometric seed-and-extend (ranks 1 and 3), learned pair affinity followed by curve reconstruction (rank 2), and transformed-space clustering with extension or merging (ranks 7, 9, and 11).*

Select the main candidate generator according to observable data structure rather than assuming one universal tracker. Strong physical transforms favor clustering; reliable layer adjacency favors combinatorial propagation; informative labeled pair features can support an affinity model, usually inside a geometric pipeline.

**Why this matters:** The naïve space of hit subsets or all hit pairs is too large. Each paradigm reduces it differently: physical transforms collapse a trajectory into a compact cluster, layer-aware propagation restricts branching, and learned affinity rejects incompatible links before graph assembly.

**When to use:** Use transformed clustering when trajectories share a low-dimensional family and common-origin assumptions are strong. Use seed-and-extend when detector surfaces are ordered and intersections can be predicted locally. Use learned affinity when residual geometry is complex but representative labels and a tightly pruned candidate neighborhood exist.

**When NOT to use:** Avoid exhaustive parameter sweeps when throughput is binding or the transform is badly misspecified. Avoid unrestricted pair classification at high occupancy. Avoid rigid layer propagation when observations are unordered, frequently missing, or not associated with known surfaces.

**How to reason about it:**
Estimate event size, plausible-neighbor count, common-origin fraction, trajectory-family dimensionality, and runtime budget. Prototype each paradigm on a small event and compare candidate recall at a fixed candidate count—not merely its score after aggressive tuning. Hybrids are often strongest: geometry proposes and a model ranks, or clustering seeds and local propagation completes.

```python
def choose_generator(stats):
    if stats.common_origin_fraction_high and stats.straightening_transform_stable:
        return "parameter_sweep_plus_clustering"
    if stats.layers_orderable and stats.local_intersection_predictable:
        return "geometric_seed_and_extend"
    if stats.labeled_pairs_available and stats.pruned_degree <= stats.edge_budget:
        return "pair_affinity_plus_graph_assembly"
    return "hybrid_multi_pass"  # Customize after candidate-recall tests.
```
