## Principle 3: Represent Hits in Physics- and Detector-Aware Coordinates

*Consensus: 6/6 exploited curve geometry; at least 5/6 also used layer, volume, duplicate-surface, or cell-direction information rather than raw Cartesian distance alone.*

Construct coordinates and topology in which valid trajectories become simple and detector constraints become explicit. Typical representations combine cylindrical variables, periodic angle encodings, ordered detector surfaces, local module coordinates, and cell-derived hit directions.

**Why this matters:** Raw Euclidean proximity is misleading: successive hits can be far apart, unrelated tracks can cross, angular variables wrap at ±π, and detector resolution is anisotropic. A detector-aware representation turns global curvature into smoother residuals and prevents impossible same-surface or skipped-surface associations.

**When to use:** Use cylindrical or helix-derived features in approximately axial fields, local surface coordinates when module orientation affects uncertainty, and layer/volume graphs whenever sensor topology is known. Encode periodic angles with sine and cosine.

**When NOT to use:** Do not force cylindrical symmetry onto detectors without a meaningful axis or helix transforms onto motion dominated by another field or force law. In those cases derive coordinates from the system's actual invariances and measurement geometry.

**How to reason about it:**
Ask which transformation makes one true trajectory compact, linear, or slowly varying. Then identify what information is lost by the transform: charge sign, branch direction, module orientation, or displaced origin may require separate hypotheses or features. Preserve detector IDs as topology, not just categorical inputs.

```python
import numpy as np

def hit_features(h):
    r = np.hypot(h.x, h.y)
    phi = np.arctan2(h.y, h.x)
    return {
        "r": r,
        "sin_phi": np.sin(phi),
        "cos_phi": np.cos(phi),
        "z": h.z,
        "surface_id": (h.volume_id, h.layer_id),
        # Add module-local coordinates and cell-derived direction if available.
    }
```
