Here, I introduce my solution.
My solution exploits the velocity of the vehicle predicted using doppler shift, which is basically much more reliable than the baseline positions.

### 1. Velocity prediction using doppler shift.
Velocity of the vehicle can be estimated using PseudorangeRateMetersPerSecond, as show here:  https://www.kaggle.com/c/google-smartphone-decimeter-challenge/code

### 2. Smoothing by CNN.
A simple 1D convolutional network is applied to the predicted velocity for smoothing.

### 3. Apply snap-to-grid to consistent groups(SJC only).
Snap-to-grid is applied to the consistent groups.
Grid points are generated by the linear interpolation of the ground truth.

 - gt: an index of the grid point to which **x**t is snapped,

The first term of g(**x**t, **v**t, **x**gt, **u**gt) is the cost related to the distance between the baseline position and the grid points.
The second term of g(**x**t, **v**t, **x**gt, **u**gt) is the cost for the deviation between the orientation degree of the velocity and that of the grid points.
The above optimization problem can be solved using Dijkstra's algorithm.

### 9. Remaining issues
 - Use IMU data to reduce prediction errors of the velocity.
 - Detect stop points and contract them to one point to reduce accumulation errors. 
Although the boundary conditions imposed by the consistent groups reduce the accumulation errors, stop points may cause a big problem to the interpolation by the velocity.
 -  For further improvement, the baseline positions must be improved.
