# Method sharing

Competition: pkdd-15-predict-taxi-service-trajectory-i
Rank: #5
Source: https://www.kaggle.com/c/pkdd-15-predict-taxi-service-trajectory-i/discussion/15020

<p>Congratulations to all the winners!&nbsp; This was a fun and challenging contest. My solution placed 5th on the private leaderboard, though only 22nd (after cheater removal) on the public leaderboard.&nbsp;</p>
<p>Here's how I approached the problem:</p>
<p>1)&nbsp; I divided the area covered by the training cases up into a grid in lat/long in order to bin different locations.&nbsp; The grid resolution was about 100m.&nbsp; The jist of my method was to compute the grid points that each trajectory passed through and correlate the test trajectories with the training trajectories.&nbsp;</p>
<p>2)&nbsp; For each grid bin, four feature values were possible, corresponding to passing through that grid with a north-westerly, south-westerly, north-easterly, or south-easterly velocity.</p>
<p>3)&nbsp; Given the number of shared &quot;features&quot; (i.e., a lat/long bin with velocity direction) between the test case and the training set, the training samples with a correlation above a particular threshold were chosen to generate the prediction.&nbsp; Each sample was weighted according to its number of shared features.</p>
<p>4) *important*&nbsp; The prediction was generated by minimizing the weighted sum of the distances between the sample destinations and the guess.&nbsp; This is not the same as minimizing the sqrt of the sum of the squares of the distance difference (i.e., taking the mean)!&nbsp; It makes a big difference for this contest's evaluation function.</p>

<p>A few helpful tweaks I found were:</p>
<p>1)&nbsp; I weighted the last feature in the test trajectory at 10-15x the other features.</p>
<p>2)&nbsp; If the taxi was stopped for more than a certain time, I only used features in the test sample after that time.</p>

<p>As far as setting up a CV goes, I was successful in that my CV seemed to indicate the relative (though not absolute) worth of different approaches well.&nbsp; It was clear to me that longer trips are more likely to end up in the test set because they are more likely to be active at the chosen sample time.&nbsp; I ended up using the same method as the moderators to create my validation set - just take the trips that were active at a certain time and cut their POLYLINE at that time.&nbsp; I wanted to create a training set the same way, but I could only get about 50k unique data points out of the 1.7M training set before distorting the probability distribution across trip time (a power law fit very well).</p>
