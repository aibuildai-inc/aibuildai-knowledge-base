## Principle 10: Optimize Trajectory-Level Objectives with Beam Search or Global Optimization

*Consensus: 2/2 top indoor navigation solutions, 1/1 top GNSS solution*

For sequential predictions, don't optimize each waypoint independently. Use beam search to maintain top-K trajectory hypotheses, scoring by cumulative penalty across all waypoints. For GNSS, use factor graph optimization to jointly refine all positions under motion and observation constraints.

**Why this matters:** Independent per-waypoint predictions violate physical constraints (impossible jumps, wall-crossing). Trajectory-level optimization enforces smoothness, respects the map, and aggregates evidence across time. Indoor navigation: beam search with trajectory penalties improved scores by ~2 meters over greedy per-waypoint selection.

**When to use:** Sequential prediction where later decisions depend on earlier ones and constraints span multiple steps: indoor navigation (path continuity, walls), GNSS refinement (motion model, clock drift). Any task with trajectory-level metrics.

**When NOT to use:** Independent predictions (check-ins at different times/places). If the search space is too large (>10^6 candidates per step), beam search may be too slow—use heuristics or limit beam width. Pure regression tasks where continuous optimization works (no discrete constraints).

**How to reason about it:**
Are waypoints independent or does the path matter? If path: use global optimization. For discrete spaces (grid waypoints), beam search maintains top-K paths at each step, expanding only feasible next steps. For continuous (GNSS), factor graphs or Kalman filters jointly optimize all states. Penalties: WiFi/observation fit + motion smoothness + map constraints. Tune beam width (K): too small misses optimal, too large is slow (K=100–2000 works for indoor).
