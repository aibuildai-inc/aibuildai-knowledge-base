## Principle 2: Apply Spatial Partitioning When the Location Space is Large

*Consensus: 5/5 absolute positioning solutions, 3/3 hybrid solutions*

Divide the geographic area into a grid (100m–500m cells) and solve the problem locally within each cell rather than globally. Reduce the candidate set from 100K+ places to hundreds per cell.

**Why this matters:** Without partitioning, KNN/KDE across 100K+ candidates is computationally intractable and statistically inefficient (most candidates are irrelevant). A local grid leverages spatial locality—nearby check-ins share similar features—and makes methods like KNN, KDE, and LightGBM practical.

**When to use:** When you have 10K+ discrete candidate locations spread over a large area AND features have strong spatial locality (x/y coordinates, WiFi signal strength, etc.). Facebook check-ins (100K places), indoor navigation (thousands of waypoints per building).

**When NOT to use:** Trajectory prediction on open roads or continuous motion doesn't need discrete grids. Precision GNSS refinement operates on continuous coordinates, not discrete cells. Small location sets (<1000 candidates) can be handled globally.

**How to reason about it:**
If your candidate count × feature dimension makes global methods slow (>1s per prediction) or if local features (WiFi, proximity) dominate, partition. Tune grid size: too small → insufficient training samples per cell, too large → too many candidates remain. Start with 100m–200m for city-scale, 5m–10m for building-scale.

```python
# Grid partitioning for check-in prediction
cell_size = 0.001  # ~100m in lat/lng
train['grid_x'] = (train['x'] / cell_size).astype(int)
train['grid_y'] = (train['y'] / cell_size).astype(int)
train['grid_id'] = train['grid_x'].astype(str) + '_' + train['grid_y'].astype(str)

# Predict within cell
test_grid = get_grid_id(test_x, test_y, cell_size)
candidates = train[train['grid_id'] == test_grid]['place_id'].unique()
```
