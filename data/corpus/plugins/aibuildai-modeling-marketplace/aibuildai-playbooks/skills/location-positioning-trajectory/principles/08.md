## Principle 8: Use ImageNet Pretraining for Vision-Based Models

*Consensus: 5/5 Lyft solutions, 1/1 NFL vision solution that mentioned it*

Initialize CNN backbones (EfficientNet, ResNet, Xception) with ImageNet-pretrained weights, even when your input is rasterized maps or overhead views, not natural images.

**Why this matters:** Pretraining provides a massive head start—Lyft: loading random weights vs ImageNet degraded score by ~1.0. The low/mid-level features (edges, textures, spatial hierarchies) transfer even to non-photographic domains. Training from scratch on trajectory data alone is too slow and prone to overfitting.

**When to use:** Whenever you rasterize spatial data into multi-channel images for CNN processing. Autonomous vehicle maps, overhead sports tracking, satellite/aerial positioning. Even if your channels are semantic (road/lane masks, agent types) rather than RGB.

**When NOT to use:** Non-vision models (LightGBM, KNN, RNN on raw sensors). Very small datasets (<10K samples) may not have enough data to fine-tune pretrained models effectively—simpler models might work better. If your raster representation is extremely domain-specific (spectrograms, heatmaps), verify transfer works.

**How to reason about it:**
Are you using a CNN backbone? Yes → start with ImageNet weights. Does your input have spatial structure (edges, objects, layouts)? Yes → low-level features transfer. Train for 1 epoch with pretrained vs random init and compare—if pretrained is significantly better (>5% metric improvement), keep it. Fine-tune all layers (don't freeze backbone)—full fine-tuning works best for large trajectory datasets.

```python
# Load pretrained backbone
from torchvision import models
backbone = models.efficientnet_b5(pretrained=True)  # ImageNet weights

# Replace head for trajectory prediction
backbone.classifier = nn.Sequential(
    nn.Dropout(0.3),
    nn.Linear(backbone.classifier[1].in_features, num_outputs)
)
# Fine-tune ALL layers (don't freeze)
optimizer = torch.optim.SGD(backbone.parameters(), lr=0.01)
```
