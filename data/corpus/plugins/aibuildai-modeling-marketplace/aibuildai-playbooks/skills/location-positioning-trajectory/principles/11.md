## Principle 11: Train with Cosine Annealing to Near-Zero Learning Rate

*Consensus: 4/4 Lyft solutions that discussed LR schedules, 1/1 NFL solution*

Use cosine annealing or other schedules that decay learning rate smoothly to near-zero (1e-6 to 1e-7) by the end of training, not exponential decay that plateaus early. Train for fewer epochs but let each epoch run to full LR decay.

**Why this matters:** Large trajectory datasets (100M+ samples) benefit from long, slow fine-tuning. Cosine annealing to near-zero allows the model to settle into sharper minima in the final phase. Exponential decay stops too early, leaving improvement on the table. The difference was ~0.5–1.0 on Lyft's metric.

**When to use:** Large-scale trajectory prediction with 50M+ samples and 1+ epoch training time. When you're using pretrained models that need careful fine-tuning. Models that are already close to convergence but need final polishing.

**When NOT to use:** Small datasets with heavy augmentation where you train for many epochs—cosine with restarts (CosineAnnealingWarmRestarts) works better. If training is GPU-bound and you can't afford long epochs, step decay is more practical.

**How to reason about it:**
How long is one epoch? If >1 day on your hardware, train for 1–2 epochs with cosine annealing to 0. If <1 hour, train 10+ epochs with restarts. The key: final LR should be 100–1000× smaller than initial. Start at 1e-3 to 1e-2, end at 1e-6 to 1e-7. Monitor validation loss—it should keep improving (slowly) until the very end.

```python
# Cosine annealing for Lyft (1 epoch on full data)
from torch.optim.lr_scheduler import CosineAnnealingLR

optimizer = torch.optim.Adam(model.parameters(), lr=1e-3)
scheduler = CosineAnnealingLR(
    optimizer, 
    T_max=len(train_loader),  # 1 epoch
    eta_min=1e-7  # decay to near-zero
)

for batch in train_loader:
    loss = train_step(batch)
    optimizer.zero_grad()
    loss.backward()
    optimizer.step()
    scheduler.step()  # step per batch, not per epoch
```
