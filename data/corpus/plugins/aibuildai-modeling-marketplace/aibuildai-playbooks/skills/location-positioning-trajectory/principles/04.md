## Principle 4: Choose Continuous vs Discrete Optimization Based on Constraint Density

*Consensus: Indoor navigation top-3 all used discrete; trajectory prediction top-3 used continuous*

Use discrete optimization (beam search, dynamic programming over grid waypoints) when hard constraints dominate (walls, corridors, known waypoints). Use continuous optimization (regression, end-to-end training) when motion is unconstrained or constraints are soft.

**Why this matters:** Discrete optimization guarantees physically valid paths (no wall-crossing) and leverages known waypoints (85% of indoor test predictions are training waypoints). Continuous optimization is faster, differentiable, and better for open spaces where exact grid matching is unlikely (roads, fields).

**When to use:** Discrete: indoor navigation with floor plans, urban road networks with known intersections, any task where >50% of targets match training locations exactly. Continuous: open-field motion (sports, drones), highway driving, tasks with smooth metrics that don't penalize small shifts.

**When NOT to use:** Don't force discrete optimization on sparse grids or open spaces—it'll fail if true positions fall between grid points. Don't use pure continuous models when wall constraints exist—they'll predict impossible paths.

**How to reason about it:**
What fraction of test data lies exactly on training positions? >70% → discrete. Is the space bounded by hard constraints (walls, lanes)? Yes → discrete with penalty for violations. Is the space open and continuous? Yes → continuous regression. Hybrid approach: continuous model + snap-to-grid post-processing.

```python
# Discrete beam search for indoor navigation
def beam_search(start_candidates, trajectory_length, beam_width=2000):
    beams = [(c, wifi_penalty(c)) for c in start_candidates[:beam_width]]
    
    for t in range(1, trajectory_length):
        next_beams = []
        for prev_waypoint, prev_penalty in beams:
            for next_wp in get_reachable_waypoints(prev_waypoint):
                penalty = (prev_penalty + 
                          wifi_penalty(next_wp, t) +
                          sensor_penalty(prev_waypoint, next_wp, t))
                next_beams.append((next_wp, penalty))
        beams = sorted(next_beams, key=lambda x: x[1])[:beam_width]
    return beams[0][0]
```
