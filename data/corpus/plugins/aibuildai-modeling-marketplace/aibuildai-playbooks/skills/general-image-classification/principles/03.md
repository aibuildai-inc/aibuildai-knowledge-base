## Principle 3: Apply Augmentation Progressively from None to Soft to Hard

*Consensus: 2/4 Inclusive Images solutions explicitly described progressive augmentation*

Train first without augmentation until plateau, then add soft augmentation (wide parameter ranges), then hard augmentation (narrow ranges or extreme values). This staged approach prevents early-training instability while maximizing generalization.

**Why this matters:** Heavy augmentation early in training can destabilize learning when the model hasn't learned basic features. Progressive augmentation lets the model first learn clean patterns, then gradually adapt to variations, improving final robustness without hurting convergence.

**When to use:** When training from scratch (no ImageNet pretraining, as in Inclusive Images), or when the task requires strong generalization across geographic/domain shifts. Also useful for very large models that train slowly (InceptionResNetV2 at 598x598 took 38 hrs/epoch).

**When NOT to use:** Fine-tuning from strong pretrained weights where the model already knows basic features—use augmentation from the start. Also skip if training time is limited; in that case, choose one augmentation strength and stick with it.

**How to reason about it:**
Stage 1: Train without augmentation until validation loss plateaus (model learns clean data distribution). Stage 2: Add soft augmentation (rotate ±30°, shift 0-20%, scale 0.8-1.2x, color jitter) for several epochs. Stage 3: Apply hard augmentation (rotate only ±30° at extremes, aggressive distortions, heavy blur/noise). Monitor: validation accuracy should improve with each stage. If it drops, augmentation is too aggressive too early.

```python
# Inclusive Images 2nd place progressive schedule
# Stage 1: No augmentation
train(model, augmentation=None, epochs=10)  # Until plateau

# Stage 2: Soft augmentation (wide ranges)
soft_aug = Compose([
    RandomRotate(limit=30),  # ±30 degrees
    ShiftScaleRotate(shift_limit=0.1, scale_limit=0.2, rotate_limit=10),
    HueSaturationValue(hue_shift_limit=20, sat_shift_limit=30)
])
train(model, augmentation=soft_aug, epochs=5)

# Stage 3: Hard augmentation (extremes or narrow range)
hard_aug = Compose([
    RandomRotate(limit=30, p=1.0),  # Always ±30 at extremes
    OneOf([OpticalDistortion(), GridDistortion()], p=0.5),
    RandomBrightness(limit=0.3), CLAHE()
])
train(model, augmentation=hard_aug, epochs=5)
```
