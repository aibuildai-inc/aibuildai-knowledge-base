## Principle 1: Choose Architecture Ensembles for Complementary Inductive Biases

*Consensus: 5/5 QuickDraw top solutions, 4/4 Inclusive Images solutions used diverse architectures*

Ensemble models with different architectural families (ResNet, ResNeXt, Inception, SE-Net, DenseNet, Xception) rather than multiple instances of the same architecture. Architectural diversity captures different feature hierarchies.

**Why this matters:** Different architectures make different errors. ResNets excel at deep residual learning, Inception at multi-scale features, SE-Nets at channel attention. Ensembling diverse architectures reduces correlated errors and improves robustness.

**When to use:** Always for final submissions when compute allows. Especially critical when train/test distributions differ (Inclusive Images geographic shift) or when the task has high visual diversity (thousands of categories).

**When NOT to use:** Early prototyping or when compute is severely limited. In QuickDraw, a single good model achieved gold; ensembles just improved the margin. Also skip if models are too correlated (e.g., all ResNet variants of similar depth).

**How to reason about it:**
Start with one strong baseline (e.g., ResNet50 or InceptionResNetV2). Add architectures from different families: one with residual connections, one multi-scale (Inception), one with attention (SE-Net). Train each separately. Check prediction correlation: argmax(model1) vs argmax(model2). If correlation >0.95, models are redundant. Aim for correlation 0.85-0.92 for good diversity without sacrificing individual strength. Weight by validation performance.

```python
# Ensemble diverse architectures
models = [
    ('resnet50', ResNet50(weights='imagenet')),
    ('inception_v3', InceptionV3(weights='imagenet')),
    ('se_resnext101', SEResNeXt101()),  # Attention
    ('xception', Xception(weights='imagenet'))  # Depthwise separable
]

# Check diversity
corr_matrix = np.corrcoef([np.argmax(m.predict(X_val), axis=1) 
                           for _, m in models])
print(f"Avg correlation: {corr_matrix.mean():.3f}")  # Target 0.85-0.92

# Weighted average by val score
weights = [1/loss for loss in val_losses]  # Or (1/loss)**k
ensemble_pred = np.average([m.predict(X) for m in models], 
                           weights=weights, axis=0)
```
