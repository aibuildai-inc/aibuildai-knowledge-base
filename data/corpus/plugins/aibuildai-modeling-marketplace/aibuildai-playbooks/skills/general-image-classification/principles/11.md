## Principle 11: Average Multiple Checkpoints or Snapshots for Stability

*Consensus: 2/8 QuickDraw solutions averaged checkpoints*

Instead of using a single final checkpoint, average the weights of the last N checkpoints (e.g., top 4 or last 10) or their predictions. This smooths out noise from stochastic training.

**Why this matters:** Training with large batches and noisy data causes weights to fluctuate. Averaging multiple snapshots captures different local minima and reduces variance. QuickDraw 21st place averaged top 4 checkpoints by validation score.

**When to use:** When training is noisy (large datasets, aggressive augmentation, large batch sizes) OR when validation scores fluctuate across epochs. Especially useful with learning rate schedules like cosine annealing that cycle through different minima.

**When NOT to use:** When training is stable and validation curve is smooth (small clean datasets). Also skip if saving multiple checkpoints is infeasible (storage limits). Not needed if you use early stopping with a clear best epoch.

**How to reason about it:**
Save checkpoints every K steps or every epoch. At the end, either: (1) average the weights of the last N checkpoints, or (2) predict with each checkpoint separately and average predictions. Option 2 is safer (no risk of invalid averaged weights). Choose N=4-10 based on validation stability. For cosine annealing: average snapshots from the last cycle.

```python
# Average last 10 checkpoints (weight averaging)
checkpoints = [load_checkpoint(f'model_epoch_{i}.pth') 
               for i in range(90, 100)]  # Last 10 epochs

avg_state_dict = {}
for key in checkpoints[0].keys():
    avg_state_dict[key] = torch.stack(
        [ckpt[key].float() for ckpt in checkpoints]
    ).mean(dim=0)

model.load_state_dict(avg_state_dict)

# Or prediction averaging (safer)
predictions = [model_from_ckpt(ckpt).predict(X_test) 
               for ckpt in checkpoints]
avg_prediction = np.mean(predictions, axis=0)

# QuickDraw 14th: averaged last 10 weights, saved every 5K steps
```
