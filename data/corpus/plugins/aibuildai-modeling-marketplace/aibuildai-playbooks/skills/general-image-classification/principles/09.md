## Principle 9: Ensemble via Weighted Averaging by Validation Performance Raised to a Power

*Consensus: 3/8 QuickDraw solutions used powered weighting*

When ensembling, weight models by (1/validation_loss)^k where k is tuned (e.g., k=24). This amplifies the contribution of the best models while still including diverse weaker ones.

**Why this matters:** Equal-weight or simple (1/loss) weighting underweights the best model. Raising to a power (k>1) concentrates weight on top performers while maintaining some diversity from weaker models. QuickDraw winner used k=24; this beats equal weighting.

**When to use:** When you have many models with varying performance and want to bias toward the best while keeping diversity. Especially useful when top model is significantly better than others (10%+ gap).

**When NOT to use:** When models have similar performance (within 1-2%); equal weighting or simple ranking weights suffice. Also skip if you lack a holdout set to tune k (risk overfitting to validation). For small ensembles (2-3 models), use equal weights or manual tuning.

**How to reason about it:**
Compute validation loss for each model. Try powers k in [1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50]: weights = (1/loss)**k, normalize. Evaluate ensemble on validation. Choose k that maximizes validation metric. Higher k -> more concentration on best model. Also consider: rank-based weights (1/rank) or score-based (score - min_score). Check correlation: if top 2 models are highly correlated (>0.95), high k risks losing diversity.

# QuickDraw winner's ensemble with powered weighting
val_losses = [0.4937, 0.5092, 0.5123, 0.5201, 0.5345, 0.5412]  # 6 models

# Try different powers
for k in [1, 5, 10, 24, 50]:
    weights = np.array([(1/loss)**k for loss in val_losses])
    weights /= weights.sum()  # Normalize
    
    ensemble_pred = np.average(model_preds, weights=weights, axis=0)

    print(f"k={k}: weights={weights}, score={val_score}")
