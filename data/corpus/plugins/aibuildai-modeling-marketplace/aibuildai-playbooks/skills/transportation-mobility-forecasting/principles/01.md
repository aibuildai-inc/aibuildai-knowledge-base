## Principle 1: Choose Modeling Approach Based on Problem Structure

*Consensus: All solutions: 5 used regression for time-based prediction, 1 used combinatorial optimization for routing*

Transportation problems split into two families requiring fundamentally different approaches: time-based forecasting (predicting durations, congestion, demand) versus routing optimization (sequencing stops to minimize cost).

**Why this matters:** Regression models learn patterns from historical data and generalize via features. Optimization problems have explicit cost functions and require search algorithms (simulated annealing, beam search). Applying the wrong paradigm wastes effort—you cannot optimize routes with regression or predict durations with SA.

**When to use:** Use regression when the target is a continuous value (trip time, sales count, congestion level) and you have historical examples. Use optimization when you're arranging entities to minimize explicit costs (distance × weight, total route length) with hard constraints (capacity limits, must-visit requirements).

**When NOT to use:** Do not use regression for pure sequencing problems with no historical labels. Do not use optimization when there's no explicit cost function or when the problem is learning patterns from noisy real-world data rather than satisfying mathematical constraints.

**How to reason about it:**
Ask: Am I predicting an outcome from inputs, or am I arranging a solution to minimize cost? If predicting → regression + features. If arranging → optimization + cost function. Check if there are labeled historical examples (regression) or a formula to score any solution (optimization).
