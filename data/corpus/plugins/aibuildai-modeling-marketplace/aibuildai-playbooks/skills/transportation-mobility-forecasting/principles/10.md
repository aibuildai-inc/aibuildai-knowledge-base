## Principle 10: Blend Models via Stacking or Bagged Ensembles with Random Hyperparameters

*Consensus: 4/5 regression solutions (NYC, Walmart #3, Restaurant #1 implicitly, Geotab)*

Train many diverse models (XGBoost, RandomForest, ExtraTrees, Linear) with different hyperparameters—either fixed configurations or random sampling. For stacking, use a meta-model (XGBoost, weighted average) on out-of-fold predictions. For bagging, train each model on a bootstrap sample and average predictions.

**Why this matters:** Single models overfit to specific patterns. Ensembles reduce variance and capture different aspects of the data. Random hyperparameters create diversity without manual tuning—some models go deep (capture complex interactions), others shallow (regularize). Stacking learns to weigh models based on their strengths in different contexts. Bagging averages away random fluctuations.

**When to use:** Always ensemble if you have compute budget. Use 2-layer stacking for max performance: diverse level-1 models (different algorithms, hyperparameters, feature subsets), simple level-2 meta-model. Use random hyperparameter bagging (train 100-1000 models with random params, average best 50-100) when you want robust results without manual tuning.

**When NOT to use:** Skip if you're compute-limited or need a simple, interpretable model. Do not over-stack (>2 layers) without large validation sets—you'll overfit the meta-model. Avoid bagging if models are identical (no diversity gained).

**How to reason about it:**
Level 1: train 10-20 diverse base models (XGBoost with different depths/learning rates, RF, ExtraTrees, Ridge). Save out-of-fold predictions for stacking. Level 2: train a meta-model (XGBoost or weighted average) on level-1 predictions. For random bagging: loop 1000 times, each time sample hyperparameters uniformly (max_depth 3-10, eta 0.01-0.5, subsample 0.5-0.9), train on bootstrap sample, predict on holdout and test. Select top N models by validation score and average.
