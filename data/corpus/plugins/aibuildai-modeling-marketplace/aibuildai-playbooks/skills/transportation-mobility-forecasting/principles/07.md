## Principle 7: Apply Log Transformation and Predict in Log Space for RMSLE Metrics

*Consensus: 4/4 competitions using RMSLE (NYC, PKDD-15, Walmart)*

When the evaluation metric is RMSLE (Root Mean Squared Logarithmic Error), transform the target with log1p (log(y+1)) and train the model to predict log-space values. Back-transform predictions with expm1 (exp(y)-1). For partial trajectory problems, predict log(additional_time) rather than log(total_time).

**Why this matters:** RMSLE penalizes relative errors—underpredicting a 10-minute trip by 5 minutes hurts more than underpredicting a 60-minute trip by 5 minutes. Training in log space directly optimizes the metric. The +1 offset prevents log(0) errors. Predicting log(additional_time) for partial trips ensures you're modeling the remaining uncertainty, not re-predicting known elapsed time.

**When to use:** Always use when RMSLE is the metric. Always use log1p to avoid log(0) issues. For partial trajectory problems, model log(remaining_time | trajectory_so_far) by predicting log(additional) and adding to log(elapsed).

**When NOT to use:** Do not use log transformation if the metric is raw RMSE or MAE and the target has no multiplicative structure. Do not use log1p if the target can be negative. For classification-style congestion (predict distribution bins), do not log-transform—use ordinal regression or classification.

**How to reason about it:**
Check the competition metric. If RMSLE, set target = np.log1p(raw_target). Train model on log-space target. After prediction, back-transform: pred_raw = np.expm1(pred_log). For partial trips: elapsed = trajectory_length * 15_seconds; predict log(additional); total = elapsed + exp(pred_log).

```python
import numpy as np

# Training
train['log_duration'] = np.log1p(train['trip_duration'])
model.fit(X_train, train['log_duration'])

# Prediction
log_pred = model.predict(X_test)
test['trip_duration'] = np.expm1(log_pred)  # Back to original scale

# For partial trajectory: predict additional time
train['log_additional'] = np.log1p(train['total_time'] - train['elapsed_time'])
model.fit(X_train, train['log_additional'])
pred_additional = np.expm1(model.predict(X_test))
test['pred_total'] = test['elapsed_time'] + pred_additional
```
