## Principle 9: Ensemble Across Diverse Splits, Architectures, and Granularities

*Consensus: 25/30 solutions ensembled multiple models; top solutions used 5-20 models with different CV splits, backbones, or site-level vs. global models*

Environmental data is noisy and no single model captures all patterns. Ensemble models that differ in: (1) CV fold, (2) architecture (GBM vs. NN), (3) granularity (site-specific vs. global), (4) feature sets. Weight by validation performance or use simple averaging. Diversity matters more than individual model strength.

**Why this matters:** A model trained on one CV fold may overfit to that fold's quirks. Ensembling across folds averages out fold-specific noise. Combining different model types (gradient boosting captures tabular interactions, NNs capture image features, random forests are robust to outliers) leverages complementary strengths. Site-specific + global models balance specialization and generalization.

**When to use:** Always for competitive performance. Prioritize diversity: 5 diverse models (different CV splits, different architectures, different preprocessing) beat 10 variations of the same model. When compute allows, train separate models per site/group and ensemble with a global model.

**When NOT to use:** When you need a single deployable model (production systems). When you're early in the competition and exploring ideas (ensembling masks which individual changes help). When models are too correlated (same architecture, same features, same CV) — adding them gives no benefit.

**How to reason about it:**
1. Diversity sources: (a) CV folds: train on all 5 folds, ensemble their test predictions. (b) Architectures: LightGBM, CatBoost, XGBoost, NN. (c) Feature sets: with metadata, without metadata. (d) Granularity: global model + per-site models. (e) Targets: log-transformed vs. raw.
2. Ensembling methods: simple mean (often best), weighted mean (optimize weights on validation), stacking (train a meta-model on out-of-fold predictions).
3. Check pairwise correlation of model predictions: if two models have >0.95 correlation, one is redundant. Keep the better one or average them as a single entry.
4. For heterogeneous ensembles (3 CNNs + 2 GBMs), upweight the minority type slightly to preserve diversity.

```python
# Ensemble pattern: diverse models, weighted average
models = [
    ('lgbm_site0', lgbm_site0),  # site-specific GBM
    ('catboost_global', catboost_global),  # global GBM
    ('resnet_fold0', resnet_fold0),  # CNN from fold 0
    ('dinov2_fold1', dinov2_fold1),  # different backbone, different fold
]
# Optimize weights on validation set (or use equal weights)
weights = [0.3, 0.2, 0.25, 0.25]  # tuned via Optuna on validation
pred_ensemble = sum(w * model.predict(X_test) for w, (name, model) in zip(weights, models))
```
