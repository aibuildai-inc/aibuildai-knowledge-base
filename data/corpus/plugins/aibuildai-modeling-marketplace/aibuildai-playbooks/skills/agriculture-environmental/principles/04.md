## Principle 4: Use Multi-Head Architectures When Targets Share Physical Relationships

*Consensus: 12/20 solutions used separate regression heads for related outputs; top biomass solutions added classification heads alongside regression*

When predicting multiple related quantities (e.g., green biomass, dead biomass, total biomass = green + dead), train separate heads for each but enforce their physical relationships through loss functions or post-processing. Auxiliary classification heads (binning continuous targets) add regularization.

**Why this matters:** Independent single-output models ignore known constraints (total = sum of parts), leading to physically inconsistent predictions. A multi-head model can learn shared representations while specializing per target. Adding a classification head for 'which quantile bin?' helps the model learn ordinal structure and reduces regression variance.

**When to use:** When targets have known additive/multiplicative relationships (biomass components, energy by meter type, multi-element soil composition). When targets vary by orders of magnitude (0.1 to 100g) and benefit from binning for regularization.

**When NOT to use:** When targets are completely independent with no shared latent factors (e.g., predicting both crop yield and an unrelated chemical property). When you have so few samples that adding heads risks overfitting the shared backbone.

**How to reason about it:**
1. List all targets and their physical relationships. E.g., Dry_Total = Dry_Green + Dry_Dead + Dry_Clover.
2. Design separate heads (linear layers) per target, sharing the CNN/backbone. Weight their losses by the competition metric weights.
3. Optionally add classification heads: bin each target into quantiles (e.g., 7 bins from min to max), train a cross-entropy loss. This forces the model to learn 'is this sample low, medium, or high biomass?' before regressing the exact value.
4. In post-processing, enforce constraints: if total < sum-of-parts, scale parts down proportionally.

    def __init__(self, backbone):
        self.backbone = backbone
        self.head_green = nn.Linear(feat_dim, 1)  # regression head

        reg_green = self.head_green(feat)
