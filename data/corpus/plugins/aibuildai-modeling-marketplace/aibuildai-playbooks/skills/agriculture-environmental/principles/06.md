## Principle 6: Engineer Log/Normalized Targets and Reverse Transform, Handling Zeros Carefully

*Consensus: 20/25 regression solutions used log1p transform; multiple solutions tried per-area or per-volume normalization*

Environmental targets often span orders of magnitude (energy: 0.1 to 10,000 kWh; biomass: 0 to 200g). Log-transforming the target stabilizes gradients and makes the model care equally about small and large values. Normalize by a relevant physical quantity (building area, image area) if it's a known scaling factor.

**Why this matters:** A model minimizing MSE on raw values will focus on large values (1% error on 1000 is 10 units; 10% error on 10 is 1 unit). Log-space loss treats relative errors equally. But many targets can be zero (no dead grass, no energy used), so log(0) fails — use log1p or clip. Normalization (energy per sqft) removes a trivial scaling factor, letting the model learn the interesting variation.

**When to use:** When the target ranges over 2+ orders of magnitude and the metric is RMSE or MAE (log-transform). When the target is a rate or density (biomass per area, energy per sqft, soil property per volume) and the denominator is known (normalize by it).

**When NOT to use:** When the target is bounded in a narrow range (pH 0-14, percentages 0-100) — log-transform won't help. When zeros are meaningful and frequent (>20% of data), ensure log1p doesn't compress them too much; consider a two-stage model (zero-or-not classifier, then regress non-zeros). When the metric is RMSLE (already in log space) — don't double-transform.

**How to reason about it:**
1. Plot target histogram. If it's right-skewed (most values small, few large), try log1p.
2. Check for zeros: if >10% are zero and it's physical (no biomass present), use log1p(y). If zeros are sensor errors, remove them in cleaning.
3. If you have a known scaling factor (building sqft, image area in pixels), try target_per_unit = target / scale_factor. Train on log1p(target_per_unit), predict, then multiply back by scale_factor.
4. Validate on CV: does log-space RMSE correlate better with LB than raw-space RMSE?
