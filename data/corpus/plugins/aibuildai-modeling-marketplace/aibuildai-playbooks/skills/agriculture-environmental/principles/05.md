## Principle 5: Train Site/Group-Specific Models When Data Exhibits Strong Clustering

*Consensus: 15/30 solutions trained separate models per site, building, or meter; ASHRAE top-3 all used site+meter-level models*

If your data naturally clusters by site, sensor type, or categorical group with different distributions, train one model per group instead of one global model. This allows each model to specialize on its group's patterns and sidesteps distribution shift.

**Why this matters:** Different sites have different sensor scales, climates, building types, or species compositions. A global model either underfits (averages away site-specific patterns) or overfits to dominant sites. Site-specific models auto-tune to each site's quirks without hand-engineering site-specific features. ASHRAE: separate models per (site, meter) captured site-specific timestamp misalignment and scale differences that were hard to fix in preprocessing.

**When to use:** When you have 10+ distinct groups (sites, building types, meter types) each with 50+ samples. When visualizations show that each group's target distribution or feature relationships differ substantially. When you have group-level metadata that affects the entire group (site timezone, sensor calibration).

**When NOT to use:** When groups are imbalanced: one site with 10,000 samples, another with 10 — the small-site model will overfit. When test sites are completely unseen (then a global model generalizes better). When you have <100 samples total, splitting into groups leaves too few per model.

**How to reason about it:**
1. Plot target distributions and key feature distributions by group (site, meter, state). If they look different, that's a signal.
2. Train one model per group if: (a) each group has >50 samples, (b) groups are known at test time (you can route test samples to the right model).
3. For imbalanced groups, use a hybrid: train site-specific models for large sites, a global model for small sites, ensemble them.
4. Tune hyperparameters per group if compute allows — different sites may benefit from different tree depths, learning rates, etc.
