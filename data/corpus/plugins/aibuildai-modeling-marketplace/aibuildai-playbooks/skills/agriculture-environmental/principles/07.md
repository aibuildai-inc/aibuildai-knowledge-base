## Principle 7: Leverage Domain-Specific Pretrained Backbones Over Generic ImageNet

*Consensus: 18/20 image-based solutions used DINOv2/DINOv3; PlantTraits winner used flora-specific DINOv2 pretrained on Pl@ntNet*

For agriculture/ecology images, foundation models pretrained on natural images (DINOv2, DINOv3) or domain-specific datasets (flora databases, satellite imagery) outperform ImageNet-pretrained CNNs. DINOv3's high-resolution dense features are especially effective for fine-grained tasks (biomass estimation, species ID).

**Why this matters:** ImageNet features are biased toward object recognition (cats, cars); environmental tasks need texture, color, and spatial density features. DINOv2 is self-supervised on diverse natural images and learns better representations for vegetation. Domain-specific pretraining (e.g., on millions of plant photos) gives even larger gains by encoding species-specific features.

**When to use:** For any image-based environmental task. When images are natural scenes (overhead pastures, plant photos, satellite imagery) rather than industrial objects. When you need dense pixel-level features (biomass per region) rather than global image classification.

**When NOT to use:** When your images are highly specialized (microscopy, infrared wavelengths not in DINOv2's training set) and no relevant pretrained model exists — then train from scratch or use a simpler CNN. When you have 100k+ labeled images and can afford full end-to-end training without transfer learning.

**How to reason about it:**
1. Check if a domain-specific pretrained model exists: for plants, use Pl@ntNet-pretrained DINOv2; for satellite, use models pretrained on Sentinel/Landsat.
2. If not, use DINOv2/DINOv3 (preferably v3 for better dense features). Use the largest variant your GPU fits (ViT-L or ViT-H).
3. Fine-tune the entire backbone (not just the head) — environmental images differ enough from DINOv2's training data that the backbone needs adaptation. Use a lower learning rate for backbone layers than the head.
4. For small datasets (<1000 images), freeze early layers and fine-tune only the last few blocks to prevent overfitting.

```python
# Pattern from PlantTraits/Biomass solutions
import timm
model = timm.create_model('vit_large_patch14_dinov2', pretrained=True, num_classes=0)  # feature extractor
# Or use domain-specific checkpoint:
# model.load_state_dict(torch.load('plantnet_dinov2_vitl14.pth'))  # flora-specific

# Two-stage fine-tuning:
# Stage 1: freeze backbone, train head (10 epochs)
for param in model.parameters():
    param.requires_grad = False
head = nn.Linear(model.num_features, num_classes)
optimizer = AdamW(head.parameters(), lr=1e-3)
# Stage 2: unfreeze backbone, train end-to-end (20 epochs, lower LR)
for param in model.parameters():
    param.requires_grad = True
optimizer = AdamW([{'params': model.parameters(), 'lr': 1e-5}, {'params': head.parameters(), 'lr': 1e-4}])
```
