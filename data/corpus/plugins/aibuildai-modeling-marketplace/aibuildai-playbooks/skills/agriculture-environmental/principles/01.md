## Principle 1: Invest Heavily in Domain-Aware Data Cleaning Before Modeling

*Consensus: 20/30+ solutions explicitly emphasized cleaning as critical, often the single largest score improvement*

Real-world environmental data contains systematic measurement errors, sensor failures, and label noise that models will overfit to if not removed. Manual visual inspection combined with domain heuristics outperforms purely statistical outlier removal.

**Why this matters:** Unpredictable anomalies (sensor drift, recording errors, extreme weather) are unlearnable patterns. Training on them inflates target means artificially and degrades predictions. ASHRAE top teams spent weeks on manual per-building cleaning; one team reported this as their largest single improvement.

**When to use:** When training data comes from real sensors/field measurements across sites or time; when visualizations show vertical 'streaks' of constant values, synchronized spikes across buildings/locations, or physically impossible values (negative biomass, energy readings during known shutdowns).

**When NOT to use:** When data is synthetically generated or comes from controlled lab conditions with verified quality control. Skip site-by-site manual review if you have 10,000+ sites (automate heuristics instead), but keep cross-site validation (anomalies affecting multiple sites simultaneously are likely real).

**How to reason about it:**
1. Visualize time series per building/site/sensor looking for: long constant-value streaks, sudden spikes inconsistent with neighbors, values violating physical bounds.
2. Cross-validate anomalies: if the same pattern appears at the same time across multiple independent sensors/locations, it's likely a real event (weather) not noise.
3. For each anomaly type, write a domain-specific removal rule (e.g., 'site-0 meter-0 from Jan-May 2016 all zeros' vs. statistical z-score cutoffs).
4. Track what you remove and validate that CV/LB improves; if a cleaning step hurts both, the 'noise' was signal.

```python
# ASHRAE pattern: site-wide synchronized anomaly removal
# Check if anomaly appears across multiple buildings at same site
site_buildings = df[df.site_id == site].building_id.unique()
for timestamp in suspicious_times:
    affected = df[(df.site_id == site) & (df.timestamp == timestamp) & (df.meter_reading == 0)]
    if len(affected.building_id.unique()) >= 0.8 * len(site_buildings):  # 80%+ buildings affected
        df = df[~((df.site_id == site) & (df.timestamp == timestamp))]  # remove entire site-time block
```
