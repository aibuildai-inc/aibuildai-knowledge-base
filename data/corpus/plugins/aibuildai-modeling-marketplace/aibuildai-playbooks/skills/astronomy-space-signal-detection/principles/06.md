## Principle 6: Choose Between Deep Learning and Classical Methods by Uncertainty Requirements

*Consensus: Ariel 2nd place used pure Bayesian inference with NO deep learning and scored 0.742; multiple solutions combined both approaches*

When the task requires calibrated uncertainty estimates (confidence intervals, covariance matrices) or has a well-understood forward model, Bayesian inference with Gaussian processes can outperform or complement deep learning. For pure classification/regression, deep learning usually wins.

**Why this matters:** Gaussian processes provide principled uncertainty quantification by returning full posterior distributions, critical for scientific applications where downstream analysis needs error bars. Deep learning achieves better point predictions but uncertainty is ad-hoc (MC dropout, ensembles). The Ariel competition specifically required sigma predictions, and the Bayesian approach excelled by modeling noise, drift, transit physics, and wavelength correlation in a unified probabilistic framework.

**When to use:** Use Bayesian/GP methods when: (1) task explicitly requires uncertainty estimates, (2) you have a parametric forward model of signal generation (transit model, drift model), (3) data is limited and you need to encode strong priors, (4) interpretability matters. Ensemble with deep learning - use GP for uncertainty, deep learning for point predictions, or use GP on top of deep features.

**When NOT to use:** Do not use GPs for high-dimensional inputs (>10k features) without sparse approximations (KISS-GP). Do not use Bayesian methods when you lack domain knowledge to specify priors - wrong priors are worse than no priors. For pure classification with abundant data, deep learning is simpler and more accurate. For multi-modal data (images + metadata), deep learning handles heterogeneity better.

**How to reason about it:**
Ask: (1) Does the task require uncertainty estimates? If yes, consider Bayesian methods. (2) Can you write down a forward model? If yes, GPs can incorporate it. (3) How much data do you have? With <1000 samples, GP priors help; with >100k, deep learning's capacity wins. (4) Hybrid approach: use deep learning to extract features, then GP to model residuals and uncertainty. The Ariel 2nd place solution modeled noise, star spectrum, 1D drift (time), 2D drift (time+wavelength), transit window, and transit depth variation as separate GP/prior elements, then solved the joint posterior.
