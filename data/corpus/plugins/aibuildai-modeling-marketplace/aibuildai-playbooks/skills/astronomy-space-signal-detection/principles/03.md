## Principle 3: Generate Synthetic Training Data from Physics Simulators

*Consensus: Top solutions in g2net (1st place) and multiple others; 10+ solutions explicitly described simulation pipelines*

Reverse-engineer or replicate the data generation process using physics packages (PyCBC/LALSuite for gravitational waves, TauREx/ExoSim for exoplanets) to create unlimited augmented training data with known ground truth and controlled SNR distributions.

**Why this matters:** Competition datasets are finite and often heavily imbalanced toward high-SNR examples, but test sets contain many low-SNR marginal cases. Synthetic data lets you control the SNR distribution to match the difficulty of test data, practice curriculum learning (pretrain on easier high-SNR, finetune on realistic low-SNR), and learn parameter regression as auxiliary tasks to improve feature learning.

**When to use:** Use when: (1) you can identify or reverse-engineer the simulator (check competition overview, read referenced papers, or probe organizer notebooks), (2) training data is limited or imbalanced, (3) test data contains signal types absent from training. Pretrain on synthetic for 2-4 epochs, then train on real data.

**When NOT to use:** Do not use synthetic data blindly with guessed parameters - the 1st place g2net team found that naive simulation with wrong parameter distributions was useless. You must iteratively tune parameter distributions by training a model to regress parameters, checking predictions against real data statistics, and adjusting. Do not let synthetic data dominate if test distribution differs substantially from what you can simulate.

**How to reason about it:**
Step 1: Identify the likely simulator from competition materials or domain research. Step 2: Generate a grid of signals with varying parameters and predict them with a model trained on real data to reverse-engineer parameter ranges. Step 3: Compare PSD of real signal+noise vs pure noise to estimate signal strength distributions. Step 4: Implement iterative loop: generate data -> train model with parameter regression -> compare predicted parameters on real data to true training statistics -> adjust parameter distributions. Step 5: Inject signals at realistic SNR following max(N(μ, σ), min_snr) based on training loss trends.
