## Principle 2: Apply Physics-Informed Preprocessing Before Learning

*Consensus: Critical in 18/19 gravitational wave solutions, 15/19 Ariel solutions, universally mentioned across competitions*

Use domain-specific signal conditioning (bandpass filtering, whitening, gain-drift correction) derived from known instrument physics and noise characteristics to enable the model to learn signal vs noise, not instrument artifacts.

**Why this matters:** Raw sensor data is dominated by instrument noise with known spectral characteristics. Without preprocessing, models overfit to noise patterns rather than learning the target signal. Whitening normalizes frequency-dependent noise to make weak signals visible; bandpass removes out-of-band contamination; gain-drift correction removes systematic detector variations.

**When to use:** Always assess data PSD (power spectral density) first. Use bandpass filtering when the signal has known frequency bounds and noise exists outside that range. Use whitening when noise power varies strongly with frequency and you have sufficient sequence length (>4s) to estimate stable PSD. Use gain-drift correction when detector response drifts over time (common in space telescopes).

**When NOT to use:** Do not apply whitening on very short sequences (<2s) without boundary handling - standard packages corrupt boundaries. Do not use aggressive filtering that might clip the signal band. The 2nd-place Ariel solution succeeded with Bayesian inference and NO deep learning, showing preprocessing can be sufficient if physics is well-understood.

**How to reason about it:**
Step 1: Estimate noise PSD from signal-free regions or negative samples. Step 2: Check if PSD is flat - if not, whitening helps. Step 3: Identify signal frequency range from physics (e.g., gravitational waves: 20-400 Hz for LIGO sensitivity). Step 4: For short sequences, extend data before windowing to reduce boundary corruption. Step 5: If test data PSD differs from training (common with synthetic data), generate custom noise matching test PSD.
