## Principle 13: Validate Preprocessing Choices by Checking PSD and Augmenting Based on It

*Consensus: G2net 1st place reverse-engineered PSD to generate noise; multiple solutions compared train vs test PSD; fundamental to signal processing approaches*

Always inspect the power spectral density of your data to understand noise characteristics, verify that preprocessing (filtering, whitening) preserves signal while removing noise, and use observed PSD to generate realistic synthetic noise for augmentation.

**Why this matters:** Blindly applying standard preprocessing can corrupt signals or leave noise. The g2net 1st place team used Welch's method to estimate PSD and discovered the noise was generated (not real LIGO data), allowing them to generate custom noise matching the competition's PSD for unlimited augmentation. They also found detector-specific artifacts (300 Hz peaks) and added a notch filter. Without PSD analysis, they would have missed this.

**When to use:** Always compute PSD for time-series data before preprocessing. Use it to: (1) identify noise frequency ranges for filtering, (2) detect artifacts (line noise, detector resonances), (3) verify synthetic data matches real data, (4) generate realistic noise for augmentation by sampling from the observed PSD. Compare train vs test PSD to detect distribution shifts.

**When NOT to use:** Do not rely solely on PSD for non-stationary data where noise characteristics change over time - compute time-frequency representations (spectrograms). Do not assume PSD from literature matches your competition data - always compute it from the data itself.

**How to reason about it:**
Step 1: Compute PSD using Welch's method on signal-free regions or negative samples: f, psd = welch(data, fs=sample_rate, nperseg=256). Step 2: Plot PSD and look for: (a) frequency bands with elevated noise -> bandpass filter, (b) narrow spikes -> notch filter, (c) differences between train/test -> generate custom noise. Step 3: Use PSD to generate noise for augmentation: sample Gaussian noise in frequency domain, multiply by sqrt(PSD), inverse FFT. Step 4: Validate preprocessing by checking that signal band is preserved and noise is reduced.

```python
from scipy.signal import welch
import numpy as np

# Compute PSD from noise-only samples
freqs, psd = welch(noise_samples, fs=2048, nperseg=512, average='mean')

# Check for artifacts
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
plt.loglog(freqs, psd)
plt.xlabel('Frequency (Hz)')
plt.ylabel('PSD')
plt.show()  # Look for peaks, elevated bands

# Generate realistic noise from observed PSD
def generate_noise_from_psd(psd, freqs, duration, sample_rate):
    n_samples = int(duration * sample_rate)
    # Random phase Gaussian noise in frequency domain
    noise_fft = np.random.randn(len(freqs)) + 1j * np.random.randn(len(freqs))
    # Scale by PSD
    noise_fft *= np.sqrt(psd)
    # Convert to time domain
    noise = np.fft.irfft(noise_fft, n=n_samples)
    return noise

# Augment training data with realistic noise
augmented_signal = clean_signal + generate_noise_from_psd(observed_psd, freqs, duration=2, sample_rate=2048)
```
