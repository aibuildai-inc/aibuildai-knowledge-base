## Principle 11: Model Irregular Sampling Explicitly

*Consensus: PLAsTiCC top solutions handled sparse, irregularly sampled multi-band light curves with Gaussian-process interpolation (1st place) or with recurrent and convolutional encoders instead of treating them as regular time series; one team found GP-based features added nothing to their CV*

When observations are irregular in time (light curves with seasonal gaps and bands observed at different times), do not force them onto a regular grid without thought. Interpolate with a model that carries uncertainty, or give sequence models the time gaps explicitly.

**Why this matters:** A regular-grid model assumes evenly spaced samples, so gaps and uneven cadence turn into fake structure. A Gaussian process fitted across time and wavelength gives a smooth curve with error bars from which peak brightness, widths, and colors can be measured; a recurrent encoder that receives flux, flux error, and time difference can reason about the gaps itself.

**When to use:** Use when: (1) sampling is irregular or sparse in time, (2) different channels or bands are observed at different times, or (3) segments are missing.

**When NOT to use:** Do not use these methods for regularly sampled data (strain time series, spectrograms, spectra, images) - CNNs and RNNs on the regular grid are faster and more data-efficient. Do not assume GP features always help: one PLAsTiCC team saw no CV gain from GP-based aggregation, and another had trouble using GP-generated samples for augmentation because detection flags and flux errors did not carry over.

**How to reason about it:**
Step 1: Plot the sampling pattern: time gaps per object, bands per night, missing segments. Step 2: Choose the representation: (a) GP interpolation, then features or a resampled regular grid, (b) a sequence model whose inputs include the time difference to the previous observation. Step 3: Compare against simple aggregate features on the raw observations under the same folds. Step 4: Keep the uncertainty (GP standard deviation or measurement error) as an input feature, because sparse regions are less reliable.

```python
# Gaussian-process interpolation of an irregular multi-band light curve
from sklearn.gaussian_process import GaussianProcessRegressor
from sklearn.gaussian_process.kernels import Matern, WhiteKernel

X = np.column_stack([lc['mjd'], lc['wavelength']])  # Irregular times, several bands
kernel = Matern(length_scale=[time_scale, wavelength_scale], nu=1.5) + WhiteKernel()
gp = GaussianProcessRegressor(kernel=kernel, alpha=lc['flux_err'] ** 2).fit(X, lc['flux'])
grid = np.column_stack([np.repeat(t_grid, n_bands), np.tile(band_wavelengths, len(t_grid))])
flux_mean, flux_std = gp.predict(grid, return_std=True)  # Features, or a regular input grid

# Sequence input that exposes the gaps explicitly
dt = np.diff(lc['mjd'], prepend=lc['mjd'][0])
seq = np.column_stack([lc['flux'], lc['flux_err'], dt])  # Feed to a bidirectional GRU
```
