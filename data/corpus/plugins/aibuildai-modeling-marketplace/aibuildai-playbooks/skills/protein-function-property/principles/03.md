## Principle 3: Use Biological Retrieval as a First-Class Expert, Not Merely a Feature

*Consensus: 3/9 CAFA writeups explicitly built sequence-, structure-, or PPI-neighbor label transfer; it is included despite lower frequency because these independently strong experts appeared in top-5 solutions in both CAFA rounds.*

Retrieve annotated neighbors under sequence, structure, or interaction similarity and transfer their labels with similarity-aware weights. Treat retrieval as a separate expert so its high precision near known families remains distinct from a learned model's smoother extrapolation.

**Why this matters:** Protein function is strongly conserved, but different similarity channels fail in different regions: sequence can diverge while structure persists, and interaction partners can inform biological process even when they say little about molecular function. A parametric model may blur these sharp local signals.

**When to use:** Use retrieval when databases provide trustworthy train labels and the test proteins have measurable sequence, structure, family, or PPI neighbors. Gate modalities by ontology aspect—for example, PPI was useful mainly for biological process in one top solution.

**When NOT to use:** Do not transfer labels from weak or biologically unrelated hits, and do not assume every modality helps every aspect. For remote proteins with no credible neighbors, fall back to learned sequence representations, taxonomic priors, or calibrated background rates.

**How to reason about it:**
Measure neighbor coverage and similarity separately from aggregate score. Ask whether performance rises monotonically with similarity, whether transferred annotations use compatible evidence and ontology versions, and whether each modality adds errors uncorrelated with the learned model.

```python
def transfer_labels(neighbors, labels, min_sim):
    scores, denom = {}, 0.0
    for protein_id, sim in neighbors:
        if sim < min_sim:
            continue
        denom += sim
        for term in labels[protein_id]:
            scores[term] = scores.get(term, 0.0) + sim
    return {term: value / max(denom, 1e-12) for term, value in scores.items()}
```
