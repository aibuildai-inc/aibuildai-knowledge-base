## Principle 9: Align Supervision with the Metric and Observation Process

*Consensus: 7/14 writeups explicitly used IA weighting, ontology-conditional training, rank targets, or rank-transformed components; the competition metrics make this principle broadly applicable.*

Train for what can be observed and what the metric rewards. For GO, account for information weights, aspect-specific evaluation, and the fact that unknown annotations are not confirmed negatives; for Spearman tasks, emphasize ordering or mutation deltas rather than absolute assay calibration.

**Why this matters:** Plain BCE treats all missing GO terms as equally certain negatives and all terms as equally valuable, which conflicts with open-world weighted F-max. Plain MSE spends capacity fitting temperature scale even though any monotone transformation leaves Spearman unchanged and study baselines may differ.

**When to use:** Use IA-aware loss or model selection when rare informative GO terms matter, and condition aspect models on the relevant knowledge state when evaluation does so. For ranking, use within-group ranks, pairwise losses, or regression followed by rank-aware validation.

**When NOT to use:** Do not apply aggressive class or IA weights without checking gradient stability; one CAFA writeup found careful scaling essential. Avoid pairwise ranking if groups are tiny or absolute calibration is operationally required beyond the competition metric.

**How to reason about it:**
Derive which prediction changes alter the official score. Reproduce the evaluator locally, inspect aspect and knowledge-state scores, and choose a surrogate that preserves those incentives without becoming numerically unstable.
