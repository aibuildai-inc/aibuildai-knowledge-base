## Principle 10: Prefer Simple, Diverse Learners Before Unvalidated Architectural Complexity

*Consensus: 10/14 writeups reported strong linear models, boosting, random forests, simple MLPs, local descriptors, or physics scores; several complex alternatives failed to beat them.*

Establish strong simple experts on high-quality biological representations before adding specialized deep architectures. Use linear models, multi-output boosting, shallow networks, or direct physics scores as distinct inductive biases rather than assuming a larger network is superior.

**Why this matters:** Pretrained embeddings and curated structural descriptors already contain much of the useful representation. In small, shifted, or sparse datasets, model complexity can amplify noise; simple models also make it affordable to cover more labels, folds, and seeds.

**When to use:** Use simple models as mandatory baselines and ensemble components, especially when sample size is small relative to feature or label dimension. Add graph or equivariant networks only after showing gains on a leakage-resistant split.

**When NOT to use:** Do not reject specialized architectures when raw spatial tensors or hierarchy interactions genuinely require them. A hierarchy-aware neural module and graph stacker were useful in CAFA, but their value came from encoded structure, not complexity alone.

**How to reason about it:**
Hold the representation and split fixed, then compare model families by score, variance, runtime, output coverage, and residual correlation. Complexity earns its place only if it contributes unique out-of-fold signal or solves a known invariance that preprocessing cannot.
