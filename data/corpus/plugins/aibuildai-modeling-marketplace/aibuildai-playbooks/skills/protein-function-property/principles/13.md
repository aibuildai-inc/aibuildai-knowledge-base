## Principle 13: Make Inference Policy Part of the Model

*Consensus: 8/14 writeups explicitly tuned propagation, top-N selection, score thresholds, smoothing, or rank-space aggregation.*

Design score propagation, calibration, sparsification, and formatting against the official evaluator and submission constraints. For GO, tune per-aspect retention and thresholds after hierarchy propagation; for Spearman, preserve robust ordering and avoid unnecessary calibration.

**Why this matters:** A strong raw predictor can lose score through inconsistent hierarchy, excessive generic labels, omitted rare terms, or brittle score extremes. Conversely, monotone transformations do not change Spearman, so absolute temperature-like values are irrelevant unless they stabilize blending.

**When to use:** Use local evaluator sweeps for GO thresholds, per-aspect top-N, known-label policy, valid-term filtering, and output caps. For rank tasks, blend normalized ranks when components have incompatible scales and smooth only when robustness checks support it.

**When NOT to use:** Do not overfit post-processing to a tiny public set or assume one global threshold suits BP, MF, and CC. Avoid arbitrary clipping or subtractive smoothing without out-of-fold evidence; stability writeups showed that public-optimal blends often reordered badly on private data.

**How to reason about it:**
Reproduce submission-time propagation exactly, then evaluate inference policies across folds and meaningful strata. Separate probability calibration from set selection: F-max depends on score ordering across thresholds, while submission caps and open-world labels determine how aggressively to truncate.

```python
def finalize_go(rows, graph, valid_terms, top_n_by_aspect, min_score):
    rows = propagate_rows_by_max(rows, graph)
    rows = rows[rows.term.isin(valid_terms)]
    rows = rows[rows.score >= min_score]
    rows = (rows.sort_values("score", ascending=False)
                 .groupby(["protein", "aspect"], as_index=False)
                 .head(rows["aspect"].map(top_n_by_aspect)))
    return rows
```
